Focused on: AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization, data attribution, subliminal learning.

## Top 10

### 1. Grad Detect: Gradient-Based Hallucination Detection in LLMs
**[2606.24790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24790)** · Kamat, Blake, Werness

- Detects LLM hallucinations using layer-wise gradient patterns from a single forward-backward pass at inference time.
- Key interpretability finding: the **final five layers concentrate over 97% of the discriminative gradient signal**, enabling lightweight deployment.
- Outperforms confidence-based and sampling-based baselines on Q&A hallucination benchmarks and model abstention prediction.
- Offers a unified framework for assessing multiple dimensions of LLM reliability with interpretable failure insights.
- *Accepted at the ICML 2026 Compositional Learning Workshop.*

### 2. Cyclic Denoising Reveals Ultrastable Memories in Diffusion Models
**[2606.24000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24000)** · Sharma, Martiniani

- Introduces **cyclic denoising** (repeated forward-and-reverse diffusion at controlled noise amplitudes) as a gradient-free extraction attack on image diffusion models.
- Drives samples toward attractors of varying stability; the most stable attractors survive thousands of cycles and correspond to memorized training images (stock photos, watermarks, web artifacts).
- Reveals a **yielding-like phase transition** controlled by noise amplitude — below threshold, samples lock into memorized attractors.
- Tested on Stable Diffusion v1.4 and pixel-space DDPM; has direct implications for privacy, copyright auditing, and model fingerprinting.

### 3. MGI: Member vs Generated Inference
**[2606.23872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23872)** · Zhao, Meintz, Xu, Boenisch, Dziedzic

- Formalizes a new data attribution problem: distinguishing whether a sample is from a model's **training set vs. synthetically generated by the model itself**.
- Existing membership inference methods fail because both training members and generated outputs produce similarly elevated likelihood signals.
- Introduces **Data Circuit Breaker (DCB)**: combines signals from a generative model's autoencoder and latent generator to separate the two cases.
- Works across autoregressive and diffusion models, including near-duplicate reproduction scenarios and models trained on generated data.
- *Accepted at ECCV 2026.*

### 4. Natural Identifiers for Privacy and Data Audits in Large Language Models
**[2606.24408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24408)** · Rossi, Marek, Boenisch, Dziedzic

- Proposes **natural identifiers (NIDs)** — structured random strings (e.g. cryptographic hashes) that appear organically in training data — as probes for post-hoc privacy audits.
- Enables **differential privacy audits without retraining** and dataset membership inference without a held-out private dataset.
- Bypasses the usual requirement for inserting specially crafted canaries at training time, making audits applicable to already-deployed models.

### 5. You Don't Need to Run Every Eval
**[2606.24020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24020)** · Zeng, Papailiopoulos

- Analyzes 84 frontier models across 133 benchmarks and finds benchmark scores have a **rank-2 structure**: a model's full scorecard is largely determined by just two numbers.
- Introduces **BenchPress**, a logit-space matrix completion method that predicts held-out eval scores within ~4.6 points.
- A set of **five benchmarks** (GPQA-D, HLE, Codeforces, MMLU-Pro, ARC-AGI-1) suffices to predict the full scorecard within 3.93 points.
- Releases score matrix, BenchPress implementation, and an interactive prediction tool.

### 6. Holistic Data Scheduler for LLM Pre-training via Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning
**[2606.24133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24133)** · Dang, Ma, Liao

- Proposes **HDS**: a framework that dynamically adjusts data mixture during LLM pre-training using Soft Actor-Critic RL.
- Reward function is multi-dimensional: data quality, inter-domain relationships, and model weight characteristics.
- Achieves comparable performance to static-mix baselines with **44% fewer training iterations** on The Pile; +7.2% on MMLU 0-shot.

### 7. AsyncOPD: How Stale Can On-Policy Distillation Be?
**[2606.24143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24143)** · Kang et al.

- Studies how **stale rollout data** affects student training when rollout generation is decoupled from learner updates (asynchronous distillation).
- Forward KL with teacher weighting is robust to staleness; reverse KL with student weighting degrades and benefits from recomputing the signal on the current student.
- Introduces multi-sample Monte Carlo to balance bias-variance under finite teacher-score caches.
- AsyncOPD achieves **1.6×–3.8× throughput improvement** over synchronous on-policy distillation at comparable accuracy.

### 8. Are Safety Guarantees in Neural Networks Safe? How to Compute Trustworthy Robustness Certifications
**[2606.23858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23858)** · Papamichail, Varsos, Flouris, Marques-Silva

- Proves that **volume-optimal, oracle-based robustness certifications are computationally intractable** — existing "safe" guarantees may be misleading.
- Introduces the *apothem measure* and **dual certifications** (upper bounds on robustness) requiring only a linear number of verifier queries.
- ParallelepipedoNN system achieves at least a **2× improvement in minimum edge length** over prior methods on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST.

### 9. Catastrophic Compositional Generation: Why Vanilla Diffusion Models Fail to Extrapolate
**[2606.23920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23920)** · Soiffer, Squires, Guan, Hartford, Ravikumar

- Shows theoretically that **no inference-time technique** can efficiently recover the target distribution for out-of-distribution compositional generation tasks in conditional diffusion models.
- Score estimation error dominates (not inference error) when sampling from OOD compositional targets — Feynman-Kac corrections help at inference time but cannot overcome this fundamental gap.
- Argues the field needs new architectures or training objectives, not just better inference algorithms.

---

### 10. Fast and Slow Variational Continual Learning
**[2606.24007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24007)** · Paul, Jung, Khan, Swaroop, Möllenhoff, Mundt

- Addresses **stability-plasticity** in continual learning via a neuroscience-inspired fast/slow mechanism within the variational continual learning (VCL) framework.
- Merges past posteriors into a slow-moving prior; fast weights adapt to new tasks under this prior via the **CoVON optimizer** (computational cost ≈ Adam).
- Improves over VCL and weight-regularization baselines across domain-incremental learning, continual pre-training, and LLM fine-tuning scenarios.
