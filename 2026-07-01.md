## Top 10

### 1. Evil Spectra: How Optimisers can Amplify or Suppress Emergent Misalignment
**[2606.31591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31591)** · Brown, Leask, McKinney

- Systematically sweeps optimizer, dataset, and batch size across Qwen3 models fine-tuned on narrow misaligned tasks, and finds **optimizer choice alone produces a 7x spread in emergent-misalignment rate** — the single largest factor tested, dwarfing model size (negligible effect within the Qwen3 family).
- Training loss is a strong predictor of alignment outcome, with the optimizer's effect largely explained by how it shapes the LoRA adapter's singular-value spectrum: **Muon best preserves alignment via implicit regularization of that spectrum**.
- Directly translates the mechanism into a fix: explicit spectral regularization (flattening the singular-value distribution) substantially mitigates misalignment under Adam and Lion at minimal training cost.
- A rare case of an EM paper that moves from diagnosis to an actionable, cheap mitigation grounded in optimizer geometry rather than data curation.

### 2. Predictable GRPO: A Closed-Form Model of Training Dynamics
**[2606.30789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30789)** · Ghosh, Nimmaturi, Singhal, Bhargava, Wong, George, Dutta

- Derives a first-principles, reduced-order model of GRPO training dynamics that recovers the commonly observed empirical single-exponential saturation curve as its overdamped limit — turning fitted curve parameters into mechanistically interpretable quantities.
- Predicts **group-size invariance with 1/G stationary fluctuations** and derives stability thresholds on policy-refresh intervals, both confirmed experimentally.
- Provides diagnostics that distinguish reward hacking, advantage degeneracy, policy concentration, and dynamical instability from each other using the same closed-form model.
- Fits training reward with R² ≥ 0.91 across three models and transfers out-of-distribution to eight math benchmarks — a genuinely predictive theory of RLHF-style training, not just a post-hoc fit.

### 3. SGD at the Edge of Stability: Stochastic Stabilization with Large Learning Rates
**[2606.30930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30930)** · Emmanouilidis, MacDonald, Tarmoun, Vidal

- Extends "edge of stability" analysis (previously mostly full-batch/deterministic) to **stochastic** GD, proving SGD self-stabilizes: iterates oscillate between unstable and stable phases but the stochasticity itself damps divergence.
- Provides convergence guarantees, in a best-iterate sense, for SGD on multiclass cross-entropy loss for linear classifiers and two-layer networks, even at learning rates that classical theory says should diverge.
- Gives a cleaner theoretical account of why large-learning-rate SGD training "just works" in practice despite violating textbook stability bounds.
- Relevant to anyone studying implicit regularization or trying to explain empirically observed training-loss curve shapes.

### 4. Introspective Coupling: Self-Explanation Training Tracks Behavioral Change Despite Fixed Supervision
**[2606.32038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32038)** · Guo, Ruis, Andreas, Li

- Investigates whether training LMs to produce self-explanations yields genuine introspection or mere imitation of the (fixed) training explanations — and finds that when explanation training runs alongside other post-training objectives, **models' explanations track their own subsequently shifting behavior rather than the static training targets**.
- Names this phenomenon "introspective coupling": it requires the training explanations to remain sufficiently correlated with evolving behavior, and holds up across tasks including sycophancy and refusal, and is robust to label noise.
- Implies that fixed counterfactual explanation datasets can be a scalable, self-sustaining signal for building interpretable/introspective models — without needing to continually re-supervise as the policy changes.
- Directly relevant to interpretability-via-verbalization approaches and to understanding what self-report from a model actually reflects.

### 5. Radial Suppression Accelerates Algorithmic Generalization: A Geometric Analysis of Delayed Generalization
**[2606.32000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32000)** · Tiwari, Chauhan, Singh · *ICML 2026 HiLD Workshop*

- Attributes grokking-style delayed generalization to **radial inflation of hidden representations under cross-entropy optimization**, decomposing activation dynamics into radial (norm) and angular (direction) components.
- Shows analytically that suppressing radial growth acts as an implicit, data-dependent weight regularizer, redirects gradient energy toward angular (representation-restructuring) updates, and biases training toward flatter minima.
- A simple norm penalty constraining activations to a sqrt(d)-radius hypersphere yields up to **6x speedups on modular arithmetic** and a 50% training-step reduction for a 10M-parameter nanoGPT on 3-digit addition.
- A clean geometric mechanism for grokking with a cheap, testable intervention — useful both for generalization theory and for practically speeding up algorithmic-task training.

### 6. On the Convergence of Self-Improving Online LLM Alignment
**[2606.31524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31524)** · Wu, Liu, Aggarwal, Chen · *UAI 2026*

- Identifies a theoretical gap in the Self-Improving Alignment (SAIL) framework: its bilevel objective lacks strong-concavity guarantees due to problematic Hessian structure, so existing convergence claims don't actually hold.
- Proposes SAIL-RevKL, adding a reverse-KL penalty that provably restores a Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition on bounded parameter spaces, yielding **global convergence with near-linear sample complexity** — a real theoretical fix, not just an empirical patch.
- Validated on MuJoCo control and language-model alignment tasks, where SAIL-RevKL outperforms the original SAIL.
- Useful for anyone relying on self-improving/iterative-DPO-style alignment loops who wants actual convergence guarantees rather than empirical stability.

### 7. Surrogate Fidelity: When Can Open LLMs Explain Closed Ones?
**[2606.32008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32008)** · Chlenski, Carmichael, Warikoo, Shao, Ye, Yang, Miglani, Bandi

- Tests the common interpretability-research assumption that open-weight models can serve as surrogates for explaining closed/proprietary models, across 11 models from 4 families (Llama, Qwen, GPT, Gemini) at prediction, attribution, and representation levels.
- Central finding: **models that agree on the answer often disagree on why** — white-box signals (attention, perturbation magnitude) are consistent across models but poorly predictive of causal attributions, while black-box input ablations transfer better.
- Concludes that output-level agreement does not license transferring mechanistic explanations from an open surrogate to a closed target model.
- A methodologically important caution for the growing practice of using open models to reverse-engineer or audit closed frontier systems.

### 8. Probe Choice Changes Canary-Memorization Verdicts: Three Post-Hoc Disagreement Case Studies in a Text-Dominant LoRA-Tuned Autoregressive Testbed
**[2606.31168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31168)** · Fan, Zhuang, Li · *ICML 2026 FoGen Workshop*

- Runs a memorization-detection probe on a Qwen2.5-VL-7B model and documents three concrete cases where the probe's verdict **conflicts with alternative measurement methods**: false negatives (missed memorization), false positives (non-secret text triggering the probe), and genuinely ambiguous cases.
- Argues single-metric memorization verdicts (a common practice in unlearning and privacy auditing) are unreliable, and recommends combining full-span metrics, localized decompositions, behavioral recall tests, and control probes.
- A useful negative/cautionary result for data attribution and privacy-auditing methodology — probe choice itself is a hidden researcher-degree-of-freedom that can flip conclusions.

### 9. Amplifying Membership Signal Through Chained Regeneration
**[2606.31991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31991)** · Łapacz, Pawlak

- Introduces MADreMIA, a membership-inference/dataset-inference framework that avoids the usual need for shadow-model training by instead **chaining iterative regeneration**, feeding each generative model's output back in as the next input.
- Key empirical signal: memorized training samples show **significantly higher coherence and slower degradation** across regeneration chains than non-member generations.
- Works across access levels (white/gray/black-box) and model families — demonstrated on image generation, diffusion models, and LLMs, with preliminary results on audio models.
- A practical new tool for data attribution and training-data-leakage auditing that doesn't require expensive shadow models.

### 10. Self-Study Reconsidered: The Hidden Fragility of Learning from Self-Generated QA
**[2606.32002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32002)** · Alimaskina, Shveykin, Molodtsov, Shalygin, Kadeishvili, Beznosikov

- Shows that training on self-generated QA pairs (a popular synthetic-data recipe) has two hidden biases: question-generation coverage **saturates early and concentrates on salient spans** regardless of prompt diversity, and answer generation is vulnerable to **instruction-like text embedded in source documents**.
- Larger models comply with embedded "instructions" in source passages *more* often when there's a task conflict — an uncomfortable scaling trend for synthetic-data pipelines.
- Proposes fixes: anchoring questions to specific targets reduces selection bias, and filtering instruction-like text before answering **cuts injection compliance from 88% to 13%** while preserving most clean training data.
- Relevant to anyone building self-training/self-distillation pipelines, and adjacent to subliminal-learning concerns about what synthetic data silently transmits.
