# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-26

Listing: **Thu, 26 Nov 2026** (143 new submissions + 128 cross-lists; replacements not considered). Summaries are based on the abstracts. Highlights: a clean demonstration that fine-tuning suppresses behaviors fast but erases the underlying representations slowly, weight-based model diffing that surfaces backdoor trigger tokens, a case study showing monitor evaluations swing wildly with the choice of attack policy, and the first controlled retraining test of whether influence-function "top examples" are actually relevant. A pair of papers on Flow-GRPO also splits RL gains into "sharpening" vs. "unlocking."

## Top 10

### 1. [Two Timescales of Forgetting in Fine-tuning of Language Models: Fast Behavioral Suppression and Slow Representational Drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33302)
- Pretrains transformers on the Random Hierarchy Model (a CFG with hierarchical latents), then SFTs on a subset of the generative rules.
- In pretraining, behavior and internal representations of a rule emerge together. In SFT they **decouple**: generation of deleted rules drops on a fast timescale (inversely proportional to token–latent correlation), while their **latent representations stay intact**.
- Those representations are forgotten only much later, through slow parameter drift. Analogous behavior appears in a natural-language setup.
- Directly relevant to why safety/unlearning fine-tunes are shallow and easily undone, and to emergent-misalignment style "dormant" traits.

### 2. [Reading Fine-Tuning Diffs Through Lenses: Model Diffing for Backdoor Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34758)
- A common token-readout scoring system for comparing weight-, activation- and output-based model-diffing methods on backdoored-vs-base pairs.
- Best single method: **ΔW decoded through the base model's J++-lens**, which ranks a backdoor-naming token in its top 10 in 11/12 settings.
- Adversarial twists: penalizing backdoor-word probability on clean inputs breaks output/activation methods (ADL, Diff Mining) but not weight-based ones; permuting hidden units or training only residual-reading layers does the reverse. **No single method resists both attacks.**

### 3. [Attack Policy Selection can Mislead Monitor Evaluators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34934)
- Case study on an ex-production OpenAI monitor: measured false-negative rate ranges **0.5%–20.8%** across RL optimization levels of one attack method, and 6.4%–23.0% across methods (optimized prompting vs. transcript editing).
- All attacks were developed against proxy monitors and transferred to a held-out GPT-5.4 monitor.
- Attack transcripts introduce artefacts (e.g., reasoning about an un-instructed "side task") that sway monitors. Using monitor evals as deployment evidence needs a separate argument for why the proxy attacks are representative, and the authors say that argument is an open problem for AI control.

### 4. [Under the Influence — of What? A Controlled Evaluation of Influence Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34373)
- First systematic **retraining-based** check of whether "influential" pretraining examples actually carry the relevant properties, for GPT-2-style models (68M–5.6B training tokens).
- Compares GradSim, TrackStar and K-FAC on linguistic phenomena and factual knowledge.
- On average the retrieved examples *are* relevant, but often not by directly bearing the target property, and the type and ratio of relevance vary by setting. This is a sober calibration point for data attribution.

### 5. [Tune for Retention, Not Just Gain: SFT Forgets Far Less than You Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34579)
- Challenges the view that SFT's off-policy nature causes catastrophic forgetting.
- **Lower learning rate and smaller batch size** give similar new-task gains with little to no forgetting. Vanilla SFT tuned jointly for gain and retention is competitive with on-policy Self-Distillation Fine-Tuning (SDFT).
- SDFT is more hyperparameter-robust but also needs a retention-aware selection criterion. Open question: what actually drives forgetting? Pairs nicely with #1.

### 6. [What Does RL Post-Training Teach Text-to-Image Models? A Skill-Wise Analysis of Flow-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33316)
- Per-skill pass@k before vs. after Flow-GRPO on SD3.5-M. On 4/6 GenEval skills the gain is **sharpening** (1 post-RL sample ≈ 4–10 base samples; pass@32 barely moves).
- On spatial relations it is **unlocking**: pass@32 goes 0.69→1.00, and prompts the base never solved are solved 93% of the time. Relations carry 35% of the gain.
- Mechanistic account: group-relative updates only re-weight outcomes present in their samples, while shared parameters let successes on some prompts repair a deficit common to many. Tested with held-out-object training, where sharpening comes first and transfers and unlocking follows.
- Companion: [How Many Samples Is RL Worth? Best-of-N vs RL for T2I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33232) reaches the same split with human labels.

### 7. [Deletion You Can Check: Auditing LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33525)
- A membership-inference AUC near 0.5 only implies forgetting if the reference population represents a valid deletion, and that is rarely checked.
- In a 2×2 (ArxivMIA/BookMIA × Llama-3.2-1B/Qwen2.5-1.5B), exact retraining validates the reference on ArxivMIA but **not on BookMIA**, where the trouble traces to population mismatch.
- On the valid axis, **no tested configuration of GA+GD, NPO-RT or SCRUB passes** the joint test of membership removal, deletion-targeted shift and utility.

### 8. [Neighbor Crowding Predicts Feature Stability in Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33182)
- Cross-seed stability of TopK SAE features is highly predictable from **six cheap statistics from a single run**, with no need to train multiple seeds.
- Strongest predictor: **neighbor crowding**. Features in dense regions of the dictionary's unit sphere are more stable than isolated ones.
- Related: [Carrying Feature Ablations Across SAE Seeds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34637) shows that cosine-matched features across seeds have the same causal ablation effect for only 12–70% of pairs.

### 9. [On Grokking and the Dynamics of Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33825)
- Extends Litman & Guo's signal-channel theory of grokking, relating training dissipation and evaluation transfer to population risk for preconditioned gradient flows.
- Shows that differences in spectral learning rates can produce **arbitrarily long delays** between low train loss and low population risk, and builds flows where the delay → ∞ while the transition itself becomes instantaneous. All of this happens **without feature learning**.
- Proves that whether and when generalization happens generally **can't be inferred from training dynamics alone**. It depends on the geometry of evaluation transfer.

### 10. [Evaluation and Restoration of Safety in Reasoning-Tuned LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33515)
- A metric for reasoning-tuned open models shows that **more reasoning raises the unsafe rate in every model family tested**.
- ESPG (evolution strategies + a post-training guardrail) restores safety with ~30 samples and inference-only memory, outperforming GRPO-based safety post-training.

## Honorable Mentions

**Safety, oversight & evaluation**
- [Single-Database Rewards Are Hackable: Counterfactual Execution for Text-to-SQL RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34363): Running queries on hidden counterfactual DB copies cuts fully paid reward hacks from 54.1% to 1.9%. It also warns that single-seed reward comparisons mislead, since one seed reverses both signs.
- [Best-of-N Test-Time Steering Can Lead to Reward Hacking in VLA Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33398): With an imperfect verifier, increasing N selects candidates that exploit verifier errors (Goodhart in robotics). Calibration and softmax selection help.
- [Unstable Action Boundaries in Lethal Drone Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34121): Some LLM agents invoke a simulated lethal tool on generic descriptors like "people" with no jailbreak. Agents that look conservative can flip under simple semantic target shifts.
- [How Much Effort Does an AI Code Reviewer Need?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33639): A preregistered study of Claude 5.5 reviewers on plausible-but-wrong SWE-bench patches. Higher effort helps, but every setting still accepts ≥45% of wrong patches.
- [Trigger-Invariant Contrastive Unlearning for Backdoor Removal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33571): Gradient-ascent unlearning can shift a trigger's influence to other classes. TICU enforces output and representation invariance on clean/triggered pairs.

**Interpretability & weight-space analysis**
- [The Reference Matters: Displacement Probing for Weight-Space Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33998): Probing the *displacement* from the shared pretrained origin explains why probing works within Model Trees, and sets SOTA on Model Jungle.
- [Scalpel and Brush: Pruning and Targeted Weight Editing by an LLM Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33426): An LLM agent edits ≤0.0084% of weights to raise BLEU. Careful controls show the gains reflect notation alignment rather than correctness, and that non-LLM procedures recover much of the effect once told where to intervene.
- [Extraneous Cognitive Load in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33197): An activation-based "extraneous load" score, plus steering toward low-load directions, improves reasoning without changing the prompt.

**Training dynamics & generalization**
- [Understanding Double Descent through Universal Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33759): Bounds excess loss with a decomposition whose MLE-vs-Bayes gap explains the interpolation peak. A Bayesian SGLD ensemble shows no peak.
- [PRIOR: Pre-Call Reasoning in SFT and Tool-Use Retention under RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33648): Whether tool use survives RL depends on how much reasoning precedes tool calls in the SFT data. Filtering on this improves post-RL accuracy.
- [Learning from Recurrence: Depth-Induced Preferences in Looped LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33468): Training a looped OLMo-2-1B's shared block on its own deeper-pass continuations ("write-back"), with no correctness filtering, improves both shallow and deep accuracy.
- [The Gradient Difference Induced by Detaching the Denominator in QK Normalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33887): A closed-form analysis of a common implementation shortcut. Detaching inflates the W_Q norm under AdamW and consistently raises loss, unless the scale is learnable.
