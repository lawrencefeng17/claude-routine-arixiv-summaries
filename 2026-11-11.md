# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-11

Source: the cs.LG listing for Wed, 11 Nov 2026 (134 new submissions + 101 cross-lists). Picks are ranked by relevance to alignment, generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics. Summaries are based on the abstracts. Theme of the day: interpretability methods that look convincing but overstate what they show (SAEs, probes, neuron circuits), plus RL post-training dynamics.

## Top 10

### 1. [Wrong the Same Way: Cross-Prompt Sharing Both Suppresses and Amplifies Reward Hacking in RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13218)
- Asks whether RLVR inherits reward hacking from the base model or amplifies it, using a toy setting where the full response distribution is computed exactly.
- At the KL-regularized optimum, RLVR only filters the accepted set, so the hack rate is inherited exactly.
- Drift away from that comes from parameter sharing through the empirical tangent kernel. A leak that few prompts accept gets pushed *below* the base rate. A leak that most prompts accept gets *amplified*.
- Practical point: fix the verifier errors the model repeats across prompts. Two patches removing equally many errors can differ by up to 38 points of true accuracy.

### 2. [Massive Activations Confound Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13548)
- Massive activations can put up to 99.9% of activation variance on PC1, which has no unique sparse decomposition, so each SAE run rebuilds it with different features.
- As a result, 47 of the top-50 "important" features don't match across runs, worse than random (about 27). A dictionary can report 99.96% variance explained while missing 33% of the non-PC1 variance.
- Fix: train on activations with PC1 removed, then add PC1 back. Top-50 feature agreement rises to 73–99.5%, and ablation damage drops by up to 44%.

### 3. [Linear Probes to Bayesian Manifolds: Geometry of Arithmetic in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12998)
- Splits "the model computes with this probed shape" into four claims: decodability, shape, ownership (the shape belongs to this concept, not a related one), and causal use. Tests each with a Bayesian pipeline.
- On 2-digit add/multiply in GPT-J, Llama-3.1-8B, and Pythia-6.9B, almost everything is decodable, but directions overlap heavily. The winning shapes are ribbons (helices with varying radius) or tori.
- 81% of shapes are *inherited* from related concepts, not owned. Owned and inherited shapes are about equally causal. Recommends reporting probe results on all four claims.

### 4. [Are Causal Neuron Circuits Unique and Transferable?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13885)
- Exhaustively tests all 4,096 subsets of 12-neuron "causal" groups.
- On a capital task, zero-ablating the group hurts, but swapping in activations from other questions doesn't. The neurons are roughly constant and carry no question-specific content.
- On two-hop chains, patching does redirect the answer to the bridge entity (8–9 of 11 chains across 3 models). But often a few neurons suffice, and disjoint subsets each flip the answer.
- Effects transfer to rephrasings but only weakly to other chains. This is a caution against reading neuron-group demos as unique circuits.

### 5. [A Theory of Policy Optimization in Post-Training: Coverage Collapse and Reference Resets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14467)
- REINFORCE, and so PPO and GRPO, can drive the probability of optimal responses to near zero even as reward rises ("coverage collapse"). This happens even with exact gradients and KL regularization, and convergence can take exponentially many steps.
- Repeatedly resetting the KL reference to the current policy (Approximate Policy Mirror Descent) fixes this.
- Gives the first global convergence guarantees that KL- or reset-style PPO/GRPO variants and squared-loss methods (e.g. Kimi's) preserve coverage.

### 6. [You Don't Need Feature Learning to Do In-Context Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13431)
- Rotation-invariant kernel machines on the Raventós et al. ICL linear-regression task give a spectral picture of memorization vs. generalization.
- With more data, the model learns successively higher-degree parts of the *memorizing* solution. The "generalizing" solution is a low-degree truncation of it, so the model generalizes first and memorizes later.
- On this view, generalization is underfitting the memorizer, and feature learning isn't needed for ICL. Trained transformers show the predicted higher-order memorization signatures.

### 7. [How Post-Training Influences Verbalized Algorithmic Computation in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13552)
- Compares Olmo-3-7B SFT, DPO, and RLVR checkpoints on multi-digit addition, using carries as known intermediate state.
- Carry information is equally probe-decodable in SFT and RLVR, but manipulating it doesn't reliably change outputs.
- RLVR-produced CoTs are more *useful*: they give higher accuracy when fed to any receiving checkpoint, and carry statements get higher importance scores. DPO is similarly sensitive to carries but gains less accuracy. This bears on CoT faithfulness.

### 8. [MicroLens: Read, Trace, and Rewire the Residual Stream with Two Lenses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13322)
- Interactive tool combining the logit lens, a Jacobian lens, token tracing, and live editing (Gemma-3-4B-it).
- The logit-lens direction edits what a token outputs. The J-lens direction edits what later tokens read.
- Two-hop trivia: the bridge entity is readable and swappable at the city token from layer 7 (92–98% redirection), yet it isn't used at the answer position.
- With step-by-step prompting, the answer is often already the top residual word before any reasoning (89–92% for retrieval questions). Ordering puzzles are the exception.

### 9. [Evaluating Compositional Generalization in Transformers: Composition Equivalence and Module Coverage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13498)
- Apparent compositional generalization of direct (answer-only) models mostly comes from *composition equivalences*: different module sequences that reduce to the same end-to-end function.
- Removing those equivalences makes performance drop sharply. Shifts in module coverage degrade both direct and step-by-step models monotonically.
- Case study: equivalences inflate Llama-3-8B's results on LiveCodeBench output prediction.

### 10. [Train on Prefill, Monitor at Decode: Using SAEs for Topic Monitoring (TopicScope)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12935)
- Per-token topic classifiers on SAE features (7 topics) that can switch labels mid-response. Goes beyond the usual binary harmful/benign monitor.
- Trained on prefill activations, they generalize to decoding on Gemma-3 1B/4B and stay robust to inserted asides and reordering (≤5 macro-F1 drop).
- Without SAE features, classifiers need to be several times larger to match. A cheap recipe for activation monitors.

## Honorable mentions
- [Sanity Checks for Attention Surgery: Null Models and Matched Interventions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13543): a causal null explains 48–63% of the head–value alignment used to motivate "self-attention removal". A pre-registered 8-seed test shows a small real benefit only for the own-value direction.
- [Auditing the Judge: Internal Signals for LLM Reasoning Assurance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14323): attention-head correctness probes catch 1.3–22.5 points more errors than the judge's verdict score at a 5% review budget, but transfer poorly across domains.
- [Third Eye: Beyond Immediate Gains in Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12988): self-updates that look neutral lose about 1.8 points after a further update. Evaluate self-updates as transitions. Related: [The RSI Tax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13524).
- [Enforcing Axioms for AI Alignment under Loss-Based Rules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13369): Pareto optimality over constitutional principles can fail for reward models that are polynomials or fixed-width MLPs. Comparison-dataset design can recover it.
- [A Margin Perspective on LoRA (MaLoRA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14397): explains LoRA's forgetting-robustness and mergeability via max-margin alignment, and gives a training-free merging rule.
- [Closing the BF16–FP16 Gap in RL Post-Training with Stochastic Rounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13545): low-LR RLVR updates "stick" under BF16 round-to-nearest. Stochastic rounding matches FP16 speed with lower KL.
- [Margin-Guided Token Credit for RLVR (MCPO)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14015): puts the advantage on low-margin "hesitation" tokens. +6.6 points over GRPO, with gains growing with pass@k.
- [Sharp Sample Complexity of KL-Regularized RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13480): tight Õ(KH³/(ε·max{ε,β})) bounds with matching lower bounds.
- [Beyond the Log-Linear Frontier: Finite-Budget Neural Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13534): explains finite-scale curvature and drift in compute-optimal allocation, and improves scaling-law fits.
- [Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505): harness/memory-based learners don't generalize to unseen tasks, while weight-based learning does.
- [Right for the Wrong Reasons? Explanation-Guided Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14134): better rationale agreement doesn't imply changed decision strategy under shortcuts.
- [DEPUTYBENCH: Contextual Safety in Enterprise Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13355): agents execute restricted actions more often when permission evidence is missing (10.3%) than when it is explicitly denied (3.5%).
