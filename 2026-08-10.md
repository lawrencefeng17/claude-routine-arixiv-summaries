## Top 10

### 1. [Transformers Struggle to Use Their Emergent World Models: Revisiting the Tower of Hanoi, and the Illusion of Thinking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07077)
*Devin Pereira, Willem Zuidema*

- Trains small transformers and probes frontier reasoning models (Qwen3.6-27B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) on the flat-to-flat Tower of Hanoi variant.
- Using interpretability techniques, finds both scales develop a genuine emergent world model — a linearly decodable, geometrically faithful representation of the puzzle's state space (a Sierpinski triangle) — encoded near-perfectly at first.
- Performance nonetheless collapses as ring count grows, and the cause is traced to *decay* of that world-model representation during planning, not its absence.
- Injecting the prompt-time representation back in partway through inference partially recovers performance — direct causal evidence that "the illusion of thinking" critiques may be misdiagnosing where reasoning models actually fail.

### 2. [Mathematical Principles and Experimental Discoveries of the Emergence of Symbolic Patterns in Artificial Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06839)
*Quanshi Zhang, Qihan Ren, Siyu Lou*

- Argues ANN inference logic can be rigorously reformulated as sparse symbolic interactions rather than treated as an inscrutable black box, and proves two mathematical criteria under which such symbolic patterns provably emerge.
- Validates the criteria empirically across diverse architectures and tasks, on most input samples, and shows the resulting symbolic explanations transfer across both samples and models.
- Links the symbolic-interaction view directly to explaining generalization capability, not just post-hoc interpretability.
- Proposes "communicative learning" — inspecting and editing inference logic at the symbolic level — as a complement to end-to-end training; framework is architecture-agnostic, suggesting similar symbolic structure could emerge in other black-box systems.

### 3. [Post-Grokking Collapse at the Representation-Readout Interface in Muon-Trained Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07436)
*Ali Janati, Kaoutar El Maghraoui, Andrei Kanavalau, Anass Belfatmi*

- Shows Muon grokks modular addition (mod 113) faster than AdamW, but the learned solution can *collapse* well after training-set loss hits zero — across nine tested configurations.
- Localizes the failure to the interface between hidden representations and the output readout layer: once the training set is solved, gradients shrink sharply and Muon's parameter-update geometry diverges from AdamW's, destabilizing that interface.
- Freezing either the embeddings or the readout layer prevents the collapse, isolating the mechanism.
- Fourier analysis shows the task-relevant circuit stays perfectly aligned in isolation but gets "outvoted" by competing signal in the full model — suggesting grokking is a metastable optimization state, not a one-way phase transition.

### 4. [Cascading Through the Hierarchy: Regularizer-Induced Feature Detection as Phase Transitions in Deep Linear Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06597)
*Björn Ladewig, Ibrahim Talha Ersoy, Karoline Wiesner*

- Uses regularization strength as a tunable knob in a theoretically tractable deep linear network model to rigorously formalize previously-observed "cascading" feature-learning phase transitions.
- Derives analytical predictions for the phase-transition locations and order parameters of learned features, connecting macroscopic training dynamics to microscopic Hessian-spectrum structure.
- Grounds prior numerical observations of cascading transitions in a proper statistical-physics framework, offering a minimal, exactly analyzable testbed for deep learning theory.

### 5. [Hidden Gauge Controls Feature Specialization in ReLU Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06766)
*Tongxi Wang*

- Studies how overparameterized ReLU networks decide *which* neuron among functionally-identical candidates ends up encoding a given teacher feature — and shows a positive-homogeneous "gauge" parameter deterministically controls the outcome.
- In a Gaussian teacher-student setup, different gauge choices produce sharply different feature-acquisition trajectories, with a Θ(D²) separation in specialization time between winning and losing neurons.
- A reaction-transport decomposition attributes the effect to different "mobilities" for a feature's coefficient vs. its direction, explaining why one neuron wins and the others get functionally pruned to zero.
- Extends the result to gradient descent with finite step size and finite samples, not just the idealized population limit — a mechanistic account of training-dynamics-driven specialization.

### 6. [Sharding Prevents LLM Oversight Failures and Adversarial Exploitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06422)
*Victor Akinwande, J. Zico Kolter, Aran Nayebi*

- Finds that LLM judges asked to render many verdicts in one call get systematically worse — agreement with human experts drops as verdicts-per-call increases, even holding compute budget fixed.
- Proposes sharding: splitting the judging workload into smaller groups handled by separate LLM calls, then aggregating — which restores expert agreement at equal total compute, across research replication, legal, and clinical-assessment testbeds.
- Shows sharding specifically closes an adversarial exploit where attackers game an overloaded judge via presentation tricks while leaving the underlying work unchanged.
- Notes sharding alone doesn't stop attacks that persuade a judge on individual criteria — that requires pairing it with debate-style opposition — a concrete, actionable result for scalable oversight design.

### 7. [Policy-Masked Private Experts: Auditable and Reversible Capability Access Control in Sparse MoE Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06690)
*Zhuoheng Huang, Mukesh Singh*

- Proposes freezing a pretrained sparse Mixture-of-Experts model and training a disjoint "private" expert branch, then gating whether the public or private pool is eligible for top-k routing before inference.
- On Qwen3-30B-A3B and DeepSeek-V2-Lite, achieves zero unauthorized private-expert execution across 64 adversarial probing scenarios, while the private branch still delivers real capability gains on tool-use tasks (+5.0 and +21.3 points on two benchmarks).
- Cleanly separates *execution control* (can this parameter path run at all) from *task utility* (does it help), and demonstrates the access control is both auditable and reversible.
- A concrete mechanism for capability gating in deployed MoE models, relevant to controlled capability elicitation and misuse prevention.

### 8. [Latent Fact-Checking: Detecting Misinformation through Activation Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06417)
*Pedro Barcelos, Otávio Parraga, Marcelo M. Mussi, Lucas M. Fraga, Lucas S. Kupssinskü, Rodrigo C. Barros*

- Treats truthfulness as a geometric property of a language model's representation space: identifies a "misinformation direction" by contrasting activations on true vs. false statements.
- At inference, projects a claim's final-layer activation onto this direction and classifies it with a lightweight MLP — no fine-tuning, no external evidence retrieval needed.
- Across 11 models (270M–12B parameters) and three benchmarks, matches or beats prompting-based fact-checking baselines, with the biggest gains on smaller models.
- Supports the claim that truthfulness is a structured, linearly separable concept in pretrained LLM latent space — direct mechanistic-interpretability evidence relevant to hallucination and honesty research.

### 9. [High-dimensional ridgeless least squares interpolation under spiked covariance structures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07281)
*Zhijun Liu, Dandan Jiang*

- Analyzes ridgeless (interpolating) least-squares regression as dimension and sample size grow proportionally, under covariance models with multiple latent "spike" factors of varying count and magnitude.
- Identifies the key mechanism for benign overfitting: alignment between the true regression coefficient and the spiked eigenspaces of the population covariance — how signal energy is distributed along spike directions determines whether overfitting is benign, tempered, or catastrophic.
- Requires only finite fourth moments (no Gaussianity assumption), and characterizes how the spike structure shapes double-descent behavior.
- A unified theoretical account of when hidden low-dimensional structure in the data helps vs. hurts generalization in overparameterized models.

### 10. [Cryptanalytic Extraction of Isolated Bias-Free GLU Feed-Forward Blocks by Antipodal Separation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06631)
*Chunhui Shi, Xinwen Fu*

- Adapts cryptanalytic techniques to extract the parameters of bias-free Gated Linear Unit (GLU) feed-forward blocks — the workhorse component in modern LLM architectures (Qwen, Llama, Gemma).
- Uses finite-difference curvature to recover candidate gate directions, then paired query observations at x and −x to separate out gate magnitude, recovering block weights with sub-percent median error in high precision (under 5% in finite-precision settings).
- Explicitly scoped to isolated blocks rather than full end-to-end model extraction — deriving the needed internal block responses purely from final model outputs remains an open problem.
- A concrete model-security/model-stealing result with direct relevance to protecting proprietary weights and to interpretability work that depends on precise weight recovery.
