## Top 10

### 1. [Safe Evolution with Circuit Anchors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05158)
*Yan Liu, Jie Fu, Tsung-Yi Ho*

- Warns that self-evolving/self-improving models can quietly "misevolve" into capable but unsafe systems if optimization runs unconstrained — capability gains don't automatically preserve safety.
- Proposes Circuit-Anchored Evolution (CAE): uses mechanistic interpretability to locate a small safety circuit (under 2% of model features) that causally mediates safety behavior, then anchors it while letting peripheral features adapt freely during evolution — a biological-development-style constraint (à la Hox genes stabilizing body plans).
- Across three model families and two evolution algorithms, CAE preserves safety with less capability loss than explicit reward-based constraints, and does so more efficiently.
- A concrete interpretability-for-safety mechanism aimed squarely at the "models drift unsafe under self-improvement" failure mode.

### 2. [The Ignition Index: Measuring Global Workspace Dynamics in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05160)
*Saman Rahbar*

- Operationalizes Global Workspace Theory's "all-or-none ignition" prediction as a scalar metric (I) by fitting a sigmoid to per-layer probe accuracy vs. input signal strength, extracting a steepness parameter that flags abrupt vs. gradual information broadcast.
- Validated across 11 models / 5 architecture families with shuffled-label controls (9.6x selectivity for genuine linguistic structure over spurious probe capacity); feedforward transformers show much sharper ignition than SSMs, and Mamba shows near-linear (non-ignition-like) profiles.
- Finds a phase transition in Pythia-410M at training step 256 that precedes induction-head formation, and that ignition sharpness appears to saturate rather than scale with model size.
- First quantitative bridge between a cognitive-science theory of global broadcast and mechanistic interpretability — relevant to training-dynamics and emergent-capability questions.

### 3. [Cross-Architecture Steering Transfer in Language Models: A Systematic Empirical Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05164)
*Ayushi Agarwal*

- Tests whether concept/steering directions learned via Sparse Autoencoders on one independently-trained LLM transfer to a different model, across 5 open-weight models, 3 scales (0.8B–8B), 2 architecture families, and 15 semantic domains.
- Finds a scale threshold: at ≥1.7B parameters, roughly half of cross-model feature pairs align well and steering transfers; below ~0.8B, transfer degrades sharply.
- Cross-model steering vectors even slightly *beat* same-model native vectors in win rate (71.0% vs. 68.0%), and a single "universal" vector reaches 67.3% success without any per-model training.
- Empirical support for the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with a direct practical payoff: interpretability/steering tools may not need to be retrained per model — but validated behavior at large scale may not transfer down to small models.

### 4. [Reasoning Errors Have a Region and a Direction in the Residual-Stream Trajectory of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05660)
*Hamed Damirchi, Ignacio Meza De la Jara, Damith Ranasinghe, Yuhang Liu, Javen Shi*

- Detects reasoning errors from *state-conditioned motion* through the residual stream — combining a coarse vector-quantized "region" reader with a fine multi-layer "direction" reader — rather than from static states or raw layer-to-layer displacement alone.
- Improves error-detection accuracy by up to 12% over prior displacement-based methods and 21% over single-layer baselines on held-out reasoning benchmarks.
- Despite training only on reasoning tasks, the detector generalizes to factual completion and verification, suggesting it captures a general correctness signal rather than something reasoning-specific.
- A mechanistic-interpretability probe with an immediately useful application: real-time reasoning-failure detection.

### 5. [CircuitSteer: Geometrically Aligned Multi-Layer Steering via Sparse Autoencoder Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05732)
*Mehrshad Saadatnia, Parsa Razmara, Ardalan Aryashad, Ali Abbasi, Seyedarmin Azizi*

- Argues single-layer steering methods like Contrastive Activation Addition apply one fixed intervention across semantically diverse inputs and often fail to hold up across layers; instead identifies coherent multi-layer "feature circuits" via SAE feature co-activation and decoder-direction alignment.
- Synthesizes dense steering vectors from these sparse multi-layer circuits and applies coordinated multi-point interventions rather than a single injection.
- Across toxicity, emotion-intensity, sycophancy, and refusal on two model families, it's the only method that consistently preserves fluency — competitors either degrade text quality or fail outright on harder behaviors like sycophancy and refusal.
- Direct evidence that behavior in LLMs is often carried by distributed multi-layer circuits rather than single directions, with implications for both alignment steering and interpretability methodology.

### 6. [GROM: Gradient-Free Rapid One-Shot Machine Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05783)
*Paweł Batorski, Przemysław Spurek, Paul Swoboda*

- Replaces iterative fine-tuning-based unlearning with a closed-form, gradient-free weight edit: frames unlearning as ridge-regularized least squares and derives an additive update from forward passes alone — no backprop, no convergence loop, seconds instead of hours.
- On TOFU-5/10%, MUSE-Books, MUSE-News, and WMDP, matches or beats state-of-the-art forgetting/utility trade-offs at a fraction of the compute.
- Because it edits weights directly rather than masking behavior, it resists the known low-bit-quantization attack that recovers "forgotten" content from gradient-based unlearning baselines — a real robustness gap in prior methods.
- A practical, analytically-grounded contribution to model-safety tooling (knowledge/data removal) with an explicit robustness argument, not just a utility-preservation one.

### 7. [Robust Context-Aware Detection of Malicious Instructions in Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05430)
*Buzhao Liu, Xinhang Ma, Yevgeniy Vorobeychik*

- Targets indirect prompt injection: classifies embedded sentences as benign or malicious using both surrounding context and the user's actual query, rather than scoring sentences in isolation.
- Hardens the detector with two adversarial training regimes — projected-gradient optimization in embedding space, and LLM-paraphrased attack simulation — to resist adaptive attackers, not just static injection patterns.
- Outperforms existing defenses on static-attack benchmarks and holds up substantially better against adaptive attacks (higher utility, lower attack success rate).
- Finds optimal defense hyperparameters are domain-dependent, implying prompt-injection defenses likely need per-application tuning rather than one universal setting.

### 8. [Conditional Cognitive Biases in LLMs: How Biased User Turns Modulate In-Context Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05166)
*Sachini Weerasekara, Sagar Kamarthi, Jacqueline Isaacs*

- Introduces a three-condition framework (24,300 validated prompts, a 9x9 bias-interaction matrix) that separates *exposure* to a biased prior user turn from the *semantic content* of that turn's bias.
- Shows biased conversational context generally amplifies a model's own bias expression relative to baseline — an in-context, multi-turn analogue of contagion/sycophancy effects.
- But finds a competing dynamic: explicitly-labeled bias cues often trigger alignment-trained suppression behavior that *reduces* overt bias — meaning surface-level safety training and underlying susceptibility can diverge depending on how the bias is signaled.
- Directly relevant to understanding how conversational context shapes (mis)alignment-adjacent behavior in deployed chat models; code, framework, and dataset are public.

### 9. [The Judgment-Consequence Gap: LLM Moral Reasoning in Healthcare Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05583)
*Hadi Hosseini, Samarth Khanna, Leona Pierce*

- Evaluates LLMs across capability tiers on clinical resource-allocation vignettes involving patient responsibility for health-harming behavior.
- Finds LLMs agree with human judgments about *who is responsible* but then refuse to let that judgment affect *resource allocation*, defaulting to random/equal allocation instead of favoring less-culpable patients the way human raters do.
- LLMs weight "information accessibility" more heavily than humans do when judging responsibility, and — notably — the human/LLM divergence *grows* with model capability rather than shrinking.
- A clean example of values/behavior misalignment that gets worse, not better, with scale — squarely in emergent-misalignment/normative-alignment territory, in a high-stakes applied setting.

### 10. [Perturbation Sensitivity at Convergence: A Simple Signal for Identifying Spuriously Correlated Samples](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05419)
*Nilesh Kumar*

- Observes that once a model has converged, loss can no longer separate spurious-correlation-following samples from the rest — but perturbation sensitivity still can, since spurious-rule samples share a common decision boundary while others rely on fragile, input-specific cues.
- Method needs only two forward passes per sample, no group annotations, and no early-stopping epoch selection — much cheaper than prior spurious-correlation detection pipelines.
- On Waterbirds, detected-sample rebalancing lifts worst-group accuracy from 57.3% to 80.8%, closing most of the gap to the 85.8% achieved with ground-truth group labels.
- A lightweight, annotation-free data-attribution-style tool for diagnosing which training samples drive shortcut learning — relevant to both generalization science and dataset auditing.
