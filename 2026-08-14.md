## Top 10

### 1. [Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13482)

- Argues that alignment introduced only after pretraining makes values a "thin overlay" on already-formed behavioral priors, facilitating later misalignment.
- Proposes installing the assistant persona from token zero: pretraining documents are annotated with value-aligned first-person reflections derived from a normative constitution, and the model is trained on both, installing the desired persona among many others.
- Post-training then performs "persona binding," tying this persona to the assistant identity via user-assistant dialogue data.
- At up to 3B parameters / 500B tokens, improves constitution-following and jailbreak robustness and reduces misalignment on OOD moral dilemmas without hurting capability; doing the same intervention only at the end of pretraining is markedly weaker, and the advantage grows with pretraining budget.

### 2. [A Probe Direction Is a Property of Its Prompt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13329)

- Critiques the standard instrument for reading "evaluation awareness" off model activations: a direction built by contrasting prompts that announce a test vs. prompts that don't — but which prompt does the announcing is a hidden, unreported free parameter.
- Holding the task fixed and varying only that prompt choice flips the reported score and even the sign of its scaling trend; two published studies that disagree on the trend's direction are both reproducible from a single design, by prompt choice alone.
- Model identity explains only a small share of variance in the reported number; most comes from a model × prompt interaction that more evaluation items cannot fix.
- A direction carrying zero real evaluation information still reproduces much of a published score, because the train/test split is separable from surface form alone — a sharp measurement-validity warning for situational-awareness research.

### 3. [Where You Measure Decides What You Measure: Position Selection in Ablation-Based SAE Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13337)

- Standard sparse-autoencoder ablation evaluation measures effect at the token where a latent "fires hardest" — a convention silently fixed by the dictionary under evaluation, not by the experimenter.
- Two Google SAEs on the same model, matched by decoder similarity for near-identical latents, pick different measurement tokens for a large share of them, so head-to-head comparisons often measure different things.
- Training six autoencoders from one initialization, differing only in fitting choices, shows most of what looks like "dictionary disagreement" (7.6%/11.9% of variance) vanishes once every dictionary is measured at the same token — and more evaluation data doesn't help, it gets worse with scale.
- The fix is one line of evaluation code; the authors audit five published papers against it.

### 4. [Neural Quadratic Forms: A Unified Minimal Model for Sudden Learning and Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13335)

- Explains both step-like loss drops and smooth scaling-law power laws from one symmetry argument: permutation-invariance of interchangeable units within a layer forces near-zero-init training dynamics into a universal quadratic form, $\mathrm{Tr}[WW^\top A(x)]$, that differs across architectures only in a single "structure matrix."
- Perceptrons, attention layers, mixtures of experts, and convolutions all become one model at different structure matrices; dynamics on the order parameter $M=WW^\top$ reduce to Lotka–Volterra equations whose modes switch on one after another.
- Smaller initial weights space switch-on times further apart, producing visible plateaus; when many modes are unresolved, the same events merge into a smooth power law whose exponent the theory predicts.
- Confirmed numerically across training methods and architectures — a mechanistic link between sudden capability jumps and smooth scaling curves.

### 5. [HiRoute: Hierarchical Routed Prompt Tuning for Safety Alignment of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12821)

- Existing prompt-tuning safety alignment relies on one static global prompt, forcing a tradeoff between cross-category safety coverage and over-refusal of benign inputs.
- HiRoute trains a lightweight router on frozen-LLM representations to detect harmful intent and predict multi-label risk scores, then learns a shared coarse-grained safety prompt plus fine-grained risk-specific prompt experts via preference optimization.
- At inference, benign inputs bypass the safety branch entirely; risky inputs get the shared prompt combined with a router-weighted mixture of risk experts.
- Across three instruction-tuned models: high safety rates, preserved helpfulness on safe responses, reduced over-refusal, and no loss on general-purpose tasks.

### 6. [Comment on "Modeling rapid language learning by distilling Bayesian priors into artificial neural networks"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12974)

- A direct critique of the prominent claim (McCoy & Griffiths 2025) that meta-learning (MAML) distills a genuine Bayesian prior into a neural network.
- Argues that under the standard meaning of "prior," MAML only initializes weights favorably — the objective function itself is unchanged, so nothing resembling a prior has actually been installed.
- Considers, and shows nontrivial problems with, a looser reading in which the system as a whole implements Bayesian inference without an explicit prior.
- Shows empirically that unlike genuine Bayesian learners, the MAML-trained model overfits and generalizes poorly out-of-distribution — a cautionary case study in over-interpreting meta-learning as inductive-bias transfer.

### 7. [TRAPSBench: Vision-Language Models Encode but Fail to Express Epistemic Restraint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13167)

- Introduces a 1,404-pair procedurally generated video benchmark of matched physics scenes where one targeted change makes the outcome genuinely undeterminable, testing whether VLMs abstain appropriately.
- Across 16 VLMs from 5 families, the best calibration score (which rewards correct answers when knowable and abstention when not) is only 0.292 — spontaneous restraint is poor.
- Yet linear probes decode answerability from hidden states at up to 0.91 AUROC, and steering a single "void direction" causally induces or suppresses abstention — models internally know they don't know but don't say so.
- The gap is worse for visual than textual uncertainty (models detect textual impossibility ~4x more readily), pointing to an output-stage rather than perceptual failure, replicated across Qwen, Gemma, and LLaVA.

### 8. [The Objective Is the Bottleneck: Latent World Models Encode What Their Planners Cannot Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12959)

- Reproduces a latent world model (LeWorldModel) and finds long-horizon planning failures aren't caused by predictor degradation — its 75-step-ahead predictions are still far better than assuming the world froze, yet the planner only ever looks 25 steps ahead.
- The real culprit is the planning objective: minimizing squared latent distance only weakly tracks true distance (r=0.426) and can even *decrease* while the agent moves away from the goal past a certain range.
- A frozen-embedding ridge probe recovers position at R²=0.99 — the information needed for good planning is present in the representation, just unused by the cost function.
- Swapping only the planning objective, with no retraining, lifts long-horizon goal success from 26% to 98%, matching short-horizon performance — a clean case where the evaluation/objective choice, not representation quality, was the bottleneck.

### 9. [Geometric and Behavioral Stratification in Transformer Residual Streams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12447)

- Investigates the "prediction direction" — the unembedding direction of the token a model currently predicts — as a content-defined privileged anchor in the residual stream, tested across 18 models (7B–120B, dense/MoE, base/instruct).
- Finds a narrow, scale-invariant "prediction interface" near this anchor concentrates readout-relevant structure, while the much larger prediction-distal complement expands with model scale and sits nearly orthogonal to the principal variance axes — so ordinary variance-based interpretability methods miss it, increasingly so as prompts diversify.
- Disrupting variance directions near the prediction causes immediate divergence and task-frame shifts; disrupting directions further out delays divergence but preserves framing — the distal complement is weakly readout-aligned yet still causally and temporally load-bearing.
- Offers a geometric account of how high-dimensional transformer computation coexists with a comparatively narrow linear readout.

### 10. [LittleLearner: Language Models Under Pedagogically Controlled Knowledge Exposure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13545)

- Web-scale pretraining corpora make it nearly impossible to characterize a model's prior exposure to any given fact or skill; this paper builds LittleCurriculum, an 88B-token corpus restricted to U.S. elementary (≤Grade 5) material, explicitly excluding higher-level concepts and vocabulary.
- Trains a 5B-parameter model from scratch on it — LittleLearner is competent enough for open-ended evaluation, yet has knowledge and capability boundaries that map cleanly onto curriculum guidelines.
- First experiments inject new knowledge via post-training and in-context learning: both help the model use its existing knowledge better, but neither raises out-of-scope capabilities.
- Positioned as a reusable sandbox for controlled studies of knowledge/skill acquisition — directly useful for training-dynamics and generalization research that's normally confounded by unknown pretraining exposure.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Excess Separability: Nuisance-Controlled Residual-Stream Probing for Benchmark Contamination Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12652)** — shows naive linear-probe contamination detection has wildly miscalibrated false-positive rates, and proposes a corrected, nuisance-controlled test that returns null on well-matched control splits.
- **[PRISM: naming-error dissociations in language models and post-stroke aphasia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12717)** — adapts human-neuroimaging subtraction analysis to perturbed transformers, finding a phonemic-favoring deep-layer cluster in LLMs that parallels frontal-perisylvian lesion patterns in 213 aphasia patients, replicated on held-out splits.
- **[SteerBench-Work: A Benchmark for Agent Steering at Action Boundaries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12654)** — benchmarks agent "proceed vs. hold" decisions at consequential action boundaries; models wrongly hold safe work 28% of the time vs. wrongly allowing unsafe work 1% of the time, and higher capability often means worse calibration, not better.
- **[Dead text or binding clause? Measuring and restoring constraint influence in black-box LLM dialogues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12599)** — measures "revocation inertia," where LLMs keep enacting a withdrawn constraint in multi-turn dialogue, and introduces a probe that predicts the failure before it happens plus a repair method.
- **[Can Spectral-Clipping Enable Better Learning While Forgetting Less for Low-Rank Adaptation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12332)** — links uncontrolled singular-value growth in LoRA adapters to catastrophic forgetting and fixes it with spectral clipping aware of the pretrained model's spectral distribution.
- **[Predicting When Random Low-Dimensional Reparameterizations Train Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12597)** — derives an orientation-resolved theory predicting the latent dimension a random-subspace training or fine-tuning method needs to succeed, validated on image and language models.
- **[Position: Reasoning is a Learnable Rule-Based Process](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12325)** — argues LLM "reasoning" research lacks agreed operational definitions, undermining the construct validity of reasoning evaluations, and proposes definitions grounded in classical logic plus a reporting checklist.
- **[I-SDPO: Instance-Level Adaptive Self-Distillation Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12957)** — fixes a GRPO blind spot (rollout groups where every sample is wrong, giving no relative signal) with per-instance self-distillation that automatically backs off as the policy's success rate rises.
- **[Training Under Challenge: Executable Certificates and Challenge-Closed Optimality for Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12655)** — a framework of "executable certificates" that construct alternative training outcomes to lower-bound how far a checkpoint is from a true global optimum, distinguishing local traps from representation limits.
