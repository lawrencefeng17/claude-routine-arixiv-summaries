## Top 10

### 1. [Scaling Model-Generated Distillation Data Can Make Latent Teacher Traits More Recoverable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26958)

- Directly extends the subliminal-learning line of work: a teacher model induced to express a target trait generates restricted, off-task data (e.g., number-only completions) that never mentions the trait, and students are distilled on varying amounts of it.
- Finds that scaling up the distillation dataset — normally assumed to just improve coverage and reduce noise — actually makes the teacher's hidden trait *more* recoverable in the student's later behavior, not less.
- Effect holds across model families, trait types, multi-trait settings, and cross-model transfer, and shows up in LoRA update analysis too — a concrete warning that "more (even benign-looking) synthetic data" is not a free safety lunch for distillation pipelines.

### 2. [Circuit Condensation: Post-Training that Concentrates a Behavior's Causal Circuit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27254)

- Proposes post-training a model to concentrate a target behavior into a much smaller causal circuit, rather than only searching for one in frozen weights — pruning low-attribution edges and training a low-rank adapter to preserve the behavior through what remains.
- Across 4 behaviors and 8 models, condensed circuits beat the best frozen-circuit baseline in 30/32 settings, by 8.1x on average (up to 316x); ablating the weight-update step (search alone) produces larger circuits, showing the gains come from training, not search.
- On indirect object identification, condensation isolates 24 heads (17 with documented roles) vs. 61 heads (36 undocumented) for the matched frozen circuit — a genuinely sufficient sub-circuit rather than a reconstruction of the "textbook" mechanism.

### 3. [Disentangling Optimization Scale from Preference Scale in DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27032)

- Shows that DPO's β coefficient — usually read as just the KL-constraint strength — secretly does double duty, also rescaling the optimization dynamics and coupling with the effective learning rate.
- As a result, at fixed learning rate the achieved policy deviation is non-monotone in β (a dead zone at small β, a peak at intermediate β, then decay), and runs with near-identical loss curves can differ several-fold in KL divergence from the reference model.
- Proposes a centered-softplus reformulation, argmin-equivalent to DPO for β>0, that makes preference-noise scale and learning-rate effects independently tunable — relevant to anyone tuning alignment training and trying to reason about what their loss curves actually mean.

### 4. [A Single Suffix to Break Them All: Basin-Aware Jailbreaks for Merged Model Families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26506)

- Identifies a jailbreak risk that survives model merging even when every constituent fine-tune is individually safety-aligned — the vulnerability is rooted in the shared pretrained backbone, not in any one merged model.
- Introduces Basin-Aware Jailbreak (BAJ), which formulates adversarial-suffix generation as a min–max optimization over the merging space, producing suffixes that transfer across merged models sharing a backbone without needing the exact merge coefficients or checkpoints.
- Achieves consistently high transfer success across backbones and merging settings, and remains effective against existing defenses — a concrete emergent-misalignment-adjacent failure mode specific to the growing practice of model merging.

### 5. [Dynamical phase selection controls compute scaling in looped transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26556)

- Finds that looped (weight-tied, iterated) transformers with identical architecture, objective, and final accuracy can nevertheless converge to distinct dynamical phases depending on initialization — and that phase, not architecture, governs how test-time compute scales.
- Characterizes the phases by their bifurcation type (a saddle-node fold vs. a Neimark–Sacker transition to bounded nonstationary motion), deriving a parameter-free relation that predicts relaxation time and spectral-gap scaling from local derivatives of the trained map in the fold phase.
- A clean example of training dynamics determining downstream (inference-time) computational behavior in ways invisible from architecture or loss alone.

### 6. [Understanding Evolution Strategies for LLM Reasoning: Broader Reasoning Coverage than GRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27351)

- Systematically studies Evolution Strategies (ES) as an LLM reasoning post-training paradigm, showing theoretically and empirically that ES achieves broader reasoning coverage (higher Pass@K) than GRPO, which suffers entropy collapse.
- Finds that despite substantial whole-model parameter drift under ES, task-performance gains trace back to a sparse subset of larger-magnitude updates — large parameter movement doesn't imply widespread functional change, and doesn't necessarily cause catastrophic forgetting.
- Proposes a sequential GRPO→ES strategy combining GRPO's Pass@1 strength with ES's Pass@K gains — useful evidence on how different RL-style post-training objectives actually reshape model behavior.

### 7. [Daydreaming: Stealing Hidden Agent Skills through Black-Box Task Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26733)

- Introduces an execution-only attack that reconstructs a hosted agent's secret multi-file "skill" (instructions, reference data, helper code) purely by submitting the ordinary tasks the service is designed to complete, never asking it to reveal the skill.
- Formalizes three threat levels (Differential, Trace, Output) and focuses on the hardest, most realistic one (Output: attacker sees only final responses/files), using attacker-controlled shadow agents to adaptively distinguish hidden behaviors.
- Recovers 86.8% of a skill's capability with a median of 32 victim calls, ~4x better than prior work (SigLeak), even with disclosure defenses enabled — shows that filtering direct requests for proprietary prompts/skills does not stop functional extraction via normal use.

### 8. [When Privacy Hurts Mergeability: Geometry-Aware Model Merging under Differential Privacy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26655)

- Studies why differentially-private fine-tuned task models are harder to merge into a single multi-task model, identifying two geometric culprits: local sharpness (task loss sensitivity to merge-induced parameter displacement) and reference drift (displacement from the shared pretrained init, which amplifies cross-task interference).
- Proposes DP-Merging, using a DP-compatible sharpness-aware objective plus a reference-alignment regularizer, and derives a merge-gap bound showing that reducing curvature and drift tightens the loss-increase bound from merging.
- Consistently improves private merged-model performance across vision/language tasks and privacy budgets while preserving DP guarantees — a useful geometric lens connecting privacy, generalization, and model-merging behavior.

### 9. [Linear Independence of Polynomial Compositions and Identifiability of Deep Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27113)

- Conjectures (and proves several cases of) that post-composing distinct nonconstant polynomials with a generic high-degree polynomial yields linearly independent polynomials, generalizing the classical Newman–Slater theorem on powers of polynomials.
- Shows this conjecture fully characterizes the identifiability (parameter symmetries) of deep fully-connected networks with polynomial activations, i.e., exactly which parameter settings yield the same end-to-end function.
- Fully resolves identifiability for shallow polynomial networks as a special case — theoretical groundwork for understanding when "different weights, same function" happens, directly bearing on interpretability and the science of generalization.

### 10. [Squeezing More from Limited Data with Recursive Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26973)

- Studies pre-training scaling laws in the small-data regime (10M–100M words), where increasing parameters helps only up to a point before overfitting worsens generalization — a different scaling regime than web-scale LM training.
- Argues standard Transformers scale down poorly here because embeddings eat a large fraction of the parameter budget while per-token compute stays tied to representational capacity; proposes recursive Transformers (a shared block reused across depth) plus factorized embeddings to decouple compute from capacity.
- Outperforms standard Transformers at 10M/100M-word budgets while staying competitive with BabyLM Challenge 2025 winners — relevant to understanding what actually drives generalization under a fixed, small data budget.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Representation Measurements Under Function-Preserving Reparameterizations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27020)** — shows a widely-used representation-analysis technique (column-permutation parallel analysis) violates basic invariance to function-preserving reparameterizations, meaning its component-count/decision outputs can reflect an arbitrary choice of hidden coordinates rather than a real model property — a methodological warning for interpretability research.
- **[Unifying Detection and Adaptation in Task-Free Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27070)** — uses Fisher-information-matrix subspace orthogonality to detect task shifts and route LoRA adaptation without needing explicit task boundaries, mitigating catastrophic forgetting in realistic task-free settings.
- **[Performance Foundations of Parallel & Distributed Reasoning Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27046)** — a compute-centric systematization of RLVR/PPO/GRPO-style post-training and the parallelism strategies (disaggregated placement, stage fusion, hybrid/async execution) needed to train reasoning models at scale.
- **[Equal Ranking Quality, Different Decisions: Training Order-Consistent LLM Scorers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26762)** — shows LLM-based rerankers/reward models with near-identical ranking quality can disagree sharply on the actual decisions their scores drive (which documents get retained, which answer a reader picks) depending on prompt ordering, and introduces order-consistency SFT to fix it.
- **[GRAS: Guided Reduced-Variance Proposals and Adaptive Selection for Training-Free Reward Alignment in Discrete Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26585)** — improves training-free, inference-time reward steering of discrete diffusion generators via variance-reduced gradient guidance and an adaptive resampling temperature, matching or beating reward-fine-tuned models without retraining.
- **[Safety by Design: Realized-Cost Constraints for Contextual Bandits with Continuous Actions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26755)** — safe-RL algorithm enforcing high-probability constraints on *realized* (not just expected) cost for continuous-action contextual bandits, with a tight regret bound — matters for any heteroscedastic safety-critical deployment (dosing, autonomy).
- **[Arrive and Survive: Scaling Safe Goal-Conditioned Policy Learning from One-Bit Failure Signals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26571)** — identifies and fixes an overestimation bias in contrastive RL that lets near-failure trajectories reinforce unsafe behavior, using only the one-bit failure-termination signal.
- **[Importance Scoring of Transformer Attention Heads in Learning Tabular Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27241)** — one of the first attention-head importance/interpretability studies specifically for tabular transformers, finding importance is scattered across layers and highly dataset-dependent (unlike vision/language).
