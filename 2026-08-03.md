## Top 10

### 1. [The Grokked Illusion: True Equilibrium Mitigates Catastrophic Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29503)
*Zhang, Chan, Shang, Yang, Zhang*

- Studies modular-arithmetic grokking and asks whether "perfect generalization" implies robustness — it doesn't.
- Compares standard AdamW-trained transformers to high-entropy models sampled via Wang-Landau Molecular Dynamics (models occupying larger volumes of parameter space).
- When forced to learn new data with random labels, AdamW models collapse (100% → <75% accuracy) while high-entropy models hold ~95%.
- SVD analysis shows high-entropy networks have higher effective rank in attention/MLP layers, suggesting richer representations resist interference and catastrophic forgetting.

### 2. [Efficient LLM Adversarial Training via Low-Rank Defense and Circuit-Guided Surrogates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28959)
*He, Lin, Tang, Xing*

- Tackles the prohibitive cost of adversarial (latent) training for LLM safety at scale.
- Identifies misalignment issues introduced by naive representation fine-tuning within latent adversarial training.
- Proposes extracting relevant *circuits* from the model to build a lightweight surrogate for attack generation — directly leaning on mechanistic-interpretability tooling for a safety application.
- Cuts per-step adversarial-training FLOPs by 48.1% while training only 0.0118% of parameters.

### 3. [Shapley-Value-Based Feature Attribution for Data Masking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28946)
*Qu, Bilson Darku, Guo*

- A data-attribution framework applying Shapley values to data privacy masking rather than model predictions.
- Reframes the privacy risk/utility tradeoff at the *feature* level instead of the usual dataset level.
- Method is agnostic to the masking technique, ML model, and risk/utility metrics used.
- Shown to reduce disclosure risk while preserving data utility more effectively than dataset-level baselines.

### 4. [LARA: Lightweight Adapters in the Residual Stream for Composable Adaptation and Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28669)
*Ekin, Choi, Jie*

- Adapts models by injecting low-rank corrections directly into the residual stream (reading hidden states at select layers) rather than modifying weights — an interpretability-flavored take on adaptation.
- Matches LoRA at equal parameter counts on code fine-tuning and preference-optimization (alignment) tasks.
- Exposes a scale parameter for smooth interpolation between base and adapted behavior at inference time.
- Supports storing/composing multiple behaviors on one frozen model with per-token routing (~33MB for 7 behaviors vs. one full model each).

### 5. [Evidence-Type Competition: When Can Interventional Data Teach Language Models Causal Direction?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29484)
*Xun*

- Controlled Simpson's-paradox experiments probing whether interventional pretraining data teaches LMs correct causal direction.
- Finding: increasing interventional-data fraction during pretraining does *not* improve causal-direction accuracy — the sign of predictions still follows observational context.
- Key insight: "the capability lives in the weights; the switch lives in the context" — activation patching localizes the mechanism to middle-layer observational representations.
- An evidence-averaging protocol at inference time cuts sign errors from 26% to 9%, without any retraining.

### 6. [PTP: Previous-Token Prediction based LLM Inversion for Near-Exact Prompt Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29378)
*Suhail, Naidu, Sinha, Sethi*

- A black-box prompt-reconstruction (LLM inversion) attack: trains an inverse LM from scratch on synthetic data generated by querying the target model, without needing weights or logits.
- Mirrors the forward next-token objective in reverse to recover prompts from outputs alone.
- Generalizes across datasets and transfers across different target LLMs — relevant to LLM privacy/security threat models.

### 7. [Persistent Convolution: A Topological Framework for AI Alignment Testing and Semantic Space Characterization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29008)
*Ashoff, Rodu*

- Proposes formal statistical/topological tests over a model's embedding space to characterize semantic structure and concept separation as a proxy for alignment testing of otherwise-opaque models.
- Uses human-curated knowledge structures (e.g., knowledge graphs) as the alignment reference.
- Framed as a model-comparison tool for deployment/selection decisions, with a stated connection to possibility theory and decision theory.

### 8. [TextCloak: Thwarting Unauthorized LLM Exploitation via RL-Driven Unlearnable Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28862)
*Zhao, Ma, Li, Jiang, Yu, Tan, Liu*

- Generates "unlearnable examples" for text: an RL policy (GRPO-UE) transforms clean text into data that degrades downstream models fine-tuned on it, while staying fluent and semantically faithful.
- Targets open-ended LLM generation settings, unlike prior unlearnable-example work limited to classifiers.
- Reward signal comes from measured degradation on fine-tuned surrogate LLMs; shown to transfer across 9 LLMs and resist adaptive attacks — relevant to data protection/subliminal-signal-style training interventions.

### 9. [When Unlearning Fails: Reliable Data Deletion under Post-Training in Agent Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28829)
*Ding, Huang, Dong*

- Identifies an "influence echo" problem: deleted data keeps affecting a model's behavior through the trajectories it already shaped, even after formal removal — a machine-unlearning failure mode relevant to alignment/safety guarantees.
- Proposes MUTE: estimates downstream influence via a lightweight server ledger, applies a forget-retain update, quarantines/down-weights high-influence retained trajectories, and audits later behavior to schedule further erasure.
- Validated on LIBERO with vision-language-action agents; reduces behavioral leakage and influence "regeneration" far more cheaply than full retraining.

### 10. [How Hard Does It Think? Analyzing Step-Aware Reasoning Energy in LLM Chain-of-Thought Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28674)
*Wei, Wu, Yu, Zhou, Jiao, Zhong, Jin, Yu, Pan, Han, McAuley*

- An interpretability method (SARE) that quantifies computational "effort" at each individual chain-of-thought step, using Centered Kernel Alignment between hidden-state Gram matrices across adjacent layers.
- Finds reasoning energy is highly non-uniform across step types, with phase-like transitions invisible to trajectory-level metrics.
- Incorrect reasoning trajectories show systematically lower energy at critical junctions — internal geometric dynamics carry predictive signal beyond output-level confidence scores.
