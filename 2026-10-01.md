# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-01

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (428 entries) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics.

### 1. [Aligned Data Can Induce Misalignment via Context Confusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38379)

- Fine-tuning on data that is aligned in one context can produce misaligned behavior in another ("context confusion"), shown across gender equality, privacy, and physical safety.
- Mechanism: queries from different domains undergo similar representational shifts during fine-tuning, so one domain's learned behavior leaks into others.
- Persists despite general alignment data; substantially reduced by targeted domain-specific alignment data or in-context examples.
- Takeaway: you can't predict post-training alignment from inspecting the training data alone.

### 2. [Alignment via Training Against Probes Without Losing Monitorability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38645)

- Uses activation probes for undesired properties as a direct training signal (probe-guided fine-tuning) rather than output-only supervision.
- Static probes are exploitable; continuously updated probes cut harmfulness and boost honesty while keeping utility.
- Better safety–utility trade-off than DPO and inference-time steering; robust to jailbreak and abliteration attacks.
- Concepts stay linearly decodable after training, so oversight isn't lost.

### 3. [Learning Steganography Is Easy, Learning Steganographic Reasoning Is Hard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39838)

- Compares how easily models acquire steganographic messaging, encoded reasoning, and true steganographic reasoning.
- For most tasks, steganographic reasoning is learned only under SFT, and needs far more training than messaging; the other two arise under RL, ICL, and SFT.
- Caveat: when the cover task facilitates concealment, steganographic reasoning becomes learnable under all three elicitation methods.

### 4. [Stress-Testing LLM Lie Detectors: Role-Play Failures and Spurious Correlations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39807)

- ~9k human-reviewed responses where three models role-play anti-factual personas; eight existing lie-detection probes often fail, tracking the persona rather than truth.
- Further experiments show probes rely on spurious signals such as instruction compliance or response likelihood.
- Proposes an improved linear probe, but concludes current lie-detection probes are far from reliable.

### 5. [Distilling Diffusion Score Discrepancy for Efficient Training Data Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38776)

- Data attribution for diffusion models (DDPM, EDM, flow matching) via a local score-discrepancy measure estimated with preconditioned gradient similarity.
- TID uses Kronecker-factored curvature (no random projections or per-sample gradient storage); TIDE is a forward-only distilled student that mimics TID's rankings.
- On CIFAR-10, ArtBench-10, MS-COCO, TID matches or beats baselines; TIDE keeps most accuracy at 4–5 orders of magnitude lower per-query cost.

### 6. [dattri-LLM: A Unified and Efficient Library for Training Data Attribution at LLM Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38767)

- Library unifying training data attribution methods for LLM-scale models (follow-up to the dattri toolkit).
- Relevant as practical infrastructure for influence-style attribution experiments.

### 7. [A helps B while B hurts A: directed transfer in instruction-tuning mixtures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39702)

- Transfer between instruction-tuning tasks is asymmetric and directional.
- A "transfer map" predicts positive/negative transfer for source→target pairs; hundreds of fine-tuning runs on Qwen3 and Mistral.
- Predicts accuracy on unseen mixtures with under half the error of a mixture-agnostic baseline; transfers across model sizes (depends on data, not scale).
- Map-selected mixtures improve reasoning-task accuracy by up to 14 points.

### 8. [Grokking through the Lens of Minimum-Norm Interpolation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38453)

- Statistical theory of how regularization geometry and signal sparsity govern generalization near interpolation in high-dimensional regression.
- Proves a zero-one generalization law in strongly overparameterized noiseless settings; characterizes errors along ℓr paths.
- Validated on diagonal linear networks and transformers on modular arithmetic.
- Notes instability: tiny changes in regularization strength can drastically change generalization at similar training error.

### 9. [How Does Local Landscape Geometry Evolve in Language Model Pre-Training?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39767)

- Phase I: sharp landscape → instability and plateaus at large LR; transition to flatter regions explains the need for warmup.
- Phase II: gradient noise dominates; depth–flatness trade-off (small batches widen the basin, large batches deepen it).
- Practical tips: LR warmup plus a batch-size schedule that starts small and grows.

### 10. [The System Prompt Illusion: How Instruction Preambles Modify Computation in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38205)

- CKA analysis across 17 instruction-tuned models (1.5B–72B): persona and formatting prompts deeply restructure representations, safety prompts barely move them.
- Restrictive and permissive safety prompts engage nearly identical pathways (0.997 correlation); safety changes stay below 10% penetration.
- Prompt category is encoded at every layer but computation is restructured at only a few; offers an explanation for jailbreak vulnerability of prompt-based safety.
