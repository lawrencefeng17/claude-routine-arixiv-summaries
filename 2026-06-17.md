### 1. Rift: A Conflict Signature for Deception in Language Models
**arXiv:2606.17229** | Petr Nyoma

- Compares a **"sleeper agent"** (trained to know the truth but lie when triggered) against a **"naive liar"** (fine-tuned to produce identical wrong answers without honest training) to isolate the internal signature of active deception.
- Finds that deceptive outputs have **2.1–2.3× higher residual rank** than naive-liar passes, enabling perfect detection of lies across multiple architectures — without requiring labels.
- The signature is robust across languages and model families, survives concealment attempts, and transfers across settings without retraining; cannot be injected artificially. Directly relevant to detecting emergent misalignment.

### 2. Noise-Driven Escape from Metastable Phases explains Grokking in Deep Neural Networks
**arXiv:2606.17120** | Ibrahim Talha Ersoy, Karoline Wiesner

- Explains **grokking** (delayed sudden generalization after prolonged overfitting) as stochastic gradient descent noise enabling escape from metastable energy wells separated by barriers.
- Shows escape times follow **Arrhenius scaling**; the number of metastable states equals the number of learnable features (one per singular value of the data covariance matrix).
- Engineers grokking with controlled timing via L2 regularization in linear networks, predicting the mechanism extends to nonlinear architectures. Provides a thermodynamic picture of training dynamics.

### 3. The Discrete-Log Clock: How a Transformer Learns Modular Multiplication
**arXiv:2606.17399** | Huu Danh Nguyen (Stanford)

- When a transformer groks modular multiplication, analyzing embeddings in the **multiplicative character (discrete-log) basis** rather than the Fourier basis reveals a highly sparse structure — **96.9% of MLP neurons are cleanly tuned to a single multiplicative frequency**.
- The model implements a **discrete-log clock algorithm**, reducing multiplication to addition in log space, matching the algebraic structure of the task.
- Demonstrates that choosing the right analytical basis (aligned with the underlying algebra) uncovers mechanistic interpretability hidden by standard Fourier analysis.

### 4. From Reasoning Traces to Reusable Modules: Understanding Compositional Generalization in Language Model Reasoning
**arXiv:2606.18089** | Lingjing Kong, Xin Liu, Guangyi Chen, et al.

- Formalizes why **SFT + RL post-training** succeeds: reasoning traces are generated by a cascade of discrete latent "module" selections; SFT provides the module library while RL identifies and recombines atomic components for novel tasks.
- Key finding: **training on compound traces yields stronger generalization than training on isolated atomic modules** — the compound traces preserve compositional structure that RL exploits.
- Controlled experiments show RL can extract latent modules from compound traces and reassemble them for held-out tasks; has implications for training recipe design.

### 5. Edge Flow: A Tractable and Predictive Continuous-Time Model for Gradient Descent at the Edge of Stability
**arXiv:2606.18080** | Pierre Marion

- Proposes a **three-equation coupled ODE system** (Edge Flow) capturing the edge-of-stability regime: one for the loss-minimizing center, one for the top Hessian eigenvector direction, and one for the instability magnitude.
- Sharpness stabilization emerges naturally from self-feedback in the coupled system, resolving the mystery of why EoS sharpness oscillates before stabilizing.
- Discretized version requires only two gradient evaluations + one Hessian-vector product per step; faithfully tracks gradient descent empirically and gives prescriptions for managing training instabilities.

### 6. Functional Equivalence in Attention: A Comprehensive Study with Applications to Linear Mode Connectivity
**arXiv:2606.17830** | Viet-Hoang Tran, Vinh Khanh Bui, Van-Hoan Trinh, Tan Lai Ngoc, Tan M. Nguyen

- Analyzes **symmetry groups** of transformer parameter space: sinusoidal positional encodings preserve standard attention's symmetry structure, while **RoPE substantially reduces the symmetry group**, explaining its superior expressivity.
- Provides theoretical justification for why RoPE has become dominant in modern LLMs through the lens of functional equivalence and parameter-space geometry.
- Shows positional encoding choice strongly determines **linear mode connectivity** patterns, with direct implications for loss landscape structure and model merging.

### 7. Conservation Laws for Modern Neural Architectures
**arXiv:2606.17816** | Viet-Hoang Tran, Vinh Khanh Bui, Tan Lai Ngoc, Nam Nguyen, Tuan Dam, Tan M. Nguyen

- Derives **conservation laws** (invariant quantities preserved throughout gradient descent) for contemporary architectures: GELU, SiLU, SwiGLU activations; multihead attention with sinusoidal and rotary positional encodings; and Mixture-of-Experts.
- Extends the conservation-law framework beyond linear/ReLU networks that prior work covered, providing a unified theoretical lens on overparameterized training dynamics.
- Validates predicted invariants empirically; conservation laws constrain the solution manifold and explain implicit biases learned by gradient descent.

### 8. Catastrophic Forgetting is Low-Rank: A Function-Space Theory for Continual Adaptation
**arXiv:2606.18024** | Ido Nitzan Hidekel, Dan Raviv

- Shows via neural tangent kernel analysis that **forgetting concentrates in a small number of old-task NTK eigenmodes**, providing a closed-form predictor for the forgetting vector before any new-task gradient step is taken.
- Derives a scaling rule for the vulnerable rank in frozen linear-head scenarios; explains why **parameter-space regularizers can miss output-space interference** that is low-dimensional but behaviorally critical.
- Motivates a targeted spectral regularizer that acts directly on the vulnerable eigenmodes; connects and clarifies prior NTK-overlap theories.

### 9. From Drift to Coherence: Stabilizing Beliefs in LLMs
**arXiv:2606.17832** | SongEun Kim, Seungyoo Lee, Edwin Fong, Hyungi Lee, Juho Lee

- Investigates whether LLMs perform implicit Bayesian inference by testing the **martingale property** of predictive beliefs — whether posterior beliefs update consistently across repeated answer sampling.
- Finds "early-stage belief drift" (initial inconsistency) followed by **self-stabilization** toward a coherent predictive distribution after sufficient resampling — the model implicitly corrects initial inconsistencies.
- Proposes seed-answer prompting and a self-consistency fine-tuning loss that substantially improve belief coherence while maintaining accuracy; has implications for alignment via stable model self-beliefs.

### 10. Loss Landscape Poisoning: Targeted Extraction of Unseen Training Data from LLMs
**arXiv:2606.17110** | Md Abdullah Al Mamun, Ngoc Phu Dou, Pedram Zaree, Ihsen Alouani, Nael Abu-Ghazaleh

- Demonstrates a **training-time attack** where poisoning creates a sharp loss minimum at a private target surrounded by elevated loss on nearby alternatives, forcing the model to memorize the target as the unique low-loss solution.
- Achieves up to **100% extraction success on language models** and 90% on vision-language models across different learning paradigms.
- Introduces a follow-up attack that probes the loss landscape directly, bypassing even **differential privacy defenses** — a significant advance in understanding the limits of DP for LLM training. Directly relevant to data attribution and AI safety.

- **arXiv:2606.15551** — *A Bifurcation Theory Framework for Gradient Descent on the Edge of Stability* (Eric Gan): Formally proves EoS training stability via flip bifurcation governed by the first Lyapunov coefficient; unifies prior EoS results including the product-stability condition. Theoretical companion to Edge Flow (#5 above).

- **arXiv:2606.17803** — *Continual Self-Improvement with Lightweight Experiential Latent Memories*: LLMs self-improve via majority-voting rewards over their own reasoning; compact latent memory modules (~0.001% of parameters) encode reasoning structure without catastrophic forgetting. Relevant to scalable alignment.

- **arXiv:2606.17872** — *AnchorKV: Safety-Aware KV Cache Compression via Soft Penalty with a Refusal Anchor*: KV cache compression that uses a difference-of-means representation engineering anchor to preserve safety guardrails; soft token-retention penalty trades minimal utility for substantially improved safety alignment.
