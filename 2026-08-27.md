## Top 10

### 1. [Training Alignment Auditors via Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25460)

- Trains LLM auditors — models tasked with surfacing hidden undesirable behaviors in target models via investigation — using RL, since current automated auditors struggle with coherent investigation and audit realism.
- An LLM judge compares investigation transcripts against reference standards to generate rewards; pairwise reward comparisons beat pointwise scoring, and mixing in unmodified (non-adversarial) targets during training pushes false-positive rate below 1%.
- Improves investigation quality and surfaces more concerning behaviors in production models, and generalizes across scaffolds, including adversarially fine-tuned targets from AuditBench — a direct contribution to scalable AI safety evaluation infrastructure.

### 2. [Refusal geometry reflects refusal training: diverse refusal prefixes can raise stable rank and weaken refusal vector ablation attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25390)

- Investigates why refusal behavior in aligned LLMs concentrates into a low-dimensional, easily-ablated activation direction, tracing it to the training dynamics of refusal-completion first-token losses rather than to the safety concept itself.
- In a case study on OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct, shows refusal brittleness is linked to repetitive refusal-start phrasing, which concentrates gradients (and thus refusal features) into a narrow subspace.
- Proposes a "hardening lever": training with diverse refusal starting phrases raises the stable rank of gradients/activation changes, making refusal harder to remove via single-direction ablation jailbreak attacks.

### 3. [Mitigating LLM sycophancy with RL-based fine-tuning: Bayesian Truth Serum approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25267)

- Targets sycophancy (models echoing a user's stated beliefs over the truth) by using Bayesian Truth Serum — a peer-prediction mechanism that rewards "surprisingly common" answers — as the reward signal in GRPO, avoiding the need for labeled honesty data.
- Cuts the answer-flip rate under user pressure from 23% to 4% and raises accuracy under pressure from 80% to 93%.
- Proves sycophantic responses have lower expected reward than honest ones under the mechanism, and shows the unsupervised approach matches supervised sycophancy-mitigation baselines.

### 4. [Demystifying Reinforcement Learning Post-Training of Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24949)

- A mechanistic primer that deconstructs RL-with-verifiable-rewards post-training in a controlled, simplified setting, isolating how the base model's prior distribution, reward granularity, prompt diversity, and model scale each shape outcomes.
- Uses policy output-distribution entropy as a lens to compare what pretraining, SFT, and RL post-training each do to model certainty.
- Shows "spurious reward" effects depend on the prompt distribution, and that RL post-training only succeeds when the base model already places enough probability mass on the target behavior — tying practical post-training success to classical RL exploration theory.

### 5. [Canalization Before Generalization: Grokking as a Dynamical Probe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25813)

- Uses grokking (delayed generalization) as an experimental window into how networks select among training-data-fitting solutions that behave very differently out-of-distribution.
- Sweeps short weight-decay pulses across the grokking plateau and finds their effect on later generalization timing is initially unordered but settles into a stable dose-response ordering well before generalization becomes visible.
- Meanwhile, loss barriers between perturbed and baseline solutions collapse to near zero even as this ordered timing sensitivity persists — evidence for a "canalization" process narrowing the solution space long before performance reveals it.

### 6. [Spectral Allocation: Why Muon Outperforms Adam, and How to Improve Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25990)

- Explains Muon's pretraining speedup over Adam via spectral probing of momentum buffers along training trajectories: loss landscapes have a volatile "head" (small optimal step, near Edge-of-Stability) and a tolerant "bulk" (much larger optimal step), and this profile is stable across batches, stages, and scales.
- Shows Muon still underutilizes the bulk directions, and introduces Spectral-Aware Muon (SAMuon/SAMuon-lite), which keeps the head at Muon's scale but amplifies bulk steps via a measured or approximated spectral prior, with no extra persistent state.
- On 124M–1B "modded-nanogpt" models, both variants beat tuned AdamW/Muon baselines, needing 13.3–24.0% fewer tokens to match Muon's validation loss.

### 7. [When Pruning Meets Interpretability: Preserving Sparse Autoencoder Robustness in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25941)

- Studies how post-hoc pruning degrades sparse autoencoder (SAE) interpretability, showing theoretically that the damage is governed by "perturbation energy," a covariance-weighted norm that magnitude pruning ignores but activation-aware methods (Wanda, SparseGPT) implicitly control.
- Finds a consistent structural vulnerability: middle layers are far more sensitive to pruning-induced SAE degradation than early or late layers.
- Proposes a layer-wise sparsity allocation strategy guided by this insight, achieving lower perplexity at matched average sparsity — practical guidance for keeping interpretability tools valid under model compression.

### 8. [Does Fine-Tuning Undo Activation Steering? Behavioural Recovery Without Weight-Edit Reversal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24988)

- Tests whether steering vectors baked into model weights before release (for refusal suppression, brevity induction) survive routine post-deployment fine-tuning, across five instruction-tuned models (3B–14B) under non-adversarial SFT and RLHF.
- Behaviorally, the steering effect degrades when fine-tuning data contradicts it (refusal ablation loses 64% of its effect on average under SFT) but persists otherwise.
- Mechanistically, the underlying weight edit survives almost untouched even when behavior reverts (vector recovery ρ = 0.004; fine-tuning update nearly orthogonal to the steering direction) — steering is mechanistically durable but functionally unreliable, so embedded alignment interventions need behavioral re-validation after any downstream training.

### 9. [Distance Is Not Enough: Forget-Retain Alignment Gap Predicts LLM Relearning Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25429)

- Addresses machine unlearning's core failure mode — brief fine-tuning reviving "forgotten" knowledge — by arguing that global weight-space distance is a poor robustness predictor, since destructive-but-non-selective updates can look large while being fragile.
- Introduces the Forget-Retain Alignment Gap (FRAG), a training-free score measuring whether an unlearning update selectively hits forget-critical weights while sparing retain-critical ones, without needing to actually run a relearning attack.
- Builds Forget-Retain Pruning (FRP) on this principle to produce measurably more relearning-robust unlearning — relevant to both unlearning robustness and data attribution.

### 10. [Learning New Facts with QLoRA: An Acquisition-Retention Frontier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25677)

- Tests the common assumption that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA) automatically preserves pretrained capabilities, using a controlled OpenStreetMap-derived benchmark where Qwen3-4B must acquire new anonymized geographic facts.
- Comparing full fine-tuning against QLoRA at ranks 8/16/32/64 reveals a clear acquisition-retention frontier: low rank preserves out-of-domain performance but learns fewer new facts, while higher rank improves paraphrase generalization of learned facts at increasing cost to unrelated capabilities.
- Full fine-tuning is a conservative baseline that retains well but never reaches the highest-acquisition regime; weight-space/spectral diagnostics show higher-rank QLoRA drifting further from the pretrained model, and the effect is strongest when *new* facts (not reinforced skills) are being installed — bears on training-dynamics and data-attribution questions about how models actually absorb new information.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Reflection Steering: Disentangling Reflection from Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25542)** — training-free activation-steering method that isolates "reflection" (re-checking) from general reasoning computation, cutting reasoning tokens 16.9% on average without hurting accuracy.
- **[Finding and using interpretable latents in a neutrino foundation model with sparse autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26090)** — first application of SAE-based mechanistic interpretability outside language/vision (a physics foundation model), with a strict validation protocol worth borrowing for LLM interpretability work.
- **[Prefix Sliding for efficient test-time scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26070)** — finds most intermediate reasoning tokens lose importance as reasoning continues, and discards them (keeping only prefix + recent window) for 3x faster long-horizon test-time scaling.
- **[Output Dilution: Redundant but Fragile Representations in MoE Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25231)** — shows MoE expert-averaging shrinks the feedforward signal reaching the residual stream, making seemingly robust distributed representations (e.g., moral valence) far more fragile to noise than in dense models.
- **[TailSFT: Filtered Fine-Tuning Improves Post-Training Performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25756)** — SFT variant that filters out already-fit sequences to focus learning on the data-distribution tail, producing checkpoints that are better RL initializations.
- **[Skill Issue: Are Skills Language-Invariant in LLMs?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25832)** — multilingual self-play study finding the same model's strategic skill (not just knowledge) varies markedly by input language, a generalization-failure mode distinct from factual inconsistency.
- **[From Memorization to Absorption: Mixed-Policy RL for Continual Knowledge Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25243)** — argues SFT-based knowledge injection memorizes facts in their training format without generalizing, and shows mixed-policy RL enables genuine knowledge "absorption" instead.
- **[Learning Mixtures of Plackett-Luce Models for Multi-Objective Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25200)** — theory/algorithm (MoPLEx) for recovering heterogeneous annotator preference mixtures from ranking data, directly targeting the identifiability limits of standard RLHF preference models.
