# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-10-30

Selected from 372 new cs.LG entries, focused on alignment/safety, interpretability, generalization, and training dynamics.

## Top 10

### 1. [Activation Probes Are Reliable With a Handful of Positive Examples](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39766)
- Studies probes for rare-misalignment monitoring in a "few positives vs. thousands of negatives" regime (spam, honesty detection).
- Training on many negatives is more positive-sample-efficient than balanced training when there are only 1-10 positives.
- LLM upsampling of positives is worth roughly doubling the real positives, but too much hurts.
- Larger models are more positive-sample-efficient to probe.

### 2. [Agentic Backdoors via Pretraining Poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37741)
- First demonstration that pretraining-data poisoning can plant a remote-code-execution backdoor in a CLI agent.
- Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B/4B pretrained from scratch, then post-trained with a clean pipeline.
- Poisoning 0.1% of the corpus implants the backdoor; the agent acts normally without the trigger.
- Attack success rises with scale (about 92% at 4B) and stays positive even at a 0.0005% poison rate.

### 3. [Does Chain-of-Thought Reliance Follow a Scaling Law?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38433)
- Tests whether answers depend on written CoT steps, using a synthetic box-swap task with known ground truth. Method: replace one step with a wrong-but-valid state and check whether the answer follows.
- 25 open-weight models: reliance rises with size and saturates (Qwen3: ~17% at 0.6B to ~100% at 14B).
- Capability is the main driver (r = 0.99 with step accuracy); post-training doesn't change reliance.
- Supports CoT monitoring on tasks that require step-by-step computation.

### 4. [Loss is Not Behavior: A Unified Output-Space Analysis of Gradient-Based Machine Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38784)
- Derives the output-space gap between a gradient-based unlearner and a retrain-on-retain-only model (closed form for linear regression, extended to nonlinear models via GGN/local-linearity approximations).
- Unifies unlearning methods by geometry, reweighting, and projection.
- Forget self-leverage and retain cross-leverage matrices capture forget/retain entanglement; existing methods handle this only partly.
- Gives precomputable diagnostics for auditing forget sets.

### 5. [Preserving Safety During Sequential Fine-Tuning: A Factorial and Geometric Analysis of Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38001)
- Separates supervised refusal replay from logit distillation during sequential LoRA fine-tuning (Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B).
- Replay cuts HarmBench ASR the most but causes a large over-refusal increase; distillation largely prevents safety erosion at much lower over-refusal cost.
- Preserving the base refusal direction is not a reliable proxy for behavioral safety (the two models diverge geometrically).

### 6. [Exposure Is Not Commitment: Double-Stratified Evaluation of Latent Monitors for Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37679)
- Argues conflicting results on probing for unsafe agent actions are partly an evaluation artifact.
- Probes trained on naive unsafe-vs-rest contrasts mostly learn injection *exposure*, not whether the agent will comply.
- Trained on exposed cases only, probes predict the agent's unsafe tool call up to two steps early (AUROC ~0.85-0.91), though a TF-IDF text baseline matches them on the main grid.
- Recommends stratifying both evaluation and training populations.

### 7. [Value Probes: Learned Value Functions for Beam-Search Red Teaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38526)
- Frames automated red-team auditing as an MDP (states: conversation history; reward: judge score).
- Linear probes on target-model activations estimate discounted future reward ("Value Probes") and guide beam search.
- About 50% higher peak audit scores across 18 behaviors and 3 model scales at matched tokens; probes can be fit with off-policy data from smaller models.

### 8. [Mode Dynamics: A Framework for Neural Network Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40143)
- Decomposes recorded parameter trajectories into collective modes and maps each mode to its output effect.
- Small sets of leading modes reconstruct behavior; induced effects depend on the training path.
- Tested on regression, next-token prediction, CIFAR-100 autoencoding, and a public Pythia-70M pretraining trajectory.

### 9. [Investigating the Early Signatures of Feature Learning in Deep Linear Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37834)
- Closed-form account of one-gradient-step feature learning in a mean-field deep linear network with spiked data.
- Each layer's kernel gets a target-aligned rank-one spike, amplified by learning rate and depth.
- Yields an "anti-shrinkage" effect in kernel ridge regression; the generalization benefit appears only with strong low-dimensional signal structure (it vanishes for isotropic inputs).

### 10. [Implicit Bias of (Stochastic) Gradient Descent on Deep Linear Diagonal Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39717)
- Extends the small-init gradient-flow sparsity result to (S)GD for any depth by viewing it as perturbed (stochastic) mirror descent.
- Proves convergence and links the limiting solution to weighted ℓ1-minimization.
- Gives step-size conditions and quantifies step-size influence on the implicit bias.
