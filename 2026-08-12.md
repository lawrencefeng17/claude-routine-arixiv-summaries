## Top 10

### 1. [Divergent Response Modes in Frontier Language Models Under Steering Pressure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06578)
*Ali Jalal-Kamali*

- Evaluates behavioral steerability across six frontier models (including GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.7, and Llama) using 300 paired base/steered items across values-conflict, reasoning-elicitation, and reasoning-suppression categories, with models cross-judging 24,480 responses.
- Finds models diverge not just in *how much* steering shifts behavior but in *what mode* of response they give — e.g., GPT-5 deflects requests to disclose its reasoning while leaving its answer intact (99% vs. 0% for other models); Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5 resist explicit suppression instructions in different ways.
- On the open-weight Llama model, a linear probe decodes the behavioral mode from the residual stream at 0.87 held-out accuracy, and injecting that direction during generation drives the behavior from 0% to 86%.
- Findings hold under a token-budget control and a hypothesis-blind judge prompt — concrete evidence that different training/safety pipelines produce distinct, causally-steerable internal "response mode" circuits.

### 2. [How to Verify Consistency of Probabilistic Claims](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11181)
*Orr Paradise, Oliver Richardson, Yoshua Bengio, Shafi Goldwasser*

- Asks whether a predictor's many conditional-probability outputs are self-consistent, and whether this can be checked in polynomial time — framed explicitly as an AI-safety problem, since safety can hinge on honest probabilistic self-report of risk.
- Constructs an interactive PCP protocol: given circuits (P, Q) implicitly specifying exponentially many probabilistic claims, a poly-time verifier checks their approximate consistency against a witnessing distribution held by an untrusted prover, querying only a few points.
- Building on Nilsson's classical result, places l2-approximate consistency of explicit probabilistic claims in NP with certificate length O(mn + log B), removing dependence on input bit-precision via a small completeness-soundness gap.
- A complexity-theoretic first step toward models that can *prove* their own probabilistic self-consistency rather than merely claim it.

### 3. [Intrinsic Structure: Spectral Identifiability for Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10172)
*Ashim Dhor, Pin-Yu Chen*

- Tackles a foundational interpretability problem: sparse autoencoders trained with different seeds/widths recover materially different features from the same activations, with no theory distinguishing genuine structure from method artifact.
- Treats the forward pass as a dynamical system and lifts it via the Koopman operator, showing its spectrum is a coordinate-free, model-intrinsic property recoverable from M calibration samples at rate M^(-1/2) — the first identifiability theorem for a mechanistic-interpretability primitive, with a matching minimax lower bound.
- Proves a dissociation theorem: whenever the realization is non-normal, the directions carrying activation variance and those carrying cross-depth information cannot coincide — "identifiable" and "legible" are not the same object.
- Validated on GPT-2 small, Gemma-2-2B, and Qwen3-8B-Base, where the predicted convergence exponent is empirically recovered (0.506 ± 0.031).

### 4. [Off-Axis, On Purpose: Where a Transformer Computes Concepts and Why it Does So](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10251)
*Mark Oskin*

- Shows a 12-layer transformer computes in two geometric phases: intermediate layers deliberately hold representations 75–96° off the unembedding read-out axis, and forcing attention values onto that axis is 64–84x more damaging than a matched random rotation — the off-axis subspace insulates token-composition from the vocabulary.
- The "concept phase" frame rotates rigidly with depth; the answer only arrives on-axis late, via addition rather than rotation.
- Pressuring every layer onto the read-out (as early-exit training does) preserves perplexity/LAMBADA/BLiMP but silently shrinks the effective concept workspace from ~25 to 14 dimensions — a change none of those benchmarks register.
- The specific rotation defining the off-axis frame is largely arbitrary (25 runs across 13 distinct rotations converge to equivalent quality), suggesting axis-separation is a structural feature of transformer computation rather than a basis artifact.

### 5. [ProbGuard: Calibrated Safety Risk Estimation from LLM Output Distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10621)
*Xinzhe Huang, Biwu Yao, Kedong Xiu, Mengnan Zhao, Di Wang, Puning Zhao, Tianhang Zheng*

- Argues that standard guardrails treat safety as deterministic token-sequence classification, discarding the LLM's own probabilistic uncertainty about whether its generation is safe.
- Proposes ProbGuard, an architecture-agnostic guardrail that models safety risk as the unsafe probability of a generation's continuation dynamics, estimated via Monte Carlo sampling over the early output distribution and calibrated via post-training.
- Achieves best-in-class calibration across all nine model–dataset combinations tested, cutting average Brier score and ECE by 79.6% and 71.9% over the best baseline.
- Limits attack success rate to at most 1% across six jailbreak attacks after observing just the first ten decoding steps, enabling early-stopping of unsafe generations before they complete.

### 6. [Measuring Semantic Abstractness of SAE Features via Nonlocality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10537)
*Chuqiao Lin, Shivaji Sondhi, Xiao-Liang Qi*

- Introduces Feature Nonlocality (FNL) — the entropy of an SAE feature's per-position activation influence — as a label-free way to distinguish genuinely high-level/contextual features from surface lexical ones, correctly ranking abstraction level in 73–84% of contextual-vs-token feature pairs.
- Auditing SAE features used for jailbreak mitigation, finds that most "effective" safety features are actually low-FNL positional features rather than features that genuinely recognize harmful intent — a caution about what steering/ablation studies are really capturing.
- Steering high-FNL features in DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B improves MATH-500 accuracy by 4.6 points over the unsteered model and beats steering low-FNL features, though gains are model-specific.
- Provides a concrete, LLM-independent diagnostic for whether a claimed mechanistic explanation is capturing real abstraction or a token-level shortcut.

### 7. [Beyond a Bag of Features: Set-Level Instability in Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11197)
*Nikolai Bolik, Lennart Stöpler, Artur Andrzejak*

- Revisits recent work showing LLM representations broadly track human category boundaries, replacing dense-embedding cosine similarity with overlap over active SAE latent sets as a more interpretable similarity measure.
- Confirms the set-level measure is meaningful in controlled toy models (recovers union-like compositional structure) and induces coherent neighborhoods in natural text.
- But extending to the human-concepts analysis, finds SAE activation sets do not recover human category boundaries or typicality any better than dense embeddings — they instead track model-internal similarity structure.
- Under controlled semantic edits, active SAE latent sets substantially mismatch human judgments of conceptual change — evidence that outside idealized settings, SAE features don't compose via simple "bag of features" semantics, a caution for feature-additivity claims in SAE-based interpretability.

### 8. [Procedural Fairness Failures in RLHF from Preference Averaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10126)
*M P V S Gopinadh, Karthik Kamuju, Kummari Avinash, John Joshua, Srinivasa Raju Rudraraju*

- Defines procedural fairness in alignment as preserving distinct preference signals during reward modeling, and shows standard RLHF violates it: aggregating heterogeneous preferences into one reward model lets majority groups dominate while minority preferences are systematically under-represented.
- Proposes Preference-Aware RLHF (PA-RLHF), which separates optimization across preference modes at the reward-learning stage rather than averaging them.
- In a controlled setting, PA-RLHF improves overall alignment accuracy from 46.9% to 67.9% and shrinks the fairness gap between best- and worst-aligned groups from 15.9 to 9.6 percentage points.
- Shows the failure arises from structural reward-modeling design choices even in noise-free settings — a warning about compounding inequity in agentic systems built on biased reward models.

### 9. [When Chain-of-Thought Helps and When It Hurts: An Empirical Investigation of the Serial-Depth Bottleneck in LLM Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09942)
*Tughanbulut Kurtulush*

- Tests the H_dp bandwidth-bound framework's prediction that CoT should help precisely when a task's per-item serial computational depth exceeds a transformer's single-pass capacity.
- Across three instruction-tuned models (Qwen-2.5-7B/32B, Llama-3.1-8B) and five benchmarks, finds a within-benchmark serial-depth gradient: no-CoT accuracy degrades monotonically with per-item serial depth, while CoT is approximately depth-invariant.
- On high-depth P-complete tasks (GSM8K, MATH), CoT yields a +54 to +68 pp recovery gap; on shallow TC⁰ tasks (MMLU, ARC) CoT is structurally redundant; the intermediate-depth class (HumanEval) shows a model-size-dependent transition from -28.7 pp (7B) to +23.2 pp (32B).
- Pre-registered on OSF, with cross-benchmark depth-recovery correlation ρ = 0.661 (p = 0.007) — reframes CoT not as a universal reasoning enhancer but as a bandwidth bypass for serial computation that strains single-pass capacity.

### 10. [Diffract: Spectral View of LLM Domain Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10850)
*Nikita Borodin, Maria Krylova, Artem Zabolotnyi, Dmitry Aspisov, Egor Shikov, Nikita Tyuplyaev, Oleg Travkin, Roman Alferov, Dmitry Vinichenko*

- Studies continual pre-training (CPT) for domain adaptation (math, instruction, code, natural text) via SVD of weight matrices, finding CPT leaves singular value spectra largely invariant — adaptation is driven mainly by changes in singular *vectors*, not magnitudes.
- Attention-head projection matrices show strong domain-dependent head heterogeneity; up to 60% of head updates can be removed without measurable quality loss.
- Selectively rewinding low-importance heads to their pre-trained state improves benchmark accuracy by up to 4% over the fully-trained baseline — a "less is more" result for which weight updates actually matter.
- Finds domain connectivity: linear interpolation between CPT checkpoints yields smooth quality interpolation across domains without degradation, and releases Diffract, an open-source toolkit for spectral analysis of billion-parameter training dynamics.
