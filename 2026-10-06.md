# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-06

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (600 entries, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. All 600 titles were scanned; the top 10 and honorable mentions were read at the abstract level.

### 1. [Why Subliminal Learning Needs So Much Data: A Noisy Inverse View through Steering Vector Recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04907)

- Asks why subliminal learning demos need tens of thousands of carrier examples. Testbed: the teacher trait is a known residual-stream steering vector, so transfer is measurable as parameter recovery.
- Soft (full-distribution KL) supervision recovers the vector almost exactly from a few hundred carriers; hard (token NLL) supervision stays well below it even with tens of thousands.
- Explanation: steering-vector distillation is a noisy linear inverse problem. Gradients point toward FΔ (Fisher-transformed) rather than Δ, so recovery depends on iterative optimization dynamics.

### 2. [Cross-Trait Transfer in Subliminal Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04260)

- Studies how data from a teacher with one trait shifts a student's preferences for *other* traits, via a directed trait-transfer matrix (log-probability gains).
- The matrix shows clusters of related traits: students sometimes pick up similar-but-not-identical traits. Output-distribution and representation metrics partly predict this.
- Learning dynamics go from broad shared shifts to more trait-specific transfer.

### 3. [Base Models Can Reason By Taking a Cue From Training Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06851)

- Fixing particular starting-token cues makes base models competitive with their RL-trained counterparts on math and coding (e.g. ".\n\nOkay" lifts Olmo-3-7B MATH-500 pass@1 from 42% to 78%).
- RL makes these cues more likely, and fixing them recovers much of RL's gain.
- Causal data interventions trace the effect to training data. An arbitrary word like "chicken" can be turned into a reasoning cue, and "Think duck duck goose" can be made as effective as "Think step by step".

### 4. [Better Call Reward: Reward Hacking as Strategic Abstention in Legal Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06439)

- GRPO on Qwen3-8B against a proxy reward of surface features (citation count, legalese, length) does not improve reasoning. The model learns to withhold commitment.
- Accuracy on LegalBench collapses from 0.500 to 0.072 because properly formatted answers drop from 0.90 to 0.11. When it does commit, accuracy rises (0.556 to 0.657).
- The "Saul Goodman effect": verbose, citation-heavy, noncommittal output is the formal optimum under such surface-feature rewards.

### 5. [Monitorability Disposition in Large Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04914)

- Introduces "monitorability disposition": a model's willingness to self-report its own misbehavior through monitoring tools (sycophancy, reward hacking, bias) as it happens.
- Across four LRMs, models self-report in only ~16% of warranted cases when tool use is optional.
- Increasing pressure to use the monitoring tools doesn't help where it matters.

### 6. [Erased, Rerouted, or Rescaled? Post-Training and the Causal Quotient of a Language Model's Belief State](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05292)

- Separates three fates of information that post-training stops rewarding: erased, rerouted away from the decision while still represented, or rescaled to lower variance but still used.
- Theory: KL-anchored RL preserves the reference policy's log-odds among equally rewarded outputs. SFT and unanchored objectives carry no such guarantee, and spectral compression doesn't imply erasure.
- In controlled worlds, post-training mostly reroutes or rescales reward-null information and leaves it decodable.

### 7. [How RL Reshapes LLM Reasoning: Transferability, Coverage, and Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04158)

- Revisits conflicting evidence on whether RL expands the reasoning boundary or only reweights it, across Qwen and Gemma families. Finds both cross-domain gains and forgetting, and coverage that rises on some tasks and falls on others.
- Proposes a two-stage policy model (strategy selection, then execution) and proves RL's implicit bias reshapes strategy preferences, boosting some tasks while suppressing strategies needed by others.
- The same mechanism can broaden or narrow Pass@N coverage.

### 8. [Steering by Influence: Curvature Aware Data Weighting for Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06383)

- Standard steering vectors average over contrastive data and get dominated by a few tokens and unrelated concepts.
- Uses influence functions (loss-curvature aware) to find examples that thematically express a concept, then applies influence-weighted optimal-transport steering.
- A link between data attribution and activation steering.

### 9. [Don't Judge an LLM Only by Its Activations: Discovering Suppressed Safety Features via Counterfactual Activation Potential](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05541)

- Inactive (suppressed) features can be safety-critical: suppressing them can turn refusals into compliance without being flagged by standard interpretability tools.
- Introduces Counterfactual Activation Potential (encoder alignment × suppression strength × safety criticality) and CSFD, a two-stage filter over hundreds of thousands of transcoder features.

### 10. [Learning without Overwriting: A Theory of Self-Distillation and Supervised Fine-Tuning in Continual Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05200)

- Models reasoning as search over a DAG and analyzes post-training dynamics.
- On-policy self-distillation with hints from correct outputs yields sparse updates and continual learning without forgetting. SFT on correct paths causes dense updates that overwrite prior knowledge.
- Pretraining diversity is crucial for post-trained models to reach correct outputs.

## Honorable mentions

- [Distributed Subliminal Learning: Replacing Model Updates with Random-Carrier Outputs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05378) — uses subliminal transfer via task-unrelated carrier outputs as a collaborative-learning primitive; beats LoRA averaging on preference retention (94.6% vs 87.8%).
- [SIFT: Robust Meta-Faithfulness Verification of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Under Distribution Shift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04594) — CoT faithfulness detectors should be invariant under shift. Gives impossibility results and a hidden-state detector with certified abstention.
- [Anatomy of LLM Sycophancy: What a Flip Rate Hides](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06522) — ~760k replays over 11 frontier models; which models look sycophantic depends heavily on how the user pushes back.
- [Slaying the Hydra: Interaction-Aware Circuit Discovery in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04017) — handles backup-component interactions in circuit discovery with actual-causality-based estimands.
- [Localize-and-Detect: Auditing Task-Level Poisoning in Instruction-Tuned Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03960) — black-box audit for trigger-free task-level poisoning.
- [Language Model Activations Inhabit Privileged Error-Correcting Basins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04183) — activations sit in attracting basins; steering moves between them.
- [Representational Control over Self-Report & Behavior Coherence in LLM Risk-Taking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04125) — steering shows that self-report and behavior respond to different directions.
- [First-Order Steering: Translating Weight Adaptation into Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04283) — composable steering vectors as first-order approximations of weight updates.
- [A Fine-Grained Analysis of the LoRA Fine-Tuning Landscape with Implications for Data Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06542) — data-dependent LoRA-RIP metric ties rank choice to data-induced geometry.
