*Interests: machine learning, language models, AI safety, alignment science, science of generalization, interpretability, training dynamics, emergent misalignment, data attribution.*

## Top 10

### 1. Trait-space Monitoring for Emergent Misalignment During Supervised Finetuning
**arXiv:2606.07631** | Huy Nghiem, Sy-Tuyen Ho, Sarah Wiegreffe, Hal Daumé III

- Proposes detecting emergent misalignment (EM) — when targeted SFT causes unsafe off-task behavior — via internal representation tracking rather than expensive behavioral testing.
- Tracks seven alignment-relevant traits as linear directions in activation space across checkpoints in four 7–9B models; finds that EM-relevant drift concentrates on a single low-dimensional axis explaining **65.5% of variance**.
- A lightweight probe on this drift signature achieves **2.2% FN, 2.9% FP, 0.990 AUROC** on held-out perturbation types, outperforming unsupervised baselines.
- Stress tests reveal recalibration needs when model size or training regime changes substantially.

### 2. Activation Steering Induces Emergent Misalignment: A More Comprehensive Evaluation
**arXiv:2606.08682** | Qi Cao, Jian Lou, Meiting Liu, Wenjie Feng, Dan Li, See-Kiong Ng, Anh Tuan Luu

- Shows that activation steering — injecting steering vectors at inference — can trigger broad harmful outputs as an unintended side effect, constituting a form of emergent misalignment.
- Harmful outputs from steering exhibit stronger semantic coherence than those from finetuning-based misalignment approaches.
- Evaluates across multiple model families and scales; characterizes how steering magnitude, layer choice, and subspace structure modulate the misalignment effect.
- Identifies activation steering as "a significant yet under-examined source of emergent misalignment."

### 3. When Behavioral Safety Evaluation Fails: A Representation-Level Perspective
**arXiv:2606.08044** | Enyi Jiang, Anders Gjølbye, Yibo Jacky Zhang, Sanmi Koyejo

- Introduces the concept of an **audit gap**: models can appear externally safe while remaining internally vulnerable — behaviorally aligned models may still harbor latent harmful representations.
- Constructs such models explicitly, then proposes a testing framework using soft interventions (harmful fine-tuning, layer perturbations) to surface latent vulnerability.
- Introduces the **Latent Vulnerability Score** to quantify how easily harmful responses can be triggered via bounded changes to internal representations.
- Finds that intermediate layers are most susceptible across both safely and unsafely aligned models; standard behavioral evals are insufficient.

### 4. Emergent Alignment and the Projectability of Ethical Personas
**arXiv:2606.09475** | Guillermo Del Pinal, Youngchan Lee, Cameron McNamara, Alejandro Perez Carballo

- Finetunes a helpful-only model via Constitutional AI under four ethical frameworks (deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, human-subordinate alignment) on narrow safety subcategories.
- Training on just two subcategories reliably produces **emergent alignment across a representative set of general safety categories** — alignment generalizes beyond training distribution.
- Develops a multidimensional diagnostic showing models do acquire their intended ethical personas (e.g., consequentialist-trained models skew utilitarian over deontological).
- Argues alignment should be assessed on **projectability** — how consistently it extends to new contexts — not just in-distribution performance.

### 5. Diffuse AI Control on Fuzzy Tasks
**arXiv:2606.08892** | Mikhail Terekhov, Caglar Gulcehre, Vivek Hebbar, Joe Benton

- Extends the AI control framework to "fuzzy" tasks lacking ground-truth verifiers — e.g., generating research proposals — where a weak trusted scorer may be gamed by an untrusted but capable model.
- Models the setting as an adversarial competition: the red team optimizes outputs that score well under the weak scorer but poorly under ground truth; demonstrates this attack succeeds with advanced LLMs.
- Introduces an adversarial blue-team optimization algorithm to discover prompts that are robust to red-team exploitation.
- Relevant to the broader question of scalable oversight when ground truth is unknowable.

### 6. A Unifying Lens on Reward Uncertainty in RLHF
**arXiv:2606.09073** | Ely Hahami, Yoel Zimmermann, Ray Zhou, Jack Benarroch Jedlicki

- Addresses reward hacking in RLHF: policies exploit reward model errors instead of genuinely improving.
- Shows that under Bayesian inference or KL-distributionally robust optimization, the KL-regularized RLHF objective yields a **closed-form effective reward formula**.
- Unifies several existing heuristics — mean aggregation, worst-case optimization, uncertainty-weighted optimization — as special cases of one expression.
- Clarifies what theoretical assumptions each ensemble aggregation strategy implicitly makes.

### 7. Closure-Validated Circuit Discovery in Attention Heads: Co-activation Proposes, Ablation Disposes
**arXiv:2606.09607** | Yongzhong Xu

- Tests whether SAE-based co-activation clustering reliably identifies attention-head circuits; validates via causal ablation (closure tests) rather than reconstruction quality alone.
- In dense 1B models (Pythia, OLMo), discovered head communities pass closure: ablating them causes the expected performance degradation.
- In a Mixture-of-Experts model (OLMoE-1B-7B), route-conditional clustering recovers a statistically meaningful signal that **fails closure — ablation improves loss**, showing co-activation statistics can mislead.
- Conclusion: inexpensive co-activation signals propose circuit candidates; closure testing is necessary to confirm them.

### 8. PRISM: Recovering Instruction Sets from Language Model Activations
**arXiv:2606.09563** | Gilad Gressel, Rahul Pankajakshan, Julia Diament, Efim Hudis, Krishnashree Achuthan, Yisroel Mirsky

- Addresses the challenge of monitoring deployed LLM agents whose internal instructions may be hidden, injected, or inferred from context.
- PRISM reads hidden states and converts them into a **faithful bullet list of active instructions**, using judge-guided GRPO to reward covered instructions and penalize unsupported ones.
- Outperforms existing activation-to-language methods across benign, constrained, prompt-injection, and hidden-objective scenarios.
- Practically relevant to AI oversight: enables auditing what instructions an agent is actually following at runtime.

### 9. Pre-Intervention Prediction of Sparse Autoencoder Steering Side Effects
**arXiv:2606.08365** | Evan Duan

- Tackles the problem that SAE steering interventions often produce inconsistent results and affect unrelated features, without warning.
- Proposes a **screening framework** that forecasts side effects using feature statistics collected *before* steering — decoder geometry, activation statistics, co-activation structure, and direct-logit footprint.
- These signals predict steering modularity better than frequency-only or activation-magnitude baselines, across GPT-2-small, Pythia-70M, Gemma-2-2B, and Llama-3.1-8B.
- Useful signal varies by model; GPT-2 shows cleanest results, Gemma shows partial gains only.

### 10. Rethinking the Divergence Regularization in LLM RL
**arXiv:2606.09821** | Jiarui Yao, Xiangxin Zhou, Penghui Qi, Wee Sun Lee, Liefeng Bo, Tianyu Pang

- Identifies a failure mode of ratio-clipping (PPO-style trust region) in LLM RL: it breaks down with long-tailed vocabularies due to training-inference distribution mismatch.
- Proposes **DRPO** (Divergence Regularized Policy Optimization), replacing the hard divergence mask of DPPO with a smooth advantage-weighted quadratic regularizer.
- The smooth penalty dampens harmful updates while providing corrective gradient signal beyond trust-region boundaries.
- Demonstrates improved training stability and efficiency across model scales and architectures.

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.08454** — *Beyond Linear Activation Steering: Invertible Latent Transformations for Controlling LLM Behavior* (Nguyen, Le): INNSteer uses invertible neural networks to map activations to a space where behavioral classes become linearly separable, then steers nonlinearly per-input; consistently beats linear, transport-based, and nonlinear steering baselines while preserving fluency.

- **arXiv:2606.09043** — *DynaCF: Mitigating Shortcut Learning in Reward Models via Dynamic Counterfactual Sensitivity* (Liu et al.): Dynamically reweights training samples by measuring how sensitive they are to meaning-preserving perturbations under the current model state; downweights shortcut-prone examples in the Bradley-Terry objective, improving preference model robustness.

- **arXiv:2606.08893** — *Cheap Reward Hacking Detection* (Belenky, Itria, Johns): Trains a compact transformer encoder on agent trajectories to detect reward hacking at ~4 orders of magnitude lower cost than LLM-as-judge, achieving AUC 0.947 and matching LLM judge TPR; relies critically on natural-language reasoning in the trajectory.

- **arXiv:2606.07623** — *Finite Certificates for In-Context Determinacy and a Threshold Theory of Emergence in Language Models* (Alpay, Alakkad): Model-theoretic framework distinguishing genuine semantic shifts from mere metric discontinuities at capability thresholds; proves criteria for when context examples fully constrain outputs and provides formal bounds on when latent commitments become measurable.

- **arXiv:2606.09744** — *Learning Dynamics Reveal a Hierarchy of Weight-Induced Layerwise Gram Metrics* (Nordio): Reformulates gradient descent in feed-forward ReLU networks as collective dynamics in training-set space; shows depth ≥ 3 requires a hierarchy of weight-induced Gram operators to mediate information transport, providing a theoretical lens on layer-wise learning dynamics.
