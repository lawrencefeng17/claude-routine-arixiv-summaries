# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-15

Note: arXiv does not announce on weekends, so the cs.LG listing still shows **Fri, 13 Nov 2026** (186 new + 154 cross-lists). This is the third digest drawn from that listing. The picks below **exclude the 47 papers already featured in the 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14 digests**, so the remaining pool is thinner and leans more toward theory and methods. Summaries are based on the abstracts.

## Top 10

### 1. [Code as Explanations: Executable and Verifiable LLM Explanations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14704)
- Proposes explaining an LLM's behavior across a whole dataset with a **program** rather than with per-instance chain-of-thought. Because the program can be executed, the explanation can be checked automatically.
- **Consistency** = the fraction of inputs on which the program reproduces the LLM's output. It is used both to evaluate candidate programs and to select among them.
- Across 3 open models and 8 tasks, the code explanations are often highly consistent and are 6–100× shorter than CoT. On linguistically complex tasks they do no better than an uninformative baseline and fall back on hard-coded patterns.
- Giving the explainer consistency feedback and code execution helps, but does not close the gap on the hard tasks.

### 2. [Symmetry in Time: A Representation Theory of Linear Recurrent Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17162)
- Uses representation theory to explain why diagonal SSMs fail at state tracking: their transitions commute, so the state records *which* inputs occurred but not *in what order*.
- Proves an SSM tracks a group at every length exactly when that group is a quotient of the group generated by its transition matrices. Diagonal real or complex SSMs can therefore only track commutative groups. Any SSM that tracks a group must have transitions that split into real, complex, and quaternion blocks.
- **HyperSSM** adds quaternion channels and keeps parallel training. It solves S5 with 32 generators exactly and generalizes to sequences 25× longer than those seen in training.
- Because the theory says what the transitions must encode, the learned weights can be **read off directly**. Demos include recovering a gyroscope's calibration and the geometry of protein backbones.

### 3. [Probabilistic State Space Enumeration in Neural Network Computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15062)
- Rewrites a transformer MLP exactly as an expectation over binary "inclusion states" of channel groups. Importance-corrected masked outputs recover the dense output exactly.
- **NORDER-II** enumerates joint states in decreasing probability without building the exponential state space, which gives an anytime approximation.
- On Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma-2-2B it reaches about 93% MLP-output accuracy, versus about 78% for channel top-k. Perplexity stays within 0.5% of dense when 3 layers are approximated at once.
- Interp angle: **joint-state structure** matters well beyond per-channel salience.

### 4. [Knowledge in the Harness, Reliability in the Weights: Teacher-Free Co-Evolution of an LLM Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15268)
- A Gemma 4 12B customer-service agent improves itself from its own deployment logs, with no stronger teacher model. It writes heavily gated rules into "skills" and also trains a LoRA adapter with GRPO on full multi-turn conversations.
- After two rounds on τ²-bench it gains +12.5 pass^1 (retail) and +19.5 (airline), and beats the reference agent that has the full policy in its prompt.
- **Key finding (2×2 ablation):** the rules rescue tasks the agent never solved and carry policy knowledge that **RL does not internalize** even when the rules are in context. The RL'd weights mainly make already-solvable tasks *reliable*. This is a clean separation of what RL does and does not teach.

### 5. [StateAlign: Internalizing State-Aware Decision-Making for Reliable Multi-Turn Tool Agents via RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16304)
- Observes that RL on tool use can raise task scores while also **raising tool hallucination**. Agents execute when they should ask for information, retrieve it, or decline, even when their own reasoning identifies the missing information.
- **State-contrastive RL:** the same reasoning and actions are scored under paired task states that require different actions, using token-level contrasts on top of outcome rewards.
- On Qwen3-8B, BFCL v4 multi-turn accuracy rises from 45.5% to 55.2%, and no-tool hallucination falls from 62.2% to 56.1% relative to outcome-only training. The method also improves recovery after tool errors.

### 6. [Joint Null-Space Projection for Data-Free Model Merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17103)
- Treats task-vector interference as a **subspace** problem. For each weight matrix it takes the top-r right-singular subspace of each task's delta and projects out the components that lie in the other tasks' subspaces.
- The method is closed-form, order-independent, and data-free. Merging 8 CLIP ViT-B/16 experts reaches 89.45% average accuracy, 11.9 points above TIES and 1 point above WUDI.
- Controls: using one shared subspace loses 2.9 points, and a sequential variant collapses. The authors report honestly that on RoBERTa-large the method underperforms plain task arithmetic.

### 7. [Factored One-sided Adam-Moment for Practical and Scalable SOAP (FOAM)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16905)
- A lightweight SOAP variant. It keeps only **input-side preconditioning**, which is exact if output-side curvature is diagonal and nearly lossless in practice. It also uses a factored row×column second moment.
- Matches SOAP and Muon validation loss in LLM pretraining from 124M to 760M parameters, with half of SOAP's optimizer state and Muon-class wall-clock time.
- Includes measurements of how much the K-FAC and diagonal-curvature assumptions are actually violated (15–20%), which is useful for the curvature-structure picture of training.

### 8. [Improved Elastic Weight Consolidation as an Optimization Constraint for Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16039)
- Reframes continual learning as a **constraint** that new training must preserve chosen functionals of old behavior. EWC's empirical Fisher falls out as the first-order case where the preserved functional is each sample's loss.
- This exposes a flaw: EWC weights samples by the size of their loss gradient, so **well-fitted samples lose protection** and corrupt samples can dominate the importance matrix.
- IEWC penalizes output changes along each sample's loss level set. It matches or beats EWC across classification, segmentation, language, forecasting, and diffusion, including +2.0 points on ImageNet-R in a held-out evaluation.

### 9. [Characterizing Local Breadth in Language Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14887)
- Theory of the **validity–breadth trade-off** (hallucination versus mode collapse) in the Kleinberg–Mullainathan "generation in the limit" model, with adversarial example order.
- Measures breadth with lower Banach density, which rules out large holes in coverage. In 1-D it characterizes the optimal density exactly, and one generator achieves the optimum for every target at once.
- For d ≥ 2 there may be **no single best generator**: improving breadth for one target can hurt another. The paper characterizes the full region of guarantees that can be achieved together.

### 10. [Prevalence Bounds for Edited Watermarked Text: Power Envelopes and Calibration Sensitivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17035)
- Auditors often need the *share* of a corpus that came from a watermarked model, not just a per-document verdict, even when documents may have been edited.
- With no limits on editing, the upper bound on that share is 1. The paper derives a power envelope from three budgets (retention, entropy, substitutions) for Gumbel-max and KGW watermarks, which yields sharp identified sets, finite-sample bounds, and confidence sequences.
- Validated on OPT-1.3B and Qwen3-1.7B text. It spells out where the bounds break: LLM paraphrases fall below the envelope, and a small error in the null can break coverage at corpus scale (100k documents).

## Honorable Mentions
- [Prediction or Persistence? What Future-Prediction Scores Miss About World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15082): pairs each video world model with a "persistence" copy whose predictor is never run. The predictor's real contribution ranges from +0.29 down to negative, and the authors argue future-prediction scores should always be reported next to this baseline.
- [Dataset Distillation via Drifting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16642): distilled samples are "drifted" in feature space toward the real data and away from each other, giving better distribution coverage. The authors also use it as a tool for visualizing what models have learned.
- [Schedule-Calibrated Commitment Order in Blockwise Masked Diffusion LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15234): shows that the apparent left-to-right unmasking order in LLaDA is largely an artifact of the block schedule. Useful caution for anyone interpreting the generation order of diffusion LMs.
- [Asymmetric Multimodal Learning with Decomposed JEPAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16639): proves that alignment-only training keeps only redundant information and discards private and synergistic structure. D-JEPA adds private per-modality latents and confirms with causal swap tests that the recovered information sits there.
- [SAME: Semantic Watermark Embedding for Open-Source Large Pretrained Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15771): a private vocabulary aligned to the model's native semantics yields ownership watermarks that survive downstream fine-tuning.
- [Recursive State-Augmented Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16375): a 64-dimensional persistent latent state gates LoRA on a frozen GPT-2 and matches a full-context baseline on multi-turn inference, even though the decoder never sees the earlier turns.
- [Adversarial Risk in the Generative AI Era Necessitates Dropping the Small Epsilon Ball](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14594): a position paper arguing that robustness within small ℓp balls gives defenders a false sense of security.
- [Improved last-iterate bounds for SGD with and without replacement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14811): tightens the SGD last-iterate rate in the interpolation regime by a log log T factor.
