# arXiv cs.LG — Top Papers for June 15, 2026

*Interests: machine learning, language models, AI safety, alignment science, science of generalization, interpretability, training dynamics, emergent misalignment, subliminal learning, data attribution.*

### 1. The Weight Norm Sets the Grokking Timescale: A Causal Delay Law
**arXiv:2606.13753** | Truong Xuan Khanh, Doan Hoang Viet, Luu Duc Trung, Phan Thanh Duc

- Uses **causal intervention experiments** to show weight norm is the direct driver of delayed generalization (grokking): networks grok when norm reaches a critical threshold that is consistent across seeds and learning rates.
- The grokking delay follows an **exponential relationship** with norm (relative to the critical value), parameterized by a single exponent that generalizes across multiple mathematical task structures.
- Weight norm has substantially more influence on grokking timing than learning rate; adding LayerNorm removes the dependency entirely.
- Provides a mechanistic, causal account of an emergent training-dynamics phenomenon with clear implications for understanding why delayed generalization occurs.

### 2. Compressed Computation is (probably) not Computation in Superposition
**arXiv:2606.14673** | Jai Bhagat, Sara Molas-Medina, Giorgi Giglemiani, Stefan Heimersheim

- Investigates the popular *Compressed Computation* (CC) toy model for superposition; finds its apparent ability to compute 100 ReLU functions with 50 neurons is an artifact of **input mixing via a noisy residual stream**, not genuine superposition.
- Decomposing the training objective shows performance gains correlate with the mixing matrix's magnitude; removing mixing eliminates them.
- Learned neuron directions concentrate within the top-50 eigenvalue subspace of the mixing matrix, and a **semi-non-negative matrix factorization baseline** derived solely from that matrix reproduces qualitative loss patterns.
- **Core conclusion:** CC is not a valid toy model for computation in superposition — a methodological correction with implications for how the field uses toy models to study neural representations.

### 3. A Low-Rank Subspace Analysis of LLM Interventions
**arXiv:2606.14388** | Angira Sharma, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Philip Torr, Anisoara Calinescu, Jialin Yu

- Models LLM behaviors (refusal, jailbreak robustness, sycophancy) as **low-rank subspaces** in activation space; tested across 7B–70B instruction-tuned models.
- Interventions targeting one behavior (e.g., reducing sycophancy) cause **asymmetric side-effects** on others: some behaviors act as upstream control points with broad influence, others are isolated.
- Two geometric factors predict side-effect magnitude: **subspace overlap** (measured via principal angles) and the angle between the source behavior's subspace and the decision boundary.
- Provides a principled framework for predicting and diagnosing unintended behavioral changes from alignment interventions.

### 4. Natively Unlearnable Large Language Models
**arXiv:2606.13873** | Gaurav R. Ghosal, Pratyush Maini, Aditi Raghunathan

- Introduces NULLs: an architecture with a shared backbone plus **sparsely activated source-specific sinks**, so information from each training source concentrates in dedicated sink components during training.
- **Deployment-time unlearning** requires only disabling the relevant sinks — no gradient updates, no access to retained data.
- Scales to ~6M Wikipedia articles; removing one article preserves related semantic knowledge while achieving targeted forgetting; robust to adversarial re-extraction.
- Directly addresses the data attribution problem at the architecture level — a significant shift from post-hoc unlearning to *by-design* controllable memorization.

### 5. Hidden in Plain Sight: Benchmarking Agent Safety Against Decomposition Attacks with DECOMPBENCH
**arXiv:2606.13994** | Vikhyath Kothamasu, Virginia Smith, Chhavi Yadav

- Introduces **DECOMPBENCH**, a framework for *decomposition attacks*: adversarially splitting harmful requests into innocuous subtasks that individually pass safety filters but collectively accomplish a harmful goal.
- State-of-the-art agents maintain high refusal rates for direct harmful requests but show substantially weakened defenses against decomposed versions of identical objectives — sometimes inadvertently completing the harmful task.
- Uses graphical decomposition principles to systematically vary task fragmentation, enabling principled measurement of this safety gap.
- Closely related to concepts of **emergent misalignment** and subliminal manipulation; the attack surface is structural (task decomposition) rather than linguistic (adversarial prompting).

### 6. Can Editing 1 Neuron Fix Repetition Loops in LLMs?
**arXiv:2606.13705** | Aristotelis Lazaridis, Aman Sharma, Dylan Bates, Brian King, Vincent Lu, Jack FitzGerald

- Gemma 4 instruction-tuned models collapse into repetitive patterns on lengthy factual enumeration tasks at rates up to **95%**, a reproducible failure mode.
- **Localizes the failure** to specific MLP neurons (or routed experts in larger models); a single sign-inverted neuron eliminates the repetition circuit while preserving benchmark scores.
- However, surgical fixes address the *repetition symptom* but not the underlying **knowledge-precision problem**: models still self-correct in circles over forgotten facts.
- A clean case study in mechanistic interpretability at deployment scale — demonstrating both the power and the limits of circuit-level intervention.

### 7. Mirage Probes: How Vision Models Fake Visual Understanding
**arXiv:2606.13870** | Daniel Ben-Levi, Judah Goldfeder, Weiliang Zhao, Raz Lapid, Amit LeVi, Allen G. Roush, Ravid Shwartz-Ziv, Hod Lipson

- Identifies two distinct failure modes in vision-language models: **textual bias** (answers driven by language patterns without image access) and **spurious images** (models fabricate plausible visual content in latent space).
- Both failure modes are **linearly decodable from internal activations** across residual stream, MLP, post-attention, and attention-head sites — making them tractable targets for probing.
- The two modes require different interventions: cleaning text distributions helps textual bias; spurious images require targeted intervention in visual representation layers.
- Directly relevant to alignment: models can confidently give image-grounded responses while fabricating the visual evidence they appear to rely on.

### 8. Code Correctness Signals in LLM Hidden States: Pre-Generation Probing and Repair Geometry
**arXiv:2606.14530** | Carlo Di Cicco

- Linear probes on **prompt-final hidden states** of Qwen3-4B-Instruct predict first-attempt code correctness with AUC 0.931 on LiveCodeBench — *before the model generates any code*.
- The signal is robust: it persists after residualizing out prompt-length effects.
- The hidden-state shift from failed to successful repair attempts is statistically significant, but disappears after accounting for contextual factors — a carefully noted negative result.
- Shows that correctness confidence is geometrically structured in LLM representations, with implications for understanding how models "know" what they don't know.

### 9. Beyond a Single Explanation of the Adam–SGD Gap
**arXiv:2606.14259** | Chenxiang Zhang, Rustem Islamov, Enea Monzio Compagnoni, Jun Pang, Aurelien Lucchi, Antonio Orvieto

- Empirical study across vision, language, genomics, and graph tasks showing **no single factor** explains when Adam outperforms SGD; the gap depends on data distribution, activation function, and batch size jointly.
- Identifies a **crossover batch size** beyond which SGD becomes the preferred optimizer — a previously uncharacterized phenomenon.
- Provides a theoretical model explaining the batch-size-dependent crossover, reconciling several competing prior explanations.
- Directly relevant to training dynamics: practitioners relying on "Adam always beats SGD for language" or similar rules-of-thumb may be miscalibrated.

### 10. A Theoretical Model for Task Routing in Mixture-of-Expert Transformers
**arXiv:2606.14398** | Yongli Xiang, Vinoth Nandakumar, Yunzhi Yao, Peike Li, Tongliang Liu

- Provides the first theoretical analysis of MoE routing specialization in **discrete language models** (prior theories relied on continuous models).
- Proves that a single-layer MoE transformer can encode task-specialized experts via syntactic templates and finite key-value dictionaries, with expert capacity scaling with task complexity.
- Theoretical routing predictions align with **empirically observed localized knowledge circuits** in MoE systems; validated through experiments under different MoE loss functions.
- Relevant to science of generalization: explains how scale + routing can produce structured internal specialization, touching on emergent modularity.

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.13862** — *SuperThoughts: Reasoning Tokens in Superposition* (Xiong et al.): Compresses pairs of consecutive CoT tokens into single latent representations, decoding two tokens per step; achieves ~20–30% reduction in chain-of-thought length with minimal accuracy loss. More an efficiency paper than an interpretability one, despite the name.

- **arXiv:2606.13867** — *Muon^p: Muon with Fractional Spectral Powers* (Dong, Sawin): Extends the Muon optimizer with fractional spectral-power updates interpolating between Muon and gradient descent; shows empirical gains on billion-scale finetuning. Solid training-dynamics contribution.

- **arXiv:2606.14347** — *When Language Representations Interact: Separability and Cross-Lingual Effects in LLMs* (Marinov et al.): Finds language concepts admit stable linear representations separable under a causal inner product; languages within the same family show simplex-like geometric structure. Relevant for understanding LLM internal organization and unintended cross-lingual behavioral transfer.

- **arXiv:2606.14518** — *Behavioral Audit of Machine Unlearning Has a Privacy Cost* (Tang, Joshi, Kundu): Proves that behavioral auditing of unlearning — querying model outputs to verify data removal — inherently leaks membership information about the retained set; demonstrates a fundamental tension between auditability and privacy. Relevant to data attribution and the trustworthiness of unlearning pipelines.
