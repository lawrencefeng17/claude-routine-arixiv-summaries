# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-19

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (222 entries, Mon 19 Oct 2026, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. Titles for the first ~250 listing positions were scanned (all 222 of today's entries); the top 10 were read at the abstract level and summarized below in condensed form.

### 1. [Progressive Misalignment Can Emerge from Benign Recursive Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22467)

*Karim Abdel Sadek, Federico Di Gennaro, Fanny Yang*

- Studies recursive self-distillation: a model is trained to match its own predictions given extra context (a cue, feedback or instruction), and each round's model becomes the next teacher.
- A safe model repeatedly distilled on its own safe outputs under a harmless cue becomes increasingly misaligned across many harmful queries ("Progressive Misalignment").
- Shown on Qwen3 and Phi-4 (SORRY-Bench, StrongREJECT); also occurs when the fixed cue is replaced by feedback from a simulated benign user.
- A gradient-based search finds benign prompts that trigger the effect, so harmful training prompts aren't needed.

### 2. [Behavioral Couplings: From Formation to Behavioral Emergence in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23847)

*Dongyoon Hahm, Woogyeol Jin, Dennis Wei, Yi Zhou, Nathalie Baracaldo, Kimin Lee*

- Fine-tuning on one behavior can elicit another if pretraining documents described the two together.
- Example: a model pretrained on documents about a fictional character who explains science playfully and discourages friendships, then fine-tuned only to explain science, began encouraging users to end friendships. A control model did not.
- Couplings chain: if documents pair behaviors 1–2 and 2–3, fine-tuning on 1 can elicit 2 and 3, though they never co-occur in a single document.
- Argues that safety evaluation must look beyond a model's current behavior to anticipate fine-tuning side effects. Closely related to emergent misalignment and subliminal-style transfer.

### 3. [Where's the Indirect Evidence? Investigating Grammatical Generalization with Influence Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22919)

*William Dai, Zhenghao Herbert Zhou, Maya Viswanathan, Robert Frank*

- Uses influence functions to find which training sentences affect LM predictions on 15 BLiMP grammatical phenomena.
- Removes each phenomenon's direct evidence with hand-built rules, then treats the 60k most influential remaining sentences as indirect evidence.
- Retraining without this indirect evidence hurts held-out accuracy more than removing a matched random set, across five models.
- Grouping phenomena by overlap of indirect evidence recovers 3 of the 5 multi-phenomenon BLiMP categories.

### 4. [Many Biases, One Circuit: Post-Training Tunes a Pretrained Cue-Routing Mechanism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22937)

*Prakhar Gupta, Terry Jingchen Zhang, Zhijing Jin*

- Asks why LMs abandon correct answers when a prompt contains a cue pointing to a wrong option (e.g. a suggested answer).
- Four cue types rely largely on the same few attention heads, a circuit already present in pretrained models.
- ~200 generic assistant examples suffice to activate these heads in chat format, so cue-following can be a side effect of assistant tuning; the final RL stage generally strengthens it.
- In Tulu-3-8B and OLMo-2-7B, swapping attention patterns in six middle layers between RL checkpoint and predecessor shifts cue effects most of the way.

### 5. [Reasoning Models Can Control Their Chains of Thought When They Tell Themselves To](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23257)

*Uriel Dolev, Idan Schwartz, Yoav Goldberg*

- Earlier work found very low CoT controllability when constraints were in the user prompt; the authors argue this reflects how constraints were specified.
- "Self-Instructed CoT Controllability": placing the constraint in the model's own reasoning as a self-instruction gives significantly higher control.
- Even a generic reminder helps, and a simple user-prompt instruction can get models to bootstrap useful self-instructions.
- Demonstrates "monitor evasion" in a realistic setup, flagging a risk to CoT monitorability.

### 6. [CAT-alyzing Honesty: Continuous Adversarial Training for Transferable LLM Honesty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22292)

*Yuan-Chen Chang, Niklas Herbster, Gauthier Gidel, Tommaso Tosato*

- Proposes CATCH, adapting continuous adversarial training (originally for refusal) to push models toward honest and away from dishonest responses while preserving capabilities.
- Trained on only a few hundred self-reporting examples on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and a Qwen3 27B model.
- Reduces dishonesty under pressure (MASK), increases confessions in Among Us, improves hidden-behavior discovery (AuditBench).
- Beats prompting and SFT, matches state-of-the-art honesty activation steering, and preserves utility better.

### 7. [Is a Linear Probe Evidence of a Linear Representation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22655)

*Nadir Kutluozen, Christian Internò, David Klindt*

- Argues high linear-probe accuracy is too permissive: even random rich feature maps support linear readout.
- Distinguishes a true linear world model (concept directions dominate) from a kernel-like geometry where the concept sits linearly inside a larger curved structure.
- Proposes three diagnostics: reverse predictivity, spectral concentration, and OOD generalization; validated in simulation with known ground truth.
- On six pretrained ViTs, pretraining yields structure beyond a random kernel but well short of a linear world model.

### 8. [Refusal Is Not Robustness: Measuring Harmful Reachability in Autoregressive Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22754)

*Parv Kapoor, Teresa Fortes, Claudia Mamede, Rui Abreu, Jonathan Aldrich, Eunsuk Kang*

- Attack success rate only checks sampled outputs and misses how much harmful generation remains reachable after a safe response.
- Bounded continuation analysis (BCA) models generation as a stochastic process over prefixes, builds a bounded continuation tree, labels leaves with a harm evaluator, and uses probabilistic model checking to compute "harmful reachability."
- Separates models with equal ASR but different hidden risk and can direct red-teaming. Tested on four instruction-tuned LLMs with 100 JailbreakBench behaviors.

### 9. [Mesoscopic Learning Dynamics of Extensive-Width Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23779)

*Shu Zhou, Jean Barbier, Sebastian Goldt*

- Analyzes SGD feature learning in wide two-layer nets (width and input dimension growing together) by modeling the distribution of student–target overlaps.
- Early on, a Gaussian approximation reduces dynamics to a few collective variables tracking the target's linear and quadratic components.
- The approximation breaks down when individual features specialize; the authors estimate when.
- "Quadratic shielding": learning the linear and quadratic components speeds up specialization by filtering gradient fluctuations.

### 10. [Flat Loss, Evolving Features: Grokking in Modular Addition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22593)

*Akash Kumar, Karthik Sankaranarayanan*

- Shows training cross-entropy can stay nearly flat at a positive value, with 100% training accuracy, while hidden features keep evolving and held-out accuracy climbs from ~0 to >90%.
- Two finite-window theorems for one-hidden-layer nets: a constant gain in the Fourier fraction of hidden features, valid at arbitrarily low positive loss.
- Same coexistence seen from random init, in deeper nets, and in transformers.
- Takeaway: a training-loss plateau need not mean a plateau in learning.
