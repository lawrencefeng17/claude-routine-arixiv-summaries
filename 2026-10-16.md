# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-16

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (306 entries, Fri 16 Oct 2026, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. All titles were scanned; the top 10 were read at the abstract level, and some details below are truncated from the abstract.

### 1. [Do Emergent Misalignment Models Share a Circuit?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20887)

- Three LoRA fine-tunes of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (harmful medical, risky financial, extreme-sports advice) each show emergent misalignment (EM).
- Direction-based activation tracing finds three attention-head locations shared among the top-5 heads of all three models.
- Swapping in the base model's contributions at those heads cuts free-generation EM (medical 38.9%→22.9%, finance 50.7%→12.5%, sports 41.7%→11.1%). It beats random layer-matched heads and keeps coherence.
- The shared heads are partially interchangeable across organisms, which suggests a common circuit.

### 2. [ContextLift: Breaking the Spurious Correlation Behind Conditional Emergent Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21633)

- Interleaving generic safety data is the best known in-training defense against EM, but misalignment returns when a prompt resembles the fine-tuning context.
- Frames this "conditional misalignment" as a spurious correlation between the domain context and misaligned answers.
- ContextLift duplicates half of the safety rows with a context sentence lifted from a random training prompt, keeping the aligned answer. It needs no extra model, labels or domain knowledge.
- On Qwen2.5-7B (bad security, legal, medical advice), misalignment under a domain cue falls from 18.9/41.9/58.2% to 2.6/6.1/12.8%, with the narrow task and benign fine-tuning intact.

### 3. [Knowledge That Is Linear Before Fine-Tuning Generalizes After It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22300)

- Separates "active" facts, which support generalization beyond their own recall, from "inert" ones that can be recalled but don't generalize.
- Test: relabel a relation's answers arbitrarily (Europe→crab), fine-tune on some subjects, and check whether held-out subjects with the same original answer get the new label.
- Across 11 LMs and 70 relations, how linearly readable the original relation is in pretrained representations strongly predicts this generalization.
- A mechanistic account of when fine-tuning generalizes, and when it only memorizes.

### 4. [Interpreting Language Model Parameters (adVersarial Parameter Decomposition, VPD)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20748)

- Decomposes an LM's parameters into subcomponents that each implement a small part of the learned algorithm, with only a few needed per input.
- Trains for decompositions that preserve input-output behavior even when many subcomponents are ablated, including adversarially chosen ablations.
- Moves parameter-space interpretability beyond activation-space analysis.

### 5. [Multi-Reward Hacking: From Aggregation-Induced Drift to Trust-Anchored Defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21404)

- Shows that symmetric aggregation of a primary reward (correctness) and auxiliary rewards (brevity, etc.) can raise the aggregate while primary-task performance falls.
- Proves that deterministic conditional-mean GRPO converges to auxiliary-favoring behavior when auxiliary rewards are easier to obtain, regardless of initialization.
- Proposes Trust-Anchored Gating (TAG), which elects an anchor channel from the policy drift each channel induces alone, then gates auxiliary credit on anchor satisfaction.

### 6. [Same Answer, New Box: How Much of RLVR's Out-of-Domain Gain Is Answer Format?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20639)

- RLVR is credited with out-of-domain gains such as higher MMLU after math training, but these are read out by answer extractors that often look in `\boxed{}`.
- On four 7B SimpleRL base/RL pairs, 82% of the reported out-of-domain multiple-choice gain disappears under a format-agnostic extractor (77% with independent LLM readers).
- Base models already state most answers in text, and RL mostly moves them into the box. Much of the claimed generalization is format learning.

### 7. [Learning to Read Out: Unembedding Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21025)

- Hidden states can encode task information before the model's own readout (unembedding) expresses it, so output-based capability timelines conflate encoding with expression.
- Introduces parameter-trajectory crosscoding, a sparse dictionary fit to every token's readout row at every checkpoint, which needs only checkpoint weights.
- Finds a narrow early window (0.4–1.1% of Pythia's pretraining) where the readout reorganizes and WordNet categories become decodable.
- Linear probes decode task information at that point even though outputs don't show it yet.

### 8. [Can Capability Enhancement be Localized?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21294)

- Asks the converse of head-ablation work: is there a small set of attention heads whose removal improves task performance?
- A two-stage screen-and-joint-ablate routine finds sparse head combinations whose removal improves task-specific performance across model families, with no fine-tuning.
- Also gives a theoretical analysis of when training dynamics could produce such harmful heads.

### 9. [Human Interventions as Labels: Localizing, Steering, and (Sometimes) Editing the Computations Behind LLM Agent Failures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21708)

- Treats a human overseer's intervention as a measurement pointing at the internal computation that failed. The test case is an agent that over-asks clarifying questions on unambiguous requests.
- In the five eligible model families (Qwen2.5-3B, gemma-2-2b, OLMo-3-7B, Mistral-Nemo-12B, Phi-4), a direction learned from intervention labels flips 72–100% of held-out over-asks at a definite residual-stream depth. Random directions flip at most one episode.
- In four families the model demonstrably knows the request is unambiguous (the belief is decodable), so this is an action-selection failure rather than a missing belief.

### 10. [Do As I Do, Not As I Say: On-Policy Distillation Inherits the Teacher in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20989)

- Compares on-policy distillation (OPD) with filtered SFT for VLM reasoning across data budgets and teachers of varying reliability.
- OPD works only when the teacher stays reliable on student-generated trajectories. On ChartQA (teacher completes 99% of solutions), OPD on 10% of the data is within 1.4 points of full-data SFT.
- Where the teacher is unreliable (Geometry3K), the student inherits the teacher's behavior, relevant to how traits transfer through distillation.
