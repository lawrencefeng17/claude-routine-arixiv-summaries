## Top 10

### 1. Transplanting, Inverting, and Preventing a Misalignment Persona: Method-Conditional Emergent Misalignment in Qwen2.5
**[2607.04510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04510)** · Drake, Eberstadt

- Extends emergent misalignment (EM) work by showing the latent "misalignment persona" direction found in Qwen2.5 is causally portable: transplanting it into a model that shares only pretraining with the source induces broad EM (2.83% misaligned vs. ~1.1% random-direction floor), and ablating a model's own direction roughly halves an inducer's broadcast (21%→10%).
- Whether a fine-tune recruits the persona is method- and capacity-dependent: on Qwen2.5-32B, low-rank LoRA on insecure code recruits it (3.4% misaligned) while full SFT on the same data does not (0.3%) and even moves against the persona axis — consistent with a "loss-reducing shortcut" account where low-capacity PEFT is more prone to recruiting the shortcut than full-parameter updates.
- The persona's causal role is itself conditional: steering a bad-medical SFT run away from the direction during training can backfire, raising broadcast from 24% to 51%, showing removing the direction is no blanket fix.
- Recruitment orders rank-perfectly with persona loss-relevance across four inducers, and the authors show it can be screened for and selectively prevented via inoculation (4.75%→0.0% broadcast) — a concrete lever for anticipating and blocking EM before deployment.

### 2. Covert Trait Propagation Is Representation Alignment: Mechanistic Evidence from Hidden-Channel Distillation
**[2607.04432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04432)** · Chauhan, Shah

- Provides a mechanistic account of subliminal learning: a student trained purely on a shared-initialization teacher's outputs on uniform noise still inherits digit-classification ability, and this paper localizes *where* the covert channel lives and what governs its capacity.
- Shows the shared output projection acts as a common "coordinate key," while KL gradients reshape the student's input projection until its hidden representations geometrically align with the teacher's (linear CKA tracks student accuracy at r=0.98) — i.e., covert trait propagation is representation alignment, not just leaked logit statistics.
- Confirms causality via targeted freezing: freezing the input projection destroys transfer while freezing the output projection leaves it intact; multi-teacher ensembles cancel the effect out despite each teacher carrying comparable label information.
- Extends the same geometric lens to instruction-tuned LLMs' cross-token behavioral entanglement, finding the effect appears activated by alignment training atop an inherited substrate, and flags that the standard log-ratio metric used to study it is partly a circularity artifact.

### 3. Out-of-Distribution Generalization of Risk Aversion in Language Models
**[2607.02755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02755)** · Zhang, Okoroafor, Maltbie, Lin et al.

- Tests a concrete alignment failsafe idea directly: if a misaligned model is trained to be risk-averse on low-stakes gambles, will that risk aversion generalize to astronomically high-stakes gambles where it would matter most?
- Using Qwen3-8B, several elicitation methods (SFT, DPO, activation steering) push a baseline 2% "Cooperate" (safe) rate up to 39–70% at stakes 98 orders of magnitude beyond training — real, if partial, OOD generalization of a trained disposition.
- A separately fine-tuned reward model reliably ranks risk-averse reasoning above risk-neutral or excessively risk-averse alternatives (99.6% pairwise accuracy), and effects replicate across model scale (1.7B–14B) and families (Gemma-3, Llama-3.1).
- Frames an open problem central to alignment science: risk aversion trained at low stakes generalizes, but not yet consistently enough to serve as a reliable failsafe against misalignment.

### 4. Individual Parameters in Weight-Sparse Transformers Appear Interpretable
**[2607.02964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02964)** · Marin-Llobet, Heimersheim

- Asks whether a single weight (not just a circuit or feature) can be understood globally across the entire training distribution, rather than only on a curated sub-distribution as standard circuit analysis does.
- Introduces an automated LLM pipeline that writes and then verifies short, human-readable descriptions of when a weight matters (i.e., where ablating it changes predictions), crediting a weight only if the description generalizes to held-out text.
- Across two sparse and two dense transformers, 12–31% of weights in sparse models get a single short description that reliably identifies their function — a fraction that's higher, with the gap widening further, than in dense transformers once unreliable descriptions are filtered out.
- Direct evidence that architectural sparsity (not just post-hoc dictionary learning like SAEs) buys real gains in weight-level interpretability at the individual-parameter granularity.

### 5. Faithfulness to Refusal: A Causal Audit of Neuron Selectors
**[2607.05355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05355)** · Eswar, Seth, Avaiya, Sankarapu

- Directly tests whether attribution methods used for pruning, interpretability, and safety editing actually identify causally important neuron rows, via paired one-shot zeroing audits rather than trusting attribution rank-stability as a proxy for validity.
- At the language-modeling level, attribution methods substantially beat activation/magnitude baselines at finding dispensable rows across five LLMs; when redirected with a contrastive harmful-vs-benign signal, the same attributed rows are sufficient to install refusal on hate/crime content while keeping over-refusal low, and layer-matched random controls fail.
- A striking negative finding: highly rank-stable selectors can be among the least causally valid — stability and causal correctness are not the same thing.
- Shows refusal lives in a redundant subspace where different attribution methods install it through largely disjoint neuron sets, meaning any single recovered "refusal circuit" is one realization among many sufficient sets, not a unique mechanism — a caution for interpretability claims about safety-relevant circuits.

### 6. Structure-Specific Representational Priors Causally Control the Grokking Delay
**[2607.04333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04333)** · Howe

- Tests causally (not just observationally) whether the grokking delay measures the time needed to form task-structured representations, by injecting representational priors of varying structural correctness into a one-layer transformer learning modular addition.
- A clean gradation emerges: priors encoding the task's true equivalence structure generalize in 22/30 runs, a coherent-but-wrong sibling structure in 14/15, and a random partition (satisfiable only by memorization) in 0/20 (Fisher exact p=1.3×10⁻⁷) — with a weight-norm-matched control ruling out norm growth as the mediating variable.
- Representation probes confirm structure formation precedes and predicts generalization across all 95 runs, and only the *true* structure accelerates grokking (up to 2.75× faster), while wrong-but-coherent structure leaves the grokking delay intact.
- A precise causal pin in the "science of generalization": grokking delay is the time to form the right feature-level structure, not just any structure, and not merely a function of weight-norm dynamics.

### 7. Grokking Is Conditional and Fragile: A Fully-Tractable, Multi-Seed Study at 12K Parameters
**[2607.05104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05104)** · Ootani

- A methodological corrective to the grokking literature: studies a fully-tractable ~11,856-parameter transformer (small enough to enumerate every weight) on modular arithmetic, measuring grokking as a multi-seed *rate* rather than reporting single training runs as if they were representative.
- Finds grokking is gated by training-set coverage, with the threshold tracking output cardinality (the modulus) more than task structure, and reproduces the Omnigrok weight-decay inverted-U as a positive control.
- Grokking sits on a genuine numerical knife-edge: CPU thread count (reduction order) and CPU-vs-GPU execution each flip a minority of same-seed outcomes with no detectable shift in the aggregate rate — implementation nondeterminism alone changes whether an individual run "groks."
- Multi-seed control under a fixed numerical environment overturns three dramatic single-run narratives in the author's own data, each traced to a seed confound — an argument that the unit of evidence for grokking claims must be a rate, not an anecdote.

### 8. How to Avoid Debate: Scalable AI Safety via Doubly-Efficient Interactive Proofs
**[2607.03561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03561)** · Chen, Kalai, Xi

- Targets a foundational assumption in AI-safety verification-via-debate protocols — that two competing AI provers have equal ability and one is truthful — by initiating the study of single-prover interactive proofs for AI safety instead.
- Prior single-prover interactive-proof results don't transfer directly to AI safety settings where the computation has oracle access (e.g., to human judgment or the web), so the paper develops doubly-efficient single-prover interactive proofs/arguments for oracle-aided ("relativizing") computations.
- Covers two structured settings: (1) robust computations where the output doesn't change if a small fraction of oracle answers are wrong, and (2) oracles that are low-degree polynomials.
- A theoretical scalable-oversight contribution: shows interactive verification of powerful AI systems is possible without needing a second, adversarially truthful AI prover, under structured or noise-tolerant oracle access.

### 9. Learning Only What Valid Adapters Can Express: Subspace-Constrained Adaptation Against Fine-Tuning Poisoning
**[2607.05300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05300)** · Polly

- Addresses a practical training-dynamics vulnerability: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) leaves a broad space of behavior-changing updates reachable, so a poisoned fine-tuning objective can be represented and optimized just as easily as a benign one.
- Constrains adaptation to a low-dimensional subspace estimated from 196 public trusted LoRA adapters on flan-t5-large, showing the functionally relevant content of an adapter lies in a shared subspace (30–38% of weight norm is redundant), and that gradient adaptation restricted to just 128 subspace coordinates matches full LoRA on clean data.
- Under targeted label-inversion poisoning, standard LoRA collapses to 3–26% exact match while the constrained learner retains 62–96% on covered tasks; the constrained learner also cannot fit corrupted data at all, giving a built-in clean-vs-garbage separation signal (120× loss gap) without needing a separate detector.
- Against an adaptive attacker optimizing within the trusted subspace, the attack is blocked (8% success vs. 100% for plain LoRA) when the poisoned target behavior is unlike anything in the trusted pool, but only partially blocked (85%) when it resembles common pool behavior — a concrete, quantified boundary on this defense.

### 10. Efficient Decentralized Multi-task Dataset Valuation via Model Merging
**[2607.03346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03346)** · Alipour, Amiri

- Targets data attribution for the harder, more realistic case of multi-task buyers and decentralized sellers, where existing Shapley- or retraining-based valuation is single-task and prohibitively expensive without a trusted central coordinator.
- DMVM infers each dataset's marginal utility purely from how independently trained models combine in parameter space (task arithmetic), avoiding retraining or raw data sharing entirely.
- Includes a secure aggregation protocol for privacy-preserving decentralized valuation (no individual model parameters or private data revealed) plus theoretical error bounds on the approximation quality.
- Validated on both vision and NLP multi-task settings — a scalable, privacy-respecting alternative to expensive retraining-based data attribution methods.
