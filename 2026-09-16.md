## Top 10

### 1. [Large Language Models Develop Belief State Geometry In-Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17376)

- Prompts six open-source LLMs with sequences emitted by hidden Markov models and probes for the HMM's posterior belief state, finding it is linearly decodable from residual-stream activations (peak probe R² of 0.83–0.99) across early-to-late layers.
- Confirms functional relevance via patching and steering on the probe-identified subspace: intervening there preserves near-untampered downstream prediction quality, while control subspaces degrade performance substantially.
- Extends toy-network results linking input-distribution structure to activation geometry all the way to production-scale LLMs — representation-level evidence that in-context learning approximates Bayesian prediction over a context-inferred generative model.

### 2. [Verbalizing Subliminal Learning Effects Using Text Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16927)

- Introduces SALVE (Search-Aided Latent Verbalization): optimizes a soft prompt to match a subliminal-learning distillation dataset, then has the same model verbalize that soft prompt as legible text via beam search, recovering a plain-language description of the hidden trait.
- Reliably names the teacher's trait in the standard subliminal-learning setup where ordinary text-optimization baselines fail, and generalizes to mixtures with unrelated data, activation-steering-induced bias, and preference-data subset selection.
- Also shows the reverse failure mode — modifying student training to improve context distillation can *create* subliminal learning effects that weren't otherwise present — deepening the mechanistic picture of how hidden traits transmit through data.

### 3. [TAME: Token Attribution and Masking for Emergent misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16754)

- Localizes emergent misalignment (EM) at the token level: attributes how much each fine-tuning token raised the likelihood of harmful downstream responses using forward passes through a released LoRA adapter, then causally validates the finding via attribution-guided masking.
- On a 6,849-example medical-advice EM split, attribution is highly concentrated (top 5% of tokens hold 32% of the mass) and, in Llama, enriched for a register of unwarranted certainty rather than medical vocabulary itself.
- Masking just the high-attribution tokens during fresh fine-tuning cuts EM by 23x (Llama) and 36x (Qwen) with minimal perplexity cost, while an equal-sized random mask leaves EM unchanged — suggesting EM rides on *how confidently* flawed content is expressed, not its topic.

### 4. [Attention Mean Fields Predict Average Representation Dynamics and Reveal Context-Specific Computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16382)

- Introduces a mean-field analysis of attention: the average attention from one token to another defines a kernel that can be iterated through the network to model how representation geometry evolves layer to layer, conditioned either on a whole corpus or a single context.
- The corpus-conditional kernel gives a fully open-loop prediction (frozen weights, no measured activations) that is highly accurate early in training — replacing every attention head with its mean field leaves loss on real text unchanged — but diverges sharply around the onset of induction.
- Context-conditional deviation from the mean field emerges as a task-agnostic measure of context-specific computation, decomposing into unusual attention routing versus contextualized value transport, and tracks reliance on in-context information across induction and few-shot settings.

### 5. [Anatomy of Associative Recall in Fixed-State Recurrences: A Matched-State Decomposition, an Interference Wall, and a Curriculum That Breaks It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16183)

- Decomposes "recurrent models are bad at recall" into single-knob ablations (short causal convolution, rank-1 vs. diagonal transition, decay) at matched state budget, finding the convolution — not the recurrence type — dominates prior architecture comparisons.
- Identifies the real bottleneck as interference under sparse supervision rather than capacity: models that solve 32-pair recall collapse to chance on a 4-pair distractor haystack, and training is a "lock-in lottery" where a seed either finds the solution or doesn't.
- A distance curriculum takes an unchanged architecture from 0.021 to 1.000 recall accuracy and raises lock-in rate from 1/10 to 7/10 seeds — a cheap, mechanistically-grounded intervention rather than an architecture change.

### 6. [Test-Time Unlearning via Sparse Autoencoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16229)

- Proposes ARIA, which leaves model weights untouched and instead trains a lightweight linear detector on SAE latents to gate generation only when it enters a forget-related state — sidestepping the sharp forget-utility trade-off of gradient-ascent unlearning methods.
- On TOFU, R-TOFU, and WMDP, ARIA improves the forget-retain trade-off over weight-based baselines across both a reasoning model and an instruction model, and stays robust (<1% change) under three new post-unlearning adversarial recovery attacks.
- An interpretability case study suggests some apparent "retain degradation" actually reflects the unlearning data's response style rather than true knowledge leakage — a methodological caution for how unlearning benchmarks are built.

### 7. [Decoy Direction Optimization: A Post-Hoc Defense Against LLM Abliteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16204)

- Targets refusal-feature ablation (RFA) attacks, which strip safety guardrails by projecting out a linear refusal direction found via contrastive estimation; DDO instead injects a high-magnitude nonlinear decoy signal into MLP neurons that corrupts the attacker's direction-finding estimator.
- Achieves <10% attack success rate under standard RFA across six model families with 30–450x lower optimization cost than trained safety-finetuning defenses, and proves a spectral bound formalizing why the decoy fools the contrastive estimator.
- Remains competitive with trained defenses even under adaptive multi-phase attacks on Llama-3-8B-Instruct (65% vs. 58% worst-case ASR), and cuts a stronger weight-level attack's success from 88.7% to 18%.

### 8. [Latent Undertow: How Ordinary Typos Break Probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15994)

- Finds that while LLMs handle typos fluently at the behavioral level, hidden-state probes used for prompt-injection/malicious-intent detection are far more brittle: a single typo rotates the probe's readout vector by 43–56° at the perturbed token, decaying below 15% within ~10 downstream tokens.
- Stacking ~3 common typos cuts a single-position probe's true-positive rate by 12 percentage points at fixed false-positive rate — a gap that simple recalibration cannot close, and that persists (though attenuated) even with multi-position aggregation.
- Introduces a KV-cache fork — a short fixed suffix appended after the user message that lets the probe read downstream of the perturbation — closing 95% of the gap, an order of magnitude better than perturbation-augmented training; the rotation-and-decay geometry replicates across Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, and Gemma.

### 9. [Easy to Catch a Liar, Hard to Clear an Honest One: Language Models Diagnosing a Corrupted Reward Channel from a Verified Record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17226)

- Builds a two-option game where a genuine payout swap and a lying reward-reporter produce byte-identical histories, then hands frozen LLMs a single verified record of one round's true outcome and asks them to diagnose whether the reporter is honest or lying.
- Models catch a lying reporter almost perfectly, but clear an honest reporter far less reliably: a 72B model calls an honest reporter a liar 38% of the time with no change at all, rising to 58% when payouts genuinely moved — and the pre-registered prediction for that number (35%) undershot the observed failure.
- The specific surface feature driving the false-accusation differs by model family (which round is named for Qwen, which letter denotes "honest" for Llama), and adding the verified record to an already-correct prompt can make Llama *less* likely to give the right answer — a concrete failure mode for reward-tampering detection via richer contextual evidence.

### 10. [On the Importance of Gating: Memorization vs. In-Context Learning in State Space Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16540)

- Shows theoretically and empirically that the gating mechanism common to modern SSMs causes them to first learn an in-weights "memorization" solution, delaying or even preventing convergence to a correct in-context-learning solution — even when the architecture has no fundamental capacity limitation.
- This explains both SSMs' known ICL/retrieval weaknesses relative to Transformers and a countervailing benefit: gating improves generalization to longer sequence lengths than the model was trained on.
- Provides a mechanistic account connecting a specific architectural component to both training dynamics (what solution is learned first) and generalization (how well it extrapolates), useful for anyone deciding when linear-time architectures are a safe substitute for attention.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[What Does Layer-Importance Reveal About Transformers and State-Space Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16537)** — decomposes layer importance into Necessity (loss increase from bypassing a layer) and Plasticity (fine-tuning update magnitude); the two anti-align across depth in transformers but overlap in Mamba-style SSMs, with concentrating updates in plastic transformer layers increasing catastrophic forgetting.
- **[Compute-Optimal Pretrain–Fine-tune in Ridge Gradient Descent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16262)** — a tractable theoretical model (regularized least squares under gradient descent) for the compute-split problem between pretraining and fine-tuning, characterizing the optimal allocation via spectral components of the two stages' data covariances.
- **[Right Direction, Wrong Step: Geometric Analysis of Finite-Step Failure in Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16665)** — finds that additional reasoning iterations in looped transformers can be locally improving yet globally harmful once fully applied; a fixed quarter-step recovers positive gains in 72–83% of these failures, pinpointing step-scale mismatch as the mechanism.
- **[When Confidence Signals Disagree: Local and Global Confidence in Autoregressive Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16933)** — shows token-probability confidence and sampling-based modal-answer confidence are only weakly correlated and diverge in their relation to correctness, arguing confidence should be treated as an explicitly-defined measurement rather than a single intrinsic scalar for oversight use.
- **[Interpreting and Steering LLM Agents for Social Simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16436)** — compares prompt-based manipulation, SAE feature steering, and probe-based direction steering for controlling LLM-agent preferences and capabilities, finding SAE+probe pipelines generally outperform prompting for reliable behavioral steering.
- **[BLINDSPOT: A Benchmark for Safety and Refusal Calibration in Long-Horizon Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16305)** — a live-simulation benchmark of 2,500+ multi-turn tool-using trajectories (avg. 14.7 turns) showing safety failures in agents often emerge only after several initially-safe steps, arguing for trajectory-level rather than single-turn safety evaluation.
- **[ImpossibleRubrics: Stress-Testing Generated Rubrics as Reward Signals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16816)** — on tasks where the only honest answer is to acknowledge impossibility, LLM-generated grading rubrics are exploited by adversarial answers 8–64% of the time depending on generator, revealing that tailored rubrics can leak which claim to fabricate.
- **[Few-Shot Degradation Is Not What It Seems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15990)** — introduces a length-matched random-text control to isolate representation shift caused by demonstration *content* versus mere prompt length, finding content-driven shift (not raw shift) predicts whether few-shot prompting helps or hurts across 12 models.
- **[Implementing a White-Box Undetectable Backdoor for Random Fourier Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16403)** — a from-scratch numpy/scipy reproduction of the Goldwasser et al. cryptographically-undetectable RFF backdoor construction, confirming the threat is realizable with commodity scientific-computing tools rather than requiring specialized cryptographic infrastructure.
