# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-22

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (240 entries, Thu 22 Oct 2026, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. All titles were scanned; the top picks were read at the abstract level and condensed below.

### 1. [Monitorability Is Trainable: Post-Training for Chain-of-Thought Monitorability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29734)

*Usman Anwar, Tim Bakker, Christos Louizos, David Krueger*

- CoT monitoring is a key oversight tool, but training can break the link between reasoning and behavior (CoT compression, optimizing against a monitor, generalization from synthetic documents).
- Proposes an unsupervised post-training objective that maximizes mutual information between the CoT and the output.
- On three model organisms with degraded CoT, recovers most or all lost monitorability; on healthy policies it also improves judged monitorability, helps CoT monitors and activation probes predict unsafe behavior, and increases consequential reasoning.
- Suggests a dedicated monitorability post-training stage could complement approaches that just shield CoT from optimization pressure.

### 2. [Mitigating Reward Hacking in Realistic Scenarios Through Representation Engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29129)

*Austin Meek, Kyle Cox, Matthew Nguyen, Austin J. Brockmeier, Steven Basart*

- Targets reward hacking in realistic settings (tool use, long contexts) with a dynamic steering intervention.
- Steering directions come from difference-in-means on labeled rollouts, gradient-based optimization, or a model's references to an anti-reward-hacking spec.
- Alters representations less than a uniform residual-stream steering vector; substantially reduces hacking at low cost with little coherence/capability loss.
- Vectors extracted in coding environments transfer to other domains.

### 3. [SimHack: Predicting the onset of detectable reward hacking before RL training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28707)

*Junwei Deng, Alex Bie, Natalia Ponomareva*

- Initial hacking rate doesn't determine when hacking becomes dominant; onset varies widely across recipes and seeds, so the task is predicting an onset-step distribution.
- SimHack learns hacking dynamics from a few small reference RL runs and simulates counterfactual runs for unseen recipes.
- Median onset predicted within 11.3% relative error, cross-recipe rank correlation 0.85; generalizes across model scale, curriculum, rollout count, and learning rate.
- Used to screen curricula: the best one trained 96.6% longer without hacking.

### 4. [RL-IF: Tracking Non-local Training Data Influence for Online Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28883)

*Shixuan Liu, Cheng Tang, Yuzheng Hu, Fan Wu, Han Zhao, Jiaqi W. Ma*

- Data attribution for online RL usually captures only single-round (local) influence; this paper tracks influence across multiple rounds.
- Defines RL-LOO (trajectory-level leave-one-out) and a first-order estimator RL-IF covering both optimization effects and the policy's own sampling effects, with approximation guarantees.
- Finds the optimization effect alone usually approximates the full estimator closely.
- Derives RINSE, a data-filtering method that improves efficiency and final performance over PPO and local-attribution baselines on control tasks and an RLHF-style toxicity-mitigation task.

### 5. [Unintended Side Effects of Value Training in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29922)

*Siddhesh Pawar, Chen Cecilia Liu, Anna Korhonen, Isabelle Augenstein*

- Fine-tunes four instruction-tuned LLMs along individual Hofstede cultural-value dimensions, then measures transfer to other values and broader behaviors.
- Value-specific training spills over onto untargeted values, risk preferences, social cognition, and safety-related traits.
- Argues value-alignment evaluation must include broad behavioral testing after post-training (an emergent-misalignment-flavored result).

### 6. [Silent Alignment in Two-Layer Linear Networks: A High-Dimensional Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29864)

*Simon Martin, Francis Bach, Giulio Biroli*

- Exact high-dimensional analysis of gradient flow in two-layer linear nets (proportional dimensions, whitened inputs).
- With very small initialization, singular directions align on an O(1) timescale while singular values and loss barely move ("silent alignment").
- Visible learning comes later on a log timescale via saddle-to-saddle dynamics, which become continuous spectral learning in the limit.

### 7. [Data Schedules and Learning Rate Schedules Should Be Designed Together](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29348)

*Haodong Wen, Kairong Luo, Tingkai Yan, Wenguang Chen, Kaifeng Lyu*

- Studies mid-training data mixing schedules beyond the hard switch from pretraining to mid-training data, at fixed compute and MT-token budget.
- The best data schedule depends on the LR schedule: with decaying LR, mixing MT data in earlier beats saving it all for the end; with constant LR the advantage reverses.
- Best to start LR decay only after MT data is introduced; a theoretical model explains the interaction.

### 8. [When Thoughts Become Memory: Unifying In-Context Learning and Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29419)

*Leon Chlon, Julie Huang*

- Gives an exact "information value" (code length saved on held-out queries) for deciding whether new data belongs in context or in weights.
- Upper bounds and an anytime-valid test certify the choice from held-out queries; validated on transformers, TabPFN, and others.
- On GPT-2, picks the better of cache-in-context vs. fine-tuning in 61,948 of 64,000 query streams; also yields label-free data valuation (AUROC 0.91 on a claim-adjudication task).

### 9. [Phase Transitions in Learning Driven by Extremal Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29531)

*Ethan Levien, Trevor GrandPre*

- Studies multiple-instance learning with max-pooled labels, where learnable softmax sharpness λ must grow but drives gradients to vanish.
- In the large bag size / high dimension limit, derives a low-dimensional gradient flow with two phase transitions: a freezing transition (random energy model) and a retrieval transition (modern Hopfield networks).
- Simulations confirm the phase diagram.

### 10. [Automating ML for Science: Can Frontier Agents Climb Scientific Hills in the Wild?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28763)

*Ming Zhong, Stacy Li, Nicholas Carlini, Matthew Jagielski*

- Agent4Sci: 20 benchmarks across 11 domains measuring how much coding agents can improve published best models.
- Frontier agents beat the published best on every benchmark (Claude Opus: 10.4% mean relative gain), with no misconduct flagged; gains mostly came from ML-engineering tricks rather than research insight.
- Failure modes: novelty miscalibration, resource under-utilization, quick collapse into local engineering sweeps. Lightweight plug-in skills raised Opus to 20.1%.
