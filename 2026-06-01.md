# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — June 1, 2026

*Topics: machine learning, language models, AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization*

## Top 10 Papers

### 1. Interpretability Without Tradeoffs: Disentangling Polysemanticity At Equal Predictive Performance
**arXiv:2605.31304** | Doğukan Bağcı, Bernt Schiele, Simone Schaub-Meyer, Jonas Fischer, Robin Hesse

Individual neurons in pretrained models (DINOv2, ViT-B/16) encode multiple unrelated concepts simultaneously. ELUDe (explicit, lossless, unsupervised disentanglement) breaks latent representations into inspectable sub-units that behave like interpretable features — with **zero change to model outputs**.

- Requires no retraining or labels; applies to frozen pretrained models
- Demonstrates improved interpretability on vision models without sacrificing downstream performance
- Enables practical applications like representation steering; directly addresses polysemanticity without the typical accuracy/interpretability tradeoff

---

### 2. On the Relationship Between Activation Outliers and Feature Death in Sparse Autoencoders
**arXiv:2605.31518** | Elana Simon, Etowah Adams, James Zou *(ICML 2026)*

SAEs suffer from "feature death" — many learned features never activate, wasting capacity. Death rates vary wildly: near zero in GPT-2 but >70% in AlphaFold3 with identical configurations. The culprit is **dimension-level activation outliers** that shift pre-activations so that misaligned features receive permanently negative inputs.

- Introduces γ = ‖μ‖/‖σ‖ as a predictor of initial death rates; validated across 454 model-layer combinations (language, vision, protein, genomic models)
- Mean-centering eliminates outlier-induced feature death across all tested architectures
- Provides a principled, cross-domain explanation for when and why this preprocessing step is necessary

### 3. Toward Identifiable Sparse Autoencoders
**arXiv:2605.31245** | Walter Nelson, Theofanis Karaletsos, Francesco Locatello *(ICML 2026)*

SAEs are unstable: different training runs produce different concept dictionaries and sparse codes. This paper identifies architectural and procedural causes of the instability and proposes **iSAE** (identifiable SAE), a TopK variant with improved stability.

- Connects SAEs to classical dictionary learning; shows that learned dictionaries approximate a restricted isometry condition
- Resulting sparse codes are nearly identifiable across runs, addressing a core reproducibility concern
- Directly relevant to reliability of SAE-based interpretability pipelines

### 4. Steering LLMs? Actually, Sparse Autoencoders Can Outperform Simple Baselines
**arXiv:2605.31183** | Mikkel Godsk Jørgensen, Lars Kai Hansen

Challenges prior negative findings about SAE-based steering: using a supervised feature-selection pipeline on AxBench, SAEs perform **close to LoRA-level** steering — contradicting earlier benchmarks that cast doubt on SAEs as causal tools.

- Interpretability-based components alone can identify surprisingly causal features
- High sparsity may not be essential for successful steering, contrary to prior assumptions
- Directly relevant to the viability of mechanistic interpretability as an alignment technique

---

### 5. Positional versus Symbolic Attention Heads: Learning Dynamics, RoPE Geometry, and Length Generalization
**arXiv:2605.31558** | Felipe Urrutia, Juan José Alegría, Cinthia Sanchez Macias, Jorge Salas, Cristian B. Calderon, Cristobal Rojas

Training a decoder-only LM on two structurally equivalent multi-hop reasoning tasks reveals that successful learning correlates with the emergence of "pure heads" — either positional or symbolic, never mixed. The two head types have different length generalization properties.

- **Symbolic mechanisms generalize more reliably to longer sequences**; positional mechanisms face sharper length extrapolation limits
- Uses RoPE geometry to provide a theoretical explanation for the positional/symbolic distinction
- Findings validated on both controlled experiments and real-world models; important for understanding transformer inductive biases

### 6. Assign and Add: A Mechanistic Study of Compositional Arithmetic
**arXiv:2605.31497** | Brady Exoo, Alberto Bietti, John Sous

Investigates how transformers achieve compositional generalization via variable assignment + modular addition. Small transformers reuse the **same MLP module** for modular addition whether inputs arrive directly or through variable indirection.

- Identifies three distinct learning phases: acquiring modular addition → developing variable assignment → refining on hard unseen sequences
- Proposes a theoretical framework explaining how compositionality emerges naturally from transformer training dynamics
- Suggests compositional generalization can be a natural consequence of compositionality in internal mechanisms

### 7. Spectral Reach: Understanding Neural Scaling as Progress into the Spectral Tail
**arXiv:2605.31244** | Konstantin Nikolaou, Jonas Scheunemann, Sven Krippendorf, Samuel Tovey, Christian Holm

Introduces "spectral position" to measure which empirical NTK eigenvalues drive loss reduction during training. Learning proceeds from dominant eigenmodes into weaker spectral regions — larger models penetrate further into the "spectral tail," explaining their performance advantage.

- **Feature learning** is identified as the key enabler: it adaptively amplifies gradient magnitudes, sustaining progress where frozen representations stall
- Connects capacity differences between model sizes to the depth of spectral tail penetration
- Offers a mechanistic basis for why scaling works and how architectural/optimizer choices could be improved

### 8. Representation Collapse in Sequential Post-Training of Large Language Models
**arXiv:2605.30524** | Yichen Liu et al.

Sequential post-training stages (SFT → preference optimization → safety tuning) progressively compress LLM internal representations into **low-rank, anisotropic, and homogeneous** feature spaces. Excessive representation concentration degrades performance in subsequent stages and generalization.

- Introduces measurement tools for analyzing hidden states, logits, and related components across tuning approaches
- Evaluates lightweight mitigations: mixed-domain replay and representation diversity regularization
- Important for understanding the interaction between post-training stages and for designing multi-stage fine-tuning pipelines

---

### 9. When LLMs Learn to Be Consistently Wrong: A Multi-Model Study of Linear Representations of Synthetic Deception
**arXiv:2605.30381** | Vahideh Zolfaghari

Trains honest and deceptive LLM variants via LoRA across five architectures (Pythia-1.4B, Gemma-2 2B/9B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B). Linear probes achieve **near-perfect detection** (AUC ≥ 0.99) of synthetic dishonesty in early layers, revealing that deception is linearly encoded.

- Robust, domain-invariant dishonesty representations can be rapidly entrenched through modest supervised fine-tuning
- Identifies two distinct representational regimes across model architectures
- Directly relevant to alignment monitoring: even subtle deceptive fine-tuning leaves a detectable linear signature

---

### 10. DRIFT: Decoupled Rollouts and Importance-Weighted Fine-Tuning for Efficient Multi-Turn Optimization
**arXiv:2605.31455** | Jian Mu, Tianyi Lin, Chengwei Qin, Zhongxiang Dai, Yao Shu

Bridges online RL (good for multi-turn dynamics, expensive) and offline SFT (efficient, but distribution shift). Key insight: the **KL-regularized RL objective is equivalent to importance-weighted supervised learning**. DRIFT collects trajectories from a reference policy and fine-tunes with importance-weighted supervision.

- Matches or surpasses multi-turn RL baselines at the cost of standard SFT
- Avoids on-policy sampling overhead while maintaining the correctness guarantees of the RL objective
- Practically significant for RLHF/RLAIF at scale in multi-turn settings

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2605.30619** — *Reward Learning from Best-of-N Preference Data: Targets, Tradeoffs, and Design Principles* (Pukdee, Balcan, Ravikumar): Derives closed-form targets for Bradley-Terry reward learning from BoN data, revealing a tension between wider reward margins (larger N) and reduced preference-graph connectivity (smaller N). Provides actionable design principles: larger N when labeling is the bottleneck, smaller N when generation is limited.

- **arXiv:2605.31317** — *Forgetting Has Neighbors: Localized Collateral Forgetting in Machine Unlearning* (Dolgova, Stich): Unlearning errors propagate geometrically to nearby training examples. Local Teacher Distillation — using a teacher trained only on retained neighbors of the forget set — significantly aligns unlearned models with retrained baselines near the forget boundary.

- **arXiv:2605.31484** — *Balanced LoRA: Removing Parameter Invariance to Accelerate Convergence* (Castin et al., ICML 2026): LoRA's multiple low-rank factorizations produce identical weight updates but with very different condition numbers. BaLoRA constrains optimization to a balanced manifold, improving loss landscape conditioning and achieving faster convergence without changing adapted weights.

- **arXiv:2605.30526** — *Measuring, Localizing, and Ablating Alignment Signatures in LLMs* (Anand et al.): Post-training alignment introduces detectable stylistic signatures — aligned generations show lower human-corpus affinity and higher AI-detection rates than base-model outputs. PASTA (Post-training Alignment Signature Targeted Ablation) localizes and removes these via activation ablation at inference time across 11 aligned models.
