## Top 10

### 1. Understanding Reasoning from Pretraining to Post-Training
**[2607.16097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16097)** · Jingyan Shen, Ang Li, Salman Rahman, Yifan Sun, Micah Goldblum, Matus Telgarsky, Pavel Izmailov

- Studies how pretraining choices shape the returns to RL post-training using **chess as a controlled testbed** (pretrain on games → SFT on reasoning traces → RL on puzzles with clean rewards), sidestepping the messiness of studying this directly in frontier LLMs.
- Post-RL performance correlates predictably with pretraining loss, and the *rate of improvement* under RL scales roughly linearly with pretraining tokens.
- RL behaves differently by difficulty: it **amplifies already-preferred moves** on easy puzzles but **surfaces previously-overlooked moves** on hard ones — a clean mechanistic split in what RL is actually doing to the policy, validated on a real LM pretrained on math text.

### 2. In-context Learning of Closed-Form Solution to Simple Linear Regression Task Using Transformer with Linear Self-Attention
**[2607.15819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15819)** · Katsuyuki Hagiwara

- Prior work showed transformers can solve in-context linear regression largely by implementing gradient descent internally; this paper asks whether a **closed-form least-squares solution** is learnable instead.
- Constructs a linear-self-attention transformer where **layer normalization** enables the closed-form solve rather than iterative GD steps.
- The closed-form circuit becomes dominant when the model is trained with L1 regularization targeting least-squares outputs — evidence that which circuit ICL learns is sensitive to training-objective choices, not just architecture.

### 3. When Does Muon Help Agentic Reinforcement Learning?
**[2607.16169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16169)** · Kai Ruan, Jinghao Lin, Zihe Huang, Ziqi Zhou, Qianshan Wei, Xuan Wang, Hao Sun

- Muon is competitive with AdamW for pretraining; this is an early look at whether it helps in **RL post-training** for agentic tasks (ALFWorld, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct).
- Applying Muon to hidden weight matrices under Group-in-Group Policy Optimization pushed validation success from 0.290 → 0.546 (+88%), with further gains from tuning the advantage estimator and learning rate jointly.
- Frames optimizer choice, advantage estimator, and learning rate as **jointly coupled decisions** in agentic RL — single-seed results, so treat as preliminary.

### 4. When Model Merging Rivals Joint Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning: A Task-Vector Geometry Analysis
**[2607.16062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16062)** · S. Aaron McClendon

- Trains Qwen3-8B task specialists via RL (LOOP) on AppWorld, then merges them (TIES, RAM+) and finds merging is **statistically indistinguishable from joint multi-task RL training**.
- Task vectors from independently-trained specialists are **near-orthogonal (cosine 0.06–0.10) despite ~65% parameter-support overlap** — direction and support are decoupled.
- Because of that decoupling, support/sign-aware merging methods (TIES, RAM) collapse to behaving like plain uniform averaging — a clarifying negative result about what merging methods actually buy you.

### 5. Rethinking Transfer in Continual Learning: A Replay-Based Realisation
**[2607.15587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15587)** · Yang Meng, Zhenya Liu, Zhuokai Zhao, Yuxin Chen

- Most continual learning work optimizes for *not forgetting*; this paper asks the prior question of **when forward transfer should even be expected to exist**, via three measurable conditions (headroom beyond the new task's own supervision, transferable info survives optimization, replay comes from compatible tasks).
- Instantiates this as Transfer-Selective Replay (TSR): a **zero-training task signature** selects which past data to replay for the incoming task rather than replaying indiscriminately, with distillation for stability.
- Consistently improves forward transfer over standard replay baselines in low-budget settings — reframes transfer as something to diagnose before engineering for it.

### 6. When Do Multi-Agent Systems Help? An Information Bottleneck Perspective
**[2607.16133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16133)** · Wendi Yu, Lianhao Zhou, Xiangjue Dong, Sai Sudarshan Barath, Declan Staunton, Byung-Jun Yoon, Xiaoning Qian, James Caverlee, Shuiwang Ji

- Formalizes single-agent vs. multi-agent LLM systems as an information-bottleneck problem: multi-agent systems relay compressed context between isolated agents, trading redundancy reduction against task-relevant information loss (controlled by β). Proves that with infinite relay bandwidth, any single-agent system can be simulated by a multi-agent one.
- Across 18 experiments (5 benchmarks × 3 model scales): multi-agent systems help most when bandwidth is *nearly* sufficient and for **weaker models**; gains shrink or reverse for stronger models when relays lose information.
- A concrete, information-theoretic account of when decomposing reasoning across agents helps vs. hurts.

### 7. Presentation, Not Mechanism: A Render Confound in Deprecation-Aware Memory Evaluation
**[2607.16019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16019)** · Zhaoyang Jiang, Zhizhong Fu, Zicheng Li, Yunsoo Kim, Jiacong Mi, Xuanqi Peng, Fei Teng, Honghan Wu

- Evaluates how systems retrieve from self-revising records (e.g., issue threads with edits/reverts), comparing flat retrieval, coarse edge-invalidation, and a fine-grained "RevisionLedger" across 2,907 questions.
- The fine-grained mechanism *looks* like it beats the flat baseline by +0.182, but once presentation format is held fixed, almost all of that gain evaporates (+0.021–0.025) — a **methodological confound**, not a real mechanistic advantage.
- A useful cautionary result for anyone evaluating LLM memory/agentic systems: apparent capability gains can be artifacts of how results are rendered rather than what the underlying mechanism does.

### 8. ASK-NN: An Asymmetric Nearest-Neighbor Test that Detects Distribution Drifts in Natural Language
**[2607.15607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15607)** · Sergey Zakharov, Rodion Oblovatny, Alexey Zaytsev

- Targets hallucination/artificial-text detection by treating it as an **asymmetric two-sample distribution test** between prompt/context hidden states (reference) and response hidden states (query), using a directed k-NN graph.
- The statistic has a known finite-sample mean/variance under the permutation null plus asymptotic normality guarantees — a statistically principled detector rather than a purely heuristic one.
- Competitive with kernel/graph baselines on synthetic benchmarks, artificial-text detection, and LLM hallucination detection directly from token-level hidden states.

### 9. Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks for Small Language Models
**[2607.15525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15525)** · Felippe Alves, Renato Vicente

- Systematic test of whether KANs (explicit learned edge functions) can replace transformer FFN blocks, at 10M and 286M (GR-KAN) parameter scales on BabyLM.
- Finds KANs offer genuine **interpretability affordances** (small-basis KANs are useful for auditing learned transformations) but **no consistent quality, benchmark, or latency advantage** over strong MLP/SwiGLU baselines — including underperforming at the larger scale.
- A useful negative result tempering "KANs are more interpretable *and* competitive" claims — interpretability doesn't come free here.

### 10. QUADS: Stabilizing NVFP4 Reinforcement Learning for MoE via Quantization-error Alignment across Dual Sides
**[2607.15810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15810)** · Zhengyang Zhuge, Hao Yu, Xin Wang, Zheng Li, Yizhong Cao, Dayiheng Liu, Jianwei Zhang

- Diagnoses why low-precision (NVFP4/FP4) rollout generation destabilizes RL training for MoE LLMs: training collapses after ~150 steps due to a widening rollout–trainer log-probability gap, traced to **activation error** (not weight error) under FP4's coarse quantization grid.
- Fixes: asymmetric quantization-aware training on the trainer side (fake-quantize weights, leave activations unquantized) plus residual activation compensation on the rollout side.
- Recovers BF16-level accuracy, +21.49 pts pass@1 over naive NVFP4 RL, and ~16% higher rollout throughput than FP8 — a concrete diagnose-and-fix training-dynamics story for low-precision RL.
