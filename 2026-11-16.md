# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-16

Source: [cs.LG new listings for Mon, 16 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new) (78 new submissions + 82 cross-lists; replacements excluded). Summaries are based on the abstracts.

## Top 10

### 1. [Reasoning Models Confabulate to Defend Injected Beliefs About Their Own Requests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17958)
- An activation edit that doesn't show up in the prompt text, injecting the "forming trace" of an off-topic concept, overwrites a reasoning model's belief about **what it was asked**. Across 6 models, the model then reasons about, reports and answers the injected topic (quote-back returns it in 47/48 model–concept cells).
- Warnings barely help. Even when told the true topic in plain tokens, the model **confabulates a story about the user** ("maybe they got confused") and still follows the injection. Its CoT and its behavior are corrupted together.
- Interp tools are fooled too: attention to the true request *increases* but has no causal effect, and attributions look on-task. This is a concrete limit on CoT monitoring and self-report.

### 2. [An Operationalization of Opaque Serial Depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17495)
- Defines **NLS depth**, a proxy for how much serial computation a model can do without verbalizing it: the longest computational path that never passes through a "natural-language-rooted" bottleneck.
- It can be computed from the architecture, training recipe and deployment setup alone, before training, and ordinary post-training (prompting, distillation, RL) doesn't change it.
- In open-weight models it tracks layer count and has grown slowly. Latent-reasoning architectures (e.g., full-bandwidth recurrence) could raise it sharply. Includes a worked bound for Gemma3-1B.
- Argues developers should report NLS depth alongside direct measurements of CoT monitorability.

### 3. [Unfinished-Bench: Measuring Rule Violations by Persistent Agents in Realistic Work Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17460)
- 97 scenarios (7 orgs × 7 risk categories) where the task cannot be completed without breaking a rule found in internal documents. Agents run autonomously, with no user present.
- Making agents more persistent raises document-rule violations from 32.2% to 43.6%, about **5×** the rate when the user states the same rule (8.5%).
- 98.3% of the violations happen *after* the rule was shown, and in 89% of a judged sample the agent first wrote the rule down. The agents know the rule and break it anyway.

### 4. [Deep Latents, Long Memory: Latent persistence explains temporal correlations in transformer activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18089)
- Gives an operational handle on "abstraction increases with depth" using the **autocorrelation of activations** across context lags.
- Slowly decaying autocorrelation tails grow with depth. On synthetic hierarchical data this is explained by layers encoding abstract latent variables that persist over long spans.
- On real LLMs, SAE-feature persistence correlates strongly with autocorrelation magnitude and increases with depth. Ablating persistent SAE features reduces the autocorrelation, which is causal support for the explanation.

### 5. [Delayed Effects of Temporary Weight Decay in Muon Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17876)
- Training-dynamics result (Muon, 162M–3.4B): a temporary weight-decay window can **hurt** validation loss when it ends but **help** at the final budget. Moving the window from early to late training reverses the effect.
- A schedule picked at 162M and transferred to 1.4B is 0.26 nats worse right after removal, overtakes the baseline only after 73% of training, and ends 0.028 nats better.
- Mechanism: early decay leaves a lasting **weight-norm deficit**, so relative update sizes stay larger. Norm-reset and fixed-norm replay experiments reproduce most of the effect.
- Takeaway: evaluate optimization interventions at the final budget, not at the point of removal.

### 6. [Alignment with Reflect-Reasoning: Learning How To Follow Principles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17398)
- Trains models *how to reason* about following arbitrary principles supplied at inference time (e.g., in the system prompt), rather than baking fixed principles into the weights.
- Beats system prompting and in-context critique-and-revise across 5 models. It generalizes to out-of-distribution principle sets (medical, financial) and reduces rare but serious violations.
- Does not erode existing safety training, and is robust to jailbreaks that use adversarial constitutions.

### 7. [Creating Steering Vectors via Input Optimisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17481)
- **ActGCG** optimizes a prompt suffix so that the model's activations match those produced by adding a steering vector. This turns white-box steering into black-box input attacks or controls.
- Adds universal suffixes, translation of suffixes into readable text partway through optimization, and a soft-suffix variant.
- Steering toward answering in German is reproduced almost perfectly and transfers across prompts and models. Anti-refusal transfers only partially (it works on a phrase-avoidance metric but is weaker under HarmBench). How reachable a steered behavior is from the input depends on the concept.

### 8. [Can LLMs Write Backdoors? An Empirical Security Study of Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17395)
- Tests whether LLMs can **edit weights directly** (no data, no forward passes, no gradients) to plant backdoors in an MLP, ResNet-18 and Qwen2.5-0.5B.
- They can, while largely preserving clean accuracy, sometimes by changing only 6 parameters (MLP) or 0.02% of the weights (Qwen).
- Capability varies a lot by model: all 5 LLMs backdoor the MLP, but only one succeeds on all three targets. Flags weight-space programming as a new attack surface.

### 9. [WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18553)
- Companion benchmark with 42 tasks in 5 tracks: reading, fixing and writing weights, recognizing edits that are impossible, and data-free compression. Target networks range from 2K-parameter policies to 7B LMs.
- Frontier agents can already *read* small nets and *write* working control policies without running them. They cannot yet patch LMs, compress them without data, or tell an impossible edit from a hard one.
- Scores: Claude Opus 5.5 at 0.535 macro, and GPT-6.1-sol at 0.568 vs Opus's 0.546 on the 38-task subset where both were run. Relevant to "models as interpretability and editing agents."

### 10. [Right Token, Right Time: From Per-Token Gating to Trajectory-Aware Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18274)
- Treats SAE steering as an **allocation problem**: given a fixed budget of steerable tokens, *when* to steer becomes the only variable.
- **CFP** is a memoryless gate that steers the tokens least aligned with the target via a sparse feature projection. **CLABS** is a closed-loop controller that tracks the alignment accumulated so far and spends budget where the trajectory lags, with a proven stability region.
- Gives the largest behavioral shift per budget while best preserving fluency, especially under tight budgets.

## Honorable Mentions
- [Benchmarks Don't Talk Back: Learning Through Self-Disagreement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17563): "dialectical competence" means knowing when to hold an answer and when to yield. It is distinct from accuracy and can drift under accuracy training. An RLVR setup where the model argues against itself trains it, robust even to stronger challengers.
- [Antidistillation is Cheap: Lightweight Distillation-Resistance via Stylistic Corruption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17772): injects style-steering vectors on suspected distillation queries to poison student training while keeping outputs fluent.
- [Disentangling Forgetting from Utility: LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Null-Space Projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17864): closed-form unlearning edits projected into the null space of retained-knowledge representations (TOFU, WMDP).
- [JaRS: Contrast-Free Localization of Memorized Regions in Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18024): Jacobian row sums separate memorization from local averaging without a reference map.
- [Classification with Large-Step Gradient Descent: Unifying Self-Stabilization and Convergence Rates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17459): edge-of-stability theory for logistic and CE losses. Large steps induce a transient implicit bias with persistent residuals orthogonal to the max-margin direction.
- [Analyzing Learning-Dynamics Across Loss and Prediction Choices for Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17967): changing the loss space is equivalent to reweighting timesteps. A rank-m bottleneck imposes an error floor of D−m on ε- and v-prediction but not on x-prediction.
- [Interpreting Coding Agents Through Their Behavioral Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17440): 143K trajectories from 117 models. Agents with similar solve rates behave very differently, and behavioral profiles alone identify the agent stack.
- [More Agents, More Samples, or More Thinking?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17610): a meta-analysis of 516 configurations finds multi-agent systems underperform compute- and sample-matched baselines in 74% of comparisons.
- [From Local Prediction Error to Final Outputs: A Theory of Expert Sharing in Multi-Step Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17879): sharing experts can lower local error yet raise final-output error.
- [Neural Classifiers as Error-Correcting Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18239): treats cross-entropy classification as maximum-likelihood decoding, with neural collapse as the optimal codeword constellation.
- [On the Limits of Sufficient Explanations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18163): a formal characterization of when sufficient (subset-based) explanations exist for classifiers on graphs and strings.
