## Top 10

### 1. [Inadvertent Context Leakage in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19857)

- Studies whether secrets sitting in a model's context (SSNs, health data, credentials) leak into ordinary, non-adversarial outputs even when the model refuses direct extraction requests.
- Across 8 proprietary models: 2-digit secrets recovered near-perfectly, 4-digit secrets at 82% exact match, via a black-box adaptive attack.
- Key finding: **more capable models leak more** — leakage scales with instruction-following ability, suggesting it's a byproduct of capability rather than a patchable bug.
- Also demonstrates a trained classifier that infers sensitive predicates (health/financial events) from routine outputs, and an RL-trained attacker that extracts full SSNs from a production-style agent.

### 2. [Mechanistic Tomography: Designed Measurement for Control-Oriented Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19338)

- Unifies patching, gradients, Hessian-vector products, and subset interventions as a single "measurement" problem: y = Ax + w, where A is the intervention design and x the target mechanism.
- Prescribes a practical protocol — cheapest measurement first, validate on held-out interventions, calibrate residuals, expand only if needed — and uses **control tasks as validation**, since a bad internal estimate visibly produces bad control.
- Applied to GPT-2-small's IOI circuit (finds Name Mover / Negative Name Mover interaction as the largest held-out predictive term) and Qwen-2.5-7B's refusal behavior (a simple additive map suffices there).

### 3. [Exact Algebraic Computation of Learning Coefficients for Two-Dimensional Singular Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20183)

- First deterministic (non-sampling) algorithm to exactly compute local learning coefficients (RLCTs) — the quantity underlying singular learning theory's WBIC — for any 2D model whose KL divergence is contact-equivalent to a polynomial.
- Provides ground truth to calibrate the sampling-based estimators everyone currently relies on, and reveals algebraic structure sampling can't see; faster than sampling in the shallow regime.
- Directly relevant to the theory of generalization in singular (deep-learning-like) models.

### 4. [AI4AI-Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agents in Algorithmic Design for Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20318)

- Directly targets recursive self-improvement (RSI) feasibility: can an agent redesign the *training algorithm* itself, not just tune hyperparameters or collect data?
- 10 frozen research repos across 10 training-algorithm families; agents get 4 hours to rewrite the algorithm, which is then rerun and scored against the shipped baseline.
- Best system closes under 1/5 of the distance to optimal (mean score 0.166, best 0.250); most submissions never actually change the learning algorithm, and the minority that do score much higher (0.226 vs 0.126) — more reasoning effort mainly buys willingness to attempt real algorithmic changes.

### 5. [Finite-Horizon Input-Output Dynamics of Minibatch Perturbations in AdamW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19762)

- Models AdamW as a finite-horizon input-state-output system (state = weights + 1st/2nd moment estimates) to track how a single minibatch's influence propagates forward through optimizer memory.
- Derives a linearized "signed response operator" mapping one gradient perturbation to its delayed effect on future loss, plus an exact multistep error decomposition with provable first-order accuracy.
- Effectively a training-dynamics/data-attribution tool: recovers how much of a training step's future effect is attributable to optimizer state versus the data itself.

### 6. [What You Can't See Is What You Learn: Restricted Evidence Visibility Favors Compositional Generalization in Shared-Genome Language-Model Societies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20054)

- Preregistered experiment: four-module "societies" share one frozen LM + one LoRA adapter, communicating only via two continuous vectors; only the attention mask (full vs. restricted evidence visibility) differs between matched pairs.
- Restricting what each module sees causes gradient descent to discover **more compositionally generalizing** solutions — restricted societies beat globally-visible twins in 9/10 pairs, including on held-out composite functions never seen in training.
- Causal-intervention audits (packet transplants) confirm restricted societies build a reusable, value-indexed communication interface, while the one high-performing global model's interface was not reusable — though the full preregistered pass/fail battery still formally fails one threshold, an unusually honest negative-result caveat.

### 7. [Credit Without Ground Truth: Auditing Step-Level Credit Assignment in LLM Agents Against Executed Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19760)

- Audits the credit signals used to train LLM agents (LLM-judge scores, logprob ratios, policy confidence) against **causal ground truth** obtained by actually resampling and rolling forward alternative actions in ALFWorld, not against annotated "correctness."
- Finds none of the standard signals identify causally pivotal steps better than chance; causal contribution is sparse (~30%) and even measurability differs 2x between similar-scale policies.
- Diagnoses the failure mode precisely: implicit credit signals mostly track the policy's own fluency (rank correlation +0.75), not actual causal contribution — a cautionary methodological result for step-level attribution in agentic RL/distillation.

### 8. [Feature Evolution and Migration during Vision Transformer Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20134)

- Uses Sparse Autoencoders on CLS-token representations across a depth × training-time grid to track individual feature dynamics during ViT training, not just aggregate representation similarity.
- Introduces "feature migration": features change which layer they're most detectable in over training.
- Finds migration concentrates early in training and toward earlier layers, and declines as features stabilize; deeper layers stabilize earlier/more strongly than shallow ones — a direct interpretability lens on training dynamics.

### 9. [Forking Fast: Efficiently Estimating Uncertainty Dynamics in Text Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19611)

- Asks what the right statistical model is for LLM reasoning-chain uncertainty (resampling at every token/sentence to see which steps determine the final answer).
- Key finding: uncertainty dynamics converge to stable patterns across many resamples, and most apparent noise is a sampling artifact rather than genuine model sensitivity to individual tokens.
- Proposes a statistical smoothing model that approximates high-sample resampling estimates from cheap low-sample rollouts, substantially cutting the cost of this kind of interpretability analysis.

### 10. [Let's Scale Step by Step: Compute-Efficient Hyperparameter Transfer for Large-Scale Mixture-of-Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20061)

- Extends μP-style hyperparameter transfer to MoE architectures (with Multi-head Latent Attention + Muon optimizer): learning rates found at small width transfer consistently to large width.
- Adds a second transfer axis — extrapolating optimal LR along the *token-budget* dimension via a fitted scaling law (R²=0.95) out to 10-trillion-token horizons.
- Validated by pretraining an actual 155B-total/17B-active MoE foundation model from scratch using only proxy-model ablations — a concrete, large-scale training-dynamics/scaling-law result.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Projector Is All You Train](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19726)** — training only the modality projector (not the LM backbone) in a 3D MLLM matches or beats joint fine-tuning, and joint training causes measurable capability drift in the base LM that projector-only training avoids by construction.
- **[Improved Confidence Estimates for Black-Box Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19323)** — lightweight classifiers trained on existing zero-shot uncertainty scores plus correctness of similar in-dataset queries reliably beat those raw scores at predicting LLM correctness, with minimal overhead.
- **[Auditing Cross-Lingual Fairness in Language Model Watermarking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20047)** — a multi-axis audit (6 schemes × 3 models × 11 languages/4 scripts) finds watermark detection/quality disparities are structural to language typology, not idiosyncratic.
- **[Beyond Imitation: Filtering On-Policy Distillation by Reasoning Progress](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19408)** — shows teacher-derived distillation rewards often conflict with genuine reasoning progress, and filtering out disagreeing reward signal during on-policy distillation improves reasoning performance.
- **[Adaptive Probabilistic Shielding by Learning MDPs for Safe Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19836)** — extends probabilistic shielding (a safe-RL action-constraint layer) to the realistic case where transition probabilities are unknown and estimated online, with the shield tightening as the estimate improves.
- **[MemTrapBench: Benchmarking Cognitive Traps in LLM Memory Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20202)** — shows faithfully-stored, relevant memories can still distort model reasoning/beliefs ("reasoning fixation," "belief distortion"); every tested memory framework underperforms a no-memory baseline, sometimes by over 10%.
- **[In Two Minds about Lifelong Learning: Exploring Hemispheric Redundancy and Specialisation in Neural Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19514)** — a biologically-inspired continual-learning architecture (asymmetric hemispheres + sleep-like consolidation phase) addressing catastrophic forgetting via memory-consolidation-style mechanisms.
- **[DeltaML-Bench: Evaluating Machine Learning Agents on Real-World Research Repositories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19653)** — a benchmark of 48 tasks requiring agents to improve published ML baselines in real repos; finds a "Modular" agent scaffold specification-games up to 47.9% of the time, while a search-based scaffold shows none.
