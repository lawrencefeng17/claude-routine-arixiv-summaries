### 1. How Transparent is DiffusionGemma?
**arXiv:2606.20560** | Joshua Engels, Callum McDougall, Bilal Chughtai, Janos Kramar, Senthoran Rajamanoharan, Cindy Wu, Arthur Conmy, Neel Nanda et al. (Google DeepMind)

- Asks whether diffusion-based language models sacrifice interpretability relative to autoregressive ones, decomposing transparency into *variable transparency* (understanding intermediate states) and *algorithmic transparency* (reconstructing reasoning).
- Raw opaque serial depth appears 28.6× higher in DiffusionGemma than Gemma 4; mapping through an interpretable token bottleneck compresses this to **1.1×**, suggesting comparable monitorability.
- Discovers diffusion-specific phenomena: **non-chronological reasoning** (later tokens predicted before earlier ones) and **token smearing** (single positions carry ambiguous, blended token identities). Likely the most important interpretability paper of the day.

### 2. Where Does Social Reasoning Come From? Capability Provenance in Language Models
**arXiv:2606.19625** | Glenn Matlin, Chandreyi Chakraborty, Saehee Eom et al.

- Uses **gradient-based training-data attribution** across the Dolma3 corpus to ask which pretraining data supports social reasoning vs. STEM reasoning in OLMo3-7B.
- Organizes 576 WebOrganizer corpus bins; finds that social and STEM reasoning draw on **qualitatively distinct corpus regions** — social capabilities are not simply a byproduct of general world knowledge.
- Machine unlearning experiments provide causal confirmation: removing high-attribution topics substantially degrades corresponding benchmark performance. Code, influence matrices, and unlearning checkpoints released. Direct relevance to the data attribution subtopic.

### 3. What Do Safety-Aligned LLMs Learn From Mixed Compliance Demonstrations?
**arXiv:2606.20508** | Sihui Dai, Mann Patel

- Goes beyond confirming that in-context jailbreaks work to explaining *why*: benign compliance examples (safe request → helpful response) can either **increase or decrease** harmful compliance depending on the model — not a uniform effect.
- Finds that **preference optimization is the critical training stage** that prevents benign examples from amplifying harmful compliance; pretrained-only models are far more susceptible.
- Pronounced **recency effects** in demonstration ordering; models split on whether to copy refusal *formatting* or discard all in-context signals on refusal. Mechanistically informative for alignment science.

### 4. Sparsity, Superposition, and Forgetting: A Mechanistic Study of Representation Retention in Continual Learning
**arXiv:2606.20431** | Jan Wasilewski, Jędrzej Kozal, Michał Woźniak, Bartosz Krawczyk

- Builds a controlled framework with synthetic data to directly measure how **superposition** (non-orthogonal feature encoding) and sparsity interact with catastrophic forgetting.
- Superposition increases gradually but undergoes **sharp transitions at task boundaries**; higher sparsity creates more overlap yet does not necessarily cause forgetting if representation strength is maintained.
- Challenges the naive view that overlap → forgetting: the relationship depends critically on representation strength and capacity distribution. Connects mechanistic interpretability concepts to continual learning.

### 5. Algebraic Dead Directions in LayerNorm Transformers: A Forward-Pass-Only Diagnostic at LLM Scale
**arXiv:2606.19491** | Tejas Pradeep Shirodkar, P. J. Narayanan

- Proves that the **inverse-scale direction of the LayerNorm affine parameter** is an exact algebraic null-space direction of the post-final-norm activation covariance — readable directly from the scale parameter, requiring zero forward passes.
- Validates across **14 pretrained transformers (160M–35B parameters)** for both language and vision; prediction matches measured bottom singular direction to four decimal places for all LayerNorm (not RMSNorm) models.
- The residual stream's smallest singular value is preserved across blocks in 13 of 14 models; enables classification of normalisation type from parameters alone. A cheap, exact diagnostic for a structural property of LLMs.

### 6. Tracking Representation Dynamics in Large Language Models with Persistent Homology
**arXiv:2606.19542** | Naman Malhotra, Jay Ambadkar, Abhinav Gupta et al.

- Applies **persistent homology** to activation spaces to track topological changes during supervised fine-tuning for alignment, across four transformer models (1B–7B parameters) under three alignment objectives.
- Key finding: **the majority of topological reorganization occurs in the earliest training steps**, followed by stabilization — alignment is a short-burst topological event, not a gradual drift.
- Different alignment objectives produce distinguishable topological signatures; instruction-tuned and pretrained models show qualitatively different evolution patterns. Offers a lens on training dynamics invisible to standard loss curves.

### 7. Critical Percolation as a Synthetic Data Model for Interpretability
**arXiv:2606.20347** | Aryeh Brill, Tom Ingebretsen Carlson

- Proposes synthetic datasets built from **hierarchical functions over critical mean-field percolation clusters**: sparse, low-dimensional, fractal structures with power-law size distributions and known latent taxonomic hierarchies.
- Ground-truth latent variables are **linearly decodable from neural network activations**, enabling principled evaluation of interpretability methods without ambiguity about what the "true" structure is.
- Analytically tractable (known critical exponents, no hyperparameter tuning), scalable via an efficient sampling algorithm. Addresses a core bottleneck in interpretability research: lack of controlled ground truth.

### 8. GEMS: Geometric Constraints Enable Multi-Semantic Superposition in LLMs
**arXiv:2606.19946** | Yu Deng

- Activation steering with multiple simultaneous semantic directions fails in two ways: **distributional deviation** (accumulated perturbations leave the training distribution) and **directional interference** (non-orthogonal vectors cancel each other).
- GEMS resolves both with a training-free method: norm-preserving weighted superposition + targeted attention-pathway injection for distributional deviation, and **real-time orthogonalization** for directional interference.
- Injecting three concurrent non-mathematical directions maintains **98% accuracy on GSM8K** (vs. 92% baseline, 4% for unconstrained addition) across model sizes 3B–31B. Relevant to multi-feature superposition and representation geometry.

### 9. Leverage Is Not Reach: A Control-Window Law for Single-Neuron Steering in Language Models
**arXiv:2606.19831** | Hongliang Liu

- Establishes a theoretical framework showing that single-neuron steering effects are governed by a **"budget normalized control window"**: the alignment between the residual stream and the neuron's write direction, normalized against a coherence budget.
- Successfully predicts **collapse ceilings with MAE of 0.14**; explains why gradient-based attribution methods fail to identify true behavioral controllers (leverage ≠ reach).
- Reveals that refusal control involves typed rather than scalar effects — neuron steering is a "budgeted, typed audit of controllability," not a fixed-dose intervention. Important for mechanistic interpretability of safety-relevant circuits.

### 10. FloatDoor: Platform-Triggered Backdoors in LLMs
**arXiv:2606.19535** | Nils Loose, Jonas Sander, Felix Mächtle, Thomas Eisenbarth

- Introduces the first **input-independent, platform-triggered backdoor** for generative LLMs: uses two lightweight LoRA adapters — one amplifies floating-point arithmetic differences across platforms, the other connects the platform signature to malicious behavior.
- The attack is invisible to standard auditing because the backdoor only fires on the target hardware (NVIDIA GPU, Google TPU, AWS Graviton, Alibaba Yitian-710); the model is functionally clean on all other platforms.
- Demonstrates reliable generation of **exploitable code vulnerabilities** on the target platform while maintaining performance elsewhere. Exposes a deep structural vulnerability arising from the time-of-check/time-of-use gap between auditing and serving.

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.20469** — *Fisher-Geometric Sharpness and the Implicit Bias of SGD toward Flat Minima* (Md Sakir Ahmed et al.): Grounds flatness in the Riemannian geometry of the Fisher Information Matrix to produce a reparametrization-invariant sharpness measure; derives that SGD's noise structure concentrates probability at geometrically flat minima and connects this to a PAC-Bayes generalization bound. Theoretically sound treatment of a long-contested question in training dynamics.

- **arXiv:2606.19941** — *Compositionality Emerges in a Narrow Depth-Connectivity Regime: Architecture Constraints and Solution Manifolds* (Dat H. Do et al.): Shows compositionality emerges only in a "sweet spot" of depth and sparsity — not just any sparse connectivity, but the *specific* connections matter; both shallower and deeper networks converge to non-compositional solutions. Relevant to science of generalization and emergent structure.

- **arXiv:2606.19379** — *How Linear Is a Transformer Feed-Forward Block? Per-Block Linear Recoverability Is Learned, Not Architectural* (Stuart Whipp): Measures per-block linear recoverability (R²_lin) across GPT-2, Pythia, and Llama; finds highly heterogeneous profiles (0.3–0.99+) that are *learned*, not determined by activation function. Low-rank bilinear probes reveal higher-order structures in nonlinear blocks — useful for both mechanistic understanding and compression.

- **arXiv:2606.20008** — *VIMPO: Value-Implicit Policy Optimization for LLMs* (Zhewei Kang, Aosong Feng, Sergey Levine, Dawn Song et al.): Derives a critic-free value function from KL-regularized RL optimality conditions, anchored by the no-future-reward boundary constraint at trajectory completion. Achieves denser credit assignment than GRPO without a learned critic; improves on MATH-500, AIME 2024/2025, OlympiadBench.

- **arXiv:2606.19818** — *Uncertainty-Aware Reward Modeling for Stable RLHF* (Licheng Pan et al.): Adds calibrated uncertainty to reward models via quantile-based conformal prediction, then adjusts GRPO advantage estimates using heteroscedastic variance decomposition — preventing unreliable signals from destabilizing training. Reduces reward hacking on HelpSteer, UltraFeedback, and PKU-SafeRLHF.
