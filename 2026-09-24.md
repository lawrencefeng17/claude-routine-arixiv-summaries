## Top 10

### 1. [Hidden not Deleted: How Networks Suppress Entangled Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27593)

- Shows linear concept-erasure methods assume features live in separable subspaces; under dense superposition (two features forced into an antipodal pair sharing one subspace), state-of-the-art linear erasure destroys *both* features, not just the target.
- Networks trained by gradient descent instead solve this non-linearly via one of two distinct circuit-level solutions ("mirror" and "shadow") depending on initialization — a stable attractor structure, not an artifact.
- Both solutions leave the erased feature's representation recoverable via a single scalar patch, with no retraining — a mechanistic, causally-validated explanation for why suppressed (rather than truly deleted) knowledge resurfaces in LLM unlearning.

### 2. [The Linear Representation Hypothesis Needs a Group Action](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27158)

- Argues the Linear Representation Hypothesis isn't one hypothesis but a family of claims, since comparing representations across models requires first specifying what counts as "equivalent" — a step usually left implicit.
- Formalizes equivalence via group actions: the representation object, the procedure that produces it, and the property ultimately asserted, while accounting for architecture-imposed symmetries.
- Uses the framework to audit common representation-similarity metrics and recent interpretability analyses, showing that different probes/interventions claiming to study "the same representation" can silently encode different hypotheses.

### 3. [What Converges in the Platonic Representation Hypothesis? Structure over Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27252)

- Pushes back on a recent narrowing of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (convergence only in local neighborhoods) by showing prior local-vs-global comparisons confound structural scale with *what* is compared: relational structure (which samples relate) vs. metric geometry (distances/similarities).
- Introduces $H_0$ skeleton overlap (a global analog of mutual k-NN) plus distance-aware variants in a controlled 2×2 evaluation framework.
- Finds relational structure converges robustly at both local and global scales across vision-language and video-text models after calibration, while metric-geometry agreement is much weaker — sharpening what "representational convergence" actually means.

### 4. [Geometric Uncertainty for Detecting and Correcting Hallucinations in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13813)

- Proposes a black-box, sampling-based framework that models a prompt-conditioned semantic distribution in answer-embedding space using archetypal analysis, unifying prompt-level and answer-level uncertainty in one geometric object.
- Distribution entropy gives prompt-level uncertainty; per-answer "atypicality" relative to the batch gives answer-level reliability, enabling not just hallucination *detection* but *correction* (select the most typical sampled answer).
- Matches or beats prior UQ methods on short-form QA and does notably better on medical datasets, where hallucination risk is highest-stakes.

### 5. [Quantization-Robust Unlearning through the Lens of Retain-Forget Loss Landscapes Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27355)

- Observes that LLM unlearning (removing private/copyrighted data influence) is substantially undone by standard post-training quantization, with forgetting degrading faster than utility.
- Traces the gap to loss-landscape curvature, identifying "sensitive weights" jointly responsible for non-robust forgetting and utility loss, then applies sensitivity-guided noisy regularization to steer convergence toward a flatter minimum.
- Adds forget-critical optimization (updating only forget-relevant layers); across MUSE/TOFU benchmarks and multiple unlearning algorithms, achieves substantially more quantization-resilient forgetting while preserving utility.

### 6. [Backdoors Leave Structural Traces: FedMAST for Backdoor Detection and Containment in Federated Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27760)

- Targets stealthy federated-learning backdoors engineered to suppress the isolated anomaly signals that existing defenses rely on.
- Shows poisoned updates still leave structural traces — combines squeeze-pair coherence scoring (coupled feature distortions) with signed spectral-drift tracking (persistent directional changes over rounds), plus tiered filtering and round-level containment.
- Across six backdoor attacks and an adaptive method-aware attack (CovertLayers, which drives FedAvg/MultiKrum/AlignIns/FLAME to 33–100% attack success), FedMAST holds attack success to ~1.5% while keeping 92–95% main-task accuracy.

### 7. [ChronosAttack: Adversarial Tool Scheduling Attacks on LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27857)

- Introduces a delay-only attack on LLM agents that never modifies, adds, removes, or accelerates tool responses — it only changes *when* authentic responses arrive, exploiting agents that process tool output as it streams in.
- Tested against GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.6 Flash, DeepSeek V4 Flash, and Claude Sonnet 4.6: GPT and Claude show strong targeted decision shifts, Gemini shifts in the opposite direction, DeepSeek is comparatively stable.
- A single scheduling inversion can flip a final decision without any persistent agent state; synchronization/order-consistency defenses mitigate it — flags response timing as an underappreciated attack surface in asynchronous agents.

### 8. [The Like Trap: Multi-Stage Poisoning against Agents in Similarity-based Recommendation Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27155)

- Studies indirect poisoning of LLM-agent pipelines: instead of exposing poisoned content directly to the agent, exploits the platform's own like-score recommendation mechanism (OASIS) so the *system itself* surfaces poisoned posts.
- Theoretically characterizes when a multi-stage chain of poisoned posts can steer an agent's feed, then builds an algorithm that crafts realistic poisoned content to trigger this.
- Demonstrates the attack succeeds even when poisoned posts fall below the retrieval-similarity threshold — a subtler variant of agent poisoning that rides a feedback loop instead of direct content injection.

### 9. [Nonequilibrium Phases of Repulsive Self-Attention: Chaos, Attention Condensation, and Emergent Locality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28448)

- Analyzes a minimal recurrent transformer ($Q=K=I$, negative value map $V=-I$) as a dynamical system: similarity-based attention pulls tokens together while the negative value map pushes them apart.
- In 2D, the "regular polygon" fixed point loses stability through a flip bifurcation as attention strength increases, producing chaos and cluster-exchange states; in the hard-routing $N\to\infty$ limit, local perturbations propagate ballistically, producing an "emergent butterfly cone."
- Maps a full phase diagram (diffuse states, consensus flips, condensed chaotic routing, fragmented clusters) — a genuinely mechanistic, statistical-physics account of how attention dynamics can self-organize into persistent activity rather than settle.

### 10. [Does Step Law Transfer to Small-Scale Language Models? An Empirical Recalibration Below 59M Parameters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27581)

- Empirically tests whether "Step Law" (power-law formulas for optimal learning rate/batch size, calibrated on 59M–1B parameter models) holds below 59M parameters — the regime relevant to single-GPU and interpretability research.
- Runs 935 training runs across 29 unique (N, D) cells on a nanoGPT/TinyStories pipeline, extracting optima via local quadratic approximation of the loss surface.
- Finds the power-law functional form is preserved but with different coefficients: direct transfer of the original Step Law systematically overestimates the optimal learning rate by ~4× (2.4×–6.6× range) in this small-model regime — a concrete, actionable correction for anyone training tiny models for research.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Semantic Self-Distillation for Language Model Uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04577)** — distills expensive sampled semantic-dispersion uncertainty into a lightweight student that predicts a prompt-conditioned answer density *before* generation, competitive with the sampling-based teacher on hallucination prediction while adding OOD-detection and multiple-choice primitives.
- **[How Sensitive Are LLM Leaderboard Claims to Hidden Model Selection?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28177)** — derives how many unpublished, privately-evaluated model variants a leaderboard margin can tolerate while still reflecting a genuine advantage; an audit of 394 Open LLM Leaderboard claims finds 391 lack statistical support even before accounting for selection.
- **[When and Where to Trust the Teacher: Unifying On-Policy Distillation and GRPO through Entropy-Calibrated Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28385)** — redistributes verifier-derived token credit in GRPO using a signed teacher–old-policy entropy gap, improving math-reasoning RL over either on-policy distillation or GRPO alone.
- **[RL Starts before RL: On Policy Distillation for Better Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28145)** — shows on-policy distillation as an RL "warm-up" improves final RL performance even when it barely changes pre-RL accuracy, attributing the benefit to distributional alignment with the teacher beyond top-1 agreement.
- **[Fine-Tuning LLMs for Translation: General Forgetting Mitigation Does Not Preserve MT-Specific Instruction Following](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28395)** — finds general-purpose catastrophic-forgetting mitigation (e.g., EWC) preserves broad benchmarks but not MT-specific instruction-following controls (formality, gender, length); only targeted data mixing helps, and even that fails to generalize to unseen prompts.
- **[On Preference Coverage Collapse from Hindsight Relabeling in Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26918)** — identifies a failure mode where hindsight relabeling collapses a multi-objective RL critic's preference coverage onto whatever narrow region the agent happened to visit, hurting 19 of 36 algorithm–environment settings; proposes a one-parameter fix that recovers most of the loss.
- **[Linear RNN Scaling Laws: When Longer Sequences Beat More Sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27964)** — a tractable teacher-student theory of scaling laws for linear-recurrent sequence models, deriving explicit approximation/optimization/statistical regimes and showing when sequence length and trajectory count stop being interchangeable.
- **[The Capability Manifold and ML Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27588)** — proposes a multidimensional "capability manifold" connecting downstream agentic capabilities (not just loss) to pretraining/post-training/test-time compute, unifying Kaplan- and Chinchilla-style scaling laws as trajectories on one manifold.
