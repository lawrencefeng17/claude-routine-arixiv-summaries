## Top 10

### 1. [Refusal Reads Only a Slice of What the Model Knows: Harm-Keyed Routing and Its Exceptions Across Model Families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14759)

- Distinguishes what alignment's refusal gate "reads" from what the model actually comprehends morally: across four open-weight models in three families, a low-rank moral subspace crystallizes during pretraining and alignment merely rotates it once, while the refusal gate is a fresh, narrow post-training construction that is largely orthogonal to that subspace.
- Causal evidence from OLMo-3: patching progressively larger slices of the moral subspace between matched requests shows moral judgment keeps absorbing more of the patch, while refusal saturates at roughly a single harm direction — about three-quarters of refusal's causal input lies outside the moral subspace entirely.
- Explains why single-direction "jailbreak" edits work so well: refusal routes around most of what the model knows, reading a thin harm percept rather than the richer moral content judged elsewhere — direct mechanistic evidence for why RLHF-style alignment stays shallow.

### 2. [Towards Identifying the Dataset Biases Causing Phantom Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14449)

- Follows up on "phantom transfer" — a subliminal-learning-style effect where a teacher model passes a bias to a student through a dataset scrubbed of every explicit reference to it, which no known data-level defense reliably catches.
- Shows a simple Sentence-BERT embedding signature can identify the *topic* of the hidden bias (Matthews correlation 0.83 when the teacher model is known, 0.46 when it isn't) — a first real foothold for detecting this kind of leakage after the fact.
- Also finds different teacher models encode the same bias through different vocabulary, suggesting the signal subliminal learning rides on is teacher-specific rather than a universal artifact.

### 3. [Data Attribution at Scale via Influence Matrix Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15044)

- Casts budgeted training-data attribution as recovering a large influence matrix from a small number of measurements, instead of running one full metagradient (MAGIC-style) differentiation per prediction.
- Shows the measurements best suited to *reconstructing* this matrix differ from those best suited to attribution itself, and introduces two algorithms — MAGE (reconstruction) and SPELL (attribution) — that reuse existing metagradient machinery at no extra cost.
- Outperforms existing baselines across model scales and measurement budgets: a direct scalability advance for data attribution, data valuation, and machine unlearning.

### 4. [What Does an LLM Learn from Reinforcement Learning? A Mechanistic Interpretability Perspective with Fixed-SAE Track](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15064)

- Introduces Fixed-SAE Track: one shared SAE per layer, trained on activations pooled across the base model and every RL checkpoint with feature directions held fixed, so representation drift over RL training can be tracked in stable, interpretable coordinates.
- Finds RL-induced drift is small, gradual, and concentrated in late layers — mostly boosting the sampling rate of formatting/scaffolding tokens (step breaks, answer delimiters) rather than reshaping problem content; steering just these features into the base model recovers ~80% of RL's performance gain.
- Strong representational evidence for the "distribution-sharpening" view of RL post-training (echoing yesterday's Decision-Flow Sampling result): RL mostly elicits capabilities the base model already has rather than installing new ones.

### 5. [The Misery of Mechanistic Interpretability: A Formal Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15533)

- Shows that interpretable replacement networks (IRNs) are fragile: even semantically minor input perturbations flip which sparse features dominate — and thus the human-readable interpretation — across five open-weight families (GPT-2, Gemma 2/3, Llama 3.2, R1-Distill-Qwen).
- Proposes the first formal verification framework for IRN faithfulness, using reachability analysis to certify a sound upper bound on the faithfulness gap under adversarial input perturbations.
- Verification-aware training substantially tightens this certified bound — a formal guarantee, rather than an empirical spot-check, for a mechanistic-interpretability claim about an LLM.

### 6. [Calibrating Interpretability Instruments Before Trusting Their Verdicts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14754)

- Documents six specific, diagnosable ways that common causal-interpretability measurements (projections, cosines, ablation deltas, interchange patches) silently fail and still return a plausible-looking number instead of erroring out.
- Examples: a covariance-matched null that saturates until every direction looks "typical"; per-head attribution that overshoots the true residual write threefold on reordered-normalization architectures; interchange patches that go sign-chaotic near a ceiling.
- Proposes a four-move discipline (positive-control ladder, orthogonal-cell certification, power analysis before spending compute, depth-referenced read-from verdicts) — a practical methodological corrective in the same vein as recent truthfulness-benchmark leakage critiques.

### 7. [The Token Before the Value Is the Key: How Hybrid Architectures Organize Induction Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15545)

- Mechanistically decomposes induction circuits (Carrying predecessor info, Matching by content, Copying values) in hybrid recurrent/local-global transformer architectures using layer-type-agnostic probes.
- Finds Carrying concentrates in efficient recurrent/local layers and Matching in global-attention layers, with the local contribution concentrated specifically on the token immediately preceding the historical value ("lag one").
- Interventions (masking, removing convolution, reducing early learning rate) can relocate Carrying/Matching between layer types, showing the circuit's division of labor is a trained, movable outcome rather than a fixed architectural given.

### 8. [Structured Features Overfit Where Random Features Grok](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15047)

- Extends a recent ICML 2026 result that over-parameterized ridge regression on unstructured random Gaussian features groks (with a 1/λ memorize-then-generalize delay); shows the grokking delay vanishes entirely for a structured, band-limited Fourier feature map.
- Enlarging the feature band at fixed weight decay drives held-out accuracy smoothly from 1.00 down to 0.07, with the collapse governed by the number of active Fourier modes (via the teacher-weighted spectrum of the empirical Gram matrix) — not by the capacity ratio that governs double descent.
- A clean empirical demonstration that grokking is a feature-geometry phenomenon rather than a generic consequence of overparameterization, a useful data point for theories of when and why generalization is delayed.

### 9. [Pick Your Poison: Learning to Select Poison Sets for Stronger LLM Backdoor Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15029)

- Shows standard backdoor-attack evaluations (fixed poison count, randomly sampled poison examples) badly underestimate worst-case vulnerability: with model, clean data, and poison count all held fixed, attack success on LLaMA-3-8B ranges from 3% to 80% depending purely on which poisoned examples are chosen.
- Introduces SAILS, which learns a set-level scorer from a few hundred finetune-and-evaluate runs, ranks millions of candidate poison sets, and audits only a small shortlist — improving held-out attack success by 30 points on average over the strongest influence-based baselines.
- A red-teaming-relevant result: backdoor defenses and audits calibrated against random poison sampling may be assessing a much weaker threat model than what a motivated attacker can actually achieve.

### 10. [When the World Lies: Backdoor Attacks on Latent World Models for Downstream Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15781)

- Identifies a new supply-chain attack surface: pretrained world models reused as dynamics backbones for control (Dreamer-style actor training, MPC/CEM planning) can be backdoored by an adversary who controls only the released checkpoint, while the victim trains and evaluates entirely on clean data and never sees the trigger.
- The attack embeds no explicit trigger-action rule; instead it reshapes latent dynamics near trigger-bearing observations so the victim's own optimization rediscovers the attacker's target action — hijacking 100% of triggered steps in the strongest settings while clean-task success stays above ~75%.
- The backdoor is temporally gated (active only while the trigger is present) and survives moderate clean fine-tuning, a concrete illustration of how reused pretrained components can carry hidden, hard-to-audit misalignment into downstream systems.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Delegating Authorization to Misaligned Agents: Coalitional Alignment and Safe Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15803)** — characterizes exactly when a panel of possibly-misaligned reviewer agents can still safely gate a proposer agent's actions ("k-robust coalitional alignment"), a threshold-approval condition necessary and sufficient for the principal to do at least as well as a baseline policy, lifted to sequential MDP control.
- **[The Filter Metric is Safety-Critical: Phantom Advantages in Group-Relative RL under Shaped Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13866)** — isolates a metric-predicate mismatch in GRPO-style dynamic sampling where filtering on shaped training score instead of task outcome creates "phantom advantages" that collapse training (EM 0.08 vs. 0.75 on GSM8K).
- **[Sharp Rates and a One-Line Correction for Spectral Representation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15825)** — proves matching two-sided transfer-risk bounds for self-supervised spectral representations, showing task diversity (not isotropy) is what makes frozen encoder features transfer, plus a one-line diagnostic/correction.
- **[Thought without systematicity? Evaluating reasoning models on rule induction tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13948)** — reasoning models that solve a rule-induction task often fail on structurally-equivalent variants of the same task, challenging claims of robust compositional generalization.
- **[Disentangling Representation Evolution in Transformers through Directional Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15975)** — decomposes transformer residual-stream updates into parallel/perpendicular components, finding large parallel (non-rotating) contributions and using the decomposition to diagnose compression error and improve pretraining loss via targeted suppression.
- **[Inherited Heads: Audio language models track speakers with their text backbone's attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14174)** — audio LMs' speaker-tracking is carried out almost entirely by attention heads inherited unchanged from their text backbone; steering the same hundred heads found via a written version of the task redirects audio speaker attribution on up to 99% of trials.
- **[Generative Interpretability via Scalable Neuro-Symbolic Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13529)** — position paper arguing post-hoc interpretability is structurally inadequate for agentic deployment, proposing architectures whose inference pass natively exposes causally-intervenable checkpoints.
- **[Symmetries and Singularities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14663)** — applies Singular Learning Theory's local learning coefficient to graph attention models, using known symmetries to make LLC estimation (normally posterior-sampling-expensive) analytically tractable.
- **[Algorithmic Information Dynamics of Learning: A Certified, Differentiable Complexity Controller for Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13197)** — turns a certified, differentiable complexity estimator into a real-time "controller" that accelerates grokking by timing loss kicks to a measured Occam-boundary transition.
