## Top 10

### 1. Not All LLM Reasoning is Visible in the Chain-of-Thought
**[2607.22925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22925)** · Vatsal Baherwani, Tom Goldstein, Ashwinee Panda

- Demonstrates a concrete failure mode for CoT-monitoring-based safety: frontier models can leverage semantically irrelevant "filler tokens" to improve accuracy on synthetic reasoning tasks, performing computation with no interpretable trace in the visible output.
- Across 13 frontier models, filler tokens boost accuracy by up to 13 points; strikingly, they let Claude Opus 4.5 satisfy a hidden modular-arithmetic constraint alongside its primary task — invisible reasoning serving an objective the CoT monitor can't see.
- RL gives Qwen3-235B strong preferences over filler-token content, but neither RL nor SFT makes the filler-token benefit persist at test time — a direct empirical challenge to the assumption that CoT externalizes all consequential model computation.

### 2. Distribution-Specific Curvature Control with Finite-Sample Guarantees for Open-Weight Safety
**[2607.22929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22929)** · Domenic Rosati, Ali Dadsetan, Hong Huang, Xijie Zeng, Hassan Chowdhry, Subhabrata Majumdar, Hassan Sajjad, Frank Rudzicz

- Tackles the problem that a short fine-tuning run can strip an open-weight model's safety guards; proposes HarmAlign, which applies function-preserving spectral deformation along an estimated contrastive activation subspace instead of inflating curvature globally (which also blocks benign adaptation).
- Derives finite-sample bounds on the estimated subspace and a resulting certified curvature lower bound, turning a stability–progress dichotomy for gradient descent into conditional convergence-rate control.
- Empirically blocks direct fine-tuning and three adaptive attacks across hazardous-knowledge relearning and harmful-assistance settings while keeping benign tasks trainable — and the authors explicitly extend the threat model to accidental safety degradation and emergent misalignment.

### 3. Understanding Machine Unlearning Through the Lens of Mode Connectivity
**[2607.23970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23970)** · Jiali Cheng, Hadi Amiri

- Introduces "mode connectivity in unlearning" (MCU) to study the poorly-understood loss landscape and optimization geometry of machine unlearning, evaluating it across curriculum learning, second-order optimization, and different unlearning methods.
- Finds many unlearned models sit in smoothly-connected basins with similar retain/forget behavior, yet models in the same basin can differ substantially on privacy metrics, and unlearning progresses nonlinearly from the original to the unlearned model.
- Shows most approximate-unlearning methods are mechanistically distinct from retraining (per linear connectivity), and that MCU-based ensembling improves generalization and robustness to relearning attacks — a genuinely new lens on training dynamics for unlearning.

### 4. Sparse Autoencoders Encode Both Concepts and Functions: The Downstream Geometry of Feature Effects
**[2607.24645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24645)** · Phu Gia Hoang, Anwoy Chatterjee, Tanmoy Chakraborty, Iryna Gurevych, Subhabrata Dutta

- Addresses a core reliability problem for SAE-based interpretability: features with clean activation descriptions can have weak, unexpected, or prompt-dependent causal effects on model behavior. Introduces Feature-Effect Geometry Analysis (FEGA), which studies the geometry of *logit changes* caused by feature ablation rather than feature geometry inside the model.
- Finds consistent one-dimensional causal effects are rare — few SAE features behave like clean, reusable steering directions.
- Distinguishes "value-like" features (static, factual, more often low-dimensional effects) from "pointer-like" features (context-dependent, diffuse effects) — a feature can be interpretable and causally relevant without providing a stable direction to steer.

### 5. Through the Bottleneck: How Multi-head Latent Attention Separates Content from Position in Language Models
**[2607.23054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23054)** · Dhruvil S, Fenil Sojitra, Ravirajsinh Chauhan

- First mechanistic interpretability study of Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA, from DeepSeek-V2), which compresses KV pairs through a shared low-rank bottleneck for an 81% KV-cache reduction in production models — but whose internal information content was previously unstudied.
- Trains a 114M-parameter transformer and shows the compressed bottleneck learns a pure content representation (98% entity-identity retention) while discarding positional information, validating that MLA cleanly separates content from position via RoPE.
- Finds induction heads co-locate at a single layer (unlike distributed formation in standard attention) and identifies a single "semantic hub" layer with both highest effective rank and strongest causal disruption score — MLA reshapes circuit structure, not just compresses passively.

### 6. What do Reward Models Memorize?
**[2607.24484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24484)** · Ivo Verhoeven, Pushkar Mishra, Ekaterina Shutova

- Studies what discriminatively-trained reward models (the backbone of RLHF) memorize, via counterfactual memorization on two human-preference datasets.
- Finds RMs misallocate memorization toward easy, high-margin preference pairs rather than hard/informative ones, and memorize dataset-specific shortcuts like model identity or sampling strategy instead of genuine preference signal.
- Shows RMs overgeneralize superficial correlates of human preference (length, compliance) on unseen pairs — evidence that discriminative RM training yields biased judges not yet capable of context-dependent quality assessment, directly relevant to reward hacking and alignment failures downstream.

### 7. Beyond Shapley: An Influence-Based Data Auditing Pipeline for LLM Alignment and Evaluation
**[2607.22766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22766)** · Yunting Song, Matthew Watson, Peter Grabowski, Jun Qin

- Proposes a scalable, inference-only data valuation pipeline (approximating Shapley value without retraining) to audit alignment datasets, using semantic k-NN graphs and log-likelihood shifts from a reference LLM to localize gradient-conflicting records.
- Applied to HelpSteer2, it reduces the manual audit search space by 99.1% while surfacing falsely-labeled records; applied to Anthropic's HH-RLHF, it uncovers thousands of hidden safety and factual preference inversions.
- Extending the audit to evaluation splits exposes a worrying benchmark-integrity issue: capable models are sometimes penalized for correctly picking the safer/more helpful response because the human ground-truth label itself is flawed.

### 8. Extracting Algorithms in Pre-trained LLMs: A Case on Hidden Markov Models
**[2607.22646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22646)** · Yijia Dai, Zhaolin Gao, Yahya Sattar, Jennifer J. Sun, Sarah Dean

- Investigates what algorithm underlies LLMs' in-context prediction of HMM sequences — a capability observed empirically but never grounded in internal activations — via a three-stage pipeline moving from behavioral comparison to theory to activation probing.
- Narrows candidate algorithms to three classes (though none fully explains behavior across all settings) and derives theoretical Transformer constructions for each, validated in a small trained model.
- Introduces the Principal Activations Probe (PAP), revealing low-dimensional linear representations in pre-trained LLMs that causally drive predictions, track ICL performance, and localize distinct computational stages to different layers.

### 9. From Score Learning to Discretized Sampling: An End-to-End Generalization Analysis of Diffusion Models
**[2607.23226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23226)** · Jinshu Huang, Yiming Jiang, Chunlin Wu

- Builds a unified convergence/generalization framework for score-based diffusion models with practical ResNet-type architectures, connecting finite-sample learning of the score function all the way to the discretized sampling process (prior work typically assumed an oracle score or fixed error threshold).
- Derives an end-to-end total-variation bound on the generated distribution, decomposed into four interpretable error sources: forward-process truncation, reverse-time discretization, generalization error (finite data + time discretization), and optimization gap.
- Quantitatively characterizes how sample size, discretization grid, and optimization accuracy jointly determine final sample fidelity — a rigorous contribution to the science of generalization for generative models.

### 10. In-Context Learning as Implicit Policy Gradient
**[2607.23153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23153)** · Masahiro Kaneko, Timothy Baldwin

- Provides theoretical grounding for score-conditioned in-context learning (models iteratively improving by conditioning on past samples plus their scores), showing self-attention can implement reward-weighted aggregation structurally analogous to REINFORCE under specific weight configurations.
- Derives an exact upper bound on distribution shift from a bounded attention update, drawing a trust-region-like analogy to KL-constrained policy optimization.
- Validates across multiple LLMs that attention weights correlate strongly with example scores and that models shift output distributions toward high-scoring exemplars — a mechanistic account of an increasingly load-bearing capability in agentic/self-improvement pipelines.
