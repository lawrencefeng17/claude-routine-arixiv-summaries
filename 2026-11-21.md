# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-21

Note: arXiv does not announce on weekends, so the cs.LG listing still shows **Fri, 20 Nov 2026** (119 new + 94 cross-lists). The picks below come from that listing and **leave out the 22 papers already in the 2026-11-20 digest**. Summaries are based on the abstracts. With the strongest papers already covered, today leans toward interpretability methodology, statistically certified inference-time compute, and memory/continual-learning dynamics.

## Top 10

### 1. [Some Memories Must Fade: Persistence and Decay of User Memories in Model Parameters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25235)
- Argues that parametric memory should not copy the continual-learning goal of keeping everything forever. A memory system must keep stable facts, reinforce recurring ones, update changed beliefs, and **let transient information fade**.
- Substrate: a fixed-capacity LoRA built from k-hot rank combinations. Facts are stored without interfering with each other, and each has its own controllable temporal decay.
- A lightweight learned controller decides, for each user event, what to store, how deeply and for how long.
- On a new long-horizon benchmark that tests recall while a fact is valid and abstention after it expires, the system reaches **86% of oracle performance**. CL baselines mostly just recall and RAG baselines mostly just abstain.

### 2. [From Geographic Memory to Spatial Generalization in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25703)
- Separates **memorizing** location–description pairs seen in training from **generalizing** to regions never observed.
- Feeds continuous spatial-encoder embeddings into the LLM as "spatial tokens" instead of writing coordinates as text.
- Across LLM families on new Los Angeles and London benchmarks, this improves captioning at unobserved locations. The generated descriptions also give a small but consistent boost as extra supervision for a grounding model.
- Layer-wise probes and clustering are consistent with the geographic knowledge being organized spatially inside the model. Methodological point: memory and generalization should be measured separately.

### 3. [How Reliable Are Explanations of Language Models That Detect Insider Threats?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25796)
- Audits feature-ablation explanations of LM-based anomaly detectors. Each test compares removing a feature against a "similar but not removed" control.
- Main contribution, the **tipping radius**: a first-order estimate of how far the similarity metric for the control must move before the removal-vs-control gap disappears.
- In 33 of 34 runs across 7 LMs, the tipping radius is at most 18% of the distance between two standard metric choices. **The conclusions are fragile to an arbitrary metric choice.** The finding replicates in 13 of 14 pre-registered runs.
- Recommendation for interpretability audits in general: fix the control metric in advance, report the tipping radius, and disclose what the features are compared against.

### 4. [Probing the "Conscious" Mind: Selective Access and Global Workspace in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26590)
- Introduces MC-Lens, a multimodal Jacobian lens. It maps vision-encoder and LM-decoder states into the decoder's vocabulary space and supports interventions on concepts.
- Concept information can be read from intermediate states and is **accessed selectively depending on the query**. Counterfactual interventions show it takes part in downstream reasoning.
- Training dynamics: in controlled VLM training, cross-modal concept alignment appears first, and **reliable selective access develops later**. The new J-space metrics track this better than hidden-state cosine.
- The patterns hold across VLM families and sizes, even though their internal organization differs.

### 5. [Before the Choice: An Evidential Framework for Studying Prospective Representations in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25170)
- Asks whether LMs internally represent the alternatives they **did not choose**, and sets out what evidence would be needed to show it.
- A pre-registered low-rank probe on a sealed test set failed its criterion (0.020 vs a 0.195 baseline). A follow-up found a "reconstruction-by-elimination" confound: knowing the chosen option can pin down the unchosen one.
- With that confound controlled, decoding stayed at chance (0.521, p = 0.18).
- A useful negative result and methods paper. **Decodability alone doesn't show the model represents something**: identifiability, sensitivity and a null model that accounts for dependence all matter. The authors frame it as groundwork for AI welfare research.

### 6. [Realized versus Population Reliability: Anytime-Valid Self-Consistency Certification under Correlated Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26592)
- Self-consistency certificates (e-processes over repeated samples) assume rollouts are independent, but reasoning rollouts are **positively correlated**.
- Correlation splits the null into a "realized" null (about this sampled belief) and a "population" null (about the model's average behavior). Standard certificates are valid only for the former.
- Effective sample size saturates at 1/ρ, so more rollouts per prompt cannot restore population validity. A prompt-level e-process can, with one rollout per prompt being optimal.
- Experiments with Claude Haiku 4.5: on AIME (ρ ≈ 0.48), the single-unit rule wrongly certifies 24% of prompts at nominal ε = 0.05, while the prompt-level test certifies none.

### 7. [Sequential Anytime Valid Tests for Efficient Inference-Time Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26413)
- Replaces fixed-budget Best-of-N with **adaptive stopping that has a statistical guarantee**. Sampling stops once one answer is certified to hold a majority under the generator's distribution.
- A Wald SPRT version works empirically but loses its guarantee, because the leading answer changes during sampling.
- e-RWSC instead runs a betting supermartingale for every answer. It bounds the chance of ever certifying a non-dominant answer by α, uniformly over time, with cost O(log(1/α)/KL(θ*‖½)).
- Gets higher accuracy than baselines at much lower token cost. This is a natural companion to #6.

### 8. [Deviations from the NTK Regime in CNNs under Joint Depth-Width Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25624)
- Numerical study of how depth L and channel width C together affect NTK concentration at initialization and kernel rotation during training in CNNs.
- At critical and chaotic initialization, NTK dispersion grows with **L/C**, and at criticality the curves roughly collapse onto L/C. This suggests a nontrivial joint scaling regime.
- Kernel rotation during training grows with L/C even in the ordered regime, where dispersion at initialization is small. **Concentration at init and stability during training scale differently.**

### 9. [The Stability Gap in Continual Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26252)
- A cheap continual-RL benchmark that changes only the observation representation and keeps dynamics and rewards fixed.
- Shows a **stability gap**: performance on earlier tasks drops sharply when a new task arrives, then partly or fully recovers.
- This happens even though the agent has full access to past tasks. Final aggregate metrics hide these transient training dynamics.

### 10. [Decision-Focused Reinforcement Learning for LLM Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25597)
- Post-trains LLM forecasters using **negative downstream decision regret** as the RL reward instead of prediction accuracy. The downstream optimizer does not need to be convex or differentiable.
- Two fixes for expensive rewards: warm-starting with cheap heuristic rewards, and an unbiased reward correction that mixes cheap estimates with occasional exact solves.
- On H&M inventory planning, letting the LLM reason before predicting cuts regret by 31.4% relative to SFT and 12.4% relative to the best conventional forecaster. It is an example of how what you reward changes what the model learns.

## Honorable Mentions

- [A Model of Diverse Sampling from Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25003): PoolDPP samples a size-K set from a DPP over a candidate pool, with a KL bound of O(1/N). It improves the quality–diversity frontier and raises diversity at about the same pass@K on GSM8K.
- [Who Moved My Z Boson? Revealing Resonance Features Inside a Neural Network](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26291): mechanistic interpretability on an LHC generative model. It finds features for the Z, J/ψ and Υ resonances that were never labeled, and steering them shifts the Z mass between about 80 and 105 GeV.
- [Physical Laws without Physics Loss? Probing Learned Dynamics in Video World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25626): forced-response tests show video models learn linear resonance, and 5 of 6 learn nonlinear softening that makes up only 0.74% of the training signal.
- [Learning to Compare Neural Representations from Parameterized Computation Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26081): a graph metanetwork predicts representational similarity across models from their weights alone, without running inputs through them. Useful for layer retrieval and stitching.
- [Learning to Read Events for Exact State Tracking with Frozen Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25211): gets around TC⁰ limits on permutation tracking by pairing a frozen LM that extracts transitions with an external register. A frozen 0.8B model read every event in 299/299 windows of about 44k events each.
- [LLM Complexity: Towards a Computational Complexity View of LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24943): token count does not track classical time complexity. A metric based on the area under the accuracy–token curve ranks tasks consistently across models and supports an easy-to-hard SFT curriculum.
- [LLMs Are Already Confidence-Aware Zero-Shot Classifiers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25289): across 11 open models, logit-based confidence is better calibrated and discriminates better than verbalized or sampling-based confidence.
- [Reward misalignment as an opportunity for materials discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26186): expert disagreement with a proxy reward is systematic rather than noise. Steering a diffusion model with learned expert preferences finds materials the reward misses.
- [Text-Aware, Vision-Blind: How Multimodal Image Editing Systems Handle Unsafe Inputs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26569): EditSafetyBench (5,940 pairs) shows that safety filters on Gemini, GPT and Qwen editing systems mostly check the text and miss risk carried by the image.
- [When Objectives and Evaluators Disagree: Post-hoc Demographic Framing Mitigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25258): rewrites can look successful by satisfying their own selector without improving under an independent evaluator.
