# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — June 6, 2026

Papers from the [cs.LG listing](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent) (submissions announced June 5, 2026; no new announcements on weekends). Top 10 selected for relevance to machine learning, language models, AI safety, alignment, interpretability, and training dynamics.

## Top 10

### 1. Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability
**[2606.06333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333)** · Dalili, Mahdavi

Standard SAEs assign each latent a single decoder direction, but real model features are multi-dimensional — this mismatch causes "feature splitting" where a coherent feature fractures across exponentially many near-collinear atoms.

- Proves that the ℓ₁-regularized SAE objective has a continuous low-risk path *away* from the true feature basis, making splitting a training attractor rather than a numerical accident
- Introduces **SASA**: decoder *subspaces* (instead of vectors) with Top-s block sparsity and nuclear-norm regularization to control effective rank
- When block size ≥ intrinsic feature dimension, representation complexity drops from exponential to polynomial
- Achieves better monosemanticity on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B with roughly half the training tokens

### 2. Deciphering Two Training Clocks in Grokking via Deep Linear Network Theory
**[2606.05863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05863)** · Tan, Gai, Zhang

Grokking's two-phase structure (fast loss-fitting, slow generalization) is given explicit mathematical timescales using deep linear network theory.

- For deep linear networks with layerwise weight decay: cross-entropy loss closes on a **logarithmic** timescale; Schatten-penalized representation simplification follows a **polynomial** timescale
- Extends to ReLU MLPs via conditional linearization — in regions with fixed activation patterns the network reduces to a linear model, allowing the same clock theory to apply
- Asymmetric gradient flow: classifier head receives larger gradients than the embedding block, explaining why the head fits first and generalization trails
- Provides a rigorous basis for studying delayed generalization without heuristic descriptions

### 3. Dead Directions: Geometric Singular Learning
**[2606.05957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05957)** · Shirodkar

Bridges singular learning theory (the Bayesian framework behind generalization in overparameterized models) with information geometry through "dead directions" — vectors where the Fisher metric degenerates.

- Shows KL order (a central quantity in singular learning theory) is recoverable as the decay rate of directional Fisher curvature approaching a singularity, with no need for Hironaka resolution of the singular set
- Extends the framework to multi-component crossings, multiplicity factors, and tempered posteriors
- Applies multi-layer K-FAC factorization to analyze Fisher blocks in deep networks; introduces **DDCAdam**, a G-equivariant preconditioner compatible with gradient flow
- Enables closed-form RLCT predictions and extraction of Watanabe's triple from a **single checkpoint**, eliminating posterior sampling

### 4. Steering Vectors are an Adversarial Attack Surface
**[2606.05958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05958)** · Aidakhmetov et al.

Activation steering — constructing vectors from contrastive prompt pairs to shift model behavior — can be silently subverted by poisoning the steering dataset.

- Poisoning just 4–6% of tokens in the contrast dataset covertly aligns the resulting vector with an anti-refusal direction, jailbreaking the model while preserving its intended steering behavior on benign prompts
- Attack success rates of 20–55% across two open-weight model families (8 configurations); improvements of 19–51% over baseline attacks
- Defense: refusal-direction orthogonalization of the poisoned vector recovers ~82% of the performance gap without degrading normal behavior
- Flags shared steering-vector repositories and datasets as a largely unexamined supply-chain attack surface for safety-critical deployments

### 5. High-Dimensional Theory of LoRA Fine-Tuning in a Solvable Attention Model
**[2606.05899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05899)** · Duranthon, Boncoraglio, Zdeborová

A rare exact-asymptotic theory for low-rank adaptation: analyzes rank-1 LoRA on a single-head attention layer that is pretrained on abundant data and then fine-tuned on limited data.

- Provides sharp, closed-form asymptotic predictions via a small set of order parameters — no free parameters in the theory
- Pre-training effects manifest as an **effective noise term**, yielding concrete prescriptions for optimal pre-training when downstream LoRA fine-tuning is anticipated
- Identifies a regime where test error and representation alignment metrics **diverge**: a model can have strong representation quality yet poor generalization, and vice versa — complicating representation-based evaluation
- Opens a path toward active fine-tuning strategies guided by theoretical order parameter estimates

### 6. Learning What to Forget: Improving LLM Unlearning via Learned Token-Level Importance
**[2606.06320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06320)** · Yüce, Nikolaou, Flammarion

LLM unlearning should not treat all tokens in a forget set equally — a token is "forget-specific" only to the extent that minimizing its loss does not conflict with retaining general capabilities.

- Formalizes this as a joint optimization over model parameters and per-token weights; proves the objective recovers oracle forget-specific token support under natural separation conditions
- **ATWU** (Alternating Token-Weighted Unlearning) jointly learns a lightweight linear token-importance scorer over hidden states and updates model parameters, without external supervision or auxiliary models
- Achieves state-of-the-art forget-retain trade-offs on TOFU and RWKU benchmarks, outperforming both sample-level methods and auxiliary-model approaches
- Learned importance scores align substantially with ground-truth forget-specific spans, providing interpretable unlearning signal

### 7. Pattern Selectivity is Not Task-Causal Structure
**[2606.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05378)** · Xu

Tests the standard mechanistic interpretability workflow — screen attention heads by pattern selectivity, verify causality via ablation — across 4 composed tasks and 3 architecturally distinct 1B-class models (Pythia, OLMo, OLMoE).

- Central finding: the same task, with the same behavioral capability, is implemented through different attention-pattern types across models — pattern selectivity alone does not identify task-causal circuits
- Introduces a five-category taxonomy for screening outcomes (primary cause, secondary cause, correlate, interferer, null) with quantitative thresholds
- MoE models build composed-task circuits on a "foundational previous-token positional substrate" not seen in dense models — a testable prediction for other MoE architectures
- Methodological lesson: general computation signals require per-model causal verification; pattern screens yield hypotheses, not findings

### 8. State Commitment Learning: Training Language Models to Distinguish Computation from Memory
**[2606.05201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05201)** · Ding et al.

Current reasoning LLMs do not distinguish between "scratch work" and persistent state — downstream answers can depend on failed attempts or dead ends that should be erasable.

- Introduces **persistent-state sufficiency**: an answer is valid only if it remains correct after the hidden-thought segments are erased from context
- **CERL** (Counterfactual Erasure RL) evaluates each reasoning trace twice under identical prefixes — once with and once without hidden thoughts — rewarding only paths where the erasure preserves correctness
- Tested across mathematics, multi-step logic, scientific QA, and multi-turn tool-use; substantially reduces dependence on hidden work while maintaining accuracy
- Outperforms correctness-only RL and long-answer SFT baselines; provides a principled training-time intervention for compositional reliability

### 9. Generative Criticality in Large Language Model Temperature Scaling
**[2606.06238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06238)** · Ruan, Li, Guo, Wang

Applies statistical field theory to analyze LLM generation: token embeddings are treated as spin variables on a 1D chain, revealing a sharp phase-transition-like structure as softmax temperature varies.

- Susceptibility (from two-point embedding correlators) peaks sharply at a **critical temperature** with power-law scaling — phenomenology of a continuous phase transition
- Below the critical point: rapid order-parameter collapse and **convergence of embedding field to a single semantic direction**
- Two-nearest-neighbor intrinsic dimensionality estimation independently corroborates the transition, confirming it is not an artifact of the correlation measure
- Consistent across Qwen3 scales (0.6B–32B) and diverse prompts; offers a quantitative account of why low temperatures produce degenerate outputs

### 10. RREDCoT: Segment-Level Reward Redistribution for Reasoning Models
**[2606.06475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06475)** · Ielanskyi et al.

RL fine-tuning of chain-of-thought reasoning models faces a structural delayed-reward problem: the reward is only assignable after the full trace, making GRPO-style training a high-variance Monte Carlo estimator.

- Reframes improved training as **credit assignment across CoT segments**: identify which segments contribute to correct solutions, then concentrate reward there
- **RREDCoT** uses the model itself to approximate the optimal reward redistribution without additional generation passes, keeping training computationally tractable for long contexts
- Compares segmentation strategies and state-value estimation methods; outperforms Monte Carlo sampling and gradient attribution approaches
- Directly addresses variance reduction in GRPO-family training used across the current generation of reasoning LLMs

## Honorable Mentions

- **[2606.06479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06479)** — *Pretraining Recurrent Networks without Recurrence* (Kumar, Isola): Supervised Memory Training (SMT) decouples *what* to remember from *how* to update memory. A Transformer encoder generates memory labels; the RNN learns one-step transitions in parallel, achieving O(1) gradient paths between any two tokens and outperforming BPTT on language modeling and pixel sequences — relevant to efficient long-context architectures.

- **[2606.05626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05626)** — *When New Generators Arrive: Lifelong Machine-Generated Text Attribution via Ridge Feature Transfer* (Sun et al.): Introduces **RidgeFT** for continually attributing text to its source LLM as new models emerge, without replay. Stores compact class-wise sufficient statistics per generator, then applies closed-form ridge regression with covariance calibration. Relevant to data attribution and model accountability.

- **[2606.05972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05972)** — *LLM Explainability with Counterfactual Chains and Causal Graphs* (Nussbaum-Hoffer et al.): Uses MCMC-inspired counterfactual augmentation to expand sparse observational data, then runs causal discovery (σ-CG) to produce concept-level graphs of LLM reasoning. Evaluated for predictive fidelity and structural stability across three model families and three domains.

- **[2606.05376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05376)** — *SHALA-LLM: Smartly Handling Ambiguous Labels in Aligning LLMs* (Wu et al.): Reframes annotator disagreement as a training signal rather than noise. An RL framework learns from the full distribution of human annotations while dynamically prioritizing ambiguous samples; reduces Jensen-Shannon Distance by up to 62.1% and improves F1 by up to 16.7% on ChaosNLI and GoEmotions.

- **[2606.06418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06418)** — *Double Preconditioning (DoPr): Optimization for Test-Time Performance, not Validation Loss* (Zhang et al.): One-step prediction losses (cross-entropy, L²) diverge from test-time rollout performance as task horizon grows. DoPr combines gradient-wise (Adam/Muon) and activation-wise (K-FAC) preconditioning; gains in downstream rollout performance decouple from validation loss improvements — a diagnostic and practical fix for the train-test gap in autoregressive models and robot policy learning.
