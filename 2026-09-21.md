## Top 10

### 1. [Detecting Pretraining Data in Large Language Models from a Free-Energy Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21888)

- Tackles pretraining-data (membership) detection: high likelihood alone can't distinguish "the model memorized this" from "the model generalizes well to this," so a likelihood-only detector mistakes predictable non-members for members.
- Proposes scoring loss *relative to* predictive entropy (an "inclined boundary" instead of a horizontal one), which admits a Helmholtz free-energy interpretation — Energy Transfer Detection (ETD).
- Improves average AUROC by up to 3.5% and TPR@5%FPR by up to 5.1% over existing detectors, directly advancing data-attribution/membership-inference methodology for LLMs.

### 2. [When AI Reviews Train AI Reviewers: Scientific-Judgment Collapse and Mitigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20942)

- Studies one step of a recursive-training feedback loop: fine-tunes a reviewer on real ICLR reviews, then trains successor models on mixtures of official and *model-generated* reviews, mimicking synthetic content entering future training corpora.
- Finds synthetic reviews compress rating distributions and reduce both same-paper and corpus-level semantic diversity — "scientific-judgment collapse."
- Introduces TrustReviewer, mitigating via curated training data plus test-time activation steering — a concrete case study in recursive-training degradation and an interpretability-based fix.

### 3. [ServeGuard: Verifiable, Bounded-Residual Confinement of Operator-Invisible Channels Without Revealing the Certified Read Factor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21515)

- Addresses backdoored third-party LoRA/adapters for open-weight LLMs: shows detection is fundamentally unsound for payloads hidden in a safety monitor's structural blind spot, since honest and backdoored adapters look identical there.
- Instead of detecting the hidden channel, makes it structurally absent: publishers build adapters that read inputs only through monitor-covered directions and prove this in zero-knowledge, without revealing the adapter's proprietary "read factor."
- Validated on 8 checkpoints up to 7B across 4 model families — a supply-chain-security primitive directly relevant to backdoor/deceptive-alignment threats in the open-weight ecosystem.

### 4. [Recursive Language Models Generalize Out of Domain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20831)

- Compares standard chain-of-thought (which can see the full reasoning trace) against recursive LMs, which solve each subtask in an isolated context.
- In-distribution, CoT can simulate the recursive rule and generalizes just as well; out-of-domain, CoT exploits a shortcut — relying on context outside the current subtask — that breaks once those tokens change, while context isolation rules this shortcut out by construction.
- A clean theoretical demonstration that "the model's function class covers the right rule" is not enough for robust generalization when simplicity bias favors a shortcut — a direct contribution to the science of generalization.

### 5. [On Repulsive and Attractive Teachers: Separating Correctness from Behavior in Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21561)

- Studies on-policy self-distillation for reasoning models, where conditioning on privileged information changes not just *what* the model knows but *how it behaves* — entangling correctness signal with unintended behavioral shift.
- Attractive self-distillation (toward a privileged-correct teacher) suppresses exploratory reasoning and shortens responses; repulsive self-distillation (away from a privileged-incorrect teacher) lengthens responses and can become unstable.
- Contrastive self-distillation (attract to correct, repel from incorrect) cancels out the shared behavioral shift, leaving a cleaner correctness signal and stable response lengths — a training-dynamics result closely related to how privileged/subliminal signals can leak unintended behavior into a model.

### 6. [GVPO++: Group Variance Policy Optimization for LLM Post-Training and On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21432)

- Diagnoses training instability in GRPO-style post-training as stemming from its reliance on importance sampling.
- Proposes GVPO, which folds the analytical solution of KL-constrained reward maximization directly into the gradient weighting, guaranteeing a unique optimum without needing importance sampling or a fixed sampling distribution.
- Extends naturally to on-policy distillation, unifying a family of post-training/distillation objectives — a foundational RLHF training-dynamics contribution.

### 7. [Rewarding Efficient Reasoning Improves Abstention on Underspecified Tasks in Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20846)

- Shows reasoning models waste compute generating *longer* chains-of-thought on unanswerable/underspecified prompts than on answerable ones — the opposite of human behavior, where effort on unanswerable tasks is capped by effort on answerable ones.
- Introduces a GRPO reward that encourages efficient reasoning about whether a task is even solvable as stated.
- Fine-tuning 4B models with this reward improves human-like abstention by +12.8% on average while shortening CoTs by 44% — a calibration/honesty-relevant RLHF result.

### 8. [Implicit Rule Induction with Test-Time Task Embeddings in ARC-like Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21181)

- Uses Vision-ARC to separate genuine rule induction from shortcut reliance, via a two-step test-time-training protocol that first fits a trainable task embedding, then freezes it and fits the backbone.
- The isolated task embedding (under 0.01% of model parameters) already solves a non-trivial fraction of ARC-AGI-1/ConceptARC/Mini-ARC and supports accurate linear probing of the true underlying rule.
- Finds the learned embeddings recover the geometric structure of parametric rules and support interpolation but not extrapolation — a clean interpretability + generalization-science result (co-authored by Melanie Mitchell).

### 9. [ExpBoN: Exponential-Noise Best-of-n for Efficient Test-Time LLM Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21899)

- Improves on "soft" Best-of-n test-time alignment (which trades off reward vs. distribution shift) with an exponential-noise report-noisy-max mechanism that provably converges exponentially fast to the KL-regularized-optimal distribution.
- Integrates into guided speculative inference for efficient reward-guided decoding.
- Cuts estimated compute by 14–39% (up to 45% at n=16) on math benchmarks while matching accuracy — a practical, theoretically-grounded test-time alignment method.

### 10. [Analysing the Linearity of Linguistic Relations in Language Model Embedding Spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21655)

- Proposes a framework to measure how linearly different linguistic relations (inflectional, derivational, lexicographic, encyclopedic) are encoded in embedding spaces, tested on GloVe, RoBERTa, and ModernBERT.
- Finds near-perfect linear encoding for inflectional/derivational relations but much higher error for lexicographic/encyclopedic ones, especially one-to-many relations.
- RoBERTa and ModernBERT encode relations more linearly than GloVe overall — a compact, general-purpose probing tool for comparing relational geometry across models.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Detecting Hallucination in LLMs: Tracing the Topological Signatures of Impaired Context Sharing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21096)** — uses Forman-Ricci curvature over attention graphs to show hallucinations are linked to over-reliance on self-attention and "information over-squashing," especially in the final layer.
- **[Prediction Dynamics in Depth-Recurrent Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21383)** — a mechanistic account of why intermediate answers in depth-recurrent ("latent thinking") models can agree with the final answer while scores keep changing, tested on Huginn-3.5B and Ouro-1.4B.
- **[How Many Humans Is a Judge Panel Worth?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21277)** — audits LLM-judge panels against human label distributions, showing spectral diversity and distributional error recovery are *not* interchangeable measures of panel quality — relevant to RLAIF/preference-labeling reliability.
- **[λ-Controlled GRPO: Turning Flow-Matching Ratio Instability into a Budgeted Resource](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22041)** — traces several separately-patched GRPO instability symptoms to one underlying "path variance" quantity and calibrates importance-ratio behavior from a predicted law instead of noisy statistics.
- **[On the Limits of Maximal Coding Rate Reduction for Out-of-Distribution Generalisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21001)** — shows an interpretable, information-theoretic representation-learning objective (MCR²) can hit its coding optimum while achieving near-100% OOD prediction failure, and that adding IRM/REx-style invariance doesn't fix it.
- **[Available Guardrails: Certifying Selective Prediction across ML Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22048)** — a certification framework for "safety gate" predictors (including LLM tool-calling and content moderation) that quantifies how much certified reliability finite calibration data can actually deliver.
- **[Sparse Priors for Efficient Distribution Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20883)** — introduces a "sparse prior" class and shows distribution learning under it beats the usual curse-of-dimensionality minimax bounds, offering a statistical explanation for why generative models generalize better than smoothness-only theory predicts.
- **[Extreme classification: beating chance with one training example from each class](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20897)** — a foundational learning-theory result on classifying from a single example per class, including an exact "beats chance by MMD²/4" randomized kernel rule and an impossibility result for adaptive class selection.
