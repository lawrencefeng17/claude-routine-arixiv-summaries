# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-23

Listing: **Mon, 23 Nov 2026** (96 new submissions + 75 cross-lists; replacements not considered). Summaries are based on the abstracts. Today has strong interpretability-methodology content (spurious causal-abstraction features, statistically reliable behavior discovery), a striking training-dynamics result on how post-training bakes in self-generated hallucinations, and several papers on hidden failure modes of evaluators and unlearning metrics.

## Top 10

### 1. [LLMs Memorize Their Own Hallucinations During Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26863)
- Asks what happens when reasoning-style post-training recipes (outcome-reward RL, injected "propose & backtrack" behaviors) are applied to **knowledge tasks**.
- Tracks self-generated hallucinations extracted during RL across six open models: recall of reinforced hallucinations rises **+4.83%** vs. +0.17% for unreinforced ones.
- Models even memorize false facts they **immediately retract** in their CoT; memorization is strongest on the hardest questions, and rewarding a hallucination reduces later abstention by ~7%.
- A concrete pathway by which training on self-generated data shapes what a model "believes" — relevant to generalization and data-attribution questions about RL.

### 2. [Parasite Features: Causal Abstraction Through Spurious Pathways](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26711)
- Shows a class of **spurious features that pass causal-abstraction equivalence tests**: if one direction detects an input feature and another steers the output, methods like Distributed Alignment Search can combine pathways through both and appear to find a "feature" the model doesn't implement.
- Demonstrated with linear DAS maps on syntactic agreement and two-digit addition.
- An important caution for interpretability methods that validate hypotheses via interventions.

### 3. [Reliable Behavior Discovery via False Discovery Rate Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27104)
- Formalizes **behavior discovery** in generative models (e.g., auditing for quirks) as a multiple-testing problem where false discoveries must be controlled.
- **DISCO**: an e-process–based method that provably controls FDR while adapting to a data-dependent number of hypotheses, with more power than prior eBH approaches.
- Evaluated in a setting where LLMs act as both the discovery engine and the audited target — useful statistical scaffolding for automated auditing agents.

### 4. [The Vulnerability Window: Decision Instability in Quantized Reward Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27155)
- 4-bit quantization of reward models/judges **flips 1.1–12.3% of pairwise preferences** on RewardBench while aggregate accuracy moves as little as 0.03 pts — errors cancel.
- Flips concentrate where the full-precision margin is small relative to quantization noise; small judges are most fragile.
- Downstream, 15–66% of best-of-64 selections change; under adversarial pressure some quantizers skew toward harmful flips. Benchmark *churn*, not accuracy, predicts the impact.
- Takeaway for oversight pipelines: accuracy-only checks hide reward-model drift.

### 5. [On the Limits of Observability for Evaluating Coverage in Machine Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27543)
- Introduces **coverage**: the range of deletion requests an unlearning method can actually fulfill.
- With 502 retrained models, hidden embeddings reveal deletions that outputs miss; theory shows distinguishing all *n* singleton deletions with linear scores needs rank Ω(log n).
- **CEO** benchmark (synthetic color-shape task with leave-one-out retraining oracles, SAE-expanded observables): across 20 baselines, **only retraining from scratch covers all targets**.

### 6. [How Language Models Compress and Compare: Understanding Selection with Token Covariance Maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26874)
- **Token covariance maps** track how token positions co-vary in hidden-state space per layer.
- Across 14 models / 12 selection benchmarks: a robust **compress-then-compare** organization — diffuse early layers, a low-rank mid-depth bottleneck, then re-expansion into option-vs-option geometry.
- The structure emerges during training; intervening on a candidate's representation near the bottleneck shifts which answer the model picks.

### 7. [Reasoning in Two Dimensions: Quantifying Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness and Efficiency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27100)
- Represents a CoT as a semantic reasoning graph and decomposes the model's predictive dependence into **semantic support vs. hidden history** (faithfulness) and **answer-path vs. off-path** (efficiency).
- Metrics respond correctly to controlled interventions; unfaithfulness tracks uncertainty but only weakly relates to correctness.
- Derives graph-aware fine-tuning objectives that improve faithfulness without hurting accuracy.

### 8. [A preference for local interaction between layers emerges during training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27258)
- Perturbs one layer and measures how much the "push" reaches other layers across training of 512 five-layer networks on modular addition (AdamW and SGLD).
- Early on, inter-layer responses are weak and unordered; as training proceeds, **neighboring layers become more coupled than distant ones** in almost every network — locality emerges despite a globally shared loss.
- A clean training-dynamics observation relevant to modularity and circuit formation.

### 9. [Renormalized Entropy from Symmetries: Simplicity Bias and Neural Heat Death](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27889)
- Derives the implicit bias induced by **parameter symmetries** via the equilibrium distribution over functions under Langevin dynamics, in the vanishing-regularization limit.
- A renormalization procedure yields an entropy term favoring **low-rank functions** in linear nets and **inactive neurons** in homogeneous nets — potentially "heat death" where mass concentrates on near-zero outputs.
- Interpreted via the NTK as a simplicity bias toward functions that vary slowly under parameter changes.

### 10. [Git History Is a Pangenome: Repetition in Code Training Corpora and What Deduplication Removes, Keeps and Misses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27154)
- Training on full repo history means massive repetition: a typical history holds **~19 copies** of current code (89 for mature repos); exact dedup removes none.
- Near-dup dedup brings this to ~2 copies, but popular implementations keep an **old version half the time** (fix: sort newest first).
- **Contamination**: a merged SWE-bench fix survives in a median of 18 later file versions, so decontamination must check every version. Directly relevant to data attribution and benchmark validity.

## Honorable Mentions
- [Weight-space programming and mechanistic interpretability for model editing in particle physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27079) — locates an IR-safety flaw in a jet foundation model to specific heads; fixes it by ablating 3 SAE directions or via LLM agents editing weights directly (50 queries).
- [Your Judge Inherits Your Prompt: In-Context Label Bias in an LLM-as-Judge Is Directional](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26881) — acceptance-skewed few-shot examples inflate judge acceptance by 20–30 pts; rejection-skew causes no matching drop.
- [Structure Over Scale: Rethinking Adaptation for RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27148) — LoRA "rank collapse" under GRPO (73% → 5% when going r=64→128); DoRA/QuanTA stay stable; spectral analysis of update geometry.
- [CreditFilter: Calibrated Token Weights over the Within-Response Entropy Rank for RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27227) — "forking value" credit assignment; token forking probability decays exponentially with entropy rank; large gains over DAPO.
- [Gumbel Substitution: Removing Sampler Drift from RL Without Trusting the Sampler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27375) — shared public Gumbel noise makes off-policy drift vanish without sampler-reported probabilities.
- [Memorization and Generalization in Generative Models: An Associative-Memory Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26797) — thesis: diffusion models as associative memories; spurious attractors mark the memorization→generalization transition.
- [Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation of RL Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27376) — distill each RL expert into a soft prompt on a frozen base; prompts compose by concatenation, matching multi-teacher distillation.
- [Don't Discard Your Rollouts: Reusing Teacher RL Traces for Student Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26803) — pass-rate ordering + teacher log-likelihood selection makes saved GRPO rollouts as good as fresh distillation data.
- [PRISM: Validation-Free Online Batch Selection for LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27578) — gradient-geometry (preconditioned ghost inner products) data selection with submodular guarantees.
- [DistDNA: Language Model Fingerprinting under Stochastic Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27831) — identify deployed checkpoints via promptwise MMD over response distributions.
- [Backbone-Specific LoRA Geometry: Orthogonality, Superposition, and Provenance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27201) — LoRA update directions act as backbone signatures; adapters remain detectable inside sums.
- [Run One, Get Two: Near-Free Streaming Safety Checks for Local LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26975) — guard model runs as an extra row in the decode step, matching dedicated guards at <4% latency.
- [Potential emergence in LLM agents: a statistical physics perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27618) — tracks emergence of a global potential over agent states during post-training.
- [ORION: Exact Canonical-State Supervision for Systematic Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27087) — auxiliary supervision of execution states fixes depth extrapolation; RoPE fixes length; effects add.
- [The Optimal Sample Complexity of Agnostic PAC Learning for Partial Concept Classes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27946) — matches the Alon et al. lower bound, O((d+log 1/δ)/ε²).
- [Search From the Start: Search-Native Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26809) — pretraining with interleaved search calls; advantages persist after SFT (1.4B ≈ FLOP-matched 3.3B).
