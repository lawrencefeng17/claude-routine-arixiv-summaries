# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-20

Source: [cs.LG new listings for Fri, 20 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new) (119 new submissions + 94 cross-lists; replacements excluded). Summaries are based on the abstracts.

A strong day for post-training science. Several papers look at what RL actually changes: which judge biases carry over, when rare successes get reinforced, steering-vector equivalence, and budget caps. There is also solid interpretability work.

## Top 10

### 1. [Blind Spots Travel, Knowledge Stays Home: What RL Inherits from an LLM Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25191)
- 814 RL runs train 7–8B models to answer closed-book questions or abstain, with an LLM judge as the reward. The question is what transfers from the judge to the policy.
- The judge's **knowledge about individual answers does not generalize**. A wrong answer the judge rewards is memorized only for that question. Even an answer key or a much stronger judge selects which questions to answer barely better than an untrained model abstaining when its own samples disagree.
- **The judge's beliefs about a category do generalize.** When a judge rejects 11 of 12 correct "capitals of former states" answers, the model learns to refuse 98% of that category on new questions, even though it still knows 94% of the answers. Hand-flipping just 12 verdicts moves refusal from 5% to 90%. Aggregate accuracy and abstention metrics hide this.
- A label-free self-consistency reward (majority vote over the model's own samples) matches the answer key's reward and keeps the category intact.

### 2. [RL Can Only Reinforce What It Sees Succeed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26084)
- Controlled setup: small LMs (1.3M–85M parameters) are pretrained on a Bayesian player's games, so the prior and the correct answer are known exactly. RL is then applied after a familiar rule stops working.
- RL turns the base model's hedging into firm decisions, in whichever direction the reward favors.
- When the correct action is rare, each run either learns it everywhere or not at all. One quantity, computed before training, predicts which runs take off: **the expected number of GRPO groups in which the action wins**. About 35 such groups gives even odds.
- The threshold moves with learning rate, optimizer, GRPO variant and model size, but the predictive count stays the same.

### 3. [RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627)
- **A fixed set of input-independent steering vectors** added to block outputs of the frozen base model (65x fewer parameters than the RL LoRA) is fit to match public RL-tuned checkpoints, with no reward in the objective.
- These vectors recover **90–96% of the reward gains** across six RL-tuned image models, and 90% and 112% on two video models.
- Trained directly with online RL from zero, the vectors beat weight-based RL on OCR and PickScore.
- Takeaway: most of what RL post-training does here is a constant shift toward behavior the base model can already produce, not new computation. This fits the "RL elicits rather than teaches" view.

### 4. [Represented but misread: Post-training changes how language models read out what they represent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26584)
- Compares linear probes with the models' own answers, using signal detection theory, across six base/post-trained model pairs.
- On human-vs-AI text judgments, probes separate the classes well but the answers do not and are often inverted. **Post-training lowers answer sensitivity** (Δd' ≈ −0.3) and makes models overreact to stated base rates.
- On the truth of negated statements the effect runs the other way: probes are near ceiling, and post-training *repairs* a readout that base models did not use.
- Checkpoint analysis places the change in SFT and at positions after the text. Adding 2k labeled examples to fine-tuning can set the readout's direction, for better or worse depending on domain.

### 5. [PosteriorBench: Evaluating Behavior Posterior Sampling in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25735)
- Formalizes **behavior posterior sampling**: efficiently drawing samples from a model's distribution conditioned on a rare behavior, which matters for studying rare unsafe behaviors.
- 21 tasks on Qwen3.5-0.8B with behavior probabilities from 1e-5 to 1e-1. Exact posterior samples come from 1B labeled responses, so methods can be scored on forward and reverse KL against compute cost.
- No method is both cheap and faithful. Prompting plateaus at high KL, fine-tuning on posterior samples costs too much, and several prior training methods do no better. The authors (Transluce) present it as an open problem.

### 6. [Budget Internalization: Fixed-Budget RLVR Trades Test-Time Scaling for In-Budget Accuracy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26172)
- The RLVR rollout cap becomes a **learned constraint**. Trained models rarely go past the training budget, beat the base model near the cap, and fall *below* it at larger budgets.
- The effect holds across Qwen3.5-4B, Gemma4-E2B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B, and transfers from math to science and coding.
- The cause is **length-censored post-training**, not RL as such. SFT on artificially truncated traces reproduces the effect, while SFT on naturally short traces does not. Forcing answers on truncated rollouts removes the effect, but also most of the gain.

### 7. [Attention Head Specialization: Phase Transitions in Sample Complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26228)
- In BERT, syntax-specialized heads appear as **dataset size** grows, not just with training time.
- In a solvable high-dimensional model of multi-head attention, specialization is a true **phase transition in sample complexity** (a non-analyticity in test error). Sometimes it comes as a cascade that recovers progressively weaker features, which acts as a statistical simplicity bias.
- The paper also separates statistical from dynamical causes of stagewise head emergence in online learning.

### 8. [A Statistical Framework for Training Data Auditing of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26264)
- One framework covers document-level membership inference, auditing an entire target collection, and identifying suspicious subsets.
- Chunk-level membership signals are aggregated with a **Cauchy combination**, so a few memorized passages are not diluted across a long document. The resulting calibrated p-values feed global tests and FDR-controlled subset selection.
- Under exchangeability it gives finite-sample validity and type-I/FDR guarantees. It is demonstrated on BookTection, Pile-arXiv, Cosmopedia and HealthCareMagic, covering both pretraining and fine-tuning audits.

### 9. [Erasing the Wrong Thing: Conditional Attribute Leakage and Concept Erasure in Shared Foundation-Model Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25640)
- Impossibility result: when task and sensitive labels are correlated, **global and task-conditional linear guardedness cannot both hold** unless the task signal is destroyed. Global erasure such as LEACE therefore leaves leakage *within* task strata.
- Derives a closed-form prediction of erasure's utility cost from pre-erasure statistics. The predictions track observed losses on 85 CelebA attribute pairs across 4 encoders and on chest X-ray data.
- Proposes **TC-LEACE** (per-stratum LEACE), which recovers most of the lost task utility while keeping conditional leakage controlled.

### 10. [Revealing Pathway Interactions in Network Computations with Complete Path Contribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26101)
- Decomposes a network's output into **mathematically complete contributions of individual input-to-output paths**. Partial sums give complete attributions for circuits at any granularity.
- An efficient top-k path search runs in roughly linear time in k rather than exponential in depth.
- Used to measure how excitatory and inhibitory pathways (for example, inhibitory vetoes) combine to produce decisions in image classifiers and in a mouse-retina model. This goes beyond pairwise circuit analysis.

## Honorable Mentions

- [Understanding MoE Decisions Beyond Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26075): an attribution method that accounts for downstream expert-routing switches, cutting error in predicting answer-score changes by 47–60% versus second-order gradient baselines.
- [RouteVerify: Routing Interventions for Step-Level Verification in MoE Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25780): step correctness can be read from router logits (AUROC 0.80–0.88), and routing swaps move verification judgments, but the movement rarely follows correctness. A cautionary result on reading causation from decodability.
- [Bradley-Terry Training Can Distort Outcome Probabilities of Initially Accurate Language World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25594): BT preference fitting moves 33–43pp of probability onto impossible outcomes. The objective's optimum predicts the distortion before training.
- [Separating Interpretability and Reconstruction in Sparse Autoencoders (DualSAE)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25165): adds a decorrelated residual branch to SAEs, giving about 29% better reconstruction and 34% better monosemanticity.
- [WeightCode: Installing New Capabilities into Neural Networks via Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25476): an LLM writes programs that edit network weights directly to install new capabilities without gradient training. The programs transfer across independently trained ViTs.
- [Watch Where You Linearize: Predicting and Correcting Model-Merging Interference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25000) and [Interference Bounds and Merge-Aware Training Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25430): task-vector interference predicted from receiver-local linearization or curvature-metric alignment.
- [A new algorithmic approach for finding flat minima: critically damped Polyak stepsizes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26475): an edge-of-stability stepsize rule that provably follows gradient descent on log-sharpness along the minimizer manifold, with a SAM-like flattening effect including in LLM fine-tuning.
- [Is a Description Worth a Thousand Demonstrations?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25560): a Bayesian ICL account of task descriptions as priors, measured as an "effective sample size". LLMs underuse specific descriptions unless reasoning is enabled.
- [Cross-Question Reliable Reinforcement Learning (CARE)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25292): a set-relative confidence reward for calibrated abstention. Coverage at 5% risk on math rises from 0.8% to 42.4%.
- [The Depth of a Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25970): the tuned-lens "resolution depth" of a word predicts human reading time beyond surprisal.
- [Does the Workspace Do the Work?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26018): a causal message-transplant protocol that distinguishes relaying from recombining in modular, workspace-style LMs.
