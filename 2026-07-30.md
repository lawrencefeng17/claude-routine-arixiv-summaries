## Top 10

### 1. Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains
**[2607.26654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26654)** · Desiree Cho, Cameron Tice, Bernie Hogan, Hunar Batra, Puria Radmard, Jun Zhao

- Tests whether inserting values-based content during *midtraining* (before SFT) — rather than bolting alignment on at the end — produces alignment that survives fine-tuning, using a 394M-token corpus built from Anthropic's Constitution at 120B scale.
- A 2×2 design (curriculum ordering × deliberative reasoning) is evaluated on alignment-under-pressure, value-conflict resolution, blackmail propensity, and emergent misalignment across post-midtraining, post-SFT, and post-benign-fine-tuning stages.
- Constitutional midtraining durably blunts SFT-induced blackmail propensity (advantage survives benign fine-tuning, -17.5pp) at no measurable capability cost — though the advantage fades in settings requiring active resistance to in-context pressure, showing durable vs. brittle alignment gains are mechanistically distinct.

### 2. ToxScreen: Detecting Whether an LLM Has Been Poisoned
**[2607.26849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26849)** · Anthony Hughes, Nicole Xing, Collin Francel, Andy Kim, Andrew Draganov

- Asks whether a defender with white-box weight access but no training data, trigger knowledge, or certainty of poisoning can recover a planted backdoor trigger — releasing ToxScreen, ~800 backdoored models spanning attack objectives, trigger types, and poisoning rates.
- Gradient-based prompt optimization fails to recover triggers, but a simple token look-up ranking candidates by attack-success rate succeeds wherever the backdoor is actually effective.
- Finds backdoors operate via mechanistically distinct weight-level strategies from jailbreaks — letting defenders filter jailbreaks out — and that a model's general jailbreakability is itself a useful anomaly signal even when the exact trigger can't be recovered.

### 3. Post-Training at the Edge of Detectability: A Game-Theoretic Approach to Fine-Tuning
**[2607.26358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26358)** · Keegan Harris, Brian W. Lee, Ian Waudby-Smith, Philip Amortila, Nika Haghtalab, Michael I. Jordan

- KL-regularized RL fine-tuning has no principled way to set its regularization coefficient; this paper reframes the choice as a sequential game between a policy-optimizing agent and a monitor testing for drift from a reference policy.
- Shows the resulting equilibrium policy is exactly the solution to a KL-regularized RL problem at an optimal coefficient — maximizing reward per unit of statistical distinguishability — solvable via concave-convex fractional programming.
- Validated on Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.2-1B, with a notable use case: auditing whether an API provider is covertly fine-tuning a served open-source model beyond a statistically detectable threshold.

### 4. Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies
**[2607.27191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27191)** · Peter Kirgis, Sayash Kapoor, Andrew Schwartz, Stephan Rabanser, David Africa, Konstantinos Voudouris

- Introduces "shadow evaluations": an agent takes on the actual open-ended research question of an unpublished NeurIPS submission, and the paper's real authors grade the output — sidestepping the narrow-task and noisy-peer-review limits of prior AI-R&D-automation benchmarks.
- Given six days and thousands of dollars of compute on two real papers, frontier agents completed all the engineering unaided but made no substantial progress on the actual research questions; both agent-produced papers were unambiguously rejected.
- Identifies five recurring failure modes — poor judgment about the publishability bar, uncreative responses to design flaws, weak backtracking, poor resource awareness, instruction drift — directly bearing on forecasts of AI-driven research-automation timelines.

### 5. On-Policy Distillation for LLM Safety: A Routing Approach to Template-Robust Realignment
**[2607.27081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27081)** · Yongjian Guo, Wanlun Ma, Lingyu Shen, Xi Xiao, Sheng Wen

- Targets a fine-tuning-as-attack-vector threat: adversaries poison downstream fine-tuning corpora so a model keeps its specialized skill but violates safety values on a triggering prompt template.
- Prior safety-realignment defenses collapse when the defender can't observe the attacker's exact prompt template, or cause catastrophic forgetting of the fine-tuned skill; this paper's ROPD instead distills against the *distributional divergence* between aligned and compromised outputs rather than fitting specific templates.
- Across four baselines, three datasets, and three base models, ROPD's robustness degrades only slightly under template mismatch versus severe collapse for existing methods, while better preserving downstream task performance.

### 6. Sky sphere representation in language models
**[2607.27092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27092)** · Aleksandr Berdnikov, Yevgeny Liokumovich

- Asks whether ~100B-parameter LLMs internally represent the night-sky map, and finds a decodable representation in most open-source models tested — one that even surfaces to top principal components when a prompt asks what's near a given celestial object.
- Leave-one-out testing shows the representation explains up to 65–85% of variance with median angular error of 12–21°, and the authors rule out that it's merely a leak from a correlated flat (non-curved) representation.
- Notable as, to the authors' knowledge, the first documented example of a curved, high-dimensional, irreducible feature manifold inside an LLM — a concrete data point for interpretability work on how models represent structured world knowledge beyond simple linear directions.

### 7. Same Evidence, Different Target: Decoding How Diagnostic Evidence Bears on Causal Questions from Language-Model States
**[2607.26929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26929)** · Weiyi Kong, Zhuoran Li

- Builds paired prompts that repeat identical diagnostic evidence verbatim while varying the causal question it's asked to bear on (different populations, outcomes, estimands, or assumptions), to separate genuine evidence-to-claim reasoning from surface pattern-matching.
- Linear probes on the final-token hidden state (penultimate transformer block) of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct recover 18–21 of 49 matched prompt pairs correctly, beating both logit-based classifiers and text baselines, and generalize to held-out diagnostic families.
- A clean interpretability result: whether evidence favors, challenges, or is simply irrelevant to a specific causal target is linearly decodable from hidden states, well above permutation-null baselines and matching human-reviewer agreement (96.9%).

### 8. Forecasting Trajectory-Level Safety Risks in Black-Box Multi-Turn Interactions
**[2607.26820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26820)** · Shi Lin, Peng Qian, Dinghao Liu, Renjie Sun, Sifan Wu, Dezhang Kong

- Argues LLM-agent safety needs to move past pointwise, turn-level violation detection since malicious intent can be decomposed across individually harmless turns and reconstructed gradually across a conversation.
- Recast retrieves risk-relevant evidence from both short-term dialogue progression and long-term history, models how risk configuration evolves over time, and uses a causal temporal encoder to predict *future* risk-emergence turns before violations occur.
- Across 7 risk categories, predicts 88.3% of eventual safety failures with an average 2.41-turn lead time and a 12.3% false-alarm rate — a concrete step toward preemptive rather than reactive multi-turn agent safeguards.

### 9. What Can Latent World Models Know? Physical Parameter Identifiability in Multimodal Predictive Representations
**[2607.27017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27017)** · Kaizhen Tan, Xin Xu, Siru Tao, Hanzhe Hong, Yang Feng, Heqing Du

- Asks which physical quantities a latent world model actually internalizes from predicting the future, using a certificate-gated protocol in a controlled environment (POKEWORLD) where visually identical objects hide different mass, drag, and contact stiffness.
- Finds two organizing mechanisms — input modality limits what *can* be known, while the prediction target decides what's *retained* — e.g., stiffness only enters the latent when touch is forecast, not merely when touch is fed as input; a vision-only latent even discards visible object state under single-step prediction.
- Drag is the frontier case: recoverable in principle (0.89 certificate) yet plateaus near 0.13 under every deterministic objective tested, while a supervised head on the same trunk reaches 0.45 — showing objective structure, not data volume, sets the ceiling on what a self-supervised representation generalizes to acquire.

### 10. ReCo: Reweighting GRPO Against Distributional Concentration
**[2607.26862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26862)** · Junoh Park, Junseo Hwang, Wonguk Cho, Taesup Kim

- Investigates why GRPO — the standard RL post-training method for reasoning LLMs — can shrink a base model's Pass@k at large k despite improving average performance, tracing it to the update concentrating on responses the base model already assigns high probability.
- Identifies two concentration mechanisms: response-level (high-probability outputs dominate the group gradient via repeated occurrence) and token-level (the importance ratio keeps reinforcing already-likely tokens), and proposes ReCo to reweight both away from this collapse.
- Across Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on five math-reasoning benchmarks, ReCo improves Pass@k at large k while matching GRPO at small k — direct evidence on how RL post-training dynamics narrow (or preserve) a model's reasoning-path diversity.
