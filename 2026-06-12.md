# arXiv cs.LG — Top Papers for June 12, 2026

*Interests: machine learning, language models, AI safety, alignment science, science of generalization, interpretability, training dynamics, emergent misalignment, data attribution.*

## Top 10

---

### 1. Beyond the Commitment Boundary: Probing Epiphenomenal Chain-of-Thought in Large Reasoning Models
**arXiv:2606.13603** | Daniel Scalena, Sara Candussio, Luca Bortolussi, Elisabetta Fersini, Malvina Nissim, Gabriele Sarti

- Uses early-exit probing to measure the causal influence of each CoT step on the final answer; finds that models exhibit a sharp "commitment boundary" — a single step where the answer probability locks in — after which all subsequent reasoning is **epiphenomenal** (causally inert).
- The commitment point typically arrives well before the reasoning trace concludes, and answer-formation states decoded via attention probes generalize to unseen tasks.
- Practical upshot: CoT length can be cut by up to **55% on average** with negligible accuracy loss by terminating after commitment.
- Has direct implications for interpretability: even elaborate-looking reasoning chains may not reflect the actual computation driving the output.

### 2. Select and Improve: Understanding the Mechanics of Post-Training for Reasoning
**arXiv:2606.13125** | Akshay Krishnamurthy, Audrey Huang, Nived Rajaraman

- Provides a mechanistic dissection of how RL post-training improves reasoning, identifying two separable processes: **strategy selection** (SFT data exposes diverse reasoning approaches, enabling the model to pick among them) and **strategy improvement** (harder RL data incrementally extends each strategy's reach).
- Controlled experiments on Qwen-2.5-1.5B with math tasks validate each mechanism independently.
- Offers concrete prescriptions: use diverse SFT to seed strategy variety; scale RL difficulty to drive improvement within strategies.
- Rare paper that gives a causal account of *why* RL helps rather than just *that* it helps.

---

### 3. Understanding Helpfulness and Harmless Tension in Reward Models
**arXiv:2606.13209** | Eshaan Tanwar, Pepa Atanasova

- Mechanistic analysis of how RLHF reward models handle the helpfulness–harmlessness tradeoff; finds that **multi-objective models underperform their single-objective counterparts** due to neuron-level interference.
- Maps individual neurons to specific alignment objectives via activation analysis and targeted ablation; shared neurons supporting both objectives are disproportionately responsible for the interference.
- Provides interpretable evidence for why naive multi-objective alignment is hard and motivates disentangled reward model architectures.

### 4. Demystifying Hidden-State Recurrence: Switchable Latent Reasoning with On-Policy Reinforcement Learning
**arXiv:2606.13106** | Jiayu Yang et al.

- Introduces SWITCH: explicit boundary tokens (`<swi>` / `</swi>`) that delimit latent computation blocks, making on-policy RL gradient flow well-defined and the latent reasoning directly probe-able.
- Mechanistic findings via causal intervention: (1) the switching signal is a **learned policy**, not a style artifact; (2) latent steps perform problem-specific computation; (3) that computation concentrates at a single hidden-state transition on entry.
- Outperforms hidden-state-recurrence baselines while recovering interpretability — a rare case where the interpretability constraint also improves training stability.

---

### 5. When Does Routing Become Interpretable? Causal Probes on Block Attention Residuals
**arXiv:2606.13168** | Aydin Javadov

- Tests whether making cross-layer routing weights architecturally visible (Block Attention Residuals replacing fixed additive skip connections) is sufficient for mechanistic interpretation; answer: **necessary but not sufficient**.
- Trained routing produces three distinguishable motifs (embedding-source, current-state, older-history pathways) that emerge only when routing participates in optimization.
- Critical dissociation: the **highest-weight routing slice is not the largest causal contributor**, so routing descriptors must be treated as hypotheses and validated via causal ablation — not read off directly.

### 6. Different Layers, Different Manifolds: Module-Wise Weight-Space Geometry in Transformer Optimization
**arXiv:2606.13276** | Kirato Yoshihara

- Studies whether different transformer modules benefit from different geometric constraints during optimization (Manifold Muon for GPT-2 pretraining, comparing Stiefel vs. DGram constraints across attention and MLP blocks).
- Key finding: **Stiefel geometry for attention + DGram for MLP** outperforms uniform-constraint baselines; uniform DGram on attention causes singular value growth → softmax saturation.
- Argues that symmetry-aware and geometry-aware optimization should be **module-specific**, not uniform across the network.
- Relevant to understanding why different parts of transformers behave differently during training.

### 7. Authority, Truth, and Citation Bias: A Large-Scale Multi-Domain Benchmark for Studying Epistemic Susceptibility in Large Language Models
**arXiv:2606.13104** | Aryan Khurana, Aravind Ramana RN, Dhruv Kumar

- Introduces **AuthorityBench** (220k+ prompts) using a balanced 2×2 factorial design varying claim accuracy × citation accuracy independently across general knowledge, science, law, and medicine.
- Headline finding: **citation presence — whether real or fabricated — consistently increases hallucination rates** by 3–22 percentage points; fabricated citations accompanying true claims are the most harmful condition.
- Effect is domain-dependent: legal claims are more robust to citation manipulation; general knowledge is most susceptible (up to 77% hallucination rate under fabricated citations).
- Directly probes a form of sycophancy / authority bias relevant to alignment and safety evaluation.

### 8. The Geometry of Phase Transitions in Generative Dynamics via Projection Caustics
**arXiv:2606.13191** | Ryosuke Sakamoto, Kotaro Sakamoto

- Provides a geometric account of why diffusion and flow-matching models exhibit sharp qualitative transitions during sampling: such transitions occur near **projection caustics** — loci where unique nearest-point projections onto the data support break down.
- Derives denoising as gradient descent on a free energy landscape; caustic geometry predicts where score directions become unstable.
- Introduces the **Critical Boundary Detector (CBD)**, which localizes mode-commitment points and predicts intervention-sensitive windows across toy models, standard diffusion models, and latent text-to-image systems.
- Provides a principled, geometry-grounded language for discussing "phase transitions" and emergent structure in generative model dynamics.

---

### 9. Detecting Explanatory Insufficiency in Learned Representations: A Framework for Representational Vigilance
**arXiv:2606.13172** | Jacques Raynal, Pierre Slangen, Elsa Raynal, Jacques Margerit

- Introduces **VER** (Vigilant Evaluator of Representations), a five-step diagnostic framework for detecting when a model's learned representations fail to capture the structure needed to explain its task — distinct from ordinary prediction error, noise, or distribution shift.
- Steps: representation identification → explanatory-domain delimitation → residual-structure detection → explanatory-resistance evaluation → vigilance signaling.
- Positions representational adequacy as an explicit object of inquiry, complementing accuracy and robustness metrics.
- Relevant to interpretability and generalization: a model can be accurate yet build its predictions on representations that would fail systematically in new contexts.

---

### 10. Getting Better at Working With You: Compiling User Corrections into Runtime Enforcement for Coding Agents
**arXiv:2606.13174** | Yujun Zhou et al.

- Identifies a failure mode in interactive agents: even advanced memory systems (Mem0) leave **57.5% of applicable user preference checks violated** across sessions.
- Introduces **TRACE** (Test-time Rule Acquisition and Compiled Enforcement): mines user corrections, rewrites them as atomic executable rules, and enforces them before task completion — complementing memory with runtime verification.
- On ClawArena tasks: reduces violations from 100% to **37.6% (in-distribution)** and **2.0% (out-of-distribution)**; maintains task completion rate.
- Relevant to agent alignment: illustrates the gap between a model "knowing" a preference and reliably acting on it.

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.13221** — *From Uncertain Judgments to Calibrated Rankings: Conformal Elo Estimation for LLM Evaluation* (Kargi, Salinas): Applies conformal prediction to Elo-style LLM comparisons, producing calibrated uncertainty intervals over model rankings rather than point estimates; addresses the known sensitivity of Elo to annotation noise in head-to-head evaluations.

- **arXiv:2606.13105** — *Disparate Impact in Synthetic Data Generation* (Andrey et al.): Shows that generative models trained on biased datasets amplify existing demographic disparities in their outputs; formalizes disparate impact metrics for synthetic data and analyzes how the data-generation process can serve as a source of compounding bias — relevant to data attribution and fairness in training pipelines.
