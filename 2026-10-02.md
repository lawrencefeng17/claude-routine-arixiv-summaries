# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-02

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics. (Honorable mentions are chosen from titles/short descriptions only; the top 10 were read at the abstract level. Only the first ~190 entries of the listing were scanned.)

### 1. [On Language Drift during RLVR Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02015)

- Proves that RLVR optimization pressure permits unbounded language drift in chains of thought, whereas SFT does not.
- Empirically, drift arises when RLVR targets novel reasoning tasks whose behavior can't be drawn out of the base model.
- Proves language drift can't be constrained without constraining expected reward.
- Implication: CoT monitorability may not be improvable without hurting frontier RLVR performance.

### 2. [Misalignment of Low-Loss Regions Causes Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00620)

- Mode-connectivity framework: grokking arises when low-loss regions induced by the train and validation partitions are misaligned.
- Finds a stable "anti-grokking" case under a symmetry-preserving train/validation split in modular arithmetic, which challenges several correlational explanations.
- Once regions align, hyperparameters alone can't produce grokking; dynamics collapse to trainable or non-trainable.

### 3. [Pre-training interventions, ex post facto: Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00767)

- Synthetic document fine-tuning (SDF) on a post-trained model causes "reality drift" (fabricated unrelated entities treated as real) and capability loss.
- Grafting: train the SDF adapter on the pre-trained checkpoint, then add the weight update to the post-trained model.
- Installs beliefs as strongly as SDF while cutting reality drift and preference-coherence loss by more than half; tested on false facts, misaligned model organisms, and a constitutional intervention up to 284B parameters.
- Lets researchers iterate on pre-training interventions with a single fine-tuning run.

### 4. [Finetuning with Sampling: SFT Learns Better Than You Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02140)

- Instead of changing the objective, uses MCMC sampling to progressively make off-policy expert traces more on-policy for the learner.
- SFT on the reshaped data rivals prevailing post-training methods, often generalizing better and forgetting less than strong on-policy baselines.
- Models learn beyond merely sharpening the base distribution.

### 5. [Every Ablation Is a Dose: Counterweights and the Semblance of Self-Repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02173)

- Argues self-repair after ablation has one explanation: a pre-existing gain, with ablations as uncalibrated points on a signed-strength axis λ.
- Repair response of a unit follows an affine law E_r(λ)=own_r+γ_rλ; 68 of 81 downstream directions fit across Gemma, Qwen, LLaMA, Mistral.
- On GPT-2 Small IOI, 7 of 10 reachable heads follow the law, all counterweights; γ is predictable from fixed weights.

### 6. [Are We Recovering Mechanisms? Objective-Level Recovery Gaps in Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02098)

- Intervention-defined faithfulness can prefer a circuit that reproduces model behavior less well, an "objective-level recovery gap."
- KL misranks 9.4%–41.2% of candidate circuit pairs across EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, Edge-SP.
- Cause: context distortion from replacing excluded signals; restoring them from the intact execution repairs 96 of 100 persistent misrankings.
- Better discovery alone is insufficient if the objective rewards the wrong candidate.

### 7. [Beyond Linear Concepts: Discovering and Aligning Non-Linear Concept Manifolds in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01821)

- Models concepts as low-dimensional non-linear manifolds in token activations; introduces a concept-based alignment (CBA) score across layers/models.
- Finds block structure in intermediate and late layers hidden from linear metrics; concept composition is syntax-dominated until late layers.
- Multilingual (English–Mandarin) concept sharing is training-dependent: strong in Qwen, weaker in Llama, absent in GPT-2.
- Across Tulu-3 stages, the largest shift is base→SFT; DPO and RLVR leave early layers largely unchanged.

### 8. [From Gradients to Capabilities: Understanding Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02179)

- Studies gradients and optimizer updates when distilling four RL-trained domain teachers into Qwen3-1.7B.
- Loss averaging implicitly weights responses (token averaging favors long ones); Adam's first moment shrinks teacher differences (cosine 0.83 between teachers).
- BF16 rounding hides small changes: ~97% of FP32 master weights move but only 7–11% of BF16 weights.
- Top-64 KL gradient approximates the full-vocab one, but its effect on math accuracy flips sign depending on averaging.

### 9. [A Safe Prototype Is Not a Safety Direction: Reference Dependence in Response-Safety Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01801)

- Audits scoring safety by cosine similarity to the mean embedding of safe responses (a proposed sleeper-agent detector).
- The safe prototype gets ROC-AUC 0.457–0.545 on human-labeled corpora (sometimes below chance); an explicit safe-minus-unsafe reference gets 0.588–0.738.
- A class mean is a location, not a safety direction; a reference with enough unsafe examples is needed to orient it.

### 10. [TrueMuse: A Benchmark for Data Attribution in Text-to-Music Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00835)

- Controlled benchmark with ground-truth attribution: 133 attributes, 648 fine-tuned models, 95,456 generated samples across melody, timbre, artist, and genre.
- Black-box attribution methods vary substantially across settings; attribution remains hard and needs more dependable methods.
