# arXiv cs.LG Daily Digest — August 20, 2026

Top 10 papers from today's [cs.LG listing](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent) (169 new submissions), selected for relevance to machine learning, language models, AI safety/alignment, science of generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics.

## Top 10

### 1. [Learned, Then Lost: A Measured Single-Example Counterfactual in Pre-training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19168)

- Data attribution done the expensive, honest way: rather than *estimating* one training example's contribution, the authors ran the actual counterfactual 24 times — 32 GPT-2 124M models trained from scratch on OpenWebText across four conditions × eight seeds, differing in one row of one batch.
- At step 200 of 9,536 they swapped a single row of a 256-row batch for a 194-token injected passage, in three variants: fluent prose with a corpus-attested subject, fluent prose with a fabricated subject gradient-matched to within 0.14%, and random keyboard characters.
- The passage is **learned from one exposure and then decays**: 50 steps later the injected arm predicts it better by 0.039–0.044 nats at 8/8 seeds (p < 10⁻⁴); by the final step the difference is undetectable (p = 0.25, 0.71) against minimum detectable effects of 0.025/0.079 nats.
- Every geometric probe comes back null after the decay — pre-registered interpolation loss barrier +0.0068 (p = 0.509), held-out CE −0.00044, per-layer CKA separating nothing at any layer.
- The one signal that survives: weight displacement reaches 44.1% of seed-to-seed Euclidean distance (92% settled by mid-training) while the loss barrier reaches only 3.0% of the seed-to-seed barrier — a ~15× gap, and a lower bound. Their reading: the injection **relocates the model within its basin without moving it out**.
- Directly relevant to anyone reasoning about influence functions, single-example memorization, or what a datapoint "does" to a finished model.

### 2. [Mechanistic Interpretability of Structure-Aware Numerical Reasoning in LLaMA 3.1 8B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18419)

- From Bau's group (Northeastern): reverse-engineers *how* an LLM does numerical sequence modeling, rather than just showing that it can.
- Constructs a task that is unsolvable without picking up structure — sample n random numbers, then repeat them at an offset — so success implies the model tracks the sequence's generative structure.
- **Probing** finds the model computes and stores first differences in its internal representations with no explicit supervision. **Activation patching** shows it retrieves the relevant first difference via an induction-circuit-like mechanism and then adds it to the current value.
- Claimed as one of the first identifications of this form of *concept induction* — induction over a computed quantity rather than over literal tokens.

### 3. [The Road Taken: The Role of Optimizers at the Edge of Stability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18415)

- Training-dynamics result that reopens a settled-looking story: many first-order methods, gradient descent included, violate the standard edge-of-stability bound by as much as **21.1×**.
- Critically, the violation is *systematic and optimizer-dependent* — existing formulations, which are stated in terms of max Hessian eigenvalue and learning rate, cannot express it.
- Proposes a new threshold derived from the **directional** Hessian and a gradient-alignment score with respect to the update the optimizer actually takes, rather than the maximum curvature mode.
- The reformulated "realized edge of stability" removes the optimizer-dependent offsets, predicts thresholds more consistently, and yields diagnostics showing how optimizers actively trade off temporal vs. spatial budgets during optimization.

### 4. [Task-Conditioned Least-Privilege Learning for Executable Terminal and MCP Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18351)

- Tackles *excess-authority errors*: a tool-using agent completing its task while exercising authority the user never granted and the task never needed — a failure mode distinct from task failure and not fully covered by permission gating.
- Audits every action twice (pre-execution and from observed effects) along six risk dimensions using deterministic verifiers scoring completion, evidence, exact state, prohibited attempts, and safe success; combined with per-task sufficient-authority envelopes, this yields a trajectory-level excess-privilege value to optimize against in post-training.
- Post-training Qwen3.5-4B on 1,500 tasks lifts safe success from **64.36% → 98.48%** across 2,896 held-out episodes and drops excess-authority events from 4.56% → 0.79%; a 400-task continuation shows generalization (−6.99 pp) with capability retained.
- Notably honest framing: learned restraint is an *additional* control layer, explicitly not a replacement for permission gates and sandboxing.

### 5. [Rethinking Privileged Information in On-Policy Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18271)

- Asks the subliminal-learning-adjacent question about on-policy self-distillation (OPSD): when a student improves from a teacher conditioned on privileged reference information, did it actually learn *the information*, or just recover reasoning behavior already latent in the base model?
- Experiments on Qwen3 1.7B–8B across science and math, with an analysis framework separating reference-induced supervision from the teacher's no-reference supervision and measuring how each aligns with changes in student predictions.
- Findings are strikingly deflationary: the correct reference gives no consistent benefit across teacher generation modes, model sizes, or datasets; students improve *without* it; and **a solution from a different problem can outperform the correct solution** on several math benchmarks.
- Student predictions align more with the base model's thinking behavior than with reference-induced supervision — but controls built from other problems reproduce much of both alignments, and stronger reference-attributable alignment doesn't reliably track larger benefit.
- Conclusion worth internalizing: performance gains and distributional alignment alone **cannot** establish what a distilled student learned from privileged information.

### 6. [Forgetting, plasticity, and co-observation: a third facet of continual learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18803)

- Argues that catastrophic forgetting and loss of plasticity — the field's two canonical obstacles — together still fail to explain the gap between naive sequential training and offline joint training.
- Isolates **data co-observation** as a distinct third factor by decoupling separate-data-access constraints from stability and plasticity, then measuring the residual gap while mitigating forgetting and controlling plasticity.
- The joint-vs-separate gap persists across supervised and self-supervised paradigms in generic data-incremental "chunking" settings, and does **not** require any distribution shift — so it's a statement about generalization, not just retention.
- Recontextualizes existing methods: distillation approaches act purely as knowledge retention, whereas replay's empirical success partly comes from reintroducing co-observation benefits.

### 7. [Evaluating and Explaining Prompt Sensitivity of LLMs Using Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18539)

- Moves prompt-sensitivity analysis from output-level comparison to a fine-grained internal account by decomposing the model's output score into a set of *interactions*, each a nonlinear relationship over a subset of input variables.
- Key observation: subtle prompt changes can cause severe instability in the interaction structure **even when the output is unchanged** — i.e., output-level metrics systematically understate the instability.
- Introduces the Interaction-based Prompt Sensitivity (IPS) metric and applies it across **50 open-source LLMs**, finding four factors that reduce sensitivity: SFT, larger scale, dense (vs. sparse) architectures, and few-shot prompting.
- Best part is the unifying mechanism: all four factors reduce sensitivity specifically of **low-order** interactions (those involving few input variables).

### 8. [Continual Reasoning Gym: Diagnosing and Harnessing Shared Reasoning in Continual RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18574)

- Studies continual RLVR — updating a reasoning model as each new task arrives, instead of rerunning expensive multitask RLVR (MTRL) — via a new environment organizing text and visual reasoning tasks into five task sequences.
- Two observations in tension: sequential RLVR shows only **modest forgetting**, yet still lands below MTRL. Decomposing final performance shows forgetting explains only part of the gap.
- The explanation for the mild forgetting is **shared reasoning**: transferable reasoning structure means training on one task supports others on average — a nice piece of evidence about what RLVR post-training actually installs.
- Continual Prompt Replay (CPR) exploits this by replaying earlier prompts and regenerating responses with the *current* policy; only CPR reaches MTRL-level performance on average.

### 9. [Think Shallow, Solve Deep: Controlling Recurrent Dynamics for Reliable Test-Time Depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18222)

- Recurrent-depth reasoners can improve, preserve, *or degrade* an answer with more test-time iterations; this paper shows the trained operator's finite-time dynamical regime (settling / marginal / drifting) predicts which happens.
- Gives a sufficient condition for **depth-safety**: once per-step displacement is small relative to the decoder margin, further iteration cannot change the decoded answer.
- Empirically, settling operators never degrade with added depth and sometimes convert it into out-of-distribution gains — Sudoku accuracy 0.19 → 0.34 *past the training horizon*, from only 800 unaugmented examples per difficulty tier.
- A single terminal fixed-point objective moves the regime and the depth behavior together: remove it and you get drift and lose the gains; add it to a generic recurrence and you get depth-safe extrapolation on carry propagation. Sanity-checked against Huginn-3.5B, which lands in the non-settling family.

### 10. [What is Missing from AI Post-Training AI: An Empirical Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19072)

- Separates two capabilities that "AI-for-AI" claims routinely conflate: **execution-level** (iterating within a chosen training strategy) and **strategy-level** (revising high-level judgment as evidence accumulates).
- Analysis of a large corpus of released post-training trajectories finds agents lock in their training strategy at the very start and spend the entire remaining budget on local adjustments inside it.
- Tests three explanations with escalating interventions: an experience-driven scaffold improves execution substantially (+12.6 on GSM8K, +40.8 on HumanEval) but leaves strategy static; human guidance redirects the *initial* strategy but the agent falls back into local loops once training begins; extra inference compute helps only on easier tasks.
- Conclusion: the missing ingredient is neither experience, guidance, nor reasoning compute, but a mechanism for **spontaneously reevaluating strategy mid-execution** — a useful concrete bound on current recursive-self-improvement claims.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Compress and Forget: bitsandbytes Quantization Amplifies Proactive Interference in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18578)** — INT4 quantization significantly worsens retrieval of repeatedly overwritten values across Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Phi-3.5-mini (e.g. 81.0% → 68.3% for Qwen, McNemar p ≤ 2.6×10⁻⁶); INT8, usually assumed safe, carries a smaller real penalty in 2/3 models. The effect is specific to semantically similar distractors, reverses under a numeric control, is mechanistically linked to same-key intrusions, and an ablation localizes it to the quantized backbone rather than the output projection — all while aggregate benchmark accuracy looks fine.
- **[To Go Far, Go Together: Diverse Preferences Induce a Curriculum for Reward Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18770)** — Argues alignment fairness work stops one step short: accurate per-user reward models aren't enough, because users whose rewards are *hard to optimize* become a newly underserved group. CurriPO grows a tree-structured curriculum over diverse user reward models, reaching 1.2–2.1× the population satisfaction of the strongest baseline with less training time.
- **[Preference Reasoning under Indeterminacy in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18631)** — Formalizes epistemic indeterminacy (incomplete/partial preferences) and structural indeterminacy (no solution exists under standard social choice concepts); SOTA models systematically fail to distinguish determined from undetermined instances and are miscalibrated even when merely verifying.
- **[Lost in Aggregation: How Benchmarks Overlook Irreplaceable Model Strengths](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18919)** — Proposes evaluating against a data-centric *peak performance frontier* (irreplaceable / sufficient / redundant / fallible). On TabArena, standard aggregation metrics are highly correlated with each other and mostly measure consistency and failure-avoidance, rewarding never-best generalists and penalizing models with unique dataset-specific strengths.
- **[SingularClip: Preventing Spectral Collapse to Maintain Plasticity in Continual and Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18319)** — Identifies growing anisotropy of weight-matrix singular values as a novel source of plasticity loss (empirically and theoretically), and mitigates it with periodic singular-value clipping.
- **[Infrared Universality of Collective Dynamics across Transformer and State-Space Architectures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18592)** — Tests whether the near-marginal long-memory time-scale spectrum found in Transformers also emerges in Mamba's selective state-space dynamics. Microscopic spectra differ substantially, yet full blocks develop a reproducible slow-mode continuum, ρ(λ) ~ λ^β with β_M ≈ −0.17 vs β_Tr ≈ −0.1 — both close to the marginal 1/t memory regime.
- **[Position: Current Model Cards Are Insufficient for Downstream Governance of Open-Weight Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18086)** — Analysis of 500 Hugging Face model cards; argues governance needs model cards + acceptable use policies + licenses as integrated safety artifacts, identifies gaps around model heritage and alignment provenance, and argues standard open-source licenses may weaken AUP enforceability.
- **[Allocating Recurrent Compute in Looped Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18230)** — Asks "what should loop?" rather than looping the whole stack. Introduces Iterative Transport Rank to characterize when an extra application adds a non-redundant cross-position influence direction; MixerLoop (repeat the mixer, apply the dense FFN once) beats FullLoop on aggregate CORE at 15M and cuts recurrent projection FLOPs 45.9%. Pairs well with **[Looped Language Models Improve Compositional Tool Calling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18171)**.
- **[Bidirectional representational alignment between biological and artificial neural networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18244)** — Attacks the known asymmetry (models predict neural responses far better than the reverse) by steering representational geometry with spectral regularization during training: 55% relative improvement in bidirectional predictivity, with forward/reverse becoming approximately symmetric at intermediate spectral exponents.
- **[Beyond Teacher Likelihood: Group-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation for Long-Context Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19181)** and **[Open-MOPD: Diagnosing and Fixing Capability Imbalance in Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19098)** — Two on-policy-distillation dynamics papers. The first diagnoses growing teacher–verifier disagreement at long context and corrects it with a signed group-normalized residual (Qwen3-8B: 35.1 → 44.7 across five benchmarks). The second builds a controlled multi-teacher benchmark with oracle routing and finds standard M-OPD captures only 35.6% of available headroom.
- **[Many Optimizers But Only One Training Path: Repeated Resampling for Adaptive Optimizer Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18810)** — Treats optimizer choice as something that should change *during* training: every b epochs, candidates scout from current weights for s epochs and the winner continues. Matches exhaustive fixed-optimizer search at 24–35% of the aggregate training cost.
- **[Temporal Multi-Signal Fusion for Token-Level Hallucination Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18115)** — Treats hallucination as a temporally extended span rather than per-token, fusing text statistics, NLI entailment, and surprisal with no model internals; 0.840 AUC on RAGTruth. A controlled decomposition attributes most of the gain to temporal *order*, and the same ~0.845 ceiling recurs across recurrent, Mamba, and attention detectors — locating the bottleneck in features, not capacity.
- **[FraudBench: Stress-Testing Policy-Grounded Banking Agents Against Adaptive Fraud](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18136)** — Dual-control executable benchmark where safety is *history-dependent*: a later, locally valid request becomes unsafe because of an earlier probe, admission, or failed attempt. Four agents score 49–65% attack-security, with money-mule and first-party fraud the common weaknesses.
- **[What Makes Software Issue Resolution Tasks Difficult for Agents?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18280)** — Task difficulty is substantially predictable from static properties (AUC = 0.863), driven mainly by patch fragmentation and repository scale, with prompt linguistic features mattering in the mid-difficulty band — enabling difficulty-controlled benchmark construction.
- **[Cacheable by Design? Training MoE Routers for Locality Against the Edge Memory-Bandwidth Wall](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18261)** — A **pre-registered negative result**, which is unusual and welcome. Also contributes real routing telemetry on Qwen3-30B: adjacent-token expert reuse at 2.0× chance, 95% of traffic through 52.5% of experts, and an LRU cache of 13.4% of experts serving 66% of requests.
- **[From Storage to Access: Verifiable Activation of Parametric Knowledge in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18581)** — Two-stage RL that disentangles knowledge elicitation from reasoning; >80% of inserted bridging triples carry factual knowledge not derivable from retrieved context and over half elicit knowledge inaccessible via direct prompting.
