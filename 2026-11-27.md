# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-27

Note: arXiv did not post a new announcement today (US Thanksgiving holiday), so the cs.LG listing still shows **Thu, 26 Nov 2026** (143 new + 128 cross-lists). The picks below come from that listing and **leave out the 24 papers already in the 2026-11-26 digest**. Summaries are based on the abstracts. Today's picks lean toward interpretability methodology (selective neurons vs. causally used neurons, representation-similarity error bars), careful ablation science on looped MoEs, a verifier that catches reward-hacked solutions, and what initialization geometry does to optimization.

## Top 10

### 1. [Agent-as-a-Verifier: From Scores to Evidence for General Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34132)
- Replaces scalar reward-model scores with an **auditable certificate**. Role-separated LLM agents follow a claim → obligation → certificate protocol inspired by formal methods.
- Builds a *verdict-blind* dependency graph of claims, compiles the task's acceptance contract separately, and derives checkable obligations, using tools to check facts.
- Beats the prior state of the art on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and improves agents on FrontierMath and CyberGym.
- Safety-relevant: it **flags reward-hacking solutions that fool the benchmark's own evaluator**. That makes it a candidate building block for scalable oversight in recursive or self-improving loops.

### 2. [Interpreting Speckle Effects Through Neuron Interventions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33632)
- An interpretability case study on a SAR-pretrained MAE (SARMAE). Synthetic scenes with known ground truth allow exact measurement of how well the scene is recovered.
- It finds 38 **speckle-selective neurons** that track the noise distribution, but restoring them to their clean values does **not** improve reconstruction.
- A *different* set of 64 neurons in the first MLP recovers 44% of the lost correlation. 96.5% of that gain survives when the selective neurons are held at their noisy values.
- The general lesson is that neurons that are selective for a feature ≠ neurons the model causally uses. This is a clean cautionary example for feature-attribution-by-selectivity.

### 3. [LoopTitan: What Does Parameter Revisit Buy a Mixture-of-Experts?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34821)
- With matched non-revisit controls, it finds that **reusing layer parameters across loops buys almost nothing by itself**. The reported gains of looped MoEs come from network shape (more layers, fewer experts each) and the residual scale.
- Proves that the residual scale acts as a **lower learning rate on revisited layers**. This helps under AdamW's uneven per-layer step sizes and vanishes under **Muon**, which keeps them nearly equal. It is a nice optimizer × architecture interaction.
- With extra compute at fixed parameters, a deeper standard MoE recovers 90% of the gain from one extra loop.
- Good research hygiene: pre-registered predictions, the full exploration history public as GitHub issues, and 17 released checkpoints.
- Related: [Mixture-of-Loops Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923) and [Scaling Looped MoE LMs under Matched Budgets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34276) both report looped-model gains, which LoopTitan suggests deserve controlled re-examination.

### 4. [Spec2Weights: Certifying Networks that a Language Model Writes from a Specification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34711)
- An LLM writes the weights of small MLPs, CNNs, RNNs and GNNs directly from a plain-language spec, with no data and no gradients. The authors then **prove the networks exact in float32** for any summation order, using enumeration, SMT, BFS and induction over recurrences.
- 25/30 first submissions from Claude Opus 5.5 certify as exact. An open 27B model passes 23/30.
- Learnability side results: input coordinates never active in training can't be learned from more in-range data, and Luhn-type checksums have **exponentially small gradient variance**. Tuned gradient descent agrees with both.
- Relevant to "compiled" networks as interpretability ground truth and to where gradient-based learning fundamentally fails.

### 5. [Transferring Architecture as an Optimization Prior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34816)
- Treats the network's **initial representational geometry** as an optimization prior that fixes which examples count as close and which features gradient descent refines first.
- Before training, it aligns a target network to the representational geometry of a *randomly initialized* guide network of a different architecture. No labels are used and no learned features are transferred.
- Shallow nets aligned to deep guides gain depth's optimization benefits, and fine-resolution guides transfer representational granularity to coarser networks.
- This is a training-dynamics result showing that some of an architecture's inductive bias lives in its init geometry and can be transplanted.

### 6. [Confidence Scores for Gated Automation of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33394)
- A **linear probe on the frozen LLM's final hidden state** predicts answer correctness. Confident answers are automated and uncertain ones go to human review.
- It beats prompted confidence and text-only metamodels and automates the largest share of the workload at high accuracy targets.
- Key observation: **prompted confidence can look calibrated yet lack the resolution needed for thresholding**.
- Caveats: a single 2B backbone, data that is mostly SQuAD, and only modest transfer to unseen datasets.

### 7. [Centered Kernel Distance: Dimension-Free Error Bounds for Comparing Neural Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33447)
- Introduces CKD, the metric counterpart of CKA/RSA, which has an **unbiased estimator**. CKA has none.
- Estimation error decays as **1/M + 1/N** (conditions, neurons), independent of the dimensionality of the condition space, so it avoids the curse of dimensionality.
- Validated on random and trained networks and on macaque visual cortex data. It gives practical guidance on how many stimuli and units are needed when comparing models or layers.

### 8. [Understanding Mode Collapse in Diffusion Models under Classifier-Free Guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33719)
- Attributes CFG's effects to a **capacity mismatch**: the conditional distribution is richer than the unconditional one.
- Solvable model: the conditional is a Gaussian mixture with means on an ellipsoid, and the unconditional is a single Gaussian. Closed-form guided distributions follow for all noise levels and guidance scales.
- CFG causes (a) a shift along a mean axis and (b) persistent skew toward and concentration around a singular point, which explains the loss of diversity. Predictions are checked on large pretrained image models.

### 9. [Change Partners at Every Layer: Balanced Random Regrouping for Model Merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33308)
- When merging N experts into K < N groups, **randomly re-partitioning the experts at every layer** beats both fixed groups and similarity-based grouping (by 1.0–6.8 points across 24 CLIP settings).
- First-order explanation: merging shifts each task's features. With fixed partners the shifts align across layers and accumulate, while with fresh partners they are independent and partly cancel.
- A clean, mechanistic account of interference in weight-space merging.

### 10. [When Every Rollout Fails: Splicing Learner-Conditioned Options into Long-Horizon Agent RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34777)
- Tackles all-fail GRPO groups, where every rollout gets zero reward and there is no gradient. A proposal model picks a **splice point inside a failed on-policy trajectory** and generates short options from there.
- Options are valued with current-policy continuations and fitted with return weighting. This keeps the training targets local and close to on-policy instead of imitating full expert trajectories.
- +2.8–9.8 points over GRPO on SWE-bench Verified and BrowseComp-Plus, and a +10.8-point graduation rate on initially failed SWE tasks.

## Honorable Mentions

- [PAC Learning to Sample: On Computational Aspects of Language Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33464): a quantitative, computational version of "language generation in the limit". Online learnability is not necessary, since an ERM-oracle algorithm suffices.
- [Tokenisation is not in APX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34938): unless P=NP, no polynomial-time tokeniser comes within any constant factor of optimal (for compression or unigram objectives).
- [Pitfalls of Dependency-Ready Decoding for Masked Diffusion LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33823): attention-based "dependency-ready" decoders mostly collapse to left-to-right decoding. When margins are large, max-margin decoding already commits dependency-ready tokens.
- [What Must LLM Agents Communicate?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33318): an information-theoretic framework that separates fundamental communication limits from agents' failures to select or use evidence.
- [Not All World Models Can Doubt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34830): RSSM (Dreamer) epistemic uncertainty misses perturbations that its own posterior registers, because of recurrent averaging and pixel-weighted reconstruction. JEPA uncertainty passes the perturbations through.
- [Strong Starts, Simple Recipes: Uni-MOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34397): a uniform merge of specialists *before* multi-teacher on-policy distillation matches 200 distillation steps from the base model, so the starting point matters more than the recipe.
- [Collect for Coverage, Replay with Evidence (CARE-OPD)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34756): efficient on-policy distillation for long-horizon agents, reaching 59.5% on SWE-bench Verified with 45% less training time.
- [HumblePRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33452): a process reward model that scores perception and calibration separately, so VLMs abstain more on ambiguous images without losing accuracy on clear ones.
- [Semantic Density Does Not Predict Agent-Memory Collapse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33184): a pre-registered negative result. Memory-store geometry statistics do not warn of accuracy decline, while token growth does.
- [Learning from Natural Images Untangles Number Sense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33615): pretraining increases the angle between the numerosity and proportion subspaces. A geometric account of how representations change with learning.
- [Plausible Is Not Proven](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34393): failure-cause labels for depth models agree with blinded human raters only at chance (κ = 0.05). A strong argument for testing explanations before trusting them.
- [FlashProve-Next](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33741): zero-knowledge proofs that a closed API actually ran the advertised model, precision and hidden prompt, without changing native serving.
- [Diverse Alternative Reasoning Traces (DART)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34928): mines rejected math traces at their uncertain steps and recovers 37% more hard problems than MC sampling.
