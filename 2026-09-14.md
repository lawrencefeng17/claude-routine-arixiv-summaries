## Top 10

### 1. [MAxBench: A Multinomial Concept Recovery Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13072)

- Introduces MAxBench, a geometry-agnostic benchmark for evaluating how well interpretability methods recover *multinomial* (multi-category) concepts, like Animals or Countries, in activation space — not just binary concepts like refusal, where a single steering direction usually suffices.
- Compares 10 localization methods spanning 5 representation geometries across 6 concepts and 4 models; finds affine subspaces steer most reliably and with the highest recall, with most of that edge coming from better non-zero offsets rather than the choice of basis vectors.
- Notably, no method consistently beats simple prompting — echoing prior findings on binary concepts — underscoring a real gap in interpretability's ability to control richer, non-binary concepts.

### 2. [Where Decoder Cosine Similarity Fails for SAE Feature Flow Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12591)

- Builds a causally-validated "transition atlas" tracing how a residual-stream feature and an MLP-update feature jointly produce a downstream feature (via targeted ablation) in Pythia-160M and Gemma-3-4B.
- Finds the standard proxy interpretability researchers use to spot these feature interactions — decoder cosine similarity between the paired SAE features — fails badly: 88.0% of the 38,125 causally-confirmed strong transitions in Pythia have cosine similarity below 0.7 (53.6% in a Gemma-3-4B cross-check).
- A direct caution for SAE-based interpretability and model-editing tooling: similarity-based feature-matching heuristics are systematically missing many real causal update pathways inside the network.

### 3. [Judging by the Cover: Cleaning LLM Truthfulness Benchmarks to Avoid Surface-Level Feature Leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13003)

- Shows that binary-choice truthfulness benchmarks like TruthfulQA leak surface-level cues: a simple six-feature logistic classifier separates correct from incorrect answers well above chance without doing any of the intended reasoning, and similar leakage shows up in other benchmarks too.
- Releases Audit-Prune, a general method for detecting and removing the most leakage-reinforcing answer pairs, plus a cleaned version of TruthfulQA with surface leakage pushed close to chance.
- A methodological warning for alignment/safety evaluation broadly: reported truthfulness gains on these benchmarks may partly reflect models learning cosmetic answer-formatting artifacts rather than genuine calibration to truth.

### 4. [Distortion of AI Alignment Revisited: RLHF is a Decent Utilitarian Aligner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12651)

- Revisits a recent claim that RLHF's "distortion" — the gap between the RLHF policy's average user utility and the optimal pluralistic utility — can blow up exponentially with the Bradley-Terry temperature β under heterogeneous preferences.
- Proves the blow-up isn't intrinsic to RLHF but stems from distribution mismatch between the preference data and the KL reference policy; derives tight bounds showing distortion is Θ̃(βB + β), where B bounds that mismatch, collapsing to the optimal O(β) when the mismatch vanishes.
- Practical upshot for alignment pipelines: prefer on-policy sampled preference data, or fine-tune the reference policy toward the preference distribution before RLHF, to avoid needless utilitarian distortion.

### 5. [Membership Inference via Pairwise Likelihood Ratios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12367)

- Proposes PL-MIA, a membership-inference attack combining a Gaussian likelihood-ratio statistic with population calibration and a Cauchy combination test to aggregate pairwise comparisons between a query point and non-member reference points, instead of collapsing each comparison to a binary vote.
- Provides theoretical conditions under which population calibration and the combination step provably improve attack power, preserving evidence strength that naive pairwise-MIA approaches discard.
- Improves true-positive rate by over 25% in the critical low-false-positive regime versus strong baselines — a direct methodological advance for data-attribution and privacy-auditing work on what training-data signal survives in a model's outputs.

### 6. [Benign Loss Landscapes Can Coexist with Worst-Case Hardness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13057)

- Studies tree tensor networks (TTNs), a model class generalizing deep linear networks and Tucker decompositions, showing they embed arbitrary read-once Boolean formulas — giving them, like real neural nets, polynomial-size targets that are provably hard for gradient descent to learn in polynomial time.
- Proves that despite containing these worst-case-hard targets, TTN loss landscapes stay "benign" for every realizable target — every minimum-norm local minimum is global — so bad local minima are not what separates easy from hard learning problems here.
- Traces real learning difficulty to high-order degenerate saddle points caused by rank-deficiency (illustrated via the parity function), giving a concrete theoretical lens connecting landscape geometry to computational hardness and to why deep nets generalize well on non-generic real-world tasks.

### 7. [Performance, Efficiency and Collapse -- Advantages and Challenges in Offline Post-training of Code LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11956)

- Tests whether RL post-training of code-generating LLMs can be done entirely offline, reusing existing datasets instead of the expensive online sampling and GPU-CPU verification loop that standard RLHF/RLVR pipelines require.
- Finds that just a few hours of offline RL substantially improves zero-shot code generation across model sizes from 0.5B to 7B parameters, with gains varying by model family.
- The "Collapse" in the title flags failure modes accompanying these efficiency gains — a relevant training-dynamics data point on where cheap offline RL trades off against the instabilities seen in standard on-policy post-training.

### 8. [Dimension-Corrected Hitting Times for Heavy-Tailed Spectral Emergence in Neural Optimizer Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12994)

- Models the emergence of heavy-tailed weight-spectrum structure — a common diagnostic of implicit self-regularization in trained networks — as a right-censored "hitting time" problem, tested across 330 completed teacher-student training runs.
- Finds the naive predictor (initial spike-bulk spectral gap alone) doesn't explain onset time; a dimension-corrected power law τ≈CΔ₁^(−γ)d^ρ fits far better (R²=0.683) and decisively beats a gap-only model (AIC 628.70 vs. 706.62).
- Confirms the qualitative signature — non-Gaussian spectral tails — in real pretrained Qwen2.5-0.5B and Pythia-70M weights, and finds GD/signGD don't reach the same onset regime that Adam/AdamW do, adding a rigorous, falsifiable law to the training-dynamics literature on when Adam-trained spectra develop heavy tails (companion theory paper in Honorable Mentions).

### 9. [Sampling via Decision-Flow: Training-Free Extraction of Improved Latent Reasoning Paths in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12317)

- Asks whether RL fine-tuning gives LLMs genuinely new reasoning capabilities or just reshapes the sampling distribution over reasoning paths already latent in the base model (the "distribution-sharpening hypothesis").
- Proposes Decision-Flow Sampling (DF-Sample), a training-free, inference-time method that builds a hierarchical reasoning tree, scores terminal nodes, and back-propagates utility to guide branching decisions — recovering high-quality, low-probability reasoning chains that standard decoding overlooks.
- On GPQA, this training-free method beats both standard power sampling (45.6% vs. 38.9%) and actual GRPO-trained RL (39.9%) across three models and four benchmarks — striking evidence that much of what RL post-training "teaches" may already be latent and extractable at inference time.

### 10. [Hierarchical Prototype Emergence in Modern Hopfield Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12079)

- Analyzes a dense Hopfield network with polynomial activation storing hierarchically-correlated memories, deriving analytic stability conditions for each level of the hierarchy to form genuine local energy minima ("prototypes").
- Uses prototype reconstruction — generalizing beyond specific stored memories and even whole groups to produce sensible new outputs — as a minimal model of generalization, finding only a quasi-polynomial amount of information is needed to generalize beyond particular memories and groups in the hierarchy.
- Reproduces a qualitatively similar phase diagram (over number of memories and activation sharpness) on real Fashion-MNIST data — a tractable, analytically grounded testbed connecting associative-memory theory to how modern generative architectures might generalize hierarchically.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[A Full Adam Theorem for Spectral Heavy-Tail Onset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12996)** — the fully rigorous theoretical companion to #8: derives the same τ≈Θ(Δ₁^(−γ)d^ρ log(Ψ₀/ε)) hitting-time law from first principles inside a closed Gaussian Stein-Hermite teacher-student model, while proving a fully general "arbitrary-gradient" Adam theorem is impossible.
- **[The Rank the Task Demands: A Causal Rank Law for Matrix Memories Trained on Group Composition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12259)** — causal evidence that gradient descent recruits exactly the matrix rank a group-composition task's algebra demands (Spearman ρ=0.97), extending a companion associative-binding result to a representation-theoretic rank law.
- **[Can We Trust LLM Judges: A Study of Capability-Dependent Biases and Multi-Judge Ensemble for Bias Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12002)** — shows more-capable examinee models get systematically more lenient judgments from every judge tested, and proposes a label-free calibrated weighted-majority-vote ensemble that tracks an oracle judge to within 0.5 percentage points.
- **[GAUGE: When Not to Trust LLM-as-a-Judge in User-Simulated Evaluation of Task-Oriented Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12191)** — finds LLM-judged user-simulator evaluations of task agents are nearly decorrelated from actual task success (57.5% of "satisfied" conversations actually failed the task) and lose ranking resolution between closely-matched agents.
- **[Diffusion Models and Concept Formation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13047)** — argues diffusion models implicitly perform the same hierarchical concept-formation computation as the classic Cobweb cognitive model, locating a diffusion "basic level" at an intermediate noise level and testing the correspondence on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST.
- **[Certifying Concept Unlearning in Text-to-Image Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12163)** — introduces statistical certification bounds for residual concept leakage after unlearning, finding certified leakage consistently exceeds standard attack-success-rate estimates by 16.2 points across six unlearning methods.
- **[SCOPE-OPSD: Fisher-Conditioned Privileged Subspaces for On-Policy Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12579)** — projects the teacher-student residual discrepancy in on-policy self-distillation onto a Fisher-conditioned privileged subspace, beating both plain self-distillation and a matched random-subspace control across Qwen3 model sizes.
- **[Breaking the Token Ceiling: Distilling Smaller, Stronger Byte Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12303)** — large-scale study distilling byte-level LLMs from token-level teachers; byte models start worse but overtake token models with more compute/data, predicted to asymptotically beat Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma-3-1B by up to 6.5-8.1% while using far less training data.
