### 1. The Stable Recovery Manifold: Geometric Principles Governing Recoverability in Continual Learning
**arXiv:2606.13637** | Ayushman Trivedi, Bhavika Melwani

- Studies what happens geometrically to "forgotten" knowledge in sequential learning on Split CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18: even after significant representational drift, forgotten task performance can be recovered by targeting a compact subspace.
- **Recovery dimensionality stays constant (~8 singular directions for 90% performance)** despite large representational changes, suggesting forgotten knowledge remains encoded but inaccessible rather than erased.
- Principal-angle drift between task representations strongly predicts recoverability (r = −0.862); a geometric model explains 82.2% of recoverability variance.
- Reframes catastrophic forgetting as a **representation accessibility problem**, not an information-loss problem — with implications for how we think about what continual learning algorithms actually need to preserve.

### 2. Dense Supervision, Sparse Updates: On the Sparsity and Geometry of On-Policy Distillation
**arXiv:2606.13657** | Guo Yu, Wenlin Liu, Yulan Hu, Hao-Xuan Ma, Jun-Peng Jiang, Han-Jia Ye

- Analyzes how on-policy distillation (OPD) — combining student rollouts with dense teacher supervision — actually modifies model weights; finds updates are **small, coordinate-sparse, and concentrated in feed-forward layers**.
- Training only the identified sparse subnetwork reproduces full OPD performance; the sparsity-inducing SGD optimizer still underperforms AdamW due to heterogeneous gradient scales preserved in the subnetwork.
- Geometrically, OPD updates are **numerically full-rank but spectrally concentrated**: they cluster where source weights are small and stay distant from the principal singular subspaces.
- Suggests OPD is a more conservative operation than it appears — it sculpts a specific subspace rather than broadly rewriting weights, with implications for understanding distillation as a training-dynamics phenomenon.

### 3. Reinforcement Learning for Neural Model Editing
**arXiv:2606.13461** | Shaivi Malik

- Frames neural model editing as a reinforcement learning problem: agents operate in two environments — **MaskWorld** (multiplicative weight scaling) and **ShiftWorld** (additive updates) — and learn editing policies from reward signals balancing utility preservation against task-specific objectives.
- On machine unlearning: achieves ~**0% forget-set accuracy** while retaining >90% of retain-set accuracy — competitive with dedicated unlearning algorithms but generalized to a policy that can be reused across targets.
- On bias mitigation: improves bias-relevant classification metrics by >5% without requiring explicit gradient-based re-training.
- Positions model editing as a **learnable skill** rather than a task-specific algorithm — relevant to AI safety insofar as behavioral corrections could be encoded as reusable policies rather than bespoke interventions.

### 4. Scale Buys Interpolation, Structure Buys a Horizon: Certified Predictability for Equivariant World Models
**arXiv:2606.13092** | Hongbo Wang

- Derives a **computable certificate** for multi-step rollout error in equivariant latent world models: error is constant across symmetry orbits and varies by channel according to the model's Lyapunov spectrum — enabling horizon prediction before seeing calibration data.
- Proves this bounded-horizon guarantee is exclusive to equivariant structures (with matching lower bounds); approximate equivariance inherently limits prediction windows in a quantifiable way.
- On 40-D Lorenz-96: only ℤ_N-equivariant networks fully recover the Lyapunov spectrum (R² = 0.98), outperforming dense and recurrent baselines; certificates successfully audit pretrained TD-MPC2 and V-JEPA models without retraining.
- **Core finding:** scale alone improves interpolation but not prediction reliability — structural choices determine trustworthy horizons. A principled answer to "when can we trust this world model?"

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.13473** — *MaxProof: Scaling Mathematical Proof with Generative-Verifier RL and Population-Level Test-Time Scaling* (Chen et al.): Trains a unified M3 model to generate, verify, refine, and rank proofs simultaneously; uses tournament-based population search at test time to achieve **35/42 on IMO 2025 and 36/42 on USAMO 2026** — both above the gold-medal threshold. A frontier capability milestone for RL-trained reasoning models.

- **arXiv:2606.13576** — *Learning with Simulators: No Regret in a Computationally Bounded World* (Voitovych, Shetty, Golowich, Rakhlin): Extends PAC learning theory to **simulatable processes** — settings where the learner accesses an approximate simulator rather than i.i.d. samples — and proves VC-dimension error bounds comparable to classical settings. An interesting theoretical generalization of when and how learning guarantees transfer from synthetic to real data-generating processes.
