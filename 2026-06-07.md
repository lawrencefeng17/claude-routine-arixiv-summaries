## Top 10

### 1. STRIDE: Training Data Attribution via Sparse Recovery
**[2606.05165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05165)** · Dagli, Harrasse, Zhang, Draye, Abdullah, Schölkopf, Jin

A new TDA framework that sidesteps gradient-based influence estimation by instead tracking how training subsets shift model *activations*, then framing the attribution problem as sparse recovery (compressive sensing).

- Perturbs lightweight steering operators and measures behavioral shifts in activation space; decomposes the result to recover per-example influence without iterative retraining
- **13× faster** than prior state-of-the-art for LLM pre-training attribution, while matching or exceeding attribution quality
- Validated on data selection, contamination detection, and qualitative analysis — moves TDA from a research tool toward production-scale use

### 2. Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability
**[2606.06333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06333)** · Dalili, Mahdavi

Standard sparse autoencoders assume each feature is one-dimensional (a single decoder vector), but model features can span a multi-dimensional subspace — causing "feature splitting" where one coherent feature is fragmented into exponentially many near-collinear atoms.

- Proves the standard SAE objective has a continuous descent path *toward* splitting, making fragmentation a training attractor
- **SASA** replaces single-vector decoders with learned decoder subspaces, using block sparsity (Top-s groups) and nuclear-norm regularization to control effective subspace rank
- When block size ≥ feature dimension: sample complexity drops from exponential to polynomial
- Matches standard SAE interpretability with ~half the training tokens on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B

### 3. Steering Vectors are an Adversarial Attack Surface
**[2606.05958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05958)** · Aidakhmetov, Crisostomi, Mencattini, Minut, Masi, Rodolà

Activation steering vectors — built by contrasting paired prompts and widely shared in repositories — can be silently backdoored via dataset poisoning.

- Substituting just **4–6% of tokens** in the contrast dataset covertly aligns the resulting vector with an anti-refusal direction, jailbreaking the model while preserving its intended behavior on benign inputs
- Attack success rates of **20–55%** across two open-weight model families (8 configurations), with 19–51 pp improvement over baseline attacks
- Proposed defense: refusal-direction orthogonalization recovers ~82% of the safety gap without degrading benign steering
- Flags shared steering-vector ecosystems as a largely unexamined supply-chain attack surface

### 4. Deciphering Two Training Clocks in Grokking via Deep Linear Network Theory
**[2606.05863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05863)** · Tan, Gai, Zhang

Provides explicit mathematical timescales for grokking's two-phase structure (fast loss fitting, slow generalization) using deep linear network theory.

- With layerwise weight decay: cross-entropy loss closes on a **logarithmic** timescale; Schatten-penalized representation simplification follows a **polynomial** timescale — the quantitative clock separation underlying grokking
- Extends to ReLU MLPs via conditional linearization: activation-fixed regions reduce to linear models, preserving the two-clock structure
- Classifier head receives asymmetrically large gradients, explaining why it fits first while the embedding simplifies later

### 5. Dead Directions: Geometric Singular Learning
**[2606.05957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05957)** · Shirodkar

Bridges singular learning theory (the Bayesian account of generalization in overparameterized models) and information geometry through "dead directions" — unit vectors where the Fisher metric degenerates.

- Shows Watanabe's KL order is recoverable as the decay rate of directional Fisher curvature along a dead direction — no Hironaka resolution required
- Applies multi-layer K-FAC factorization to deep networks (residual streams, layer norm, attention), yielding **closed-form RLCT predictions per architecture**
- Introduces **DDCAdam**, a G-equivariant Adam-family preconditioner compatible with gradient flow symmetries
- Watanabe's triple (RLCT, singular fluctuation, free energy) is extractable from a **single checkpoint** via forward/backward passes — eliminates posterior sampling

### 6. Pattern Selectivity is Not Task-Causal Structure
**[2606.05378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05378)** · Xu

Tests the standard mechanistic interpretability circuit-finding workflow — pattern-selective screening followed by causal ablation — across 4 composed tasks and 3 architecturally distinct 1B-class models (Pythia 1B, OLMo 1B, OLMoE 1B-7B).

- Central finding: **the same task is implemented differently across models** — pattern screens don't transfer; circuits are model-specific
- Introduces a five-category screen-outcome taxonomy (primary cause, secondary cause, correlate, interferer, null) with quantitative thresholds for rigorous reporting
- MoE models build composed-task circuits on a "foundational previous-token positional substrate" absent in dense models — testable prediction for other MoE architectures
- Methodological implication: pattern selectivity is a hypothesis generator, not a finding; causal verification must be repeated per model

### 7. High-Dimensional Theory of LoRA Fine-Tuning in a Solvable Attention Model
**[2606.05899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05899)** · Duranthon, Boncoraglio, Zdeborová

A rare exact-asymptotic theory for parameter-efficient fine-tuning: analyzes rank-1 LoRA on a single-head attention layer pretrained on abundant data, then adapted on limited downstream data.

- Sharp closed-form predictions via a small set of order parameters — no free parameters in the theory
- Pre-training influence on LoRA reduces to a single **effective noise term**, yielding concrete prescriptions for optimal pre-training when downstream LoRA is anticipated
- Identifies a **test error / representation alignment divergence regime**: strong representation quality can coexist with poor generalization, and vice versa — warns against representation-based evaluation proxies

### 8. Epidemiology of Model Collapse
**[2606.05168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05168)** · Wang

Models the spread of synthetic data contamination through the AI training ecosystem as an epidemic using a bilayer coupled SIR/SIRS framework with two interacting populations: data corpora and AI models.

- Derives a basic reproduction number (R₀) via the Next Generation Matrix; calibration on public AI-text prevalence data yields **supercritical dynamics (R₀ > 1)** across multiple scenarios
- Dose-response degradation in GPT-2 experiments (192 runs) and multi-source mixing (1,088 runs) confirms predicted patterns; data diversity modestly attenuates collapse
- Highest-leverage interventions: **detection-based filtering** and herd immunity (maintaining substantial human-generated data)
- Frames model collapse as a systemic ecosystem risk, not an isolated training failure

### 9. Learning What to Forget: Improving LLM Unlearning via Learned Token-Level Importance
**[2606.06320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06320)** · Yüce, Nikolaou, Flammarion

LLM unlearning should not treat all tokens in a forget set equally — a token is "forget-specific" only to the degree that removing it doesn't conflict with retaining general capabilities.

- Formalizes via a joint optimization over model parameters and per-token weights; proves the objective recovers oracle forget-specific token support under a natural separation condition
- **ATWU** (Alternating Token-Weighted Unlearning) learns a lightweight linear token scorer over hidden states without external supervision, alternating token-weight updates with parameter updates
- State-of-the-art forget-retain trade-offs on TOFU and RWKU, outperforming sample-level methods, probability-based heuristics, and auxiliary-model approaches
- Learned scores align substantially with ground-truth forget-specific spans, making unlearning interpretable

### 10. State Commitment Learning: Training Language Models to Distinguish Computation from Memory
**[2606.05201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05201)** · Ding et al.

Reasoning LLMs currently treat all tokens in the context equally — including scratch work and failed attempts — so downstream answers can become dependent on transient intermediate steps that should be discardable.

- Defines **persistent-state sufficiency**: an answer is valid only if it remains correct after erasing all hidden-thought segments
- **CERL** (Counterfactual Erasure RL) evaluates each reasoning trace twice — with and without hidden thoughts, under identical prefixes — rewarding only paths where erasure preserves correctness
- Tested on math, logic chains, scientific QA, and multi-turn tool use; substantially reduces dependence on hidden work while maintaining accuracy
- Outperforms correctness-only RL and extended SFT; provides a principled training-time intervention for compositional reliability and test-time compute efficiency

## Honorable Mentions

- **[2606.06238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06238)** — *Generative Criticality in Large Language Model Temperature Scaling* (Ruan et al.): Treats token embeddings as spin variables on a 1D chain; susceptibility peaks sharply at a critical temperature T_c with power-law scaling — a continuous phase transition. Below T_c: rapid semantic collapse to a single embedding direction. Corroborated via intrinsic dimensionality estimation; consistent across Qwen3 0.6B–32B.

- **[2606.06475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06475)** — *RREDCoT: Segment-Level Reward Redistribution for Reasoning Models* (Ielanskyi et al.): Reframes GRPO-style RL for reasoning as a credit assignment problem across CoT segments. Uses the model to approximate optimal reward redistribution without extra generation passes; outperforms Monte Carlo sampling and gradient attribution for variance reduction in chain-of-thought training.

- **[2606.05376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05376)** — *SHALA-LLM: Smartly Handling Ambiguous Labels in Aligning LLMs* (Wu et al.): Reframes annotator disagreement as a signal rather than noise; an RL framework learns from full annotation distributions while prioritizing ambiguous samples. Reduces Jensen-Shannon Distance by up to 62.1% and improves F1 by up to 16.7% on ChaosNLI and GoEmotions.

- **[2606.05972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05972)** — *LLM Explainability with Counterfactual Chains and Causal Graphs* (Nussbaum-Hoffer et al.): MCMC-inspired counterfactual augmentation expands sparse observational data; causal discovery (σ-CG) produces concept-level graphs of LLM reasoning pathways, evaluated for fidelity and stability across three model families.
