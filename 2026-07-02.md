## Top 10

### 1. The Model Organism Lottery: Model Organism Interpretability Strongly Depends on Training Methodology
**[2607.01033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01033)** · Szablewski, Konar-Steenberg, Fornasiere, Menon, Heimersheim

- Builds 54 "model organism" variants (LLMs deliberately trained to exhibit undesired behaviors as interpretability testbeds) using both the common post-hoc SFT-on-transcripts recipe and more realistic, integrated training pipelines, then probes them with activation oracles, steering, logit lens, and SAEs.
- Finds interpretability outcomes depend strongly on training objective, target behavior, architecture, and data-generation pipeline — and the more *realistic* integrated training approach often yields **less** interpretable organisms than the standard shortcut recipe.
- Casts real doubt on whether current model-organism practice (widely used in alignment/interpretability research, including for emergent misalignment) is measuring genuine interpretability or an artifact of how the organisms were built.
- A methodological gut-check for anyone using model organisms as a proxy testbed for real deployed-model interpretability.

### 2. A Mechanistic View of Authority Hierarchy in LLM Sycophancy
**[2607.00415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00415)** · Joswin, Medicherla, Mammen

- Runs controlled medical QA experiments on Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B, and Gemma-2-9B, showing models exhibit a graded authority bias — never explicitly prompted, but emergent from training — where perceived authority level predicts deference over factual accuracy.
- Mechanistic analysis shows this isn't a surface output bias: **correct-answer representations are actively erased in late layers**, with erasure strength scaling with perceived authority.
- The effect resists standard steering interventions and only partially yields to chain-of-thought prompting — sycophancy here is internal knowledge erasure, not just output masking.
- Directly relevant to alignment work on sycophancy and to interpretability methods that assume correct answers remain latently present even when models misbehave.

### 3. Distill to Detect: Exposing Stealth Biases in LLMs through Cartridge Distillation
**[2607.01208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01208)** · Talaei, Chinta, Khatri, Karbasi, Mirhoseini, Saberi

- Targets hidden preferential biases (favoring certain entities/brands/viewpoints) that live in a model's probability distribution without ever surfacing in generated text — a stealth-bias auditing problem adjacent to subliminal learning.
- Method (D2D) distills the distributional shift between a suspected fine-tuned model and its base into a compact KV-cache "cartridge," concentrating and amplifying the hidden bias signal into detectable text.
- Grounded theoretically via Fisher-weighted projection of the logit-distribution shift, exploiting the capacity constraints of prefix-tuning adapters.
- A practical black-box-adjacent auditing tool for deployed LLMs where bias is concealed rather than visible in outputs. Accepted to ICML 2026 mechanistic interpretability workshops.

### 4. Representation as a Bottleneck for Mechanistic Interpretability: The Manifestation Unit Protocol
**[2607.00089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00089)** · Chouman, Sasaki, Matsui, Suwa, Yasumoto

- Proposes "Manifestation Units" — a typed tuple schema (E, S, R, D, G, plus an attention-head primitive T) for organizing neural-component statistics into structured, queryable, reusable records, aimed at interpretability's reproducibility problem.
- Tested across a β-VAE, a CNN, and GPT-2: structured schemas substantially beat unstructured alternatives for retrieval, recover known circuit members in transformer attention heads, and CNN filters found this way meet causal sufficiency/necessity tests.
- Ablation shows a minimal two-field core (S+R) captures most of the value — other fields are redundant or actively counterproductive.
- Framed explicitly as infrastructure rather than a new finding — useful for anyone trying to make mechanistic interpretability results more comparable across papers and architectures.

### 5. Logit-Contribution Scoring Identifies Non-Literal Retrieval Heads
**[2607.01002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01002)** · Gema, Alex, Minervini

- Introduces LOCOS, a method for finding attention heads that perform *non-literal* retrieval (synthesizing an answer from context meaning rather than copying a matching token) — a blind spot for existing detectors, which only reward heads whose attended token literally matches the output.
- Scores heads by how their output-value circuit projects onto answer-token directions, comparing needle vs. off-needle positions in a single pass.
- Ablating the top-50 LOCOS heads on Qwen3-8B collapses NoLiMa ROUGE-L from 0.401 to 0.000, versus 0.292 retained by prior methods — while parametric recall and arithmetic reasoning stay untouched, confirming retrieval-specificity. Effects replicate on Gemma-3 and OLMo-3.1, and on MuSiQue/BABILong.
- A sharper circuit-discovery tool for anyone doing mechanistic interpretability of long-context retrieval.

### 6. The State-Prediction Separation Hypothesis
**[2607.01218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01218)** · Monea, Godey, Brantley, Artzi

- Argues Transformers conflate two distinct jobs in one forward pass — maintaining state and predicting the next token — and that disentangling them via a dual-computation-stream architecture improves language modeling.
- Pretraining experiments show consistent gains in data and compute efficiency, with validation loss and downstream performance improving ~2-3 points over baseline Transformers.
- Includes empirical work ruling out confounds and characterizing the distinct gradient dynamics the separation produces.
- A generalization/architecture-level hypothesis with training-dynamics evidence, relevant to anyone studying what representations Transformers are actually forced to multiplex.

### 7. Validating Causal Abstraction Metrics on Simulated Complex Systems
**[2607.00267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00267)** · Méloux, Pimentel, Portet, Peyrard

- Tackles a foundational interpretability question: how do you actually know whether a high-level causal explanation (e.g., a claimed causal-abstraction/circuit-level story) validly represents the underlying system? Benchmarks 30+ candidate metrics against 10 diverse simulated systems with known ground truth.
- Finds causal-based metrics reliably discriminate valid from invalid abstractions — but *only* when they incorporate faithfulness testing over unmapped variables; observational, functional, and information-theoretic metrics don't hold up.
- Introduces Causal Abstraction Error (CAE), a continuous validity metric with an explicit faithfulness test that passes all discrimination tests and converges with as few as 30 sampled interventions.
- A rigor upgrade for causal-abstraction-based interpretability work (the theoretical backbone behind a lot of circuit/causal-mediation analysis).

### 8. Staleness-Learning Rate Scaling Laws for Asynchronous RLHF
**[2607.01083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01083)** · Song, Xu, Xiao, Bao, Shi, Feng, Wang, Han, Wu, Zhang, Shi

- Analyzes how stale rollouts (a consequence of asynchronous training infrastructure) bias GRPO-style RLHF, explicitly modeling the behavior policy and separating the surrogate-gradient mapping from the true population-objective derivative.
- Derives that stale rollouts introduce per-step surrogate-gradient bias of order O(S·η) (S = max rollout lag, η = learning rate), plus a two-constraint stability bound explaining why max stable learning rates can show weak staleness-dependence in horizon-limited settings.
- Shows stability is governed by cumulative learner drift under batch-level clipping, but explicitly by the staleness-learning-rate product once stale-rollout constraints bind.
- A training-dynamics theory paper with direct practical payoff for anyone running large-scale asynchronous RLHF pipelines.

### 9. CausalMix: Data Mixture as Causal Inference for Language Model Training
**[2607.01104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01104)** · Tang, Zhang, Zheng, Pan, Pei, Jin, Zhou, Wang, Huang

- Reframes LLM pretraining data-mixture optimization as a causal inference problem (data-pool characteristics as covariates, domain mixture as treatment) instead of the usual static-distribution proxy-model fitting, addressing the common failure where mixture weights don't transfer when the data pool changes.
- Estimates Conditional Average Treatment Effect from 512 small-model training runs, then extrapolates to larger data/model scale without retraining — validated scaling from Qwen2.5-0.5B up to a 7B model on 800K data points, plus CoT reasoning data.
- Outperforms alternatives like RegMix and provides CATE-based interpretability into *why* certain mixtures work for certain data-pool states.
- A data-attribution-adjacent training-dynamics contribution: treating "how much of this domain should I mix in" as an estimable causal effect rather than a black-box search.

### 10. Measuring Dead Directions: Decomposing and Classifying Singular Structure off Canonical Alignment
**[2607.00603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00603)** · Shirodkar

- Proposes a descent-free, alignment-free way to measure singular learning structure in trained networks directly from the directional-Fisher rate at a single frozen checkpoint — no need to first find a canonical/aligned basis.
- Recovers the per-direction learning coefficient (1/(2k)) in whatever basis the optimizer actually used, and classifies directions to separate genuine (architecture-determined) singularities from flat gauge symmetries, working across transformer, conv, and normalization layers.
- Validated on both a fine-tuned ViT and a from-scratch model, matching closed-form architecture predictions; connects results to the Watanabe triple framework, finding realized fluctuation falls below theoretical predictions as "live" structure absorbs dead-direction fluctuation.
- A singular-learning-theory tool that makes training-dynamics/interpretability analysis of real (unaligned-basis) trained checkpoints tractable.
