## Top 10

### 1. Minimizing Targeted Activations: Input-Only Suppression of Evaluation-Awareness Latents in Large Language Models
**[2607.25907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25907)** · Deepanshu Mody, Samarth Agarwal, Utkarsh Mittal, Dipesh Mahato

- Targets "evaluation-awareness" latents — internal representations that could let a model behave differently once it detects it's being tested — and asks whether prompt-only interventions (no weight edits) can suppress them.
- Using token optimization plus fluency regularization on Llama models, a causally-validated SAE evaluation-awareness feature is driven to near-zero activation — but random control directions suppress just as easily.
- The sobering finding: activation-readability is not behavioral controllability. Suppressing the visible latent signal did not reduce the model's actual behavioral response during evaluation, a direct challenge to interpretability-based safety-eval pipelines that assume readable latents are causally load-bearing.

### 2. Where Steering Signals Come From: Activation Source Selection in Activation Steering
**[2607.25270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25270)** · Jiaran Ye, Lingxu Ran, Zijun Yao, Chenpeng Wang, Yong Jiang, Lei Hou, Juanzi Li, Liangming Pan

- Most steering work treats *where the steering vector is extracted from* as an afterthought; this paper isolates "source selection" as its own variable, holding the downstream intervention fixed.
- Across 3 instruction-tuned models and 4 steering task families, changing only the source context substantially changes steering success — and success is not explained merely by whether the target behavior appears in the source text.
- Strong signals instead come from "execution-boundary" states, right before the model is about to produce the target behavior; their proposed "tail subtraction" technique strips shared prompt/continuation semantics to yield cleaner, more reliable steering vectors.

### 3. Bridging Compute- and Data-Optimal Pretraining
**[2607.25271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25271)** · Tian Qin, Kimia Hamidieh, David Alvarez-Melis

- As pretraining runs into data limits, classical compute-optimal scaling laws (which assume unbounded fresh data) break down; proposes unified Compute-Data (CD) scaling laws spanning compute-optimal and data-optimal regimes.
- Introduces a token-effectiveness function η measuring how "derived" tokens (via repetition or paraphrasing) compare to fresh ones, tested on 14M–600M-parameter models on Dolma-3; effectiveness depends on model size, token/parameter ratio, and derived-data volume, with diminishing returns from substituting compute for data.
- Partitions training into compute-bound, data-bound, and model-bound regimes — a direct contribution to the science of generalization and data attribution as data scarcity becomes the binding constraint on scaling.

### 4. Forgetting Is Not a Fix: Path Dependence in Sequential Engram Editing
**[2607.24805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24805)** · Ferdinand M. Schessl

- Tests the "commutative manifold" hypothesis from prior AI-Engram work on sequential model edits/unlearning — that integrating multiple edits reaches the same equilibrium regardless of order — and finds it does not hold.
- Across three model configurations, zero-shot edit composition diverges from sequentially recalibrated editing by 61–71% of the edit magnitude; order matters substantially under concept overlap, and layer-input covariances drift continuously with each successive edit.
- Most concerning for compliance/unlearning use cases: previously "erased" knowledge partially re-emerges after later, unrelated edits — erasure certified today does not certify the artifact after its next edit.

### 5. Bits and Memories: Measuring Verbatim Extraction Across LLM Quantization
**[2607.25451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25451)** · Akshay Sasi

- Moves past membership-inference proxies to directly measure whether quantized models still reproduce memorized training sequences verbatim, across Pythia models at 3 sizes and 5 precision levels (full precision down to 4-bit) with two quantization algorithms.
- Finds quantization is a "selective forgetter": verbatim memorization degrades faster than general capability at every precision and model size — but not fast enough to serve as a real privacy defense, since 4-bit models still reproduce most memorized sequences at comparable perplexity.
- Larger models retain a higher fraction of memorized data after quantization; recommends practitioners use extraction metrics rather than membership inference to assess privacy risk in compressed models.

### 6. Mechanisms of Width Scaling in Normalized Residual Networks: The Effective Alignment Dimension
**[2607.24887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24887)** · Jinhao Zhang, Zeyu Liu, Zicheng Yan, Yunquan Zhang, Guangming Tan, Fangming Liu, Daning Cheng

- Asks a generalization question existing width theory can't answer: does a width-expansion direction found on finite training data actually help on unseen data? Introduces the "effective alignment dimension," a measurable signal-noise geometry statistic for activation gradients.
- Derives a finite-sample bound on the probability that train/test gradients misalign, requiring only finite second moments (no spectral assumptions or prescribed width-growth rates), turned into a certificate for when residual expansion improves test risk.
- Validated on width-controlled LLaMA-style Transformers, Pythia, and ResNet-20: wider models show larger effective alignment dimensions and lower empirical misalignment, and direct residual interventions confirm the statistic predicts the sign/magnitude of held-out loss changes.

### 7. Algorithmic Separation between Constant-Depth and Logarithmic-Depth Neural Networks
**[2607.25200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25200)** · Yunwei Ren, Zihao Wang, Jason D. Lee

- Proves the first *algorithmic* (not merely approximation-capacity) separation between constant-depth and log-depth networks, identifying Boolean functions with hierarchically structured Fourier spectra that log-depth networks learn efficiently via layerwise coordinate descent.
- Shows a subclass of these functions is provably unlearnable by constant-depth, polynomial-width networks with regular activations, which are stuck at constant L² approximation error on the uniform hypercube.
- Extends depth-separation theory (previously largely confined to 2–3 layer shallow nets) into a genuinely algorithmic account of why deeper networks generalize better on structured tasks.

### 8. Inverse RL Helps Align AI by Imitating Humans
**[2607.24900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24900)** · Michał Wiliński, Liu Leqi, Chirag Nagpal

- Proposes PARED (Projected Alignment Reward Estimated from Demonstrations), recovering an explicit, inspectable reward function from human demonstrations alone via inverse RL, using a lightweight discriminator to separate demonstrations from policy samples — no preference annotations required.
- The recovered reward supports inference-time reranking and adversarial on-policy RL, improving policy quality without a supervised loss, with further gains when combined with SFT.
- Enables contextual alignment — a single policy tailored to different audiences' preferences — reframing alignment supervision around a recoverable, reusable reward object instead of an opaque preference-only pipeline.

### 9. Architectural Backdoors in Vision-Language Model Supply Chains via Representation Steering
**[2607.25479](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25479)** · Maria Rosaria Briglia, Igor Maljkovic, Antonio Emanuele Cinà, Luca Oneto, Iacopo Masi, Fabio Roli

- Introduces a supply-chain attack where a malicious model provider embeds dormant, trigger-gated steering logic directly into a VLM's architecture (not the training data or weights alone), staying silent until activated and evading standard weight/data audits.
- Demonstrates the attack compromises safety enforcement, retrieval ranking fairness, VQA, and text-to-image generation across multiple VLM families while preserving normal behavior when the trigger is absent.
- Proposes auditing the executable logic shipped with model artifacts, not just learned weights — a concrete new threat model for open-model supply-chain security relevant to safety-critical deployment.

### 10. Decision-Level Hijacking: Injecting Cognitive Bias into Large Language Models via Bit-Flip Attacks
**[2607.25227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25227)** · Yu Yan, Jiahao Chen, Siqi Lu, Yongjuan Wang, Ziming Zhao, Zhaoxuan Li, Tianyu Du, Qingjun Yuan, Shouling Ji

- Shows a handful of targeted bit flips in an LLM's weights — via the CogBias framework, which pairs a differentiable sentiment evaluator with "BitScout" to locate critical bits — can covertly shift a model's "cognitive stance" on target topics while leaving general capability intact.
- Tested on Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B, and Qwen2.5-14B: sparse bit flips reliably induce significant, targeted stance shifts with limited collateral impact on non-target tasks and overall output distribution.
- A concrete, low-cost hardware/weight-level attack for covertly misaligning deployed models — relevant to AI safety threat modeling and emergent-misalignment-adjacent robustness questions.
