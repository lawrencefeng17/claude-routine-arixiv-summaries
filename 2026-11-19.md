# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-19

Source: [cs.LG new listings for Thu, 19 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new) (118 new submissions + 100 cross-lists; replacements excluded). Summaries are based on the abstracts.

A lighter day for core alignment work; the strongest items are on monitoring, erasure robustness, training dynamics, and distillation theory.

## Top 10

### 1. [GateWatch: Low-Overhead LLM Monitoring with Boolean Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24619)
- Safety/behavior monitors expressed as **explicit Boolean logic over a few binarized SAE features**, which makes them both interpretable and cheap.
- Across 24 tasks on Gemma-2-9B: 80.5% mean accuracy with ~3.8 features and ~3.5 gates per probe, only 1.2pp below full 16K-feature SAE probes (matches or beats them on 10 tasks), with 99.98% less probe memory.
- Inside vLLM, 1,000 concurrent multi-layer GateWatch probes keep 97–98% of baseline throughput.

### 2. [A Rank-One Bypass of Fixed Linear Erasure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23880)
- If an attacker can edit the layers *upstream* of a fixed linear concept-erasure layer (as with open weights), erasure fails **iff** the layer passes even one direction visible to an allowed downstream classifier, and a **rank-one affine update** is enough to exploit it.
- On CIFAR-10 the bypass restores nearly all pre-erasure performance while leaving a deployed benign head's scores unchanged. A 10% feature edit calibrated on 2k unlabeled examples recovers about 91% of the gain.
- Takeaway: linear guardedness describes the current representation. It does not guarantee resistance to tampering.

### 3. [Stability Does Not Mirror Plasticity: A Module-Level View of Declarative and Procedural Learning in Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23427)
- Double dissociation in a controlled synthetic setup: **QK/VO are most plastic for procedural tasks, MLPs for declarative memorization**.
- Forgetting is asymmetric. Learning a new procedure by updating QK keeps more than half of the stored facts, while updating VO loses about 80%.
- Reset-and-recovery experiments show plasticity tracks a module's primary role, while stability depends on *every* way existing capabilities route through that module. The pattern broadly carries over to a realistic model.

### 4. [Which Tokens, in What Order, and How Fast? A Theoretical Analysis of On-Policy Distillation Objectives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23397)
- Sequence-level on-policy distillation has a **"future agreement bias"**: it learns in reverse order, matching the teacher at later positions first and possibly favoring teacher-improbable tokens early. Token-level objectives avoid this.
- The choice of divergence matters for optimization. **Forward KL has a well-behaved landscape**, while reverse KL and other f-divergences can have vanishing gradients even when the student is far from the teacher.
- Validated across LM post-training settings.

### 5. [Learning Trajectories Leave Decodable Traces in Neural Network Weight Space and Internal Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24782)
- Final weights retain **selective history**: all 30 networks were matched to their own initialization among 10 candidates, and a linear probe decoded the order of two earlier tasks with 100% accuracy after further training on a common task.
- Individual initial weights can't be reconstructed, and perturbed networks recover performance without returning to their original paths.
- 2,157 of 2,160 aligned endpoint pairs showed no loss barrier at the sampled interpolation points: the solutions are distinct but connected. This bears on provenance and data/history attribution.

### 6. [Equal Steps, Unequal Curvature: Where Muon's Update Goes Wrong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24644)
- Uses Hessian-vector products to measure the optimal per-band step multipliers for Muon in a 124M decoder. **Muon over-steps the high-curvature top singular directions.** A Lean-proved criterion says when this diagnostic justifies an intervention.
- Fixes that target this measured miscalibration help: orthogonalizing Q/K/V separately (−0.025 nats) and a σ^(-1/4) spectral tilt in the cooldown (an additional −0.012, additive with NorMuon, −0.019 at 355M).
- Geometrically "principled" changes that don't act on the miscalibration (exact polar factors, input whitening) don't help.

### 7. [A Mechanistic Analysis of In-Context Learning and Out-of-Context Hallucination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24000)
- Treats ICL and unfaithfulness to the context as one mechanism: functional "role" information can be **represented internally without constraining the output**.
- A first-order decomposition shows the role effect needs both enough contribution magnitude and coherent signed aggregation. Causal patching shows that failing token choices flip once role-span support overcomes the deficit.

### 8. [Learning through Discernment: when a small language model learns from user feedback on the job, and when it decays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23716)
- Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B models with LoRA were fine-tuned nightly on accepted attempts across 127 simulated "lives" of up to a year on WebShop and τ²-bench.
- When the model judges its own attempts, performance **improves then decays**. A judge that reads polite give-ups as success collapsed 3 of 5 lives. A "discerning" judge stays near the oracle for about 2 weeks, then fades.
- The share of failures among training lessons strongly predicts the outcome (ρ = −0.81). When users can't see the outcome, 80% of lessons were failures the customer had thanked the agent for, a concrete case of feedback-driven misgeneralization.

### 9. [Transferring an Analysis of Human Brain Organization to Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24626)
- Diffusion-map "gradient" analysis of base vs. reasoning-trained models (3 families) finds **localized changes in a contiguous layer span**.
- **Grafting that span's weights** into instruction-tuned hosts improves math reasoning with little interference, and gains grow with region overlap. The span forms gradually during the early rise in performance. The method also finds a transferable span in a medical model.

### 10. [What Interpolators Store and What They Express: An Information-Theoretic Framework for Benign Overfitting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23630)
- Every interpolator stores exactly nH(Y|X) bits of label noise, so storage-based bounds (including CMI bounds) **can't distinguish interpolators**.
- **Expressed information V**, the entropy over training samples of the prediction at a fresh input, bounds excess risk from both sides. In this view benign overfitting is vanishing expression, and the framework covers k-NN, minimum-norm least squares, and double descent.
- V works as a label-free model-selection criterion: 60% less regret than holdout, and it matches 5-fold CV at a quarter of the cost.

## Honorable Mentions
- [Replay, Don't Resample: Off-Policy Consolidation of Long-Horizon RL Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24059): merges RL experts by replaying their logged training rollouts in order. It beats multi-teacher OPD at ~1/7 of the experts' training compute, and **log order matters more than amount** (logs act as a curriculum).
- [Response-Relevant Information Is Not Sufficient for Neural Training-State Equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23245): weight-spectrum and geometry descriptors predict how a modular-addition transformer responds to perturbation, but they don't beat a placebo control when matching checkpoints.
- [Rethinking Fixed-Horizon Latent Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24001): latent CoT goes through stages, and later steps can weaken support for the correct candidate. Adaptive stopping gives up to +7.2pp with 29% less compute.
- [The Elephant in the Question: Unified Models Picture the Concepts Behind Their Answers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24793): the "V-lens" uses a unified model's image generator to visualize hidden states and reveals unnamed intermediate concepts. Supervising it with pictures also improves text answers.
- [Decomposing SGD Dynamics in Neural Networks: Teacher-Induced Spikes and Variance Inflation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24908): in a multi-index teacher setting, SGD creates teacher-dependent spectral spikes in the weight components orthogonal to the teacher subspace, which hurts generalization.
- [OpenGNS: An Open Dataset of the Gradient Noise Scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24331): 400+ GNS trajectories (ResNet/GPT/DiT, up to 1B parameters, μP). GNS acts like a "temperature of training", and its relation to the critical batch size shows task-dependent biases.
- [Bias-Only Training Dynamics in ReLU Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24797): bias-only training reduces to a sequence of least-squares problems on activation codes and becomes linear in the infinite-width limit.
- [When Does Subspace Direction Matter for LoRA?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23428): the initialization direction matters when data is scarce and the activation subspace aligns with the task. Proposes PivotLoRA.
- [AutoPI: Taxonomy-Guided Adaptive Red Teaming for Prompt Injection in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24313): a red teamer optimized for coverage across injection techniques, not just success rate.
- [GUARD: Stabilizing Recursive Self-Improvement with Regret Guidance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23476): grader-free harness self-editing that gates each edit on non-regression.
- [Can Weight-Space Programming Alter Learned Ethical Priorities?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24330): targeted weight edits shift a promise-vs-benefit trade-off in a concept-supervised text model.
- [Harmonic Circuit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24453): circuit discovery for chord-progression prediction in symbolic music LMs.
