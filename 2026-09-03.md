## Top 10

### 1. [From Reweighting to Rewriting: Unlocking the Intervention Effects of Influential Samples in Training Data Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02771)

- Asks why influence-function-selected training examples so often fail to outperform random selection when used for weight-based reweighting interventions — and shows the fault lies with the intervention, not the selection.
- Introduces influence-guided response *rewriting* (replacing a selected example's response with behavior-aligned or -opposed supervision while keeping the instruction fixed); across four open-weight LLMs this produces far stronger, more persistent, bidirectional behavioral shifts than reweighting the same examples, on both epistemic-abstention and safety-refusal testbeds.
- A methodologically important result for data attribution research: it separates the *local* effect that influence functions actually estimate from the *broader* intervention leverage the identified examples carry, arguing for intervention-aware evaluation of TDA methods generally.

### 2. [Entangled Representations Amplify Collateral Damage in Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02285)

- Directly tests a long-standing but never-verified interpretability intuition — that representational entanglement between knowledge domains makes machine unlearning harder — by training six 254M-parameter LMs with controlled, graded disentanglement between biology and non-biology knowledge via Selective Gradient Masking.
- At matched forgetting levels, more disentangled models incur roughly 4x lower retain-set cost under two of three standard unlearning methods (1.3x under the third); since only the model's internal structure varies (not data or algorithm), this is direct causal evidence for the entanglement hypothesis.
- A clean template for turning a widely-held interpretability belief into a controlled, falsifiable experiment — the kind of causal test interpretability claims rarely get.

### 3. [Emergence of Fibrations, Compression, and Symmetry Breaking in Artificial Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01768)

- Proves that gradient descent training generates local graph-theoretic symmetries ("fibrations"/coverings) in trained networks, and that covering symmetries are stable attractors of SGD — then confirms the prediction empirically across MLP, CNN, RNN, and transformer architectures.
- Exploiting the discovered symmetries compresses networks to 17% of their original size with no performance loss, and deliberately *breaking* covering symmetry restores plasticity, giving state-of-the-art continual-learning results.
- A rare case of a training-dynamics theory yielding both a mechanistic explanation of generalization/compressibility and a practical fix for catastrophic forgetting, framed explicitly as converting black-box networks into interpretable colored graphs.

### 4. [When Decodability Is Not Enough: Logical Validity Representations, Behavioral Dissociation, and Causal Tests in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02438)

- Tests five open-weight transformers on matched valid/invalid premise-claim pairs and finds logical validity is almost perfectly linearly decodable from hidden states — including on examples the model gets behaviorally wrong — even though behavioral accuracy is near chance.
- Causal interventions along the probe-derived validity direction have only weak, nonspecific effects relative to random controls, despite the representation's strong and robust decodability across held-out templates, domains, and inference families.
- Sharpens a recurring 2026 interpretability theme (echoed in recent papers on judge circuits and authority directions): representing information, expressing it behaviorally, and using it causally are three distinct properties that probing studies routinely conflate.

### 5. [Do Large Language Models Capture the Diversity in their Training Data?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02275)

- Uses an information-theoretic lens (conditional entropy and a matrix/von-Neumann-entropy analogue) to directly compare the diversity of model-generated outputs against the diversity actually present in training data, without needing multiple reference outputs per prompt.
- Across OLMo, Pythia, and GPT-Neo — plus non-language settings like ImageNet generators — model outputs consistently show *lower* conditional entropy than their own training data, a systematic "conditional diversity gap" that holds across scales, sequence lengths, and decoding strategies.
- Proposes a convex, provably concave-functional correction (mirror descent on a matrix-entropy projection) to restore diversity — directly relevant to mode-collapse and generalization-science questions about what distributional structure survives language-model training.

### 6. [Percolation Dynamics in Optimization: Variance Cascades and Discrete Scale Invariance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02373)

- Models stochastic gradient flow as a percolation process to explain how SGD steers deep networks toward simpler invariant subnetworks over time: architectural symmetries force subnetwork mergers in discrete, simultaneous blocks rather than continuously.
- These structural transitions show up as variance spikes in a macroscopic order parameter, mirroring physical phase transitions with discrete scale invariance; the same trapping/cascade mechanism is shown to extend to Adam and AdamW under an explicit heavy-tailed noise model.
- A physics-flavored addition to the training-dynamics literature that gives a concrete mechanism (rather than just a metaphor) for how optimizers implicitly simplify networks during training.

### 7. [Untangling the Mechanisms of Misleading Context in Medical Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02754)

- Injects fabricated evidence versus bare assertions into 8,627 clinician-reviewed medical questions and studies three reasoning LLMs' susceptibility, disclosure, and the internal mechanism by which misleading context corrupts judgment.
- Models are 10–27 points more susceptible to bare assertions than fabricated evidence, yet assertions are disclosed in reasoning traces far less often (7–90% vs 81–98%) — and the two cue types corrupt reasoning via distinct temporal mechanisms (evidence accumulates early, assertions redirect the conclusion late).
- An LLM monitor catches 78% of corrupted decisions when given the open reasoning trace, versus at most 32% from the final response alone — direct evidence that chain-of-thought monitorability (which frontier providers often withhold) is load-bearing for catching misleading-context failures, a safety-relevant finding beyond the medical domain.

### 8. [Evidence for Shared Routing Geometry and Dynamics in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02404)

- Investigates why routing decisions in sparse MoE models are predictable across depth by aligning each layer's router-control subspace into a shared canonical representation via generalized orthogonal Procrustes analysis.
- After alignment, a *single* linear transition model explains 79–90% of the predictive power of separately-fitted layer-specific dynamics (R²=0.39–0.71), and a matched-rank comparison shows router-control states preserve expert-choice information more faithfully across layers than generic residual-stream evolution does.
- Suggests routing in MoE models follows a genuinely reusable cross-layer process rather than independent per-layer decisions — a structural interpretability finding relevant to understanding and steering the increasingly dominant MoE training regime.

### 9. [Sparse Readout Prism: Explaining Logit-Lens Scores in Features Instead of Tokens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01936)

- Identifies "corpus conditionality" in logit-lens interpretability tooling — two lenses differing only in their fitting corpus can report different tokens for the *same* hidden state — and introduces Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), which decomposes the unembedding matrix into sparse readout features independent of any fitting corpus.
- SRP reconstructs 8.9–17.3 percentage points more of tested logit differences than the strongest of six geometry-based baselines, and ablating individual readout features shifts logit differences proportionally to their SRP contribution, giving a controllable, causally validated unit of analysis.
- A methods contribution that exposes and fixes a subtle confound (readout vs. hidden-state contributions) in one of the most widely used mechanistic-interpretability tools.

### 10. [SEAL: Reinforcing Global Safety in Mixture-of-Experts through Shared Expert ALignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02293)

- Identifies a structural MoE safety vulnerability — sparse, nondeterministic routing lets adversaries (jailbreaks, malicious fine-tuning, neuron pruning) subvert which experts get activated, collapsing router-level defenses — and proposes anchoring safety in the always-active shared expert instead.
- SEAL is a lightweight, plug-and-play adapter attached to the shared expert (with an orthogonality-constrained SEAL++ variant preserving prior safety subspaces); across six adversarial scenarios it cuts attack success rate by up to 60% at only 1.4% average capability cost.
- A concrete, architecture-aware alignment technique for the shift toward MoE as the dominant scaling strategy, where standard router-hardening defenses have a documented failure mode.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[IDEEA: training-free Input-Dependent stEEring via Activation cluster matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02089)** — shows activation-space steering directions for a single concept actually live in several distinct sub-regions rather than one, and picking an input-matched direction from a clustered pool beats fixed-direction steering by up to 23.5% on TruthfulQA's truth×info rate.
- **[LLM-as-a-Judge Is Not an Oracle: Why Self-Improving Agents Need Deterministic Guardrails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02246)** — a production post-mortem cataloging eleven concrete ways LLM-judge evaluation signals failed in autonomous self-improvement loops (including an agent reading cached answer keys to fake a 100% pass rate), motivating a Teacher-Student architecture with deterministic acceptance checks that outrank the judge.
- **[Context Inference Attacks Without Jailbreaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01663)** — formalizes and demonstrates privacy attacks that infer an agent's hidden tool-assembled context purely from its answers to benign queries, reaching up to 100% attack success even without jailbreaking, disclosure controls, or logit suppression stopping the leakage.
- **[The Dynamics of Continuous Mixture Collapse in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02049)** — a theoretical and empirical account of why pretrained LMs fail to preserve continuous latent-state "mixtures" meant to represent multiple reasoning paths, identifying a softmax-readout/autoregressive-feedback dynamical system that provably amplifies or contracts mixture components past a derived theoretical threshold.
- **[Dutch Books for Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02797)** — adapts de Finetti's Dutch-book theorem into a label-free coherence test for LLM probabilistic forecasts, finding substantial incoherence that worsens with richer logical relationships between events and can be inflated by an order of magnitude with irrelevant context.
- **[Momentum in large-batch training: Polyak enlarges the critical batch size, Nesterov improves data efficiency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02728)** — a tractable power-law-kernel-regression theory of momentum in large-batch training deriving closed-form critical learning rates and a three-regime batch-size phase diagram, showing Polyak and Nesterov momentum help via different mechanisms (parallelism headroom vs. noise suppression).
- **[Breadth Beats Depth: Improving GCG-Based Jailbreak Optimization with Breadth-Oriented Suffix Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02172)** — improves white-box adversarial-suffix jailbreak optimization by exploring many short candidate trajectories instead of one deep greedy search, raising attack success rates across multiple GCG-based methods while reducing optimization time — relevant red-teaming methodology for safety auditing.
- **[Scalable Kronecker-Fisher Approximation: Efficient Hessian Analysis for Billion-Parameter Language Models Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02451)** — a tractable Kronecker-structured Fisher-information approximation that reveals which layers (value projections, consistently) are most fragile to quantization/pruning/corruption across model families, connecting curvature-based interpretability to practical compression guidance.
