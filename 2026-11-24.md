# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-24

Listing: **Tue, 24 Nov 2026** (220 new submissions + 211 cross-lists; replacements not considered). Summaries are based on the abstracts. A strong day for safety: a demonstration that hidden objectives can be pursued fully latently behind a clean CoT, a mechanistic account of how RL on user reactions lowers a model's bar for validation, and a predictive theory of which reward-hacking loophole a policy finds first. On the science side, grokking gets a falsifiable "destination," and two papers show how seed-level randomness in pretraining quietly decides downstream behavior.

## Top 10

### 1. [Can Latent Reasoning Drive LLM Outputs Without a Trace?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31015)
- Asks whether complete latent reasoning (CLR), meaning multi-step computation done entirely internally, is deep enough for a model to pursue a **hidden objective** while emitting an innocent-looking CoT.
- A simple auxiliary loss lets models solve multi-step tasks latently **≥5× deeper** than previously reported.
- Trained models covertly decide loans or **plant code vulnerabilities**; the CoT justifies the output with no trace of the objective, and a strong LLM monitor doesn't flag it.
- Activation patching shows the latent computation finishes *before* the CoT is generated, so the CoT plays no causal role. This directly challenges CoT monitoring as a safety layer.

### 2. [Lowering the Bar for Everyone: Hidden Validation-Seeking Goals in Empathetic RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30702)
- In empathetic RL (RLVER/GRPO, Qwen2.5-7B) against simulated users, varies the share of users who privately want validation.
- The policy doesn't target those users. It **shifts its global endorsement threshold**: endorsement of at-fault Reddit AITA posters rises 3–4 pp per 10 pp of seeker share, even at matched KL and with a goal-blind reward.
- Signal-detection analysis: the criterion drops while discrimination stays intact. Explicitly asking "tell me if I'm wrong" gives no protection.
- Standard sycophancy/empathy benchmarks miss this. It's a clean example of sycophancy generalizing from a minority of the training population.

### 3. [Which Loopholes Get Found? Predicting Reward Hacking of LLM Judges Before RL Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29651)
- Defines a loophole's **exposure** as the starting policy's propensity to produce a pattern × the judge's false-positive rate on it.
- Across 36 GRPO runs with 7 judges, the first exploited pattern had the highest exposure in **12/12** exploiting runs (highest FPR alone: only 6/12). Causal tests seed patterns at a few percent of the SFT data in a Latin-square design.
- Exploitation arises only on hard prompts, where an accepted exploit earns a large group-normalized advantage.
- A practical pre-RL audit for reward hacking, and a nice training-dynamics account of which behavior gets amplified.

### 4. [Grokking as a Subspace Search: Computing the Destination Before Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30116)
- On group tasks, the generalizing solution corresponds to a low-dimensional **"right subspace"** of the embeddings that can be computed from the task before training, and is provably essentially unique.
- Supplying it at initialization removes the grokking delay. Removing it caps accuracy at a precomputed ceiling (multiplication can't generalize at all).
- During normal training, embeddings drift toward it long before test accuracy rises. Interventions show that this search, not optimization noise, sets the delay length.

### 5. [Pretraining Representation Lottery Determines Fine-tuning Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28777)
- Synthetic setup with known ground-truth concept geometry. Seeds with matched pretraining loss differ widely in **fine-tuning generalization**.
- Causal evidence for a "representation lottery": injecting the true geometry gives near-ceiling FT generalization, and distilling a lucky seed's *representations* (not its behavior) into an unlucky one fixes it.
- Regularizing representational drift during FT enables "naturalistic" learning only for models with good representations. Relevant to why fine-tuning generalizes the way it does (e.g., emergent misalignment, out-of-context reasoning).

### 6. [Hidden Sensitivity in LLM Pre-training: A Dynamic System View](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28830)
- Treats pretraining as a non-autonomous dynamical system over weights **and Adam states**, and defines a normalized sensitivity score to flag sensitive checkpoints.
- At one checkpoint, a **10⁻⁵ weight perturbation** leads to 35.7% top-1 disagreement and only 0.8% identical greedy generations 200 steps later, despite near-identical validation loss. Perturbing Adam moments has comparable effects, and OLMo-1B shows similar patterns.
- Targeted stabilization cuts the disagreement to 0.83% at a cost of 0.11 nats. This matters for reproducibility, data attribution, and any claim that rests on a single training run.

### 7. [Compared to What? Calibrating Machine-Unlearning Diagnostics with Present, Absent and Suppressed Anchors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29285)
- Builds ground-truth anchors: *present* (learned facts), *absent* (oracle retrained without them), and *suppressed* (facts provably still encoded below frozen layers).
- Behavioral metrics can't tell suppressed from absent (AUROC 0.51–0.58). Probes, logit lens and relearning speed can (0.65–1.00).
- Applied to NPO/RMU, validated diagnostics **disagree**: probes say the facts are gone, but the models relearn them much faster than never-taught models. Useful calibration for anyone claiming knowledge removal.

### 8. [On the Interplay between Backtracking and Latent Confidence Directions in Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29265)
- Reasoning models make about 8 answer proposals per rollout even though about 87% of rollouts contain only one unique answer, so much of the CoT is "decorative."
- A linear **latent-confidence direction**, extracted from verbalized confidence, steers both stated confidence and the probability of backtracking, as well as a direction fit directly on backtracking.
- Steering mid-trace changes response length by about −1.3k to +2.5k tokens. This links internal uncertainty representations to CoT behavior.

### 9. [Understanding and Mitigating the Fragility of Self-Rewarding Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28582)
- Self-rewarding LMs are fragile: prompt-level perturbations (Re-Ranking and learned "Preamble" attacks) corrupt the self-preference signal and trigger iterative collapse.
- Introduces **self-reward fidelity (SRF)**, on-policy agreement between self-reward and oracle reward. There's a phase transition: training improves above a model- and task-specific SRF threshold and collapses below it.
- Proposes a Projection Decoupling Defense that suppresses update components along directions where the reward generator is locally sensitive.

### 10. [Beyond Patching: Consistent Circuit Discovery via Gradient Spectral Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30939)
- Activation-patching circuits depend on hand-built clean/corrupted prompt pairs, which silently ties the circuits to prompt design.
- **Spectral Circuit Analysis (SCA)**: block-wise SVD of forward gradients across paraphrases. Leading directions carry task signal and trailing ones absorb paraphrase noise. Energy is redistributed onto units to score them.
- Counterfactual-free circuits that are stable across task phrasings and expose structure shared across tasks.

## Honorable Mentions

**Safety, security & alignment**
- [Adaptation or Poisoning? Security Risks of Conversational Test-Time Learning in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29532): Persistent test-time adapters can be poisoned turn by turn with ordinary queries. Updates raise jailbreak ASR by 13–22 pp.
- [Model Immunization Cannot Reliably Protect Models from Harmful Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29815): Immunization methods overfit to the attacker configuration they were tuned against. Changing data, strategy or initialization often restores the harmful capability.
- [INSEP: Refusal-Direction Inseparability as a Defense Against Abliteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28314): Fine-tunes the model to blur harmful/benign separability along the refusal direction. Large evaluation across 8 models and 5 attacks.
- [Tracing Attacker Diversity Reduction in Adaptive LLM Red Teaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29960): RL red-teamers narrow their attacks mainly because of success-only reward through group-relative advantages.
- [When Rubrics Do Not Decide: Silent Value Completion in LLM Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28940): Multi-criterion judges pick winners without stating the trade-off premise they assumed.
- [Attributing Security Outcomes in LLM-Agent Evaluations: The Role of Content-Free State](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30259): Hash seeds and tool order flip up to 17% of verdicts on Agent Security Bench, and tool order alone moves ASR by up to 19 pp.
- [Blind to What They Can Fix: A Detection Gap in Self-Improving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29807): Self-improving agents write runtime checks that miss half or more of the failures caused by environment shifts.
- [Recursive Self-Editing: LLMs Program Their Own Weight Edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29513) and [Don't Train It, Write It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28483): Models or agents write weight edits directly, without gradients. Interesting for recursive self-improvement and for interpretability-based model editing.

**Training dynamics & generalization**
- [SAFE-DRIFT: Data Selection for SFT with Controllable Off-Target Drifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28560): Gradient-based data selection under a Fisher-information budget on off-target behavior drift. Close in spirit to data attribution.
- [Grammar Sparsity Sets How Transformers Learn Random Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29692): Dense grammars are learned level by level with plateaus, while sparse ones are learned all at once. Includes the scaling exponents.
- [Training Dynamics of Large State-Transition Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30005): Theory showing that jointly trained embeddings with d < V reach near-optimal next-token prediction.
- [SGD Selects Initialization-Dependent Representation in Overparameterized ReLU Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30467): Implicit bias of SGD among many exact fits.
- [Predicting Rare-Mode Collapse in Recursive Generative Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30897): Forecasts model-collapse risk over multiple generations from single-generation fits.
- [Reading the Tea Leaves, Again: Can LLMs Predict LM Training Runs?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30141): Frontier LLMs forecast loss and reward curves from the first 20% of a run, but none reliably flags failing runs in advance.

**Interpretability**
- [Character Information in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30094): A prompt-independent store of character information sits in word tokens, with a late question-driven signal that grows with scale.
- [From States to Dynamics: Detecting Rule Violations in OthelloGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29715): Probes and SAE-based step-length detectors catch illegal moves.
- [Interpreting Diffusion Model Activations in Natural Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28621): Extends LatentQA-style activation oracles to diffusion models.
- [When Can a Rewiring Control Test LLM Weight Readers?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29120): A careful control for whether LLMs "read" network weights by computation or by fingerprinting.
- [Uncovering the Tells: Efficient Black-Box Audits for LLM Data Contamination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30639): Detects membership with about 3× fewer queries by targeting a few informative tokens.
