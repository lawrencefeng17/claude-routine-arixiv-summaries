# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — May 24, 2026

Papers from the [cs.LG listing](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/recent) (submissions from May 22, 2026; 263 total). Top 10 selected for relevance to machine learning, language models, AI safety, alignment, interpretability, and training dynamics.

## Top 10

### 1. Represented Is Not Computed: A Causal Test of Candidate Algorithmic Intermediates in a Transformer
**[2605.22488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22488)** · Ishita Darade, Sushrut Thorat

A causal interpretability study on arithmetic in transformers. A model trained to extract base-digit representations achieves 99.83% accuracy, and linear probes successfully decode intermediate values that match the closed-form algorithm — yet causal patching shows these intermediates are **not transmitted through the circuit**. The actual computation routes N, B, and D separately and combines them late.
- Demonstrates that probing alone can yield systematically misleading conclusions about model computation, even with explicit algorithmic hypotheses
- Strong methodological lesson for mechanistic interpretability: representation ≠ computation

### 2. Post-Training is About States, Not Tokens: A State Distribution View of SFT, RL, and On-Policy Distillation
**[2605.22731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22731)** · Dong Nie

Proposes that the key differentiator across post-training methods (SFT, RL, distillation) is **where** supervision is applied — i.e., which (prompt, generated-prefix) states are trained on — not the loss function form.
- Mild fine-tuning improves performance with little knowledge loss; aggressive fine-tuning degrades retention significantly
- On-policy distillation from a *degraded* teacher can still outperform that teacher, because training states matter more than supervisor quality
- Lightweight RL improves task performance while preserving general knowledge

### 3. Reading Task Failure Off the Activations: A Sparse-Feature Audit of GPT-2 Small on Indirect Object Identification
**[2605.22719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22719)** · Mahdi Nasermoghadasi

Uses sparse autoencoder (SAE) features to identify which features in GPT-2 small correlate with failures on the IOI task (79.7% accuracy on 300 prompts). Finds 146 significantly associated features; feature 17,491 is most predictive (93.3% failure rate for "the keys" as object vs. 7.5% otherwise).
- Causal ablation shows correlation but not causal sufficiency — the feature predicts but doesn't cause failure
- Validates an interpretability audit pipeline; highlights limits of SAE-based causal claims

### 4. Why SGD is not Brownian Motion: A New Perspective on Stochastic Dynamics
**[2605.22644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22644)** · Igor Ignashin et al.

Challenges the standard treatment of SGD as a Langevin process. The Brownian motion assumption fails at practical learning rates; instead, the authors derive a **discrete Fokker-Planck equation** that diverges from the Langevin form at O(η²).
- Near critical points, SGD splits into confined modes (flat directions have no stationary distribution — variance grows indefinitely, corresponding to diffusion along loss valleys) and confined modes in sharp directions
- Empirically validated on vision and NLP models with qualitative separation between diffusive and confined eigenmodes
- Directly relevant to implicit regularization and generalization theory

### 5. Value-Gradient Hypothesis of RL for LLMs
**[2605.21654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21654)** · Arip Asadulaev et al.

Provides a theoretical account of why critic-free RL methods (PPO, GRPO) work for LLMs: the backward pass through attention **propagates costates whose conditional expectation equals the value gradient**, under mild conditions. Error depends on sampling variance and policy entropy.
- Decomposes RL's effect into value gradient contribution and available reward capacity
- Suggests an optimal placement in the pretraining trajectory for applying RL
- Bridges the empirical success of RLHF-style training with formal RL theory

### 6. On-Policy Consistency Training Improves LLM Safety with Minimal Capability Degradation
**[2605.21834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21834)** · Andy Han et al.

Proposes **OPCT**, which trains safety invariants using contrastive prompt pairs where the model generates its own responses. Addresses sycophancy, jailbreaks, and safety warnings simultaneously.
- Reduces sycophancy from 15.4% → 8.1%; maintains ~99% jailbreak defense on held-out attacks
- Avoids the 28-point MATH-500 drop that SFT baselines incur
- On-policy generation is key: SFT on the same contrastive data is substantially weaker

### 7. Clipping Bottleneck: Stabilizing RLVR via Stochastic Recovery of Near-Boundary Signals
**[2605.22703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22703)** · Shuo Yang et al.

Identifies a failure mode in RLVR (RL with Verifiable Rewards): PPO-style hard clipping discards informative near-boundary tokens, creating a training signal bottleneck. Proposes **Near-boundary Stochastic Rescue (NSR)**, which stochastically retains slightly out-of-bound tokens.
- Plug-and-play modification; shows consistent gains over DAPO and GSPO baselines across model sizes
- Addresses a practical stability issue in LLM post-training with RL

---

### 8. Vector Policy Optimization: Training for Diversity Improves Test-Time Search
**[2605.22817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22817)** · Ryan Bahlous-Boldi et al.

Standard scalar RL post-training produces low-entropy outputs that are poorly suited for best-of-N or evolutionary inference-time search. **VPO** modifies the GRPO advantage estimator to optimize for vector reward spaces, producing response sets where outputs specialize in different trade-offs.
- Matches or exceeds scalar RL baselines on pass@k and best@k; gaps widen at larger search budgets
- Enables evolutionary search that GRPO-trained models cannot achieve
- Suggests diversity in training may become essential as test-time compute scaling standardizes

---

### 9. Same Architecture, Different Capacity: Optimizer-Induced Spectral Scaling Laws
**[2605.21803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21803)** · Nandan Kumar Jha, Brandon Reagen

Shows that scaling laws are not just a function of model size and data — **optimizer choice determines how much spectral capacity is realized**. AdamW scales width-to-capacity weakly (β=0.44); Muon achieves near-linear scaling (β=1.02), a 2.3× improvement.
- Matched validation loss does not imply identical representation structure
- Optimizer-induced spectral differences can exceed effects from architectural changes
- Argues for optimizer as a first-class axis in representation scaling research

---

### 10. Relational Linear Properties in Language Models: An Empirical Investigation
**[2605.22532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22532)** · Giovanni Valer et al.

Investigates whether the embedding of a subject linearly predicts the unembedding of its relational object (e.g., *Paris → France* under a "capital of" relation). Introduces a KL-divergence-based probing technique that improves on Jacobian approximations.
- Relational linearity varies across models and is layer-dependent, aligning with known structure of how linguistic information is encoded
- Relational phrasing affects linearity, suggesting it's sensitive to surface form and not purely semantic

## Honorable Mentions

- **[2605.22217] Survive or Collapse: The Asymmetric Roles of Data Gating and Reward Grounding in Self-Play RL** — Finds that strict data gating (not reward design) is the key factor preventing collapse in self-play RL for LLMs; identifies the "Grounded Proposer Paradox."

- **[2605.22481] When Stronger Triggers Backfire: A High-Dimensional Theory of Backdoor Attacks** — Proves in closed form that attack success peaks at an intermediate trigger strength and then *declines*; the most damaging trigger direction is the minimum eigenvector of data covariance. Theoretical + CIFAR-10 validation.

- **[2605.21856] The Illusion of Reasoning: Exposing Evasive Data Contamination in LLMs via Zero-CoT Truncation** — Proposes the Zero-CoT Probe: truncating chain-of-thought generation exposes latent shortcut memorization. Introduces a continuous "Contamination Confidence" metric and handles paraphrase-based concealment.

- **[2605.22005] Check Your LLM's Secret Dictionary! Five Lines of Code Reveal What Your LLM Learned (Including What It Shouldn't Have)** — SVD of the unembedding matrix exposes semantically interpretable subspaces (no inference needed). Finds ethically concerning subspaces survive post-training alignment; proposes VCS/WPS metrics for safety auditing.

- **[2605.22472] Winner-Take-All Bottlenecks Enforce Disentangled Symbolic Representations in Multi-Task Learning** — Proves that WTA bottlenecks extract categorical latent factors in multi-task settings; empirically shows improved generalization; bridges symbolic and subsymbolic AI.
