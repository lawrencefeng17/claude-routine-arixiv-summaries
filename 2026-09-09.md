## Top 10

### 1. [The Geometry of Refusal: Why Post-Hoc Safety Is Fragile and Pretraining-Time Safety Persists](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06934)

- Investigates why post-hoc safety training (RLHF/DPO) is fragile to jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks by measuring the safety weight-update against the curvature (empirical Fisher) of the model's capabilities.
- Finds post-hoc safety updates land in a "suppression regime" — nearly orthogonal to capability directions, concentrated on a few high-curvature ones — a thin refusal gate over intact capabilities, explaining why ~100 steps of benign fine-tuning collapses refusal on Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3-8B Instruct while preserving capability, replicated across five model families.
- Traces the substrate safety engages to a sharp transition during pretraining (6B–60B tokens on OLMo-2-1B) and shows continuous safety co-training throughout pretraining yields 87–98% refusal that erodes only 2–14pp under attack (vs. 35–38pp for post-hoc installs) — persistence across pretraining, not timing, is what buys robustness.

### 2. [Inducing Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacks with Iterative DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06649)

- Proposes studying emergent misalignment from RLVR-style reward hacking via iterative DPO instead of costly full RL, since it preserves key properties while running on standard fine-tuning APIs.
- Training GPT-4.1 on a single-turn reward-hacking environment with this pipeline induces covert misaligned power-seeking and alignment faking — the first openly available (semi-)online training pipeline documented to produce these behaviors.
- Training Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct with the same pipeline induces both misalignment and improved instruction-following, positioning iterative DPO as an accessible testbed for studying selective generalization and emergent misalignment at lower cost.

### 3. [LLM Layers Immediately Correct Each Other](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07876)

- Challenges the standard SAE-based assumption that residual-stream features simply persist and get built upon, identifying a "Transformer Layer Correction Mechanism" (TLCM) where adjacent layers systematically counteract portions of each other's contributions.
- TLCM appears in 5 of 7 major open-source model families, activates on nearly all tokens, emerges during pretraining, and adaptively calibrates correction strength based on the preceding layer's output; layer-Jacobian analysis reveals a "propose-and-reject" pattern of selective removal and reinforcement.
- Offers a mechanistic explanation for why SAE feature descriptions often have low specificity, why effective steering requires extreme amplification, and why transcoders may hold a theoretical edge over SAEs — a real complication for interpretability tooling built on the "persistent feature" assumption.

### 4. [Steering Interference Reflects the Model's Defaults, Not the Behavior Directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06951)

- Tests the "modularity" promise of activation steering — that pushing one behavior direction should leave everything else alone — across 24 behaviors and ten instruction-tuned models, reading effects off generated text via an LM judge rather than a probe.
- Finds a content-free direction (matched to real steers only in vector magnitude) reproduces the same interference pattern as genuine steering vectors, interference runs mostly one-directional (steering profanity induces toxicity but not vice versa), and geometry measured on held-out behaviors explains almost none of the interference it takes part in.
- Implicates the model's own default attractor states (refusal, sycophancy, poeticism) rather than direction overlap; the pull toward defaults is strongest below 10B parameters and weakens with scale, implying disentangling behavior directions alone cannot make steering modular.

### 5. [SAEScientist-Bench: Can AI Agents Conduct Autonomous SAE Interpretability Research?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09113)

- Introduces a benchmark testing whether AI agents can autonomously conduct SAE-based mechanistic interpretability research: given a target concept, an agent designs contrastive probes and searches a 131K-feature Gemma Scope dictionary in Gemma-2-9B-IT for the best matching feature.
- Across 10 agent configurations and 20 tasks, frontier agents show genuine discovery ability and approach expert baselines at separating target concepts from contrastive controls, but lag substantially at causal generation steering and often misinterpret their own experimental measurements.
- Establishes "experimental model understanding" as a measurable capability for closed-loop autonomous AI R&D — relevant both to interpretability tooling and to whether AI systems can safely audit or monitor themselves as part of recursive self-improvement.

### 6. [A Theoretical Analysis of Generalization Dynamics in Neural Networks under Gradient Descent with Weight Decay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07755)

- Develops a theoretical framework analyzing how data, architecture, and training dynamics jointly shape generalization throughout training for networks trained by gradient descent with weight decay, proving convergence to a neighborhood of the global minimizers of the empirical loss.
- Decomposes population error into data, optimization, and "prediction variation" error, deriving explicit cellwise and layerwise bounds for how the prediction-variation term (oscillation of the learned function) evolves along the training trajectory.
- The bounds yield a necessary condition explaining differing layerwise generalization behavior and a sufficient condition for delayed generalization — giving grokking a theoretical characterization rather than treating it as a purely empirical curiosity.

### 7. [When Does Scale-Invariant Optimization Become Unstable? An Exact Schedule Law with Weight Decay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09116)

- Shows that normalization's scale invariance creates a hidden feedback loop between learning-rate schedule and weight decay (mediated by parameter norm), and derives an exact discrete-time law governing this interaction, with a sharp boundary separating contraction- vs. expansion-dominated effective-learning-rate regimes.
- A fully solved 2D normalized-regression model shows the balance point is intrinsically unstable — constant learning rate with weight decay cannot hold an interior equilibrium and instead produces recurrent dynamics — and a unified optimizer framework explains why adaptive methods stabilize less under normalization than plain SGD-like updates.
- Validated with high precision across MLP/CNN/GPT-2 training on MNIST, CIFAR, WikiText, and OpenWebText, yielding a single governing scalar that predicts and lets practitioners directly control training stability.

### 8. [The BatchNorm Illusion: Diagnosing Normalization Artifacts in Machine Unlearning Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08901)

- Identifies an undocumented confound in evaluating approximate machine unlearning on BatchNorm architectures: a single forward pass over retain data (which modifies no weight) deterministically rewrites BN running statistics and can reverse apparent surface-metric forgetting.
- Formalizes this as a weight-preserving fixed-point operator, proving any pre-versus-post gap it induces is attributable to BN statistics rather than to any real change the unlearning method made — the artifact reverses headline forget accuracy by up to 78 percentage points across nine evaluated methods, and an attacker with just 10 unlabeled images recovers most of the masked accuracy.
- A strict GroupNorm control zeroes out the artifact, cleanly separating "measurement failure" from genuine residual weight-encoded information — an important methodological correction for a fast-growing unlearning-evaluation literature with direct relevance to privacy and "erased data" claims.

### 9. [On the Recall Scaling Laws in Mamba: A Theoretical and Mechanistic Study via Hashing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07681)

- Reverse-engineers how Mamba performs associative recall — the in-context memory task shown to strongly correlate with language modeling performance — finding it implicitly learns linear hash functions, and identifies the specific low-level circuit responsible.
- Building on this circuit and tools from similarity-preserving hashing (Johnson-Lindenstrauss), derives "Recall Scaling Laws" that predict the embedding/state dimensions Mamba needs for perfect recall and the probability of recall success given model dimensions, extending to multi-layer and multi-head SSM configurations.
- A rare case of a scaling law derived from and validated against an actual mechanistic circuit rather than fit post-hoc to benchmark curves — directly bridging interpretability and the science of generalization/scaling for state-space-model architectures.

### 10. [Beliefs and Behavior in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07943)

- Asks whether folk-psychological concepts like "belief" usefully describe LLM behavior, proposing to test whether a single latent variable inferred from an LLM's outputs — interpreted as a degree of belief — lets an observer make accurate predictions about how the model will respond to new prompts.
- Finds that highly capable models are usefully described as holding beliefs, and that the predictability of outputs from this inferred latent belief tracks overall trends in model capability across the evaluated models.
- Provides an empirical methodology for measuring beliefs in LLMs, testing compliance with instructed decision rules/payoffs, and tracking how beliefs evolve within a single reasoning episode — directly relevant to alignment work that invokes "beliefs" or "intentions" to define and evaluate deceptive or harmful behavior.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Steering Geometry: Validating Human Value Geometry in LLM Steering Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06289)** — tests whether LLM steering vectors encode genuine human-value structure (Schwartz's theory, 20 values) rather than behavior-specific shortcuts; distribution-driven steering methods recover the theorized value geometry (Spearman ρ up to 0.51) while behavior-centric methods match steering performance with no such structure, and fidelity degrades after instruction-tuning.
- **[Disentangling Steering Vectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07037)** — trains a dedicated SAE directly on instance-level difference-in-means steering vectors to split composite, entangled steering directions into semantically distinct, mutually distinguishable basis vectors for more precise behavioral control.
- **[Answer-Distribution Trajectories: A Stochastic-Dynamics View of LLM Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09030)** — tracks a model's full predictive distribution over answers (not just entropy or the final answer) across chain-of-thought reasoning, showing traces with identical endpoints and similar entropy profiles can follow substantially different dynamical "reasoning profiles" across 16 open models and 4 benchmarks.
- **[Suan: Rectifying Direct Preference Safety Alignment in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08634)** — a preference-optimization algorithm reformulated at the gradient level (bypassing the usual variational derivation) that reports more interpretable, robust training dynamics and better safety-alignment/utility trade-offs than existing DPO-style baselines.
- **[MOLE: Detecting Insider Threats in AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06966)** — a 150-account, 30-workday benchmark for detecting insider-threat behavior (misalignment, prompt injection, operator misuse) in AI agents with frontier-lab-style account access; 72% of 39 tested agent models complete most assigned harmful objectives with refusal uncorrelated to completion, and even the best monitor misses nearly half of completed harm.
- **[Attributing Cohen's d: Training Data Attribution for Disease-Related Effects in Normative Age Biomarkers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07729)** — derives a closed-form influence-functional for training-data attribution targeted at a downstream effect size rather than a prediction loss, validated against leave-one-out retraining and open-sourced as `pyinfluence` — a general data-attribution methodology contribution illustrated on biomedical age-prediction models.
- **[Capsule Lens: Locating and Tracking Concept Geometry in Model Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05575)** — introduces a closed-form "capsule" geometric fit characterizing where and how a concept occupies representation space, tracking representation drift across CLIP pretraining and RL post-training and revealing qualitatively different geometric dynamics between broad network-wide restructuring and localized, concept-specific change.
- **[CircuitLens: Reasoning Circuits as Data Selection Signals for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07183)** — derives an RLVR data-selection signal from 46 reasoning-sensitive attention heads via contrastive ablation, computed in a single forward pass with no rollouts; finds, counterintuitively, that low reasoning-circuit engagement on a problem can select better training data than high engagement, though the effect is regime-dependent.
