## Top 10

### 1. The Curse of Multiple Mediators: Hidden Interaction Effects in Activation Patching
**[2606.27510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27510)** · Vaidyanathan, Arbour, Mueller, Niekum, Jensen

- Reveals a fundamental flaw in the standard **activation patching** estimand (NIE): it does not isolate the causal effect of a single component, but also absorbs interaction effects (INT) from how that component's output interacts with other components' states.
- Demonstrated on the GPT-2 IOI circuit: conditionally important components become either **invisible** (suppressed) or **artificially inflated** depending on the sign and magnitude of INT.
- Proposes treating INT as a diagnostic tool rather than noise — a large INT signals that a component's relevance is context-dependent, and that greedy circuit-search methods may misorder components.
- Core implication: widely-used mechanistic interpretability results built on activation patching may attribute effects incorrectly when the circuit is not fully factorial.

### 2. Learning to Reason with Curriculum II: Compositional Generalization
**[2606.27721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27721)** · Rajaraman, Huang, Dudik, Schapire, Foster, Krishnamurthy

- Studies compositional generalization through learning semiautomata simulation (state-tracking / sequential computation), providing a rigorous theoretical setting for chain-of-thought reasoning.
- **Autocurriculum**: recursively decomposing long sequences into shorter problems reduces supervised token requirements from polynomial to **subpolynomial** in sequence length — an exponential-in-log improvement.
- In RL with verifiable rewards, curriculum learning relaxes the reference-model requirement: instead of coverage at full sequence lengths, coverage at shorter block lengths suffices.
- A 82-page theoretical framework with direct implications for how LLM post-training should decompose complex tasks to achieve out-of-distribution generalization.

### 3. Democratic ICAI: Debating Our Way to Steering Principles from Preferences
**[2606.28294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28294)** · Kingslin, Natekar, Ranjan, Srivastava, Bhat, Karande

- Addresses a key weakness of preference-based alignment: single-pass preference explanations are too shallow to capture multi-criteria reasoning and produce weak steering principles.
- Proposes **structured persona debate** — multiple competing rationales generated by distinct LLM personas — to elicit richer decision signals from preference data.
- The resulting constitutions improve preference prediction accuracy and are rated more human-aligned than ICAI baselines on MuCE-Pref and LiTBench benchmarks.
- Highlights that *why* a preference holds matters as much as *which* option wins — the gap existing alignment pipelines systematically ignore.

### 4. VASAE: Naming SAE Dictionary Directions with Vocabulary-Aligned Anchoring
**[2606.27941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27941)** · Zhang, Yu, Abdallah, Lewis

- Addresses the ad-hoc nature of SAE feature naming by **training features to align with token vocabulary embeddings**, giving each feature an intrinsic name without post-hoc analysis.
- In GPT-2-small, ~90% of shallow-layer features align to a vocabulary token; Llama-3.1-8B achieves 92.8% alignment in early layers.
- Reconstruction quality is maintained — vocabulary anchoring does not trade off interpretability against fidelity.
- Limitation: deeper final layers show substantially less alignment, suggesting a qualitative shift in feature geometry at depth. Accepted to ICML 2026 Compositional Learning Workshop.

### 5. The Weakest Link Tells It All: Outcome-Supervised Process Reward Modeling via Learnable Credit Assignment
**[2606.27739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27739)** · Jia, Fang, Ding, Qiu, Yang, Wu, Chu, Zhao, Wang

- Trains **process reward models (PRMs)** from outcome labels only (no expensive step-level annotations) by framing the problem as Multiple Instance Learning.
- Core insight: "a reasoning chain is as strong as its weakest link" — introduces **Softmax-Weighted-Sum (SWS)** pooling to concentrate credit on the most likely-erroneous step.
- Theoretically justified via Bayes consistency proofs; empirically outperforms other outcome-supervised PRM approaches across multiple tasks and models.
- Closes a major practical gap in scalable reward modeling for math and coding domains.

### 6. CBD: API-Only LLM Black-Box Unlearning through Controlled Behavioral Divergence
**[2606.27683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27683)** · Xie, Lin, Gao, Kim

- Tackles LLM **machine unlearning via API only** (no access to weights), a critical safety scenario for proprietary deployed models.
- Key challenge: when forget and retain data share structural patterns, behavioral divergence methods over-unlearn; CBD uses **gradient-based statistics on auxiliary models** to isolate target-specific information.
- Achieves competitive performance to white-box and gray-box unlearning baselines on WMDP hazardous knowledge benchmarks while preserving general capability.
- Demonstrates that meaningful unlearning is tractable even without parameter access — relevant to auditing and compliance for third-party APIs.

### 7. How Width and Data Shape Generalization Scaling Laws in Quadratic Neural Networks
**[2606.28242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28242)** · Girardin, Troiani, Xu, Erba, Krzakala, Zdeborová

- Provides a **theoretical analysis of generalization scaling laws** in two-layer quadratic networks under ℓ₂-regularized ERM with finite samples and structured data.
- Generalization error follows **data-dependent power laws** controlled by the spectral structure of the target function, not just model size.
- Identifies distinct scaling phases as width grows, including transitions at the interpolation threshold, with phase boundaries determined by data covariance structure.
- Connects the theory of neural scaling laws to the geometry of the learning problem — coefficients (not just exponents) carry signal about data and architecture.

### 8. Aurora: A Leverage-Aware Spectral Optimizer
**[2606.27715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27715)** · Dewulf, Pai, Yang, Zhang, Keigwin

- Identifies a failure mode of the **Muon optimizer** on tall projection matrices: update row norms become highly non-uniform, causing some neurons to receive negligibly small updates across all of training.
- Aurora enforces **row-uniform update norms** while preserving Muon's polar-factor geometry, fixing this leverage imbalance without abandoning spectral preconditionng.
- Outperforms Muon in pre-training experiments; gains scale with MLP expansion factor, suggesting compounding benefits for wide networks.
- Practically relevant: Muon is now widely adopted for LLM pre-training; Aurora is a targeted, low-overhead fix to a systematic bias in that pipeline.

### 9. From Tokens to States: LLMs as a Special Case of World Models and the Continuous Path Beyond
**[2606.28127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28127)** · Dubois

- Reconceptualizes the LLM-vs-world-model debate: argues LLMs are **a degenerate special case of world models** where tokens constitute the full state space and appending tokens is the only action.
- Proposes a continuous spectrum from next-token prediction → multi-token prediction → future-summary prediction → next-latent prediction (JEPA), with existing work at various points.
- Identifies two open questions for moving up this spectrum: (1) whether large-scale self-supervised data exists for continuous-state prediction, and (2) whether transformers can generalize beyond token space.
- A useful conceptual frame for researchers thinking about post-NTP architectures and the theoretical foundations of "world models" as a category.

### 10. Retroactive Advantage Correction: Closed-Form V-Trace Bias Correction for Delay-Aware RLHF
**[2606.27580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27580)** · Raj

- Addresses a practical but underexplored issue in RLHF infrastructure: **reward signals often arrive asynchronously** (due to code execution, human review, or rate-limited verifiers), introducing policy-lag bias into advantage estimates.
- Retroactive Advantage Correction (RAC) queues pending rewards and reintegrates them as clipped residuals in future optimization steps, reducing policy bias by up to **47.9× at two-slow-channel configurations** in tabular MDP experiments.
- Integrates with PPO and GRPO via minimal code changes; lower compute cost than synchronous baselines.
- Accepted at ICML 2026 Workshop on RL from World Feedback; provides a formal framing of asynchronous advantage bias as a concrete training dynamics problem.
