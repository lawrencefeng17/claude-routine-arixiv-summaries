### 1. SAE Interventions are Unreliable: Post-Intervention Recovery of Suppressed Behavior
**arXiv:2606.18322** | Mingyue Cui, Linghui Shen, Xingyi Yang

- Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) are increasingly deployed as AI safety tools — but clamping identified "unsafe" features does not contain the underlying behavior.
- The authors frame this as a **post-intervention recovery** problem: even with interventions held active during generation, models achieve a **95.8% recovery rate** on refusal-steering targets by routing around the clamped features through the SAE reconstruction residual.
- Conclusion: feature-level control ≠ behavioral containment; the SAE residual stream provides an alternative pathway that bypasses interventions. Critical result for anyone using mechanistic interpretability as a safety tool.

### 2. From Sparse Features to Trustworthy Proxies: Certifying SAE-Based Interpretability
**arXiv:2606.18383** | Dibyanayan Bandyopadhyay, Asif Ekbal

- Complements paper #1: provides a formal certification framework for when SAE explanations *are* faithful, via sparse proxies that replace hidden activations with SAE reconstructions.
- Derives an upper bound on proxy risk from four measurable terms: reconstruction gap, concept-pool mismatch, proxy risk, and sparse complexity — all **non-vacuous at practical sample sizes** on GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2B, and Llama-3-8B.
- Key finding: **later layers of Llama-3-8B are easier to certify** (stronger local fidelity, weaker error propagation); feature-shuffling experiments can distinguish genuine semantic patterns from statistical sparsity.

### 3. What Does the Weight Norm Control in Grokking? Logit-Scale Mediation under Cross-Entropy
**arXiv:2606.18465** | Truong Xuan Khanh

- Isolates the mechanism by which weight norm drives grokking delay: it acts **indirectly**, primarily by scaling the output logits — not through a direct path to generalization.
- Controlling logit scale alone (via output temperature) reproduces the full grokking delay range; **logit scale accounts for ~97% of delay variation (R² = 0.97)** while weight norm contributes only 1–2% additionally.
- Proximal mechanism is **softmax saturation**: once logits are large, cross-entropy loss gradient becomes too small to escape memorization. This is loss-function dependent — under MSE the relationship breaks down.

### 4. Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection
**arXiv:2606.19168** | Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu

- Addresses the threat that models can **compose benign knowledge into unsafe behaviors** even when trained entirely on safe data — standard data-filtering is therefore insufficient for alignment.
- Proposes **Safety Reflection Pretraining**: short safety reflection passages are regularly interspersed into pretraining corpora, embedding self-monitoring directly into the language-modeling objective.
- 1.7B-parameter experiments show improved safety classification and reduced attack success; a controlled synthetic environment (MedSafetyWorld) confirms the method outperforms data-rewriting approaches at preventing unsafe generalization.

### 5. SFT Overtraining Predicts Rank Inversion via Entropy Collapse Under RLVR
**arXiv:2606.18487** | Siddharth Aphale, Kelly Liu

- Identifies a **counterintuitive failure mode**: selecting the SFT checkpoint with highest pass@1 for GRPO can degrade RL performance because high SFT accuracy compresses the rollout distribution.
- On Qwen2.5-Coder-3B, peak GRPO pass@10 falls from **0.806 to 0.481** when initializing from an overtrained SFT checkpoint; pre-RL entropy is positively correlated with RL outcome.
- Standard fixes (KL-to-reference regularization, label smoothing) do not rescue the collapsed model — the damage is structural. Proposes entropy monitoring as a diagnostic to identify at-risk SFT checkpoints.

### 6. Effects of Sparsity and Superposition on Loss in Simple Autoencoders
**arXiv:2606.18538** | Mriganka Basu Roy Chowdhury, Eric McLaughlin Weiner

- Provides the **first rigorous mathematical analysis** of superposition — the phenomenon (from Elhage et al. 2022) where distinct features are encoded as non-orthogonal directions in compressed spaces.
- Establishes tight upper and lower bounds on L2 reconstruction loss for sparse-input autoencoders with power activation functions, with particularly sharp bounds in the high-sparsity regime.
- Lays theoretical foundations for understanding when and why superposition arises, and highlights several open problems in the geometry of feature representation.

### 7. DRIFT: Refining Instruction Data via On-Policy Data Attribution
**arXiv:2606.18307** | Zefan Wang, Lincheng Li, Tianyu Yu, Yuan Yao

- Standard influence-function data attribution suffers from **proximity gaps** (off-policy validation targets) and bias toward gradient norms; DRIFT addresses both.
- Uses the model's own **on-policy rollouts as validation targets** and applies weighted scoring by trajectory correctness plus a gradient-norm debiasing correction — no external reference data needed.
- Consistently raises the performance ceiling on 7B-parameter models compared to existing data curation approaches; relevant to understanding which training data shapes model behavior.

### 8. Explaining Attention with Program Synthesis
**arXiv:2606.19317** | Amiri Hayes, Belinda Li, Jacob Andreas

- Converts attention head behavior into readable Python programs by extracting attention patterns and using an LM to synthesize programs that replicate them from input text alone.
- Fewer than **1,000 generated programs reproduce attention behavior with >75% similarity**; replacing a quarter of all attention heads with their program surrogates causes only a 16% average increase in perplexity while preserving QA capabilities.
- Represents progress toward symbolic mechanistic interpretability: opaque neural computations replaced by human-readable, executable code.

### 9. Sparsity Curse: Understanding RLVR Model Parameter Space from Model Merging
**arXiv:2606.18521** | Chenrui Wu, Zexi Li, Jiajun Bu, Jiangchuan Liu, Haishuai Wang

- RLVR training produces sparse parameter updates — but rather than enabling clean merging, this creates a **"sparsity curse"**: updates are distributed in near-orthogonal subspaces, making aggregation destructive.
- Proposes SAR-Merging, which resolves conflicts in overlapping update regions via **Fisher Information-based sensitivity arbitration**, followed by magnitude-aware sparsification to preserve fragile reasoning pathways.
- Substantial improvements on mathematical and coding benchmarks for both single-model enhancement and multi-capability fusion; reveals non-obvious structure in how RLVR edits the parameter space.

---

### 10. Mechanism-Guided Selective Unlearning for RLVR-Induced Reasoning
**arXiv:2606.19222** | Chenyu Zhou, Qiliang Jiang, Shuning Wu, Xu Zhou

- Introduces MAST, a selective unlearning method that targets only a **small subset of attention-projection tensors** (ranked by task-specific metrics) rather than all parameters.
- On Qwen models, achieves meaningful forgetting on target benchmarks (MATH performance: 45→37) while **preserving retained tasks** (GSM8K, general math) — full-parameter unlearning causes collapse on protected datasets.
- Demonstrates that RLVR-induced reasoning is localized enough to be surgically removed, raising questions about where and how reasoning capabilities are stored in the parameter space.

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.18697** — *Stealthy World Model Manipulation via Data Poisoning* (Yibin Hu et al.): Bilevel optimization poisons a small subset of training data to steer world models toward adversarial dynamics while evading residual and CUSUM-based detection; undermines current defenses in continuous control. Relevant to AI safety and data poisoning.

- **arXiv:2606.19036** — *Geometric and Stochastic Analysis of Discontinuities in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts* (Tho Tran Huu et al.): Classifies discontinuities in SMoE by order using measure theory, proves that random inputs almost surely hit an order-1 discontinuity first (with explicit probability bounds), and proposes a smoothing mechanism that improves performance on language and vision tasks.

- **arXiv:2606.18694** — *Attention as Frustrated Synchronization* (Joshua Nunley): Proposes token representations as phases on a torus governed by a learned complex coupling kernel; beats a tuned RoPE-SwiGLU transformer at the 1M parameter scale, positioning next-token prediction as synchronization frustrated by data. Mechanistically suggestive.

- **arXiv:2606.18810** — *Learning from Own Solutions: Self-Conditioned Credit Assignment for RL with Verifiable Rewards*: Addresses reward sparsity in RLVR by conditioning credit assignment on the model's own verified solutions, improving training efficiency and stability; relevant to scalable alignment and RLHF dynamics.
