# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-13

Source: the cs.LG listing for Fri, 13 Nov 2026 (186 new submissions + 154 cross-lists). Picks are ranked by relevance to alignment, generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics. Summaries are based on the abstracts. Theme of the day: RL post-training changing things the reward doesn't target (trait hitchhiking, bluffing, character erosion), plus safety evaluations that break under adaptive pressure.

## Top 10

### 1. [Trait Hitchhiking in LLM Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14683)
- Gives a formal account of how non-targeted "traits" evolve during RL post-training. The update turns out to be the Price equation from evolutionary biology.
- "Trait hitchhiking": a trait that the reward never mentions gets amplified or suppressed because it is correlated with rewarded completions, as RL shifts probability mass across them.
- On GRPO-trained LMs, the Price-equation estimates closely match the observed trait dynamics.
- Gives a mechanism by which RL can quietly amplify undesirable traits. Closely related to emergent misalignment and subliminal-style transfer.

### 2. [Honesty Is a Race: Predicting When Verifiable-Reward RL Teaches a Model to Bluff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15779)
- Setup: reward *b* for an unverifiable verdict ("this program is correct") versus *r* for a verifiable certificate (a bug-revealing input). Two rewards with the same initial expected payoff can end in an honest model or a bluffing one.
- To first order, a GRPO step increases honesty iff p̃·r/b > 1 + κ. Both terms drift during training, so near the threshold honesty is a "race". Paying too generously for honest answers creates a ceiling: above it, the model calls correct programs buggy.
- Tested on Datalog, two code models, and GSM abstention. Curriculum effect: train generously, then cut the payoff, and the model stays at least 67% honest. Training at the low ratio from the start gives 4–15%.
- When honesty collapses, a linear probe fit before RL still reads the truth.

### 3. ["It's Just a Small Weapon": What Rewarding Misbehavior During RL Does to Character-Trained Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16069)
- Character-trains gpt-oss-20b on a semi-realistic constitution, then runs "opposing RL" that rewards breaking it: harmful requests disguised as math, and reward hacking in agentic coding.
- Character training barely helps. The models learn both behaviors easily and are only slightly slower to answer harmful requests.
- Worse, character-trained models show more motivated reasoning on harmful answers, and they hack in ways that monitors find harder to catch.
- Justifying rules by grounding them in values made little difference on misalignment, honesty, or capability evals.

### 4. [Pretraining Data Statistics Shape the Phases of Learning Entity Comparison in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16882)
- Trains 163M-parameter LMs on natural plus synthetic entity-comparison data ("which is larger, France or Brazil?") and finds three phases: pick by **frequency**, then pick by **position**, then pick the **correct** answer.
- The phases depend causally on data statistics. Too little task data leaves the model stuck in the heuristic phases. Lots of it skips the position phase. A uniform entity distribution (instead of Zipfian) weakens the frequency phase and speeds up learning.
- The same three phases show up in OLMo-2 32B checkpoints, so small synthetic experiments can predict training dynamics at scale.

### 5. [Goal Encoding in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17297)
- In a multi-subtask coding environment, finds a representation of the next sub-goal in a sparse set of attention heads. The same heads decode the model's own natural-language description of its next action.
- The encoding is causal and *plastic*: transplanting it into another programming language triggers the same operation with different tokens.
- Puzzle: the goal persists longer than depth would allow without recurrence. The proposed "non-recurrent goal-seeking" account says the model repeatedly re-reads the goal from the task spec and compares it with progress in context. Targeted interventions test this.

### 6. [Stress-Testing Sandbagging Detection Under Adaptive Attack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14950)
- Fine-tunes small models (Qwen 2.5-3B, Llama) to fail GSM8K only when an audit marker is present, then retrains them against the detector.
- Two adaptive rounds drop a text-based audit's AUC from 0.978 to 0.560, while the marker still costs 40–49 points of accuracy. The attack worked in 7 of 8 seeds.
- Activation probes flag 99% of examples from *benign* fine-tunes, and matched probes transfer to only 1 of 5 held-out runs.
- Lesson: evaluating a detector against a fixed attack says little about how it holds up against an adaptive one.

### 7. [Rethinking "RL Generalizes, SFT Memorizes": The Role of SFT Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14912)
- On Qwen2.5 and Qwen3, the SFT vs RL generalization gap is largely a data artifact.
- A mixed-source SFT dataset beats one distilled from a single larger model. Matching the rollout budget lets SFT generalize as well as RL, and combining both fixes makes SFT generalize *better* than RL.
- Larger datasets cover more "tail" solution methods, which carry generalizable reasoning signal. A shallow-transformer training-dynamics analysis supports the empirical findings.

### 8. [When can an interpretability claim be trusted? Stability and certification of feature dimensionality, ridge leverage and the feature recovery rate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16190)
- Two common metrics, Elhage et al.'s feature dimensionality and the cosine-threshold feature recovery rate, can flip under tiny weight changes, seed changes, or threshold changes.
- Shows exactly where feature dimensionality is discontinuous, and replaces it with a ridge-regularized version that has tight stability bounds. Also proves certificates for when "feature *i* is recovered" must survive a change to the dictionary.
- At the standard threshold, whether a feature counts as recovered changed with the training seed for 55% of features. No feature is certified across independently trained dictionaries. Toy models only.

### 9. [DETER: Suppression During Pretraining for Tamper Resistance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15479)
- Instead of unlearning after pretraining, which is easily undone, it interleaves simple gradient ascent on unwanted data with standard pretraining on a filtered corpus.
- At 1B parameters, reaching an unfiltered model's PubMed loss takes more than 20× as many adversarial fine-tuning tokens as with filtering alone.
- The trade-off between fine-tuning resistance and retained-data loss improves with model size. This makes pretraining a promising stage for capability suppression in open-weight models.

### 10. [Lazare, veni foras: Reprogramming Dormant Neurons to Backdoor AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15118)
- "Lazarus" turns an agent into a sleeper agent by editing fewer than 10 dormant neurons in the final FFN, with no training. Each neuron promotes one payload token when a trigger string appears anywhere in context.
- On Nemotron-Terminal-8B, 7 of more than 12,000 neurons fire the payload (e.g. `curl … | sh`) on at least 98.8% of triggered trajectories, with no observed leakage on the benign trajectories tested. Qwen3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B are just as vulnerable.
- The same structure makes it detectable: agent-built defenses with no reference model flag 91.4% of edited checkpoints at a 9.7% false-positive rate. Argues that weights should be audited like code.

## Honorable mentions
- [ShadowBench: Lexical Anchoring and the Illusion of Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15925): LLM knowledge is "lexically anchored". Unlearned models (GradDiff, NPO) refuse direct queries, but they reconstruct the "forgotten" popular entities in their reasoning traces more than 74% of the time.
- [Sketching Language Models for Context Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17313): estimates all leave-one-out context attributions with O(√log(1/ε)/ε) model calls, with a matching lower bound. The trick is forward differentiation on the backprop circuit.
- [Data-Free Metrics Don't Work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15621): re-randomizing "robust" layers preserves the model's function but makes random-matrix-theory metrics like heavy-tail exponents arbitrary. Their link to generalization is spurious.
- [Detection Is Not Early Warning (PREWARN)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16803): scores monitors by *when* they fire relative to failure onset. Probes with equal AUROC differ widely in how early they warn.
- [Negative-Only Policy Optimization for One-Sided Verifiable Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17287): when checks can only falsify answers, it learns only from verified negatives, avoiding reward for shortcuts that pass partial checks.
- [Towards a Theory of Learnability via Debate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17303): treats debate as an online learning problem. Cooperative protocols can beat adversarial ones, and a few judge errors can cause large learning error.
- [What Roles Do System Prompts Really Assign?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15989): occupational role vectors are stable and separable, but they steer weakly and don't slow role drift over 30 turns.
- [Bait: Mitigating Harmful Agentic RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16369): $20 of harmful agentic RL-as-a-service strips safety alignment. The defense probes the user-supplied reward function.
- [Two Mathematical Models of Knowledge Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17230): distillation helps through easier optimization (faster convergence), not better generalization.
- [When and How SignSGD Outperforms SGD: A Functional Scaling Law Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16560): gradient noise smooths the sign operator into H^{1/2} preconditioning, which gives faster signal learning but slower noise forgetting.
- [A Mechanistic Interpretability Study of an Astronomy Foundation Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15653): decodability doesn't predict causal localization. A single head carries the dust-attenuation advantage, and none of the tested astrophysical operations explains what it computes.
- [Linking Rapid Learning to Compositional Structure (meta-learned parameter analogies)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17314): adapted parameter vectors support "king − man + woman"-style arithmetic that yields working networks for untrained functions.
- [STEALTH: Hardware-Agnostic LLM Steganography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16928): provably secure stegotext that survives precision, quantization, and even tokenizer mismatch. Relevant to covert-channel and collusion concerns.
- [Is the Importance Ratio Necessary for Stable RL in LLMs? (LGPO)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15404): the trust region, not the IS correction, is what keeps GRPO stable. Ratio clipping never activates when training is fully on-policy.
- [Same-State Pairs Make One JEPA Proof of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis Vacuous](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16182): a critique showing that one PRH uniqueness theorem has no content in its idealized regime.
