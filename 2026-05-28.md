# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — May 28, 2026

*Topics: machine learning, language models, AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization*

## Top 10 Papers

### 1. Transformers Provably Learn to Internalize Chain-of-Thought
**arXiv:2605.28600** | Yixiao Huang, Hanlin Zhu, Zixuan Wang, Jiantao Jiao, Stuart Russell, Somayeh Sojoudi, Song Mei

First theoretical analysis of Implicit Chain-of-Thought (ICoT): multi-layer transformers can provably learn to absorb explicit reasoning steps into hidden representations, recovering the sample efficiency of CoT while removing inference-time token overhead.

- Proposes **Log-ICoT curriculum** that removes thinking tokens in geometric batches rather than one-at-a-time, enabling an L-layer transformer to master k-parity with polynomial samples and logarithmic stages
- Proves progressive layer-wise internalization of reasoning, confirmed empirically
- Addresses a key open question in LLM reasoning: *why* scratchpad removal often degrades performance, and when it need not

---

### 2. Fine-Tuning Dynamics of In-Context Factual Recall in Transformers
**arXiv:2605.27774** | Ruomin Huang, Eshaan Nichani, Jason D. Lee, Rong Ge

Theoretical study of how fine-tuning teaches transformers to combine in-context learning with retrieval of parametric knowledge. Introduces the **IC-recall task** (infer a hidden relation from context pairs, then answer a query) with MLP associative memory modeling stored knowledge.

- Proves a one-layer transformer converges to a specific pairwise attention pattern enabling IC-recall, using only polylogarithmic samples relative to stored knowledge triplets
- Characterizes which attention structures emerge and when, bridging mechanistic understanding with learning theory
- Empirically verifies the predicted attention pattern arises even with pretrained (not constructed) MLP layers

---

### 3. Sign-Aware Gated Sparse Autoencoders: Modeling Anticorrelated Features with Bi-Jump-ReLU Activations
**arXiv:2605.28149** | Bartosz Wieciech, Zmnako Awrahman, Marcin Czelej, Victor Hugo Jaramillo Velasquez, Wioletta Stobieniecka

Standard SAEs require non-negative activations, which wastes capacity when opposite concepts (e.g., "hot" vs. "cold") share a direction. **SA-GSAE** introduces signed-magnitude gating so one latent encodes both polarities.

- Polarity-sensitive gate + Bi-Jump-ReLU activation; auxiliary reconstruction prevents gate collapse
- Half-width SA-GSAE matches or outperforms full-width Gated SAE (double width) on Pythia-1B and SmolLM3-3B
- Achieves **100×–500× dead-feature reductions** on MLP-output cells — a significant efficiency win for SAE-based interpretability pipelines

---

### 4. Semantic Optimal Transport for Sparse Autoencoder Feature Matching and Circuit Compression
**arXiv:2605.28567** | Tue M. Cao, Nguyen Do, My T. Thai

Addresses two core SAE analysis problems — cross-layer feature matching and circuit compression — by representing each feature as an activation-weighted distribution over hidden states and comparing via Wasserstein distance in a shared semantic space.

- Proven invariant to activation rescaling, stable under perturbations, and recovers true matches under finite-sample margin conditions
- Outperforms decoder-vector baselines for feature matching; enables **automatic compression of feature circuits** into interpretable components
- Provides a principled, geometry-aware alternative to cosine similarity for feature correspondence

---

### 5. Mitigating Adaptive Attacks against Reasoning Models with Activation Consistency Training
**arXiv:2605.28467** | Avidan Shah, Jannik Brinkmann, Rico Angell

Proposes **Activation Consistency Training (ACT)** — a self-supervised defense for reasoning LLMs that supervises internal representations rather than outputs, comparing activations between clean and adversarially modified prompts.

- ACT is competitive against prompt injection and jailbreaks across five reasoning models, using only clean/modified prompt pairs (no labeled attack data)
- Defense is encoded as a **roughly linear shift in activation space**, enabling recovery of a single steering direction that controls refusal behavior
- Remains effective even when chain-of-thought traces are replaced with those from undefended models — activations matter more than reasoning text

---

### 6. Understanding Generalization and Forgetting in In-Context Continual Learning
**arXiv:2605.28705** | Guangyu Li, Meng Ding, Lijie Hu *(ICML 2026)*

First formal theoretical framework for in-context continual learning: analyzing how transformers process sequences of heterogeneous tasks within a single prompt via shared attention.

- Standard attention mechanisms **inevitably induce inter-task interference** through context aggregation, creating systematic bias
- Derives a **bias-variance-interference decomposition** characterizing when historical context helps vs. hurts performance
- Formally explains order sensitivity and performance decay in longer prompts — observed phenomena now have rigorous mechanistic accounts

### 7. PEFT-Arena: Understanding Parameter-Efficient Finetuning from a Stability-Plasticity Perspective
**arXiv:2605.28819** | Yangyi Huang, Ruotian Peng, Zeju Qiu, Jiale Kang, Yandong Wen, Bernhard Schölkopf, Weiyang Liu

Benchmarks PEFT methods through the lens of stability (forgetting resistance) vs. plasticity (target-task adaptation), with geometric analysis of weight-space spectral properties and activation-space representations.

- **Orthogonal fine-tuning** achieves the best stability-plasticity Pareto frontier among all methods tested
- Final checkpoints often *overshoot* a better operating point; **path-wise rewinding** as a post-hoc strategy recovers it without retraining
- Provides a principled diagnostic framework for selecting PEFT methods based on how much forgetting is acceptable

### 8. Activation Steering for Synthetic Data Generation: The Role of Diversity in Downstream Safety Detection
**arXiv:2605.28664** | Vijeta Deshpande, Tootiya Giyahchi, Veena Padmanabhan, Leman Akoglu, Anna Rumshisky

Systematic evaluation of whether activation steering can generate high-quality training data for safety classifiers, covering multiple models, steering methods, and evaluation axes.

- Increasing steering strength reduces response diversity — the key bottleneck for downstream utility
- AS-generated data outperforms prompting-based data on 3 of 4 safety concepts, but only in a narrow regime jointly satisfying success, coherence, and diversity
- Proposes the **harmonic mean of these three metrics** as a practical selection heuristic; it correlates more reliably with downstream classifier performance than any single metric

---

### 9. Law of Neural Interaction: Depth-Width Shape, Interaction Efficiency, and Generalization
**arXiv:2605.27989** | Wenjie Sun, Jinning Yang, Shuai Zhang, Mengnan Du

Extends the concept of superposition to gradient space, defining **neural interaction** as a measure of representational efficiency, and finds that good generalization correlates with operating near an efficient-interaction interval determined by depth-width ratio.

- The efficient interaction interval remains relatively stable as compute budgets scale — suggesting a transferable architectural principle
- Models initialized near this ratio perform consistently better on MMLU-Pro benchmarks
- Bridges gradient dynamics, architectural choices, and downstream generalization in a unified framework

---

### 10. IRDS: Interpretable RLVR Data Selection via Verifier-Coupled Sparse Autoencoder Coverage
**arXiv:2605.28247** | Yuhan Li, Mingxu Zhang, Dazhong Shen, Ying Sun

Uses SAE-derived clusters to select RLVR training instances in an interpretable, auditable way, solving a **verifier-coupled coverage objective** via greedy optimization to find instances that are hard but learnable.

- +3.9/+4.0 pp accuracy improvement on two Qwen models; +0.5 pp on Llama-3.1-8B across six math reasoning benchmarks
- Far more compute-efficient than trajectory-based data selection alternatives
- Makes the data selection rationale transparent — each selected instance is traceable to an SAE feature cluster the model underperforms on

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2605.28649** — *Interpretability-Guided Layer Selection over Subspace Projection: SAEs as Stethoscopes, Not Scalpels, for Raw Task Vector Model Editing* (Lei et al.): SAE subspace projection discards ~97% of task vector energy; instead uses SAE-derived specificity metrics to select which layers receive unmodified task vectors — improves Number Theory accuracy from 29.6% to 39.4% on Gemma-3-4B-IT.

- **arXiv:2605.28751** — *Extrapolative Weight Averaging Reveals Correctness-Efficiency Frontiers in Code RL* (Zheng et al.): Training Code-RL checkpoints under varying reward constraints traces a correctness-efficiency Pareto frontier; extrapolated weight-averaged checkpoints serve as complementary policies, improving pass@250 on hard LeetCode benchmarks by 3.3% over single-checkpoint baselines.

- **arXiv:2605.28328** — *Learning the Error Patterns of Language Models* (Kim, Berg-KirkPatrick, D'Antoni): LLMs fail in predictable, domain-specific ways; proposes **prefix filters** (symbolic constraint functions) learned efficiently via the Palla algorithm, boosting Qwen2.5-1.5B TypeScript compile rates by 60%+ to near Llama-3.1-8B performance.

- **arXiv:2605.28573** — *Efficient Pre-Training of LLMs through Truncated SVD Layers* (Kamali et al.): TSVD maintains both low-rank representations and orthonormal weight matrices during training with a spectral-energy-based adaptive rank heuristic — achieves comparable-or-better performance than full-parameter training at significantly lower compute cost.
