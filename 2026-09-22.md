## Top 10

### 1. [Why Ghost Outputs Teach: A Kernel-Based Understanding of Subliminal Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23260)

- Gives the first learning-dynamics-based theory of subliminal learning — where a student model picks up a teacher's unrelated behavioral traits by matching semantically-irrelevant "ghost" outputs, with no task labels ever shown.
- Derives a chained cross-task kernel linking ghost-output supervision to task-prediction changes via shared backbone representations, proving the transfer operator is Positive Semi-Definite under shared initialization (so it can't help but align student with teacher's true objective).
- Explains why ghost-output dimensionality bottlenecks transfer and why high-entropy random noise outperforms structured data as a subliminal-transfer probe — three separate empirical puzzles resolved from one framework.

### 2. [On Mitigation of Subliminal Learning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22215)

- Tracks subliminal trait acquisition *throughout* fine-tuning (not just final checkpoints) across Qwen/Gemma/Llama 1.5B–8B models, finding it's highly non-monotonic — transient spikes, reversals, trait-specific transfer failures.
- Introduces "liminal training," an annealed KL-regularized fine-tuning method that constrains early drift from the base model, beating paraphrasing and layer-freezing as mitigations.
- Shows the effect generalizes beyond animal-preference toy tasks to language-style transfer, and that regularization *timing* matters — early KL constraints work; late ones don't.

### 3. [Look Before You Steer: Geometry Predicts SAE Feature Steerability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22782)

- Asks whether SAE decoder-space geometry — computable before any forward pass — predicts which features are cheap vs. expensive to steer, removing the need for per-feature intervention sweeps.
- Neighbor density and max cosine similarity to nearby decoder directions rank feature steerability (ρ up to −0.546, AUROC up to 0.822), replicating across Gemma-2 (2B/9B), two SAE widths, and cross-architecturally on Llama-3.1-8B.
- Finds a training-regime-dependent boundary condition on Qwen3-8B/BatchTopK SAEs and a depth boundary where the signal weakens — useful practical screening tool for interpretability/steering work.

### 4. [From Trait Vectors to Circuits: Tracing Refusal and Sycophancy Through Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22224)

- Tests whether steering a behavior through an activation-space direction uses the *same* computational pathway the unmodified model uses to produce that behavior naturally, on refusal and sycophancy in Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- For refusal, reconstruction and transmission circuits are both compact and faithful — restoring just the trait coordinate recovers nearly all ablated behavior at half the edges of a direct circuit.
- For sycophancy, transmission is compact but reconstruction is broad and only partially faithful — a concrete case where "circuits that transmit an intervention" and "circuits that produce the behavior" diverge, directly relevant to interpreting alignment-relevant steering vectors.

### 5. [Topographic Training Concentrates Causal Circuits Without Improving Neuron Monosemanticity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24379)

- Tests whether a spatial-locality training loss (TopoLoss) — a training-time prior rather than a post-hoc SAE fix — can improve mechanistic interpretability of vision transformers by mitigating feature superposition.
- At loss weight α=1.0, topographic clusters become 2.79× more causally sufficient than random unit sets (via activation patching), with the effect growing monotonically in α.
- Standard neuron-level monosemanticity scores don't budge even as SAE dead-feature fraction rises 19×, suggesting current monosemanticity metrics are blind to a real class of circuit-level interpretability gains — a training-dynamics contribution to the interpretability toolkit.

### 6. [Circuit-Diff: Factual Edit-based Intervention Method for Localizing Knowledge in Attribution Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23892)

- Attacks the "unlabeled node" problem in Cross-Layer Transcoder attribution graphs: reading a circuit currently means pruning it and manually guessing what each surviving feature means.
- Circuit-Diff intervenes with a low-rank factual edit and flags the attribution-graph features whose role *changes* under that edit as related to the edited knowledge — automatically surfacing features for history, geography, and associations around the edited fact.
- Validated causally via patching on up to 24 CounterFact edits; ships as an open-source tool on top of `circuit-tracer`, directly useful for knowledge-localization and data-attribution-style interpretability work.

### 7. [From Concept Alignment to Causal Grounding: An Intervention Test of Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23065)

- Reframes CoT faithfulness as internal concept grounding — does a model's CoT engage the same internal concepts as its direct prediction, and do shared concepts *causally* drive the answer?
- Uses a single shared SAE across prediction and CoT passes plus a causal ablation metric (Δp), finding correlational concept-alignment is high but causal faithfulness varies substantially by depth, peaking mid-to-late layers rather than the final ones.
- Crucially, causally-important shared concepts aren't always verbalized in the CoT — direct evidence that surface-level or representational correspondence alone can't certify CoT faithfulness.

### 8. [Do Student LLMs Inherit OOD Robustness? Invariance-Weighted Distillation for Reliable Knowledge Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22566)

- Identifies two compounding mechanisms behind distilled students' OOD degradation: learning spurious correlations in the distillation data, and standard KD ignoring whether the *teacher* itself relied on causal vs. spurious features for a given sample.
- Proposes Invariance-Weighted Distillation, reweighting samples by teacher prediction invariance across synthetic environments (higher weight = more causal reliance), theoretically shown to reduce the student's spurious-to-causal gradient ratio.
- Wins 15/16 OOD benchmarks across DeBERTa-v3 and Qwen-2.5, with up to +14.9pp OOD gains — a concrete generalization-science lever for a very subliminal-learning-adjacent failure mode (unwanted trait/shortcut transfer through distillation).

### 9. [Dissecting Hierarchical Reasoning Models: A Mechanistic Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22197)

- Mechanistically studies Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM) on Sudoku, Maze, and ARC-AGI-2 using causal state interventions, linear probes with random-direction ablation controls, and SAE feature ablations.
- Finds recurrent models beat one-pass baselines, but single-state recurrent Transformers already match HRM — the hierarchical structure itself isn't doing the heavy lifting many assumed.
- Task variables are linearly decodable from recurrent states, yet ablating the probe direction behaves like a random control — a clean cautionary result on probing vs. causal necessity, and a call for better mechanistic-interpretability tools tailored to latent recursive reasoning.

### 10. [When and Why Do Linear Bias Probes Fail? A Geometric and Statistical Theory of Bias Detectability in Large Language Model Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22337)

- Builds a geometric/statistical theory of when linear bias probing can and can't work, modeling representations as class-conditional clusters with Mahalanobis separation on a curved manifold.
- Proves a finite-sample generalization bound, an exact "purity law" for max probe AUC as a function of how much demographic information is explicit in inputs, a curvature ceiling on separation, and a hard detectability threshold below which no probe can distinguish an unbiased model from an underpowered one.
- Validated on six open-weight models × four bias dimensions, turning bias auditing into a principled power analysis — broadly applicable to any linear-probing interpretability claim, not just bias.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[SafeTune: A Unified Faithful Library for Auditing and Repairing Safety Drift in Fine-Tuned LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22153)** — an open library unifying weight recovery, safety-constrained fine-tuning, gradient-based unlearning, and inference-time steering for auditing/repairing safety drift after fine-tuning.
- **[Luck Is Not Skill: When Do Paired Rollouts Help Group-Relative RL of LLM Agents?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24144)** — a careful training-dynamics study distinguishing "improving reward comparisons" from "reducing gradient variance" from "improving learning" in GRPO-style paired rollouts, with a pre-registered protocol.
- **[Testing the Construct Validity of a Functional Valence Axis in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22850)** — asks what evidence is needed to say a contrastive activation direction represents a genuine construct (e.g., "valence") rather than a surface correlate of how it was extracted.
- **[Multilingual Safety Signals Are Multi-Layered: Filtering Safety-Degrading Data for Safer LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22144)** — shows safety-relevant signal in fine-tuning data is distributed across multiple layers differently per language, and that multi-layer filtering cuts harmful-response rate by 60% vs. random filtering.
- **[Triggers and Diagnostics for LLM-Based Interpretability Failures in Active Inference Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23215)** — audits LLM "explainer" modules attached to autonomous agents and finds them fluently narrating corrupted beliefs and sycophantically rationalizing wrong actions 80–95% of the time, with no built-in truth-check.
- **[Defusing Explosive Prompts: Understanding and Preventing Trigger-Based Prompt Injections in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22510)** — introduces "explosive prompts," dormant conditional payloads that act as training-free, inference-time backdoors planted in retrieved content, and a detector that cuts live attack success from 34% to ~8%.
- **[onPanda: Efficient Annotation of On-Policy Alignment Data for LLMs and Agents via Token-Level Correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24983)** — a token-level locate-correct-continue annotation tool for building on-policy SFT/preference data that halves annotation time versus manual post-editing.
