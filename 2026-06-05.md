*Topics: machine learning, language models, AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization*

## Top 10 Papers

### 1. Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability
**arXiv:2606.06333**

Current SAEs assign each latent feature a single decoder direction, but real features are multi-dimensional — this mismatch drives "feature splitting" where exponentially many atoms are needed to reconstruct a d-dimensional feature.

- Proves a continuous path exists from the true feature basis to strictly lower risk in the ℓ1-regularized SAE objective, confirming that feature splitting is a training attractor, not just a numerical artifact
- Introduces **SASA** (Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders): learned decoder *subspaces* instead of vectors, with Top-s group gating for block sparsity and nuclear-norm regularization to adapt rank
- When block size ≥ intrinsic feature dimension, a single group becomes the global minimizer — reducing sample complexity from exponential to polynomial
- Demonstrates reduced feature splitting/absorption on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B while matching baseline performance with ~half the training tokens

### 2. Steering Vectors are an Adversarial Attack Surface
**arXiv:2606.05958**

Activation steering — constructing vectors from contrastive prompt pairs to shift model behavior — can be silently subverted through data poisoning of the steering dataset.

- Replacing a small fraction of tokens in the contrast dataset quietly aligns the resulting vector with anti-refusal directions, effectively jailbreaking the model while preserving intended steering on benign prompts
- Attack success rates of 20–55% across multiple open-weight model families; the attack is robust to reasonable variation in poisoning rate
- Refusal-direction orthogonalization of the poisoned vectors recovers ~82% of the performance gap without degrading benign behavior
- Highlights that the data pipelines used to construct steering vectors are a largely unexamined attack surface for safety-critical deployments

### 3. Deciphering Two Training Clocks in Grokking via Deep Linear Network Theory with Conditional ReLU Reduction
**arXiv:2606.05863**

Grokking involves two clearly separable timescales: fast classification-loss decay and slow representation simplification — this paper gives each its own theoretical clock.

- For deep linear networks with layerwise weight decay: classification loss closes on a **logarithmic** time scale; Schatten-penalized structural energy closes on a **polynomial** time scale
- Extends to ReLU MLPs via conditional linearization in regions where activation patterns remain stable, showing the classifier head receives larger effective gradients than the embedding block (explaining why the head fits first)
- Two-layer ReLU analysis confirms the empirical observation that generalization trails loss minimization and emerges from ongoing representation change
- Provides rigorous foundations for studying delayed generalization without relying on heuristic descriptions of grokking

### 4. Dead Directions: Geometric Singular Learning
**arXiv:2606.05957**

Bridges singular learning theory (which grounds the Bayesian theory of generalization) with information geometry by introducing "dead directions" — unit vectors where the Fisher metric degrades.

- Shows that KL order (a key quantity in singular learning theory) is recoverable as the decay rate of directional Fisher curvature approaching a singularity, *without* requiring coordinate resolution of the singular set
- Extends the framework to multi-component cases, multiplicity factors, and singular fluctuations
- Applies K-FAC factorization to analyze Fisher blocks in deep networks; introduces **DDCAdam**, a preconditioner maintaining gradient-flow invariance
- Enables closed-form per-architecture RLCT predictions and extraction of Watanabe's triple from a **single checkpoint** — eliminating the need for posterior sampling

### 5. High-Dimensional Theory of LoRA Fine-Tuning in a Solvable Attention Model
**arXiv:2606.05899**

A rare exact-asymptotic theory for low-rank adaptation: analyzes rank-1 LoRA on a single-head attention layer pretrained on a data-rich task then fine-tuned on limited data.

- Provides sharp asymptotic predictions in the high-dimensional regime, described by a finite collection of order parameters — no free parameters in the theory
- Pre-training effects translate into an **effective noise** component, yielding concrete prescriptions for optimal pre-training when downstream LoRA fine-tuning is anticipated
- Identifies a regime where test error and representation similarity metrics **diverge** — a model can have strong representation quality yet poor test error, with implications for representation-based evaluation
- Opens a path toward active fine-tuning methodologies guided by theoretical order parameter estimates

### 6. Learning What to Forget: Improving LLM Unlearning via Learned Token-Level Importance
**arXiv:2606.06320**

Machine unlearning for LLMs should not treat all tokens in a forget set equally — tokens vary in how much their forgetting conflicts with retaining general capabilities.

- Formally defines a token as "forget-specific" to the extent that minimizing forget loss on it does not conflict with retain optimality, then casts this as a joint optimization over model parameters and token weights
- Proves the objective recovers oracle forget-specific token support under natural separation conditions
- **ATWU** (Alternating Token-Weighted Unlearning) jointly learns a linear token-importance scorer over hidden states and updates model parameters, with no external supervision or auxiliary model
- Achieves state-of-the-art forget-retain trade-offs on TOFU and RWKU benchmarks, outperforming both sample-level methods and auxiliary-model approaches

### 7. Trust, but Don't Verify: Epistemic Blind Spots in LLM Source Evaluation
**arXiv:2606.05403**

LLMs can detect fabricated statistics when examining a single source (76–100% accuracy) but systematically fail to apply this capability during multi-source synthesis.

- Source influence is governed by a **"methodology-register gate"**: models weight sources by the analytical register (tone, structure) of the text, not by numeric accuracy — statistically impossible confidence intervals are treated as credibly as valid ones
- Replicates across 5 models from 3 families (Claude, Qwen, OLMo) and 3 professional domains, suggesting a structural property of how LLMs handle synthesis
- Mechanistic analysis via causal tracing and linear probes shows models encode methodology-register representations while *suppressing* numeric-validity signals during synthesis
- Prompting interventions and oracle checklists trigger indiscriminate skepticism rather than targeted verification; authors frame this as "epistemic alignment" — a deployment-choice failure, not a capability gap

### 8. RREDCoT: Segment-Level Reward Redistribution for Reasoning Models
**arXiv:2606.06475**

RL fine-tuning of chain-of-thought reasoning models faces a structural delayed-reward problem: reward can only be assigned after the full trace is complete, making GRPO and variants high-variance Monte Carlo estimators.

- Frames improved training as a **reward redistribution / credit assignment** problem: identify which CoT segments contribute to correct solutions, then concentrate reward there
- **RREDCoT** uses the model itself to approximate the optimal redistribution without additional generation passes, keeping the approach computationally tractable for long contexts
- Compares against Monte Carlo sampling and gradient attribution methods across different CoT segmentation and state-value estimation strategies
- Directly relevant to reducing variance in GRPO-family training used across the current generation of reasoning LLMs

### 9. State Commitment Learning: Training Language Models to Distinguish Computation from Memory
**arXiv:2606.05201**

Current reasoning LLMs treat every generated token identically, allowing intermediate "hidden thoughts" to persist in context and influence downstream predictions even when they shouldn't.

- Introduces a **"persistent-state sufficiency"** criterion: an answer is valid only if it remains correct after erasing hidden-thought segments from the context
- **CERL** (Counterfactual Erasure RL) evaluates reasoning traces both with and without hidden-thought content under identical context, rewarding only paths where erasure preserves correctness
- Tested on mathematics, logical reasoning, scientific QA, and multi-turn tool use; substantially reduces answer dependence on hidden work while maintaining accuracy
- Outperforms standard RL and SFT alternatives; provides a principled training-time intervention for improving compositional reliability in extended reasoning

---

### 10. Generative Criticality in Large Language Model Temperature Scaling
**arXiv:2606.06238**

Applies statistical physics concepts (susceptibility, order parameters, phase transitions) to analyze how softmax temperature governs LLM generation.

- Defines susceptibility via two-point correlations of token embeddings along the generation chain; identifies a **critical temperature** with power-law susceptibility scaling
- Below this threshold: rapid order-parameter collapse and **embedding convergence to a single semantic direction** — a sharp, phase-transition-like phenomenon
- Two-nearest-neighbor dimensionality estimation independently corroborates the critical behavior
- Consistent across Qwen3 scales (0.6B–32B) and diverse prompt types; offers a quantitative lens for understanding why temperature is so consequential and why too-low temperatures produce degenerate outputs

## Honorable Mentions

- **2606.06479** — *Pretraining Recurrent Networks without Recurrence*: Proposes Supervised Memory Training (SMT) to sidestep BPTT credit-assignment issues in RNNs — uses a Transformer encoder to generate memory labels for supervised learning on transitions, enabling parallel training with O(1) gradient paths between any two tokens. Outperforms BPTT on language modeling and pixel sequences, potentially relevant to efficient long-context architectures.

- **2606.05486** — *Localizing Prompt Ambiguity in Large Language Models with Probe-Targeted Attribution* (PRIG): Trains a linear probe to detect prompt ambiguity in the residual stream, then attributes probe scores to specific tokens. Achieves 0.840 AUROC on a synthetic benchmark spanning code, math, and writing; a practically useful interpretability tool for diagnosing which prompt components cause model failures.

- **2606.05606** — *Cross-Epoch Adaptive Rollout Optimization (CERO) for RL Post-Training*: Frames rollout budget allocation across prompts as an online resource allocation problem with diminishing returns, deriving a Fenchel-dual reformulation with O(√K) regret. Outperforms GRPO on math reasoning across multiple open-weight LLMs — a more principled take on sample efficiency in RLVR training.

- **2606.05335** — *A Prism Hierarchy of Learning Regimes in Large Linear Autoencoders*: Identifies five extreme regimes (large-data, small-data, mean-field, narrow-latent, free) for weight-tied linear autoencoders and derives closed-form expressions for training and population loss dynamics under gradient flow for four of them. Extends the theoretical toolkit for understanding representation learning geometry.

- **2606.05972** — *LLM Explainability with Counterfactual Chains and Causal Graphs*: Uses an MCMC-inspired counterfactual augmentation procedure to expand sparse observational data, then runs causal discovery to produce interpretable concept-level graphs of LLM reasoning. Evaluated for predictive fidelity and structural stability across three model families and three domains.
