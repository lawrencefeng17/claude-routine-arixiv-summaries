## Top 10

### 1. Extractable Memorization From First Principles
**[2607.12649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12649)** · A. Feder Cooper, Marika Swanberg, Jamie Hayes, Lea Duesterwald, Christopher De Sa, Daniel E. Ho, Mark A. Lemley, Percy Liang

- Argues that prior "extractable memorization" claims conflate memorization with base-rate generation of common substrings, and proposes a matched-comparison standard: compare a training sequence's generation probability against comparable non-training sequences before calling it memorized.
- Introduces two concrete tools — a conformal test calibrated to a target false-positive rate, and a per-document "census" procedure — for substantiating memorization claims rigorously.
- On Wikipedia with OLMo 2 32B, many previously-flagged "extracted" sequences turn out to be non-training text reproduced at only ~24% the rate of true training sequences, a legally and scientifically consequential correction (fittingly cross-disciplinary author list spanning CS, law, and policy) to how memorization/data-attribution claims get made.

### 2. A Shared Subcircuit Lets LLMs Count Down Across Tasks
**[2607.12279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12279)** · Jacob Dunefsky, Wes Gurnee, Emmanuel Ameisen

- Mechanistically isolates a "countdown subcircuit" in Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct responsible for token/length counting, first surfaced via a controlled fixed-length-sentence-writing task.
- Shows the same circuit motif was previously identified in a different frontier model on an unrelated task, suggesting this counting mechanism is a convergent, reusable computational primitive rather than task-specific memorization.
- Unsupervised probing on naturalistic text shows the subcircuit activates across many tasks, including when the target count must be inferred from context — a clean case study in circuit-level generalization from Anthropic's interpretability team.

### 3. Belief-reality separation lives in routing over a shared value slot in language models
**[2607.11945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11945)** · Oliver Steele, Jiangtao Wen, Yuxing Han

- Locates the mechanism by which LMs track "what a character believes" vs. "what's actually true": a shared value slot stores the attributed content, while a router at the query token decides which frame (belief vs. reality) to read from.
- Distinguishes directly-asserted beliefs from beliefs inferred via a character's inferred visibility, and shows routing subspaces flip between frames without altering the stored value itself — the separation lives in routing, not in labels on the slot.
- Consistent across 3 architectures and 5 model families, emerging between 3B–7B parameters, and appears to generalize to counterfactuals, fiction, and tense — directly relevant to interpreting deception-adjacent and theory-of-mind-like representations.

### 4. What Makes a Representational Prior Work? Feature Families, Label-Free Invariances, and Critical Windows in Grokking
**[2607.12735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12735)** · Gunner Levi Howe

- Follows up on the finding that grokking delay equals the time needed to build task-structured representations, running 188 experiments to dissect exactly what makes a contrastive representational prior accelerate grokking.
- Finds priors act at the level of feature families (a coherent-but-wrong prior blocks generalization as much as a random one), that a fully label-free invariance prior beats a supervised one (2.7x speedup, 17x combined with weight-norm clamping), and that priors matter overwhelmingly in an early critical window (first 2000 epochs).
- Replicates across modular-arithmetic tasks and architectures — a systematic dissection of a training-dynamics lever for the science of generalization.

### 5. From Geometric Recovery to Causal Validation: A Reproducible Audit of Sparse Autoencoder Features
**[2607.12166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12166)** · Mohamed Abdessalem Bal

- Challenges the standard practice of validating SAE features via decoder-geometry recovery metrics alone, showing this conflates two distinct properties: decoder-geometry alignment and encoder-activation (causal) relevance.
- Causal ablation/steering experiments find up to 77% of features passing a standard geometric-recovery bar in a degraded SAE — and 9% even in a well-trained one — are causally inert despite looking "recovered."
- Releases a reproducible, model-agnostic causal-audit tool and documents "atom-collision" failures where one dictionary atom is the nearest match for unrelated concepts — an important methodological corrective for the SAE interpretability literature.

### 6. Same Loss, Same Noise, Opposite Schedules: Noise Structure and Optimizer Normalization Jointly Determine Whether Learning-Rate Cooldown Helps
**[2607.12360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12360)** · Subham Singh, Ashutosh Mishra, Subha Raut

- Resolves the puzzle of why LR cooldowns help in some training runs but not others: it depends on the interaction between gradient-noise structure (multiplicative vs. additive) and whether the optimizer normalizes updates.
- Under multiplicative noise, plain SGD achieves geometric convergence at a constant LR (cooldown unnecessary), while sign-based/normalized methods get stuck at a noise floor scaling with η² — with closed-form results for signSGD and extensions to normalized SGD, momentum, and heavy-tailed noise.
- Validated on simulations and real classification tasks with measured gradient noise — a genuinely predictive theory for a ubiquitous but poorly understood training-dynamics knob.

### 7. Reducing information dependency does not cause training data privacy. Adversarially non-robust features do
**[2607.12354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12354)** · Rasmus Torp, Shailen K. Smith, Adam Breuer

- Overturns the standard explanation for training-data reconstruction attacks: defenses that reduce measured "information dependency" between inputs and outputs don't actually reduce vulnerability to model-inversion attacks, and heavily-memorizing models can still resist them.
- Shows models trained on as little as 3% of pixels can still be reconstructed, and traces the real cause to adversarially non-robust features — the same imperceptible, unstable features implicated in adversarial examples.
- Proposes "Anti Adversarial Training," which deliberately cultivates these features and beats existing defenses on both reconstruction-resistance and accuracy — a genuine privacy/robustness tradeoff finding relevant to memorization and data-attribution research.

### 8. Gradient Flow Dynamics and Implicit Bias of Diagonal Linear Networks under Infinitesimal Initialization
**[2607.12332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12332)** · Jiajie Zhao, Jianxing Wang, Junjie Yang, Zhiwei Bai, Yaoyu Zhang

- Extends the classic diagonal-linear-network implicit-bias analysis (vanishing initialization → sparse/ℓ1-like solutions) to deeper diagonal networks and a broader class of two-layer networks.
- Shows training trajectories converge to solutions of a modified ℓ1-norm minimization problem, and identifies a governing geometric object — the "Structural Invariant Manifold" — that organizes the whole gradient flow.
- A theory-heavy contribution to understanding how initialization scale determines implicit regularization: foundational training-dynamics/generalization theory.

### 9. Sparse Inter-Layer Dependencies of Transformer FFN Neurons
**[2607.11990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11990)** · Johannes Knittel, Hanspeter Pfister

- Introduces a training-free attribution method testing whether an FFN neuron's activation can be explained by a small subset of upstream neuron activations and attention outputs, despite the dense, superposed residual stream.
- Finds small upstream subsets suffice to reconstruct target-neuron activations with high fidelity, and that masking neuron-specific sparse subsets simultaneously across all layers leaves model perplexity largely unchanged.
- Suggests transformers harbor latent sparse, structured computational pathways beneath their dense parameterization — a practical, scalable tool for circuit-level mechanistic interpretability.

### 10. Fisher Rank Inflation: A Spectral Signature of Memorization under Label Noise
**[2607.12438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12438)** · Satwik Bathula, Anand A. Joshi

- Identifies "Fisher Rank Inflation": the effective rank of the per-example gradient (Fisher) spectrum temporarily expands during the memorization phase of training on noisy labels, then contracts, because corrupted labels inject gradient mass into previously unused eigendirections.
- Derives per-sample attribution formulas and validates on CIFAR-10/100/10N across SmallCNN/ResNet18/ViT: mislabeled samples dominate the highest-contributing samples at peak rank (69–96% in synthetic noise, 94.4% on real label noise), with peak rank rising monotonically with corruption severity.
- In some settings, rank-inflation onset precedes visible test-performance degradation — a spectral early-warning signature connecting memorization dynamics to label-noise detection.
