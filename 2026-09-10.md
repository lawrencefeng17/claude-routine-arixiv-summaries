## Top 10

### 1. [How Fragile Is Safety Alignment at Frontier Scale? A Single-Direction Attack on a 320B MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09793)

- Tests whether directional ablation — the canonical white-box "refusal direction" attack, previously established only on dense models up to ~70B — survives the jump to frontier mixture-of-experts models with fragmented residual streams and quantized weights, applying it to GLM-5.3-Flash (320B params, 288 routed experts, block-FP8).
- The attack still works, but not where the standard recipe looks: editing attention, dense, and routed-expert writers individually removes only 3.9%, 1.6%, and 14.8% of refusal respectively, while editing all three jointly removes 77.6% — meaning 74% of the effect is invisible to module-name matching and the conventional recipe silently captures only a small fraction of it.
- Achieves 41–89 percentage-point reductions in refusal across seven harmful benchmarks with no detected capability loss, though a category-concentrated residue (violence, sexual content, hate) survives every ablation rank tried — a concrete data point on how far single-direction safety attacks generalize as models scale and architectures diversify.

### 2. [Through the Looking Glass: Directly Reading and Writing Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10210)

- Introduces a training-free method to identify, for any prediction, the minimal sufficient set of transformer components (units/channels) that produce it — despite thousands to hundreds of thousands of components having nonzero signed contribution to a logit, dividing by the net contribution collapses this to dozens; across 12 models (124M–7B params) the sufficient set spans 2–16 components, a share of the model that does not grow with scale.
- Everything is read directly from weights and activations with nothing trained or fitted: components can be named on both sides (what they write and what they read), grammatical categories the embedding itself cannot see emerge from sorting by upstream source, and three-quarters of a layer's update turns out to be a fixed linear map of its input state.
- Demonstrates the framework is actionable, not just descriptive: installing an association the model doesn't hold costs 1/40th of a rank-one weight update for comparable held-out loss, and composing reads/writes across layers lets an edit be made conditional (e.g., "only if a token occurred earlier in context") — a genuinely new, lightweight tool for mechanistic interpretability.

### 3. [Active Adaptation, Not Static Defense: Temporal Dynamics of Preventative Steering in Adversarial Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10142)

- Investigates why "Preventative Steering" (injecting undesirable-trait persona vectors during fine-tuning, then removing them at eval time) provides lasting protection against malicious fine-tuning attacks, finding the defense arises from an early compensatory-adaptation phase followed by a steady-state phase where the corrective signal decays, with attention output projections as the dominant route for defensive weight updates.
- Shows via "Intervention Delta Preservation" experiments that simply preserving or reinjecting the learned weight offset fails to maintain protection — the defense depends on active, ongoing adaptation during training rather than a static learned correction, a mechanistic finding with direct implications for how such defenses should be designed.
- Proposes Progressive Intensity Scheduling (ramping injection strength up as static-strength alignment begins to decay), which improves safety robustness over fixed-strength steering on Qwen2.5 and Gemma-3 — connecting training-dynamics analysis directly to a better defense recipe.

### 4. [A Dominant Diffuse Phase in the Sparse Autoencoder Phase Diagram](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10299)

- Runs a controlled synthetic-dictionary-recovery experiment (the MAIS-O43 open problem) across the full 165-cell grid of nesting fraction, sparsity penalty, and dictionary size to map when SAEs recover a true feature dictionary versus merge distinct features — and finds neither outcome: across 3,500+ fits, every run instead converges to a reproducible "diffuse phase."
- In this phase reconstruction is nearly perfect, but learned atoms sit far from ground-truth features (median cosine similarity 0.5–0.7 against a 0.95 recovery threshold) and learned codes are an order of magnitude denser than the true sparse code.
- Since the exact sparse-coding objective's global optimum is known to merge nested features, the result implies trained SAEs (via standard Adam) don't actually reach that optimum — meaning the phase diagram of trained SAEs can differ fundamentally from the phase diagram of the objective they nominally minimize, a cautionary methodological finding for SAE-based interpretability work.

### 5. [Strangers to Themselves: What Language Models Say About Themselves Is Generic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09899)

- Tests whether LLM self-reports (e.g., "would you cave to pushback? lie under pressure?") reflect genuine self-knowledge by comparing a model's predictions about its own behavior against predictions made by other models about that same model, and against predictions about "capable AI agents in general."
- Finds direct self-report barely predicts actual behavior (r = +0.04, rising only to +0.24 with item-level detail), and critically, asking the identical question about "AI agents in general" does just as well (+0.28) — other models' guesses about the target model are at least as predictive as its own self-report, and frontier scale doesn't change this pattern.
- First-person framing does have one robust, non-epistemic effect: it systematically shifts self-reports toward flattering, understating harmful behavior relative to third-person framing — a directly relevant caution for any alignment work that treats model self-reports as evidence about internal states, beliefs, or dispositions.

### 6. [Training Trajectories Determine Circuit Removability in Annealable Soft-Prior Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10287)

- Uses a transformer whose positional-attention prior can be learned, smoothly faded, or hard-zeroed to test whether retrieval circuits that formed with an inductive-bias "scaffold" in place remain functional once that scaffold is removed — on associative recall, unforced models collapse from 77% to 9.5% accuracy at zero gate, but smooth fade-to-zero training preserves 73% accuracy at zero gate.
- Forced-zero training, hard switching mid-training, and post-hoc continuation all fail to reproduce this robustness — the same final architecture and same final gate value yield entirely different circuit removability depending on the training trajectory that reached it, with circuit consolidation occurring only after the gate reaches zero even though which heads become responsible varies across seeds.
- A clean, controlled demonstration that the path taken through training — not just the converged solution — determines whether a learned circuit is fragile or robust, directly relevant to how curricula, scaffolding, and annealing schedules shape what circuits models end up relying on.

### 7. [High-probability guarantees for linear accessibility in feature superposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09556)

- Frames "linear accessibility" of superposed features (how many concepts a network can pack into fewer dimensions while keeping them linearly readable, despite cross-feature interference) as a compressed-sensing problem, deriving high-probability bounds under subgaussian noise.
- Proves the sufficient embedding dimension scales linearly in sparsity and log(number of features) rather than the quadratic scaling implied by prior worst-case analyses — a substantially more optimistic bound for how much superposition a network can support while remaining linearly decodable.
- Directly quantifies the geometric constraints behind the linear representation hypothesis, giving a theoretical framework for evaluating how well sparse autoencoders should be expected to recover features, and for reasoning about compositional generalization from superposed representations.

### 8. [A Later Test Set Is Not a New Domain: Pretraining Familiarity Survives a Contamination-Free Hold-Out](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10357)

- Builds a genuinely post-hoc, contamination-free benchmark (every observation published after the last evaluated model's release) for time-series foundation models and finds pretrained models still win 5 of 7 test groups — showing that a temporal hold-out removing literal memorization does not remove the advantage of domain familiarity from pretraining.
- The largest gain (28% lower error than the best classical method) falls precisely on Wikipedia pageviews, the domain one evaluated model's authors describe as the bulk of its pretraining corpus — and standard intrinsic difficulty measures (seasonal strength, spectral entropy) fail to explain the win/loss pattern, while corpus familiarity does.
- A methodologically important negative result for benchmark design broadly: a "held-out, non-contaminated" test set can still silently reward memorized domain familiarity rather than genuine generalization, arguing benchmarks need domain hold-outs stated relative to disclosed pretraining corpora, not just temporal cutoffs.

### 9. [Forgetting Only What Matters: Layer-Selective Unlearning toward Robust LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10439)

- Proposes selecting which transformer layers to update for machine unlearning via a forget-to-retain significance score (high influence on the forget set, low sensitivity to the retain set) rather than applying broad, fixed parameter updates across the whole model.
- Across TOFU, KnowUnDo, and MUSE-style evaluations, the targeted approach reduces residual memorization versus strong baselines (GA, NPO, KLD, SURE, ReLearn, LUNAR) while keeping retain-set utility close to the unmodified model.
- Notably improves robustness under post-training quantization (8-bit and 4-bit), where forgotten knowledge often partially re-emerges in prior unlearning methods — concentrating updates in fewer, more significant layers makes them less likely to be erased by low-bit rounding, and reduces adversarial-prompt recovery of "forgotten" content.

### 10. [CompassOPD: Cross-Family On-Policy Distillation via Within-Family Likelihood Shifts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10154)

- Investigates why on-policy distillation (dense token-level supervision from a teacher on student-generated trajectories) works well when teacher and student share a model family but degrades in cross-family settings even after tokenizer alignment — even much stronger external teachers add little.
- Decomposes the cross-family distillation signal into an offset (low-capability teacher-family reference vs. student) and a within-family likelihood shift (reference to strong teacher), showing standard OPD lets the offset dominate the update and obscure the actually useful teacher-capability signal.
- CompassOPD removes the offset and anchors updates to the student's own initial policy via a frozen reference, improving average reasoning accuracy by up to 5.50 points over standard cross-family OPD — a training-dynamics diagnosis with direct practical payoff for distillation pipelines that mix model families.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Teacher Geometry Shapes Learnability in Teacher-Student Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09595)** — shows that the geometric structure of a randomly-generated teacher network (not just architecture or optimizer) strongly determines student learnability via the balance of two distinct suboptimal local-minima types, with maximally vs. minimally dissimilar teacher weights inducing markedly different convergence rates.
- **[Learning with Synthetic Data via SGD in High-Dimensional Linear Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09572)** — derives finite-sample risk bounds showing model collapse under synthetic-data training is not inevitable but depends on the training protocol: mixing synthetic data throughout training induces a collapse floor, while confining it to an early stage avoids the floor entirely.
- **[What Fixed-Rollout pass@k Evaluations Can Identify](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09245)** — proves that extrapolating pass@k benchmarks to k beyond the number of rollouts actually sampled is fundamentally non-identified (not just noisy), quantifying on real data how ambiguous such extrapolations can be by factors of 1.5–2,600x.
- **[World-Time Compute with Verified Code World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09163)** — fine-tunes LLMs on trajectories from many independently-authored, verified symbolic world models to improve generalization to held-out domains, showing exact labels (not task diversity) drive the gains and that benefits concentrate in smaller/weaker models, consistent with capability saturation at scale.
- **[Direct Diversity Optimization for Diverse Successful Trajectories in Preference Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10052)** — an offline post-training method for LLM agents that explicitly preserves multiple distinct successful strategies from shared decision states, rather than collapsing to a single mode, improving both task success and strategy coverage over successful-only imitation baselines.
- **[Critical initialization destabilizes higher input derivatives in wide scalar-input networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09244)** — derives mean-field recursions showing that at the edge-of-chaos initialization that famously preserves first-order signal propagation, second- and higher-order input-derivative variance instead grows with depth, with direct relevance to physics-informed losses and score-matching objectives that depend on those derivatives.
- **[Explaining f-Divergence-Based Regularization via Local Curvature and Sharpness-Aware Minimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09367)** — shows divergence-based regularization and Sharpness-Aware Minimization are locally equivalent curvature-penalizing methods under a second-order expansion, unifying two previously separate approaches to improving generalization via flatter minima.
