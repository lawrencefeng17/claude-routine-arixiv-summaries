## Top 10

### 1. Invariant Learning Dynamics of Transformers in Inductive Reasoning Tasks
**[2607.11875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11875)** · Tiberiu Musat, Tiago Pimentel, Nicholas Zucchet, Thomas Hofmann

- Proves that attention training dynamics on a broad class of inductive tasks (in-context n-grams, multi-hop reasoning) collapse onto a low-dimensional, interpretable invariant manifold shared across tasks and initializations.
- Uses the manifold to explain the competition between in-context vs. in-weights learning circuits and how initialization determines which one gets learned.
- A step toward a genuinely predictive theory of Transformer training dynamics, letting the authors auto-detect which circuit a trained model actually implements.

### 2. When Data Imbalance Helps: Robust Generalization Through Shortcut Saturation
**[2607.10116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10116)** · Cheng-Ting Chou, Duc Binh Hoang

- Studies a spurious-correlation setup where a shortcut is correlated with the label in training but anti-correlated at test time, and finds — counterintuitively — that *more* imbalance toward the shortcut helps sufficiently large models generalize (77% success at correlation 0.90 vs. 0% at 0.50 for a 2-layer transformer).
- The effect vanishes entirely in 1-layer models, isolating capacity as the key variable.
- Mechanistic analysis (gradient dynamics, circuit ablations) explains how extra capacity lets imbalance push models toward genuine feature learning instead of shortcut reliance — a direct contribution to the science of generalization.

### 3. From Direction to Magnitude: How Multimodal Instruction-Tuning Reorganizes the Geometric Encoding of Identity-Specifying Prompts in Transformer Hidden States
**[2607.09842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09842)** · Jorge A. Castillo, Marco Torres Yévenes, Juan Carlos Lanas

- Compares geometric "fingerprints" of identity-specifying system prompts across four post-training regimes (base, multimodal RLHF, RL distillation, plain SFT) using Ollivier-Ricci curvature on hidden-state trajectory graphs.
- Finds a qualitative reorganization specific to multimodal instruction-tuning: identity encoding shifts from being direction-coded in the base model to magnitude-coded after RLHF — a shift that's absent under RL distillation or plain SFT.
- A concrete mechanistic account of how a specific post-training recipe reshapes internal representations, relevant to anyone tracking how alignment training changes model internals.

### 4. Two Confounds in Cross-Model Value Comparison: Response Determinism and the Access Harness
**[2607.10202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10202)** · Hong-In Won, Jinseok Jang, Hyoseop Kim

- Identifies methodological confounds in LLM "value comparison" alignment audits: models differ substantially in how deterministically they commit to a stated choice (0.66–0.95 range), which alone inflates apparent value differences between models.
- Shows the deployment "access harness" (API vs. consumer client) shifts measured value profiles substantially — one client flipped 4/18 responses and moved a value-distance metric by 0.31.
- Demonstrates causally that deployment system prompts can turn a mostly-refusing base model into a highly compliant one, a cautionary result for alignment-evaluation methodology broadly.

### 5. HyperSafe: Inference-Time Safety Recovery for Fine-Tuned Language Models
**[2607.11475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11475)** · Aznaur Aliev, Carlos Hinojosa, Abdelrahman Eldesokey, Bang An, Bernard Ghanem, Yibo Yang

- Targets safety-alignment fragility under fine-tuning: a hypernetwork maps layer-wise activation fingerprints of a fine-tuned checkpoint to a per-model "Safe Side Network" that classifies prompt-level harm, requiring no weight edits or safety data at deployment time.
- Reduces harmful-response rates from 19–31% down to under 1% on Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B while preserving downstream task accuracy.
- A practical answer to the well-known problem that fine-tuning silently erodes safety alignment.

### 6. modelDNA: Calibrated Lineage Verification and Merge Decomposition from Sampled Weight Fingerprints
**[2607.10617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10617)** · Muhammad Awais Bin Adil, Saad Aamir

- Builds model "fingerprints" from small partial weight downloads (100–300MB) to verify parent/lineage relationships among open-weight models without needing the full checkpoint.
- Achieves perfect AUROC (1.0) on a 15-model lineage benchmark and additionally recovers merge-mixture weights via constrained least squares.
- A direct contribution to model-level data/training attribution and provenance — useful for auditing undisclosed fine-tunes, merges, or distillations.

### 7. Reference-Based Distillation Detection in LLMs
**[2607.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09692)** · Rajat Rawat, Sizhe Chen, Akshay Anand, et al.

- Detects whether a model was distilled from another model's outputs using reference-based membership inference against an earlier checkpoint in the same lineage, rather than requiring access to the (often unknown) teacher.
- Infers proxy prompt templates directly from model outputs to handle hidden or unknown distillation pipelines, including o1/o3-style reasoning-trace distillation.
- Applied to contemporary models, surfacing suspected distillation relationships involving QwQ, DeepSeek-R1, and GPT-OSS — closely tied to data/training attribution.

### 8. Spectral Origins of the Self-Correction Blind Spot in Autoregressive Generation
**[2607.09803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09803)** · Ingrid Petrova, Luan Vejsiu

- Proposes SPARC, a spectral-algebraic theory explaining why autoregressive LLMs reliably fail to self-correct their own errors but readily fix identical, externally-attributed errors: the blind spot arises exactly when the spectral radius of the product of per-step attention Jacobians is ≥ 1.
- Derives a sharp activation threshold for correction markers (e.g., "Wait") that reproduces an empirically observed 89.3% blind-spot reduction, plus a convergence guarantee for RL-based verifier-corrector training tied to the coupling matrix's spectral norm.
- Validated across four backbones and a visual autoregressive probe, unifying the phenomenon across text and image/video generation — a strong mechanistic account of an important model-behavior failure mode.

---

### 9. Inside the Unfair Judge: A Mechanistic Interpretability Account of LLM-as-Judge Bias
**[2607.11871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11871)** · Zixiang Xu, Sixian Li, Huaxing Liu, Xiang Wang, Shuai Li, Zirui Song, Xiuying Chen

- Mechanistically localizes LLM-as-judge bias: biased judging inputs are displaced along a low-dimensional, bias-type-specific subspace relative to a tight "baseline" activation manifold formed by unbiased judgments.
- Causal manipulation along this direction reproduces or reverses biased scores, establishing the subspace as causally responsible rather than merely correlated.
- The geometric bias signature predicts judge failure on unseen benchmarks better than text-based detectors — directly relevant to reward-model and evaluation reliability.

---

### 10. How to Tame Grokking: Representation Geometry as a Control Signal
**[2607.11666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11666)** · Maksim A Kazanskii

- Finds that dimensionality collapse of hidden representations consistently precedes grokking across modular-arithmetic and permutation tasks, in both MLPs and Transformers.
- Introduces Geometric Dimensionality Regularization (GeomDR), a spectral regularizer that directly targets this collapse.
- Accelerates grokking onset by up to 52x, turning a previously mysterious late-training phase transition into a controllable phenomenon.
