## Top 10 Papers

### 1. When Do Attention Circuits Form? Developmental Trajectories of Capability and Attention-Sink Emergence Across Three 1B-Class Architectures
**arXiv:2606.02378** | Yongzhong Xu

Tracks induction head circuits and attention-sink heads across pretraining checkpoints in three 1B-parameter LMs using spectral analysis and capability-specific screening.

- Induction circuits form **10–20× earlier** (in tokens) than attention-sink heads in DCLM-trained models
- Some architectures exhibit sharp phase transitions rather than gradual emergence; others are more continuous
- Stable circuits identified within just 0.3–2% of total training tokens via targeted screening
- Argues circuit formation involves **two distinct transitions**, not one — refining prior accounts of how transformer capabilities crystallize

### 2. Massive Spikes in LLMs are Bias Vectors: Mechanistic Uncovering and Spike-Free Quantization
**arXiv:2606.02288** | Yung-Chin Chen, Chung Peng Lee, Ze-Wei Liou, Naveen Verma

Reframes the well-known activation spike problem in LLMs: spikes are not scalar outliers but **structural bias vectors** in specific tokens that converge to fixed directions post-normalization, driving attention sink and value-state drain phenomena.

- Projection weights actively coordinate to amplify and preserve these biases against positional perturbations
- Proposes INSERTQUANT: clamps spikes and restores model function via pre-calculated template vectors
- Achieves strictly spike-free activations and robust low-bit quantization; generalizes to Vision Transformers
- Provides a mechanistic explanation that unifies several previously disconnected LLM anomalies

### 3. Ablating Archetypes: The Stability of Archetypal SAEs is an Artifact of Initialization and Metric Design
**arXiv:2606.02061** | Michał Brzozowski, Neo Christopher Chung

Challenges the claim that archetypal SAEs provide superior stability in dictionary learning from neural networks.

- The apparent cross-run stability is an artifact of **deterministic k-means decoder initialization** creating identical starting points, not a property of the archetypal constraint itself
- Distinguishes *stability* (agreement across independent runs) from *stabilization* (convergence toward a common solution); archetypal SAEs achieve only the latter trivially
- Removing deterministic initialization eliminates the stability advantage entirely
- Recommends trajectory diagnostics and initialization ablations as evaluation baselines for any SAE stability claim

### 4. A Local Perturbation Theory for Cross-Domain Interference and Recovery in Multi-Domain RL
**arXiv:2606.02398** | Lei Yang, Siyu Ding, Deyi Xiong

Explains why training LLMs with RL on one domain degrades others — even when gradients are nearly orthogonal, ruling out catastrophic forgetting as a complete explanation.

- Identifies a **low-dimensional shared conflict subspace** where sparse parameter updates from different domains overlap and create directional conflicts
- Proves theoretically that later-domain training damages earlier domains via a second-order term concentrated in this subspace
- A brief targeted domain refresh selectively recovers prior performance with minimal collateral damage
- Demonstrates recovering math reasoning from 57.7 → 66.0 after multi-domain RL; average score 66.4 across domains

### 5. Policy and World Modeling Co-Training for Language Agents
**arXiv:2606.02388** | Ning Lu et al.

PaW extracts free world-modeling supervision from on-policy RL rollouts: every (action, next observation) transition already encodes what a world model needs.

- No separate simulator or extra data collection required; co-training is integrated directly into standard RL training loops
- Three stabilizing additions: action-entropy-based data selection, noise-tolerant loss functions, adaptive loss balancing
- Consistent performance gains over pure RL baselines across multiple agent benchmarks
- Suggests world modeling is an underutilized signal already present in standard RL training pipelines

### 6. How Hard Can It Be? Hardness-Aware Multi-Objective Unlearning
**arXiv:2606.02119** | Jiangwei Chen, Xinyuan Niu, Rachael Hwee Ling Sim, Zhengyuan Liu, Nancy F. Chen, Bryan Kian Hsiang Low

Proposes HAMU, a theoretically-grounded constrained optimization approach to machine unlearning that formalizes the tradeoff between forgetting and retaining.

- Quantifies **unlearning hardness** as the distribution similarity between forget and retain data — higher similarity means harder to separate objectives
- Provides guarantees: specified improvements in forget quality, minimal retain utility degradation, and explicit alerts when both objectives cannot be jointly improved
- Works on non-convex models; parallelizable; validated on image and text with large models
- Important for privacy-safe and alignment-motivated model editing

### 7. On the Scaling of PEFT: Towards Million Personal Models of Trillion Parameters
**arXiv:2606.02437** | Mind Lab (52+ authors)

Reframes PEFT not as a cost-reduction technique but as a substrate for **persistent, personalized representations** layered on top of shared foundation models.

- Explores three scaling dimensions: stronger base models, minimal adapter size with maintained performance, and managing large numbers of adapter instances simultaneously
- MinT infrastructure handles adapter lifecycle: identity tracking, evaluation, and deployment at scale
- Proposes that PEFT adapters can function as compact persistent personal models — analogous to long-term memory attached to a shared compute backbone
- Relevant to understanding how scaling interacts with fine-tuning granularity and model customization

### 8. BlockGen: Flexible Blockwise Sequence Modeling with Hybrid Samplers
**arXiv:2606.02241** | Justin Deschenaux, Caglar Gulcehre

Compares uniform-state and masked diffusion models for discrete sequence generation within a unified blockwise framework, with a novel hybrid sampler.

- Introduces AR-informed predictor-corrector sampling (ARPC): combines AR and diffusion predictions to regenerate unlikely tokens **without an auxiliary verifier**
- Uniform diffusion outperforms masked diffusion under simple sampling with few steps; masked diffusion dominates at higher compute budgets with ARPC
- Findings suggest the choice of discrete diffusion variant should depend on inference-time compute budget rather than a fixed preference
- Tested on GSM8K math reasoning and OpenWebText generative perplexity

### 9. Repurposing Adversarial Perturbations for Continual Learning: From Defense to Active Alignment
**arXiv:2606.02322** | Ran Liu et al.

AdvCL reinterprets adversarial perturbations as **geometric control signals** for stable continual adaptation in large language models, rather than threats to defend against.

- Three modular components: Intra-Smooth (promotes local representational smoothness), Proto-Clip (prevents excessive alignment to current-task prototypes), Inter-Align (reduces representational gaps to prior-task prototypes)
- Each module is independently plug-in compatible with existing continual learning frameworks
- Reduces catastrophic forgetting while improving knowledge transfer and robustness across tasks
- Offers a mechanistic handle on representation geometry during sequential fine-tuning

---

### 10. FOAM: Frequency and Operator Error-Based Adaptive Damping Method for Reducing Staleness-Oriented Error for Shampoo
**arXiv:2606.02365** | Kyunghun Nam, Sumyeong Ahn

Addresses the practical tension in Shampoo optimization: skipping expensive eigendecomposition updates saves compute but introduces staleness errors that degrade convergence.

- Theoretical analysis shows **adaptive damping** effectively suppresses staleness-induced instabilities
- FOAM estimates staleness-related errors online to jointly adapt damping strength and eigendecomposition frequency
- Achieves faster wall-clock training than standard Shampoo while preserving reliable convergence
- Relevant to training dynamics of large models where second-order methods are practically desirable but computationally constrained

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.02521** — *Drifting Preference Optimization for One-Step Generative Models* (Jiang, Wen, Liu): DrPO applies preference fine-tuning to single-forward-pass image generators without policy likelihoods or denoising trajectories — ranks candidates in feature space, reducing HPSv3 training compute by 3.5× over reward-backpropagation baselines. Relevant as an alignment-adjacent technique for preference optimization.

- **arXiv:2606.02031** — *OpenWebRL: Demystifying Online Multi-turn Reinforcement Learning for Visual Web Agents* (Yang et al.): Full open-source RL training pipeline for visual web agents on live websites; OpenWebRL-4B reaches 67% success on Online-Mind2Web with minimal initialization. Analyzes how RL shapes reasoning in multi-turn agent settings.

- **arXiv:2606.01973** — *A Closer Look at In-Distribution vs. Out-of-Distribution Accuracy for Open-Set Test-time Adaptation* (Li, Shelhamer): Evaluates SAR, OSTTA, UniEnt, SoTTA; finds current methods struggle to balance InD/OOD accuracy and imperfectly filter unknowns during adaptation. Proposes sigmoid-based multi-label outputs as a structural fix.

- **arXiv:2606.02134** — *Rethinking Evaluation Paradigms in IBP-based Certified Training* (Kaulen, Shavit, Hoos): Uses Pareto-front comparison with automated multi-objective HPO to reveal that published certified training results are substantially undertuned; prior improvements were less significant than claimed, and new performance tradeoffs are uncovered.
