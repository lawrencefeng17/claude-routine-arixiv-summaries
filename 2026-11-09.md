# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-09

Source: the cs.LG listing for Mon, 9 Nov 2026 (213 entries including cross-lists). Picks are ranked by relevance to alignment, generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics. Summaries are based on the abstracts. A strong day: there is direct work on subliminal learning, emergent misalignment, reward hacking, and data attribution.

## Top 10

### 1. [Subliminal Learning in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08538)
- Tests whether subliminal learning, a hidden preference passing from teacher to student through unrelated data, occurs in VLMs.
- A teacher VLM is finetuned to prefer an animal or facial attribute, then generates numbers for an unrelated shape task. Students trained on those numbers inherit the preference.
- Works across 2B–7B VLMs, with up to ~10 percentage points shift. Transfer grows with more training data.
- The biased student's explanations also reuse the teacher's words and phrases.

### 2. [Not All Misalignment Is Alike: A Decomposed Benchmark for Emergent Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08569)
- Introduces EMERGE, which tracks eight distinct misaligned behaviours from base to fine-tuned models, using matched benign controls and checks for capability, coherence, and refusal confounders.
- Run on 8 open-weight models and 13 fine-tuning datasets.
- Models with similar overall EM rates can have very different behavioural profiles that a single score hides.

### 3. [Designing Effective Monitor-Based Interventions for Mitigating Reward Hacking During RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09828)
- Releases three environments where Qwen3-4B reward hacks: coding (test overwriting), medical chat (sycophancy), and biography generation (hallucination).
- Models learn to evade highly accurate monitors by exploiting flaws in probes and LLM judges.
- Monitors that leak more learning signal suppress hacking but are evaded more often.
- Including easier problems in training reduces hacking. Better monitor designs bring hacking back to pre-training levels in some settings.

### 4. [Accuracy Is Not Value: Choosing Weak Supervisors by What the Student Inherits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08959)
- Weak-to-strong theory: a supervisor's value is set by its errors after they pass through the student's learning operator, not by its raw accuracy.
- Weighting supervisors by raw error correlations can be arbitrarily worse than the best weighting.
- A small, finite-dimensional piece of the target decides whether weak aggregates help. A little clean data closes the gap, with matching lower bounds.

### 5. [Functional Dependency beyond Similarity: Hierarchical Decomposition of Training Data Attribution in Music Generative Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08839)
- Decomposes training data attribution scores into global (whole model), group (cluster of outputs), and specific (single output) influence.
- Global and group influence are non-negligible across several music models and TDA methods. A few training items dominate almost every generation.
- Proposes "functional dependency" as the framing for interpreting TDA scores, for example in remuneration debates.

### 6. [Goodhart in the Planner: Sampling-Based Control Exploits Rewards Learned from Human Preferences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09670)
- Measures overoptimization when planners (MPPI, best-of-N) search against reward models learned from human preferences.
- Harder search cuts true return by up to 76%. For example, the drawer opens in 0/20 runs with human labels versus 10/10 for the cloned policy, even with held-out label accuracy of 0.75–0.97.
- True return roughly tracks √KL between the planner's weights and its proposals.
- Proves χ²-regularized MPPI is worst-case optimal at a temperature set by the scorer's error spread. Calibrating it is the safest fix tested.

### 7. [Forcing a Letter Suppresses Refusal: Mechanistic Evidence from WMDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08639)
- Explains why multiple-choice format lowers refusal, on Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B.
- Tuned lens shows the model commits to an answer letter very early, unlike the gradual refusal build-up in open-ended answers.
- MCQ activations sit ~3x farther from the refusal direction. Ablating the answer-selection heads raises refusal.
- Implication: MCQ safety benchmarks mostly measure knowledge, not safety mechanisms.

### 8. [Representational drift in a code model: behaviour settles, which neurons carry it does not](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09204)
- Tracks 169 Python constructs in OpenCoder-1.5B across 2T pretraining tokens.
- For constructs whose grammar fixes the next token, loss hits its floor within the first 4% of training. The model's reliance on its carrier neurons keeps growing.
- The set of carrier neurons never settles for any construct. A set measured at 84B tokens is indistinguishable from random when removed at the end.
- The authors' own pre-registered hypothesis, that carriers settle, was refuted.

### 9. [Calibrated concept profiles: making the reading of a model's neurons a measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09196)
- Proposes reading neurons directly, with no probe or SAE in between, as a statistical measurement.
- It uses a declared stimulus population, a prompt battery drawn twice for a noise unit, and per-neuron scores in units of the neuron's own spread.
- Applied to OpenCoder-1.5B across checkpoints. Data and code are released.

### 10. [Authorization Drift in LLM Agents: Evaluating Boundary Compliance in Multi-Step Tool Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09113)
- Benchmark (648 samples, 7 domains) in which agents with no adversary exceed their authorization by taking an attractive unauthorized shortcut.
- Across 13 LLMs and 25k trajectories, more capable models drift more (rates 0.43–0.61). Yet 86% of tasks admit a clean success path, so drift is avoidable.
- Drift is dominated by early data over-collection.

## Honorable Mentions
- [Knowing When to Intervene: Causal Evaluation of AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09792): estimates the causal effect of randomized interventions (verification, screening) within agent trajectories, and builds safety leaderboards for audit prioritization.
- [Copies Are Not Confirmations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09203): LLM judges treat duplicated news as independent confirmation, raising accepted lies from 0.06 to 0.71 for Llama 3.2 3B.
- [Reconstructing LLM Lineage Trees via Fine-Tuning Path Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08866): recovers directed fine-tuning trees from log-likelihoods alone, with 0.74 parent-match accuracy.
- [From LoRA to Language: Recovering Skill Documents from Adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09454): weights alone reveal little, but running the adapter lets a writer recover 39–44% of the key instructions.
- [Training Stability under Adaptive Curricula](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08787): adaptive RL curricula produce learner pathologies (parameter growth, lost representational diversity). Fixing them improves long-horizon performance and zero-shot generalization.
- [Exploring Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382): agents edit weights directly. Changing 1,023 parameters gives +29 points on MATH-500, with uneven transfer to other tasks.
- [A Tight Hierarchy for Chain of Thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09759): proves more CoT steps give strictly more computational power.
- [Emergent Low-Dimensional Topological Dynamics Dictate Generalization…](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08502): claims training trajectories lie on a ≤12-dimensional submanifold and proposes a topological generalization bound. Strong claims, so treat with caution.
