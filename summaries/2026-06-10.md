# arXiv cs.LG Digest — June 10, 2026

> Topics of focus: language models, AI safety, alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, science of generalization, data attribution.

## Top 10 Papers

---

### 1. [Predicting Future Behaviors in Reasoning Models Enables Better Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11172)
*Evgenii Kortukov, Piotr Komorowski, Florian Klein, Paula Engl, Gabriele Sarti, Seong Joon Oh, Sebastian Lapuschkin, Wojciech Samek*

- Distinguishes **detection features** (identify already-generated behavior) from **prediction features** (forecast future behavior); detection features are poor intervention targets.
- Activation probes trained on intermediate reasoning steps achieve **64–91% accuracy** at predicting likely future behavioral outcomes.
- Introduces **Future Probe Controlled Generation**: samples candidate sentences and selects optimal outputs via behavioral prediction probes, succeeding where traditional activation steering fails with near-zero output quality degradation.
- Directly relevant to AI safety and behavioral alignment of reasoning models.

---

### 2. [From Observation to Intervention: A Causal Audit of Expert Importance in Mixture-of-Experts Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10703)
*Leonard Engmann, Christian Medeiros Adriano, Holger Giese*

- Tests whether observational routing metrics (utilization, activation norms) predict the causal effect of removing individual experts in LLMs.
- Key finding: **no observational metric predicts causal expert importance** after multiple-comparison correction across all tested architectures (60 metric-layer combinations).
- Existing pruning succeeds not by identifying expendable experts but because **early-layer redundancy** makes most selection strategies functionally equivalent.
- A direct challenge to a widely used interpretability assumption — that population-level statistics support claims about individual token-level interventions.

---

### 3. [Recoverable but Not Stationary: Local Linear Structures in Weights and Activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10929)
*Irina Piontkovskaia, Sergey Nikolenko*

- Studies whether learned behaviors can be controlled via linear directions in weight and activation space of transformers.
- Finds strong **local low-rank task-gradient patterns** but no globally stable task planes — useful bases shift substantially across ~100 training steps.
- Trajectory-prefix basis captures **77% of recovery displacement**; a single gradient step produces activation shifts with **0.58 cosine similarity** to labeled contrast vectors.
- Develops a Gaussian local-linear theorem explaining why random parameter search works in high dimensions.
- Has direct implications for activation steering, LoRA, and our understanding of how linear representations evolve during training.

### 4. [A Unifying Lens on Supervised Fine-Tuning Through Target Distribution Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11189)
*Tong Xie, Yuanhao Ban, Yunqi Hong, Sohyun An, Yihang Chen, Cho-Jui Hsieh*

- Reframes SFT as **target distribution design** rather than one-hot token matching, arguing that ignoring the pretrained model's knowledge prior is suboptimal.
- Introduces a Q-target framework decomposing fine-tuning supervision into: how much to trust the observed token, and how to spread remaining probability mass.
- Shows existing SFT variants (label smoothing, knowledge distillation, etc.) are implicit special cases.
- **Target-SFT** built on this principle improves consistently across 10 reasoning datasets — a principled new axis for understanding what SFT actually optimizes.

---

### 5. [Beyond Uniform Token-Level Trust Region in LLM Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10968)
*Renjie Mao, Xiangxin Zhou, Lvfang Tao, et al.*

- Identifies two problems with PPO-style trust regions for RLHF: (1) **uniform thresholds ignore compounding divergence** from early-position errors, (2) no accounting for cumulative prefix drift across steps.
- Proposes **CPPO** (Cumulative Prefix-divergence Policy Optimization): position-weighted thresholds enforce stricter constraints early in sequences; a prefix budget tracks historical deviations to prevent error compounding.
- Improves training stability and reasoning accuracy across multiple model scales.

---

### 6. [Overcoming Rank Collapse in Feedback Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11123)
*Gauthier Boeshertz, Razvan Pascanu, Claudia Clopath*

- Identifies why Feedback Alignment (a biologically plausible backprop alternative) fails to scale: its error signals have **much lower effective rank** than backprop, confining learning to a restricted subspace.
- Two fixes tested: Muon (orthogonalizing weight updates) and hidden activity normalization — both substantially improve performance.
- Achieves a **+9 pp accuracy gain on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18**.
- Relevant to training dynamics and to understanding what makes gradient-based learning work at all.

---

### 7. [Conservation Laws from Data Symmetry in Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10913)
*Jakob Galley, Vahid Shahverdi, Axel Flinth*

- Asks whether training data symmetries generate conserved quantities during gradient-flow training (a Noether-style question for neural networks).
- Key finding: for analytic, non-polynomial losses, data symmetries typically **do not** generate additional conserved quantities — but MSE loss with data augmentation **can**.
- Introduces *tensorizable networks* (linear, polynomial, Lightning Attention) as the family where such conservation laws arise.
- Contributes to the theoretical science of generalization and training dynamics.

---

### 8. [XtrAIn: Training-Guided Occlusion for Feature Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10877)
*Thodoris Lymperopoulos, Ioannis Kakogeorgiou, Denia Kanellopoulou*

- Traditional occlusion attribution suffers from: (1) bias from hand-crafted baselines, (2) **attribution shift** — occluding a feature nonlinearly changes scores for remaining features.
- Moves occlusion from input space to **parameter space**: tracks training trajectory and measures how feature-associated parameter updates influence output logits.
- Two variants: Xstep (efficient approximation) and XtrAIn+ (target-class-aligned).
- Produces cleaner, more interpretable attribution patterns; connects interpretability to data attribution / training dynamics.

---

### 9. [When to Align, When to Predict: A Phase Diagram for Multimodal Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11190)
*Ilay Kamai, Hugues Van Assel, Aviv Regev, Hagai B. Perets, Randall Balestriero*

- Derives a **phase diagram** for multimodal learning: when does cross-modal alignment work vs. cross-modal prediction, vs. neither?
- Using a spiked signal-plus-noise model, shows alignment fails with strongly correlated nuisance factors; prediction depends on source-modality quality.
- Four regimes identified; provides a data-driven diagnostic procedure requiring minimal labeled data.
- Relevant to science of generalization and to understanding where cross-modal training is beneficial or harmful.

---

### 10. [Unifying Data, Memory, and Compute Efficiency in LLM Training: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10706)
*Vanessa Schmidt, Huy Hoang Nguyen, Cédric Jung, Shirin Salehi, Anke Schmeink*

- Surveys resource constraints in LLM development across three axes: **data efficiency** (selection and pruning per token), **memory efficiency** (GPU bottlenecks during fine-tuning), and **compute budget** awareness.
- Key insight: optimal training subsets depend on task objective and resource budget — no universal best data selection strategy.
- Frames training and inference under a unified resource-conditioned decision-making framework; proposes stopping rules when marginal gains fall below budget-dependent thresholds.
- Useful broad reference for data attribution and training efficiency connections.
