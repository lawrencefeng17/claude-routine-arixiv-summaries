# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-05

Source: the cs.LG listing for Thu, 5 Nov 2026 (~400 entries including cross-lists). Picks are ranked by relevance to alignment, generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics. A thin day for emergent-misalignment, subliminal-learning, or data-attribution work, so several picks are adjacent.

## Top 10

### 1. [Do unlearning algorithms learn the right features?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06681)
- Matching outputs after unlearning can hide different internal features than a retrained model has.
- Proves that agreement on outputs can break after one gradient step on the heads if the features differ. Deleting a tiny fraction of data can make retraining pick qualitatively different features.
- Introduces a feature-recovery measure separating missing from extraneous features.
- Across 8 unlearning methods, good standard metrics often coexist with poor feature recovery. The mismatch predicts deviations under relearning, and correcting it reduces them.

### 2. [Understanding the Out-of-Distribution Generalization of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06438)
- Frames CoT as compositional OOD generalization: a shared single-step predictor is applied repeatedly to compose in-distribution transitions.
- OOD risk is bounded by summed ID sub-task risks times an O(k) factor independent of the number of steps T. The estimation term scales as O(√(T/n)).
- Gives a lower-bound construction. Experiments on Atom-Task and GSM8K support the theory and show explicit state tracking is crucial.

### 3. [On the Generalization Limitation of On-Policy Distillation for Post-RL Consolidation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05609)
- Near-perfect in-distribution results for (multi-teacher) on-policy distillation mask poor OOD behavior.
- On Qwen3, OOD scores sit 2.5–16% below the base model, and composed tasks are 18–24% below the best teachers.
- Teacher capability and data coverage are the two bottlenecks. Fixing both removes OOD forgetting.
- Update geometry is smaller and more spectrally concentrated than RL. Proposes geometry-based patching and base-preserving replay.

### 4. [How Far Can You Steer? Behavior-Capability Frontiers in Contrastive Activation Addition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06001)
- Studies CAA through a behavior-capability frontier: maximize behavior change under an explicit capability-loss budget.
- 4 models, 7 behaviors. Validation-selected effects transfer to held-out data (ρ=0.945). Joint success rises from 37.5% (1% budget) to 72.7% (3%).
- Controls show the effects come from learned directions, not generic perturbations. Representational geometry only modestly predicts cross-behavior transfer.
- Steering that passes protected benchmarks can still hurt untested capabilities. It is not a universal control knob.

### 5. [When Are Two SAE Latents the Same Feature?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05655)
- Optimal-transport framework for comparing SAE dictionaries: latent correspondence, dictionary-level discrepancy, and per-latent decomposition.
- Relates decoder, activation, and causal similarity, and clarifies feature splitting and absorption.
- Gives asymptotic inference and simultaneous bootstrap confidence bands. Experiments on four LMs locate cross-seed discrepancy.

### 6. [The Behavioral Rate of Fine-Tuning: Compression as a Measure of Useful Information](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06598)
- Defines a behavioral rate R*: the smallest compressed LoRA adapter that keeps a fixed fraction of the fine-tuning gain.
- The rate depends on the dataset–model pair, not the dataset alone, and is not explained by description length or reconstruction error.
- Compressing adapters trained on corrupted data improves them (GSM8K 23.7% → 82.9%, above the 77.6% frozen model). Compression filters the damage in the top singular directions.
- The compressed corrupted adapter also generalizes better off-task than a clean fine-tune.

### 7. [The Alignment Tax Is Graded: Task-Selective Capability Loss Under Length-Normalized DPO with SFT Retention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06476)
- Pre-registered study (Qwen3.5-4B, LoRA, RPO objective) of how capability changes as the DPO reward margin grows.
- Loss is task-selective: extraction preserved (−0.55 pts), JSON slight (−3.5), router severe (−30), with a transient format-break spike.
- ≥96% of the margin comes from suppressing rejected completions while the chosen reward stays flat.
- Small scale (one model, synthetic preferences), but a clean look at training dynamics.

### 8. [Reward Budgeting Reduces Premature Convergence in Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05942)
- Raising entropy alone doesn't reliably lift the RL plateau. Analyzes the implicit target policy and a sharpness factor κ.
- Reward budgeting gives each prompt a fixed total reward budget, a drop-in advantage construction with diminishing returns.
- Gains on Qwen3-8B and Llama3.2-3B (up to +12.5 on AMC23) without explicitly preserving entropy.

### 9. [SafeStart: Mitigating the Overlooked Safety Cost of Active Expert Reduction in MoE LLM Serving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06147)
- Running fewer experts per token for speed weakens refusal under jailbreaks (+6.6 to +18.4 pts harmful compliance on 3 of 9 models), while MMLU drops <2 pts.
- Most of the effect arises during the prompt and first few generated tokens.
- SafeStart runs all experts for the prompt and first 8 tokens, keeping ≥85% of the speedup and cutting the rise to 0.6–2.6 pts.
- Lesson: test refusal, not just accuracy, when changing inference.

### 10. [Objective-Conditioned Active Preference Learning for Multi-Objective Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05506)
- OGC-DPO treats each (response pair, objective) as a query and scores informativeness by the policy's implicit gradient, with no external reward model.
- Picks queries that expand each objective's gradient coverage under the same annotation budget.
- Finds that informative conflicting preferences are a useful training signal rather than noise.
