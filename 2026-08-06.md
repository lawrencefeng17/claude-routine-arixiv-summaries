## Top 10

### 1. [Looking in the Mirror: Introspecting Side-Effect Misalignments Induced by Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04347)
*Yoshida, Gomezjurado Gonzalez, Yamamoto, Naraki, Shimizu, Wang*

- Studies "side-effect introspection": using LoRA-based introspection adapters to detect and explain alignment shifts that emerge as *unintended* side effects of fine-tuning on ordinary, safety-unrelated tasks — the realistic emergent-misalignment scenario, as opposed to prior work that fine-tunes in a behavior on purpose and then asks the model to explain it.
- Introduces the Delta-Aware Introspection Adapter (DAIA), which processes both base-model activations and the activation *differences* induced by fine-tuning to increase sensitivity to what changed.
- Shows introspection learned this way generalizes to unseen fine-tuned models and unseen safety categories, with DAIA consistently beating existing introspection adapters.
- A direct, practical contribution to detecting emergent misalignment in deployment-realistic fine-tuning settings.

### 2. [Manipulation-Proof Oblivious Audits against Deceptive Model Providers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04365)
*Godinot, Azogagh, Ferry, Gambs*

- Targets the algorithmic-governance problem that audits are typically declared or detectable in advance, letting providers strategically manipulate outputs (e.g., inferring protected attributes and equalizing rates) to pass fairness checks without being genuinely fair.
- Proposes an oblivious audit protocol using Private Information Retrieval: the provider must label a large superset of instances without knowing which subset the auditor will actually score.
- Gives theoretical guarantees that hiding unfairness under this protocol requires falsifying a much larger number of responses, raising both the cost and detectability of manipulation, with efficient overhead and no changes to the audited model or pipeline.
- Directly relevant to AI safety governance and deceptive-model-provider concerns — a cryptographic tool for trustworthy third-party evaluation.

### 3. [A Counterexample to Fourier Alignment in Single-Neuron Modular Addition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04451)
*Memana*

- Refutes a conjecture (MAIS-O60) in the mechanistic-interpretability/grokking literature that a single ReLU neuron trained on modular addition always converges to a Fourier-aligned (single-frequency) solution.
- Constructs an explicit counterexample: an initially active neuron that dies in finite time and freezes at a limit whose Fourier energy is spread evenly across *all* nonzero frequencies, occurring with positive probability under standard Gaussian initialization.
- Extends the result (with an appendix generated by GPT-5.6 Sol) to every Clarke subgradient trajectory, smooth dead-zone ReLU approximations, and fixed-step full-batch gradient descent — showing the failure is robust, not a pathological edge case.
- A cautionary result for the "grokking → clean Fourier circuits" narrative that underlies much toy-model interpretability work on modular arithmetic.

### 4. [Protoreasoning in Tiny Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04980)
*Valle, Reid*

- Studies whether chain-of-thought helps ~1M-parameter transformers learn *genuinely general* algorithms rather than "bags of heuristics," using reasoning-friendly Dyck-language tasks (balanced bracket sequences) that are tractable at a scale amenable to detailed analysis, unlike frontier LLMs trained on opaque data.
- Finds that "protoreasoning" traces substantially close the out-of-distribution generalization gap.
- Ablations confirm the *content* of the reasoning trace — not merely the extra computation/tokens it buys — drives the improvement.
- A clean, small-scale science-of-generalization testbed for questions about what CoT reasoning actually teaches a model, directly relevant to understanding reasoning generality in larger LMs.

### 5. [The Loss Does Not See the Basis, but Adam Does](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05136)
*Singh*

- Explains why gradient descent on a factored model (W = UV^T) is implicitly biased toward low-rank solutions while Adam, from the same initialization, is not: gradient flow's low-rank mechanism is only inherited by optimizers that are equivariant to the loss's gauge symmetry (U,V) → (UQ, VQ), and Adam/RMSProp (coordinate-wise preconditioners) break that symmetry while GD, Muon, and Shampoo preserve it.
- Proves a structure theorem characterizing exactly which memoryless update rules are gauge-equivariant, and a transfer theorem showing gradient flow's implicit-bias properties carry over to that whole class.
- Empirically sorts nine optimizers by low-rank recovery error on matrix sensing, and shows in transformers that Adam separates gauge-equivalent initializations at the very first training step, leaving per-head weight-product invariants 56% apart in relative Frobenius distance by the end of training — a gap no post-hoc rotation can undo.
- A rigorous training-dynamics result showing "which optimizer you use" is really a decision about which solution basis gets selected, not just a convergence-speed knob.

### 6. [Robustness Emerges Early in Training Dynamics, but Is Not Preserved](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04442)
*Yang, Shi, Hu, Xing, Liu*

- Identifies a "robustness fading" phenomenon: shallow layers spontaneously develop robust representations and flat loss landscapes early in training, but standard optimization convergence erodes these properties rather than preserving them.
- Proposes two parameter-free interventions — Early-Phase Stabilization (EPS) and Asymmetric Weight Reversion (AWR) — that stabilize or recover the early-emergent robust configurations without architecture changes or extra learnable parameters.
- Demonstrates gains in downstream transfer, dynamic adaptation, and robustness benchmarks across multiple architectures.
- A concrete training-dynamics finding in the same family as "critical learning periods" work: what a network learns early can be structurally different from — and fragile against — what training converges to later.

### 7. [Privileged, but Biased: How PI-Conditioned Teachers Break Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04794)
*Harne, Karkar, Pandya, Awadallah, Nambi*

- Tests whether self-distillation (a self-teacher conditioned on privileged information like a reference answer, teaching a student that never sees it) works as a standalone training signal outside the narrow easy settings where it was reported to succeed — and finds that on harder QA, math, coding, and agentic tool-use tasks, per-token loss falls steadily while validation accuracy stalls or degrades.
- Traces the failure to "PI bias": the teacher's target is pulled toward the one specific reference trajectory it saw rather than toward correctness in general, so the student is trained to match low-information tokens (stopwords, punctuation, hedging) rather than the tokens that actually determine correctness.
- Notably, the loss lands hardest on exploratory tokens within otherwise-correct rollouts, penalizing the "hesitation" that reasoning requires.
- A rigorous negative result on a widely-hoped-for RL alternative, with a clean causal story about how distillation signals can decouple from task success — relevant to the training-dynamics side of the subliminal-learning/hidden-signal-transfer literature.

### 8. [Not Every Divergence Should Be Suppressed: Counterfactual Recoverability in On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04408)
*Jiang, Zhang, Yuan, Ma*

- Points out that standard on-policy distillation treats every student/teacher divergence the same, even though some erroneous prefixes are still recoverable by later correct reasoning and others are not — and shows divergence magnitude alone is a poor predictor of which is which (AUC 0.392).
- Introduces "counterfactual recoverability": replaying each error state through budget-matched teacher-continuation and rollback branches to label states recoverable, irreversible-but-avoidable, or ambiguous, then using that label to decide whether to retain, roll back, or conventionally supervise the trajectory.
- The resulting recoverability-aware control substantially outperforms divergence-based baselines on AIME and GPQA-Diamond (e.g., AIME2024-25 average@32 up from 0.2656 to 0.3125).
- A methodologically careful training-dynamics contribution to distillation — directly complementary to paper #7 above on when distillation signals help vs. hurt.

### 9. [What We Observe as LLM Behavior Can Be a Side-effect of Inference Backend](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04714)
*Masoudian, Shafaei, Swain, Schedl*

- Runs a fully-crossed study (3 models × 5 inference frameworks × 6 benchmarks × 4 generation modes) and finds that switching the serving backend (HuggingFace, vLLM, Ollama, etc.) — a detail almost never disclosed in papers — significantly and structurally changes benchmark scores, even under greedy, noise-free decoding.
- Decomposes the variance a practitioner sees "out of the box": roughly 39% is attributable to backend choice alone, with the rest split between sampling noise and each framework's default generation hyperparameters.
- Finds the effect is more pronounced on factual benchmarks than social-bias benchmarks, and recommends disclosing backend/version/generation config and using deterministic decoding for cross-backend comparability.
- An important methodological warning for the science-of-generalization and evaluation-validity literature: apparent capability or behavior differences between "the same model" in different papers may be measurement artifacts, not real differences.

### 10. [Suppression Sticks, Locality Is Fragile: A Closed-Loop Target-and-Control Audit of Task-Vector Negation in VLA Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04692)
*Wang, Guo, Dai, Zhao, Guo, Xiong*

- Audits task-vector arithmetic (subtracting a per-skill task vector to suppress that skill) in multitask vision-language-action policies, testing whether the edit is truly *local* — i.e., whether it harms only the targeted skill — under closed-loop robot control rather than one-shot benchmarks.
- Finds three distinct regimes across the ten LIBERO-Goal skills: clean target/control separation for five skills, resistance to suppression for three, and global collapse for two; even where suppression cleanly succeeds (target success drops to 0%), mean control retention on unrelated skills is only 52%, and every target-suppressing edit measurably harms at least one nominally unrelated skill.
- Shows a single-skill relearning probe is more consistent with the skill being "masked" than truly unlearned — a certified-unlearning concern for model-editing claims.
- A rigorous interpretability/model-editing audit showing that a popular activation/weight-space steering technique is fast and gradient-free but behaviorally far less local than commonly assumed — relevant to anyone using task vectors as an interpretability or control tool.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[DeepInvert: Semi-Supervised Embedding Inversion Against Obfuscated Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04477)** — shows popular "obfuscate-then-transmit" prompt privacy defenses leak far more than believed; a semi-supervised inversion attack recovers up to 73.5% of original tokens (vs. 26.2% prior best), and finds a fundamental utility/inversion-resistance tension in this defense class.
- **[SPOT: Sparse Probing and Outcome Calibration for On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04419)** — a third paper in today's on-policy-distillation cluster, using entropy/top-k probing plus verifier-scored student rollouts to decide where and what to distill, rather than blanket reverse-KL supervision.
- **[Relational Response Fields: A General Theory of Black-Box LLM Response Consistency and Recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04552)** — a theoretical framework identifying exactly when a collection of black-box LLM responses is recoverable from corruption at all, with a matching minimax lower bound; explicitly notes that consistency is not truth (shared hallucinations are invisible to relation-only repair).
- **[Provable Limits and Certified Deferral for Verbalized Uncertainty in Small Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05064)** — proves temperature scaling provably cannot calibrate models whose confidence stays above 50% while accuracy falls below it, and finds only 3 of 22 model-task pairs earn a certified-safe-to-defer guarantee at a 20% risk budget.
- **[Non-asymptotic implicit bias of logistic regression at early-stage gradient descent dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04382)** — gives a tight non-asymptotic bound (O(exp(exp(-δ)))) for how fast gradient descent weakly aligns with the max-margin direction, explaining the empirical "train a little longer, generalize better" effect well before the (much slower) asymptotic regime kicks in.
- **[Eliciting Intrinsic Hallucinations in LLMs via Semantically Equivalent Adversarial Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04286)** — shows meaning-preserving paraphrase attacks degrade RAG faithfulness by up to 50% (GPT-5-mini), even in state-of-the-art models, indicating grounding to retrieved evidence is fragile to surface query form.
- **[When More Becomes Less: Position-Dependent Repetition Effects in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04021)** — shows repeated-target priming effects invert into a U-shape when the readout slot is displaced into a fresh sentence frame, replicating across 13 models and 4 languages; a methodological caution for cloze-probe-based interpretability studies.
