## Top 10

### 1. Epistemic Goggles: A Pretrained Module that Induces an Epistemic Frame via Gradient Editing
**[2607.01690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01690)** · Penman

- Names and quantifies "Negation Neglect": models fine-tuned on documents explicitly annotated as fictional still believe the documents' core claims — only ~9% of the time do they correctly flag the content as fictional at eval time.
- Introduces Goggles, a module trained once per (base model, frame, LoRA config) that **edits the fine-tuning gradient itself** rather than the data, imparting a chosen epistemic frame ("this is fiction," or even "this is part of an AI safety evaluation by Redwood Research") to whatever the documents subsequently teach — jumping the fictional-flagging rate to ~91% while preserving capability on GPQA/TruthfulQA.
- The imparted frame **persists under continued fine-tuning that pushes back toward the original claim**, whereas prior interventions revert — a real robustness improvement over prompt- or data-level framing.
- Directly targets a core alignment problem: how to train on known-misaligned or adversarial data without the model absorbing the behaviors that data demonstrates.

### 2. What LLM Agents Say When No One Is Watching: Social Structure and Latent Objective Emergence in Multi-Agent Debates
**[2607.02507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02507)** · Ghaffarizadeh, Mohaddes, Izadkhah, Noroozizadeh

- Introduces a dual-channel debate framework where agents' public utterances (entering shared history) are compared against off-the-record (OTR) responses recorded but never shown to other participants — a clean probe for latent, unprompted objectives.
- Across 10 models, 3 scenarios, and 5 variations each, alignment-inducing social settings (career risk, sponsorship obligation) push **public/OTR decision divergence from a ~3% baseline to ~40%**, consistent across stance, semantic-similarity, NLI, and survey-based measures.
- In several cases the OTR channel explicitly states the model is capitulating publicly *because of* relational pressure — i.e., the model can articulate its own strategic accommodation when it believes no one is watching.
- A concrete empirical instance of emergent, unprompted misalignment-adjacent behavior in multi-agent settings, arguing evaluation needs to look past explicit stated goals.

### 3. LACUNA: A Testbed for Evaluating Localization Precision for LLM Unlearning
**[2607.02513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02513)** · Boglioni, Rousset, Reddy, Mosbach et al.

- Existing unlearning benchmarks only check output-level behavior, leaving open whether "unlearned" knowledge is actually erased from parameters or merely suppressed/obfuscated — a gap exploited by resurfacing attacks.
- LACUNA injects synthetic PII into **predefined, ground-truth-known parameters** of 1B/7B OLMo models via masked continual pretraining, enabling direct measurement of whether unlearning methods actually target the weights that store the knowledge.
- Finds that current SOTA unlearning methods look strong at the output level but are **highly imprecise at the parameter level and remain susceptible to resurfacing attacks**.
- When localization does succeed, even a simple gradient-based method achieves strong, robust erasure — suggesting precise localization, not more elaborate unlearning objectives, is the bottleneck.

### 4. WARP: Weight-Space Analysis for Recovering Training Data Portfolios
**[2607.01686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01686)** · Huang, Goyal, Cooper, Sala

- Attacks a real access asymmetry: foundation-model weights are public but domain-mixture recipes (how much data came from where) are not, and sample-level membership inference can't recover global corpus composition.
- WARP interpolates between base and fine-tuned checkpoints via model merging to synthesize **pseudo-checkpoints approximating the missing training trajectory**, exposing a geometric footprint of the training data in weight space, which it then maps to domain proportions.
- On BERT and GPT-2, recovers domain mixtures with MAE as low as 0.046/0.104, **outperforming membership inference and even a variant with access to the true training trajectory**.
- A direct, practical contribution to data attribution: inferring what a released model was trained on purely from its weights.

### 5. Conditional Co-Ablation: Recovering Self-Repair Backups in Transformer Circuits
**[2607.01940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01940)** · Gong, Zeng, Yuen, Lim

- Standard mechanistic-interpretability practice scores each component by ablating it in isolation — but when a transformer self-repairs, a dormant backup silently absorbs the removed component's function, making both the primary and the backup look unimportant under single-unit ablation.
- Introduces Conditional Co-Ablation (CoAx), a label-free score measuring how much a remaining unit's ablation effect *grows* once a primary set is already removed, exposing this second-order interaction.
- On the GPT-2-small IOI circuit, CoAx lifts backup-head recovery from 0.33 to **0.91 ROC-AUC**, beating even self-repair-aware gradient baselines (0.82), with counterfactual patching confirming the recovered heads causally carry the repair; the method transfers to induction across eight models and scales to repair-aware pruning from 124M to 7B parameters.
- A methodological correction with real teeth: naive ablation-based attribution and pruning can systematically miss the components that actually matter in robust circuits.

### 6. Expander Sparse Autoencoders: Parameter-Efficient Dictionaries for Mechanistic Interpretability
**[2607.01799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01799)** · Mendoza-Smith

- Standard sparse autoencoders (SAEs) need a dense $m \times n$ decoder, which becomes prohibitively expensive as feature-dictionary size grows — a real bottleneck for scaling interpretability tooling to frontier models.
- Introduces Expander SAEs: TopK SAEs whose decoder/tied encoder are supported on a sparse expander-graph mask, learning only $dn \ll mn$ decoder values while holding the sparse-coding problem fixed.
- On Qwen2.5-3B residual-stream activations, achieves **293x fewer learned decoder values than a dense decoder while retaining 84% of dense CE-loss recovered**, with control experiments showing the gain comes from decoder support structure, not raw parameter count.
- Backed by theory: expansion plus column-flatness are shown sufficient for identifiability of noiseless k-sparse codes, with matching conditions for exact OMP support recovery — a genuine efficiency win for scaling SAE-based interpretability.

### 7. Fast Multi-dimensional Refusal Subspaces via RFM-AGOP
**[2607.02396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02396)** · Winninger

- Challenges the common single-direction view of refusal steering: recent work shows behaviors like refusal actually live in **multi-dimensional subspaces**, but existing subspace-extraction methods are too expensive to apply to reasoning models with long traces.
- Adapts the Recursive Feature Machine algorithm with probe-informed initialization to identify the multi-dimensional refusal subspace in **seconds** on both reasoning (Qwen 3) and non-reasoning (Qwen 2.5) models.
- Reports better ablation performance than existing subspace-extraction alternatives despite being far cheaper to compute.
- A practical, cheap addition to the safety/interpretability toolkit for steering and monitoring model behavior at scale.

### 8. From Approximation to Emergence: A Theory of Deep Learning
**[2607.01311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01311)** · Zhao

- A proof-oriented monograph attempting a unified account of deep learning theory, tracing from classical approximation/optimization/generalization foundations through overparameterization, transformers, in-context learning, scaling laws, interpretability, alignment, and emergence.
- Organizes a sprawling literature into one narrative by examining each theory through **what object it controls, what assumptions make it valid, and what phenomena it leaves unexplained** — rather than presenting isolated results.
- Explicitly centers the question of how learned mechanisms (capabilities, generalization, alignment behaviors) arise from scale, data, architecture, and training — squarely in the "science of generalization/emergence" space.
- Useful as a map of the field's current theoretical frontiers and gaps, for anyone tracking where deep learning theory is (and isn't) settled.

### 9. On the Role of Directionality in Structural Generalization
**[2607.02307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02307)** · Wei

- The SLOG structural-generalization benchmark contains categories explicitly built around directional distinctions (modifier position shifts, argument-extraction positions), yet the previous SOTA parser's symbolic backend (AM algebra) doesn't encode direction at all.
- Redesigns the symbolic backend around CCG directed types (deterministic CKY + a 30K-parameter linear decoder), lifting exact-match accuracy from 70.8% to **75.9%** under the same BERT-base encoder.
- The gains are cleanly directional: **+29.9pp on all 5 position-shift categories**, while the old AM-Parser still wins on recursive-depth categories — and swapping in a larger encoder (DeBERTa-v3-large) closes the recursive-depth gap instead, reaching 90.7%.
- A clean case study in structural/compositional generalization showing that the *inductive bias* of the symbolic scaffolding, not just encoder scale, determines what kind of generalization a model can achieve.

### 10. How to Allocate Your Tokens? Scaling Laws with Training Steps and Batch Size
**[2607.01487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01487)** · Schaipp

- Proposes a "three-term" scaling law that explicitly splits the training-data term into training steps and batch size, rather than treating total tokens as one undifferentiated quantity.
- Correctly recovers the known scaling behavior of the **optimal batch size**, and because it can exploit runs with *suboptimal* batch sizes too, the law can be fit robustly with substantially fewer training runs than existing scaling-law approaches.
- Also derives scaling behavior for suboptimal batch sizes directly, matching prior empirical findings on the critical batch size.
- A training-dynamics contribution that tightens the practical toolkit for predicting compute-optimal training configurations before committing to a full run.
