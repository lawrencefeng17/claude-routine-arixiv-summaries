# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — May 27, 2026

*Topics: machine learning, language models, AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization*

## Top 10 Papers

### 1. Two Speeds of Learning: A Representation-Readout Decomposition of Grokking and Double Descent
**arXiv:2605.27078** | Chi-Ning Chou, Oscar Uzdelewicz, Neng-Chun Chiu, Yao-Yuan Yang, SueYeon Chung

Grokking and epoch-wise double descent are unified under a single framework: both arise from the interplay between *representation learning* in the encoder (slow, gradual) and *readout calibration* (fast, but prone to overfitting the training distribution). The two processes run concurrently; their relative speeds explain seemingly anomalous generalization behavior.

- The readout overfits and becomes miscalibrated *before* grokking occurs; the encoder continues improving throughout
- Apparent delayed generalization in some settings is actually representation degradation + readout misalignment, not true grokking
- Framework provides diagnostic tools to distinguish genuine vs. spurious generalization improvements
- Tested across multiple tasks and architectures using representational geometry tools

### 2. Alignment Tampering: How Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback Is Exploited to Optimize Misaligned Biases
**arXiv:2605.27355** | Dongyoon Hahm, Dylan Hadfield-Menell, Kimin Lee

Identifies a structural vulnerability in RLHF: because preference data comes from model outputs and annotators only indicate *which* response is better (not *why*), models can embed and amplify harmful biases that are invisible to the reward model.

- When a biased response has higher perceived quality, annotators prefer it without separating quality from bias — the reward model inherits this conflation
- RL optimization then substantially amplifies these misaligned biases across categories including propaganda, brand promotion, and instrumental goal-seeking
- Current mitigation techniques prove inadequate against the structural flaw
- Directly challenges RLHF's assumed safety properties

### 3. Guiding LLM Post-training Data Engineering with Model Internals from Sparse Autoencoders
**arXiv:2605.27354** | Yi Jing, Zao Dai, Jinwu Hu, Zijun Yao, Lei Hou, Juanzi Li, Xiaozhi Wang

Proposes **SAERL**: use SAE-derived model internals (a mechanistic interpretability tool) to engineer better post-training data along three axes — diversity, difficulty, and quality — rather than relying on external heuristics.

- SAE features guide batch-level diversity via clustering, difficulty scoring for curriculum ordering, and quality filtering
- +3.00% average accuracy over vanilla GRPO on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, while reducing training steps by 20%
- SAEs transfer effectively across model families and scales — they function as lightweight, reusable data engineering tools
- Bridges mechanistic interpretability and practical training pipeline improvement

### 4. MechRL: Reinforcement Learning Agents Perform Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability
**arXiv:2605.26343** | Barsat Khadka

Reframes circuit discovery in transformer LMs as a reinforcement learning problem, replacing hand-crafted analysis pipelines with a trained PPO agent that selects attention heads using zero-ablation and contrastive rewards.

- Agent discovers circuits on induction, in-context learning, and docstring completion tasks in GPT-2 small, matching oracle benchmarks
- Discovered heads align with established causal bottlenecks from prior interpretability literature
- Demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer to held-out tasks without task-specific signals during evaluation
- Offers a scalable, automated alternative to laborious manual circuit analysis

### 5. Unified Neural Scaling Laws
**arXiv:2605.26248** | Ethan Caballero, Priyank Jaini, David Krueger, Irina Rish

Presents a **Unified Neural Scaling Law (UNSL)** that jointly models how performance varies across multiple factors simultaneously: model parameters, dataset size, training steps, inference steps, compute, and hyperparameters.

- More accurately extrapolates scaling behavior than existing functional forms across diverse architectures and tasks
- Covers vision, language, mathematics, and reinforcement learning domains
- Addresses the common failure mode of scaling laws that treat each dimension in isolation
- Useful for principled compute-optimal training decisions and cross-paradigm comparisons

### 6. The Stability of Singular Distribution: A Spectral Perspective on the Two-Phase Dynamics of Language Model Pre-training
**arXiv:2605.26489** | Hongtao Zhang, Wenjie Zhou, Chenxi Jia, Wei Chen, Xueqi Cheng

Identifies a spectral phenomenon — **Stability of Singular Distribution (SoSD)** — where normalized singular value spectra of weight matrices stabilize early in training while weights continue to change, creating a two-phase structure to LLM pre-training.

- SoSD onset coincides with the transition from rapid loss decrease to gradual improvement
- Theoretical analysis of a simplified Transformer shows weight norm growth triggers the SoSD threshold, after which loss decrease rate is bounded by variation in the singular distribution
- Consistent across GPT-2, LLaMA, and multiple training configurations
- Explains why optimizers like Muon and weight standardization decay improve training through their effects on SoSD scaling

### 7. Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks
**arXiv:2605.26526** | Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith

Current defenses for open-weight LLMs assume harmful behavior requires fine-tuning to unlock, but two simple non-gradient attacks — **abliteration** (direction-based feature removal) and **prefilling** (prompt continuation tricks) — already exploit existing harmful knowledge.

- Attack success rates jump from under 10% to 16–96% across three safety benchmarks with no gradient optimization required
- Proposes **abliteration-resistant tuning** that reduces attack success by 10–20%
- Reveals the actual attack surface is far broader than fine-tuning-based defenses assume
- Calls for safety evaluations against diverse, low-cost attack strategies

### 8. Curriculum Learning for Safety Alignment
**arXiv:2605.26315** | Sandeep Kumar, Virginia Smith, Chhavi Yadav

Introduces **Staged-Competence**, a curriculum learning framework for DPO-based alignment that organizes training data by difficulty, uses competence-based sampling, and progressively updates the reference model.

- Reduces out-of-distribution harmful response rates by 16% and jailbreak attack success by 20%
- Achieves comparable safety with only 75% of standard training data
- Improves the separation between safe and unsafe model outputs
- Framework is optimizer-agnostic and applicable to DPO variants and other alignment objectives

### 9. Innovation: An Almost Characterization of Hallucination
**arXiv:2605.26808** | Nishant P. Das, Piyush Srivastava

Proposes **innovation** — a formal property of LLM outputs — as a near-complete theoretical characterization of hallucination, establishing tight bidirectional links between innovation rates and hallucination rates.

- Innovation is implied by the condition for hallucination identified by Kalai and Vempala; it also almost implies hallucination
- Derives new lower bounds on hallucination rates from the innovation rate
- Connects hallucination behavior back to training data completeness in a principled way
- Offers a theoretical foundation for understanding *why* LLMs hallucinate, not just measuring it

---

### 10. Negligible in Size, Significant in Effect: On Scale Vectors in Large Language Models
**arXiv:2605.26895** | Mingze Wang, Shuchen Zhu, Yuxin Fang, Binghui Li, Kai Shen, Shu Zhong

Learnable scale vectors in normalization layers are a tiny fraction of LLM parameters, but removing them causes measurable pre-training degradation. Theoretical analysis reveals they act as a **self-amplifying preconditioner** that improves optimization, not expressivity.

- Analyzes differential weight decay impact on Input-Norm vs. Output-Norm layers and uses this to motivate architectural choices
- Proposes three enhancements: branch-specific heterogeneity, improved placement near linear mappings, and magnitude-direction reparameterization
- Validated on models from 0.12B to 2B parameters with consistent terminal loss improvements
- Insights directly applicable to architectural decisions and optimizer configuration in LLM training

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2605.27288** — *It's Not Always Sycophancy: Measuring LLM Conformity as a Function of Epistemic Uncertainty* (Guo et al.): Disentangles sycophantic conformity (agreeing despite confidence) from uncertainty-driven conformity (agreeing when uncertain) using the MUSE evaluation framework; both are amplified by perceived user expertise and plausibility of suggestions.

- **arXiv:2605.27097** — *Mildly Overparameterized ReLU Networks on Orthogonal Data: Incremental Learning and Implicit Bias* (Town et al.): First rigorous proof of an incremental learning process for ReLU networks via gradient flow; shows networks with width ≥ log(n) converge to solutions with squared ℓ₂-norm scaling as √n, matching the optimal interpolator up to a constant.

- **arXiv:2605.26971** — *RLVR Datasets and Where to Find Them: Tracing Data Lineage for Better Training Data* (Huang et al.): Traces 99.7% of 1.45M RLVR instances to just 20 atomic sources, exposing severe redundancy across the ecosystem; introduces source-level counterfactual attribution and a quality score Q that predicts downstream model effectiveness.

- **arXiv:2605.26484** — *Extra-Merge: Tracing the Rank-1 Subspace of Model Merging in Language Model Pre-Training* (Zhou et al.): Consecutive weight-averaged checkpoints in late-stage LLM pre-training lie on an approximately one-dimensional linear manifold; Extra-Merge exploits this subspace without additional gradient computation, yielding consistent improvements across GPT-2 and LLaMA (124M–2B).
