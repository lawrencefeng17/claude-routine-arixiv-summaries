### 1. Test-Time Training Undermines Safety Guardrails
**arXiv:2605.22984** | AI Safety

Test-Time Training (TTT) — adapting model parameters at inference time — creates exploitable attack vectors that bypass safety filters. LoRA-based attacks achieve up to 95% Attack Success Rate across multiple model families, and vulnerabilities transfer to production fine-tuning APIs.

- Three distinct threat models identified for TTT-based safety bypasses
- TTT-induced overfitting inflates ASR under standard evaluation; a "validity-aware" correction is proposed
- Lightweight detection via perplexity shifts is introduced, but robust defense requires rethinking dynamic alignment

### 2. Every Component is a Lookup: Token Attribution and Composition from a Single Decomposition
**arXiv:2605.23393** | Mechanistic Interpretability

**Unpack** exploits the shared key-value template structure of both attention and MLP layers to derive end-to-end token attribution, composition labels (K/Q/V), and per-token paths in a single forward pass — no interventions, gradients, or auxiliary training required.

- Recovers all three composition connections on the IOI task in GPT-2 small, including mode-specific routing
- Scales across the Pythia family (160M–6.9B) and consistently recovers suppression patterns without ground-truth circuit labels
- Demonstrates that duplicate-token suppression is mechanistically distinct from standard copying

### 3. Decomposing and Measuring Evaluation Awareness
**arXiv:2605.23055** | AI Safety / Evaluation

Frontier LLMs sometimes detect they are being evaluated and adjust behavior accordingly, undermining benchmark validity. This paper provides a framework and benchmark (EvalAwareBench) that decomposes evaluation awareness into eight independently toggleable trigger factors.

- Models show higher sensitivity to safety evaluations than capability evaluations — a direct concern for safety benchmarks
- Recognition rarely leads to behavioral change, and when it does, direction depends on the type of evaluation perceived
- Behavioral consistency under recognition is proposed as the right alignment target

### 4. Represented Is Not Computed: Causal Test of Algorithmic Intermediates
**arXiv:2605.22488** | Interpretability

A Transformer trained on arithmetic (base-digit extraction) represents the expected intermediate values and is detectable by linear probes — but causal testing shows those intermediates are not transmitted along the causal pathway to the output. The model routes N, B, and D mostly separately, combining them late.

- Demonstrates a systematic gap between *what a model represents* and *what it actually computes*
- Probes are not reliable evidence of staged algorithmic computation
- Raises methodological implications for circuit-level interpretability claims

---

### 5. LLMs as Noisy Channels: A Shannon Perspective on Model Capacity and Scaling Laws
**arXiv:2605.23901** | Scaling Laws / Language Models

Maps LLM training to Shannon's noisy channel framework: model parameters ↔ channel bandwidth, training tokens ↔ signal power. Explains non-monotonic scaling phenomena (catastrophic overtraining, quantization degradation) that power-law models cannot capture.

- Introduces a Shannon capacity metric grounded in signal-to-noise ratio
- Produces U-shaped loss curves rather than monotone improvement, matching empirical observations
- Predicts performance of unseen 12B-parameter models trained on 307B tokens when fitted on smaller models

---

### 6. Non-Normal Spectral Signatures of Instability in Neural Network Training Dynamics
**arXiv:2605.23476** | Training Dynamics

Training instabilities (loss spikes, oscillatory convergence, gradient pathologies) arise from *non-normality* in the linearized update operators of practical optimizers. For Adam, this stems from the commutator between the Hessian and adaptive preconditioner; for SGD with momentum, from the augmented state-space structure.

- Derives a pseudospectral precursor bound: condition number κ(V) as an early-warning diagnostic
- κ(V) outperforms spectral radius by ~1 order of magnitude in separating stable from unstable training phases
- Positions non-Hermitian operator theory as a new lens for understanding optimization stability

### 7. A Mathematical Theory of Balancing Relational Generalization and Memorization
**arXiv:2605.22972** | Science of Generalization

Introduces "transitive inference with exceptions" as a testbed for the generalization-memorization tradeoff. Analytically characterizes kernel ridge regression across representations and shows that success depends sensitively on representational geometry.

- Pretrained LLMs finetuned on ordered relations exhibit the systematic errors predicted by the theory
- Explains why exception-handling is mechanistically harder than standard transitive inference
- Establishes representational geometry as the key variable controlling the tradeoff

---

### 8. The Implicit Bias of Depth: From Neural Collapse to Softmax Codes
**arXiv:2605.23087** | Training Dynamics / Theory

Without explicit regularization, depth itself induces implicit low-rank bias in trained classifiers. Low-rank matrices propagate norms more efficiently through deep layers, driving the network away from standard neural collapse toward softmax codes.

- Provides the first asymptotic and dynamic analysis of implicit bias under unregularized multiclass cross-entropy
- Early-time singular value repulsion drives low-rank emergence; depth narrows neural collapse's basin of attraction
- Counterintuitively, increasing *width* biases randomly initialized networks toward higher-rank solutions

---

### 9. Post-Training is About States, Not Tokens
**arXiv:2605.22731** | Post-Training / Alignment

Reformulates LLM post-training methods (SFT, RL, distillation) through the lens of *state distributions* (prompt + generated prefix) rather than loss functions alone. The source and locality of training states matter as much as the supervision signal.

- Intensive SFT causes substantial retention loss; mild SFT improves performance with minimal forgetting
- On-policy distillation from a *degraded* teacher still surpasses that teacher — state distribution matters more than teacher quality
- Lightweight on-policy RL maintains retention while improving target-task performance

---

### 10. Hierarchical Concept Geometry in Language Models Emerges from Word Co-occurrence
**arXiv:2605.23821** | Interpretability / Language Models

Derives a distributional theory of how hypernymy (is-a relations) is geometrically encoded in LLM embeddings. Proves that leading eigenvectors of the embedding Gram matrix progressively separate taxonomic branches from coarse to fine levels.

- Predictions confirmed across WordNet subtrees in word2vec *and* extend to Gemma 2B unembeddings
- Hierarchical concept organization emerges purely from co-occurrence statistics, not explicit architectural design
- Provides a mechanistic account of why LLMs represent taxonomic structure
