## Top 10

### 1. [Debate Training Reduces Reward Hacking in RLAIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17776)

- From a Google DeepMind alignment team (Kenton, Shah, Jaques, et al.): RL-finetunes an LLM via *debate* — a two-player adversarial game between a generator and a critic, adjudicated by a deliberately weaker LLM judge — and compares it to a standard RLAIF baseline.
- On verifiable math tasks, debate prevents the policy from exploiting the weak judge's systematic errors throughout training, while the RLAIF baseline reward-hacks; yields a 45% performance-gap improvement over the single-player baseline.
- Shows debate incentives override prompted misalignment, and that adding more debate rounds substantively compensates for judge weakness.
- A rare training-time result that scalable-oversight schemes actually blunt reward hacking, not just reduce it in principle.

### 2. [On the Fragility of Self-Improving Agents: Variance, Task Order, and Underspecification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18066)

- Stress-tests memory-based self-improving agents (agents that learn from online task streams via textual memory banks) across multiple runs and shuffled task orderings — a reliability check largely missing from prior self-improvement papers.
- Finds evaluation noise gets amplified once self-improvement loops are added, and performance depends heavily on task order — prior work's "default" orderings function as implicit, unacknowledged curricula.
- Traces the fragility to task/environment underspecification via manual memory inspection; adding rubrics and environment feedback partially, but not fully, closes the gap.
- A concrete case for treating agent self-improvement as unreliable-by-default until proven otherwise — relevant to anyone evaluating claims of emergent agent competence.

### 3. [MemCatalyst: Amplifying Data Auditing on Vision-Language Models via Data Poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17722)

- Addresses data attribution from the data-holder's side: how can an artist or data owner reliably prove their content was used in VLM training, given that membership-inference (MI) signal is normally weak?
- Introduces two poisoning strategies (text and image) that force the VLM to over-learn specific image-text inconsistencies during training, deliberately amplifying its later susceptibility to MI auditing.
- Poisoned samples transfer across different VLM architectures in a black-box setting, and markedly boost MI AUC across five audit methods and two VLMs with a small poisoning budget and negligible hit to model utility.
- A provocative attacker/defender-style contribution to the data-provenance and unauthorized-training-data problem.

### 4. [Mixture-of-Expert Blocks Contain Strong Hallucination Detection Signals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17687)

- Interpretability angle on MoE-specific internals: router entropy, expert disagreement, and expert usage patterns are signals unavailable in dense models and previously unexploited for hallucination detection.
- Introduces InnerExpert, which fuses these routing-level signals with standard transformer features into per-token vectors, classified by a lightweight detector trained on LLM-as-judge labels (so no manual labeling needed).
- Achieves up to 0.91 answer-level and 0.76 token-level AUROC across five datasets and two MoE architectures, using only a single forward pass — beating existing detection approaches.
- Suggests MoE routing carries real epistemic signal about the model's own uncertainty, not just a load-balancing artifact.

### 5. [Thinking in a Low-Resource Language: What SFT Builds, What RL Fixes, What Accuracy Cannot See](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17744)

- Fine-tunes three frontier MoE models to reason in Greek and finds standard accuracy benchmarks are nearly blind to what's actually happening — random-seed noise exceeds any measured intervention effect.
- SFT gets ~98% of reasoning traces into the target language but introduces new failure modes: a quarter of answers skip the requested format, answers leak into the reasoning channel, and explicit "think in English" instructions are followed under half the time.
- RL with verifiable, pre-registered rewards fixes the formatting collapse (24% → 2.5% fallback errors) and eliminates reasoning leakage entirely — a clean SFT-vs-RL dissociation in what each training stage actually changes about model behavior.
- Introduces six behavioral dimensions designed to see past accuracy-metric artifacts, releasing checkpoints and instruments for other low-resource languages.

### 6. [When to Review: Spaced Repetition for Continual Pre-Training of Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17530)

- Frames continual pre-training as an adaptive review-scheduling problem instead of a fixed old/new replay-ratio problem, since different examples are forgotten at different rates.
- Introduces Spaced Repetition Training (SRT), which adapts the SuperMemo-2 algorithm to LLM pretraining — maintaining per-example review state and mapping per-example perplexity to a recall-quality signal that schedules rehearsal.
- Recovers 5–37 percentage points of old-knowledge accuracy lost to naive continual pretraining across model scales, while preserving new-knowledge acquisition, with no changes to model, objective, or optimizer.
- A training-dynamics contribution that treats catastrophic forgetting as a scheduling problem, with an approach that reportedly extends beyond language to vision and tabular data.

### 7. [Understanding Curriculum Learning in Large Language Models via Cross-Difficulty Optimization Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17268)

- Investigates why easy-to-hard curriculum learning helps inconsistently across reasoning tasks by proposing that the *transfer relationship between difficulty levels* is what governs curriculum optimization dynamics.
- Introduces "Relative Transfer," a metric for cross-difficulty knowledge transfer, and Transfer-aware Dynamic Curriculum Sampling (TDCS), which adjusts the training sampling distribution online based on estimated transfer.
- TDCS consistently beats standard curriculum scheduling strategies across tasks, model scales, and training paradigms.
- Offers a unified optimization-based account of curriculum learning, rather than the usual heuristic easy-to-hard story.

### 8. [Leveraging Association Context Retrieval in Knowledge Editing to Build White-Box Attacks on LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17836)

- Repurposes locate-then-edit knowledge-editing methods as an attack vector: models edited this way assign unusually high probability to the edit target, which the authors exploit for inducing unsafe behavior.
- Extends the attack by retrieving associative knowledge from the model itself, generalizing constraint removal to an entire thematic category rather than being limited to a fixed prompt dataset.
- Demonstrates improved attack effectiveness across architectures relative to competing white-box attacks, without materially degrading general model performance.
- A useful reminder that interpretability/editing tooling built for beneficial control is directly repurposable as an attack surface.

### 9. [An Empirical Study of Reward Specification and Benchmark Reliability in GRPO-based LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17804)

- Identifies an underspecified third behavior in LLM unlearning beyond "suppress target knowledge" / "preserve utility": when a target-adjacent prompt admits a broader, non-leaking answer, the model should answer at that level rather than leak, evade, or refuse.
- Compares four reward designs (lexical suppression, anti-refusal shaping, rubric-based broad answering, explicit refusal contrast) in a controlled LoRA-GRPO RWKU setup, with and without SFT warm-up.
- Shows optimization success ≠ behavioral unlearning: RWKU forget scores, held-out completion audits, rollout audits, and training dynamics frequently disagree with each other.
- Traces the disagreements to reward hacking, GRPO policy-support limits, and benchmark probes that miss real endpoint changes — a cautionary methods paper for anyone trusting a single unlearning metric.

### 10. [Understanding the Surprising Generalization Properties of Tabular Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17957)

- Investigates why in-context-learning-based Tabular Foundation Models (TFMs) generalize so well, finding that surprisingly strong transfer can emerge from self-supervised pre-training on just a single real table.
- Feature count (not instance count) is the stronger predictor of a pretraining table's downstream usefulness; column-level preprocessing helps, but dataset-level filtering doesn't.
- Proposes a task-centric account of TFM pretraining — the number and quality of implicit tasks embedded in a table matters more than raw scale — plus a retrieval-based framework where in-context learning works by finding and aggregating relevant examples from the context.
- A concrete, non-LLM data point in the broader science-of-generalization conversation about what in-context learning actually is doing mechanistically.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Hierarchical Data Selection via Manifold Coverage (MASS)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16927)** and its companion **[Data-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16926)** — two model-aware data-selection methods for LLM post-training (one via coarse-to-fine manifold + sparse-feature coverage, one via DPO-style pairwise preferences derived from one-step model probing), both matching full-dataset performance from a subset.
- **[GUPO: Gradient Uncertainty-aware Policy Optimization for Post-Training LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17411)** — treats GRPO's conflicting per-group gradients as random variables via a Dirichlet-based Bayesian formulation, using the resulting uncertainty estimate to calibrate gradient aggregation.
- **[TokEval: A Tokenizer Evaluation Suite](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18062)** — intrinsic tokenizer metrics (UTF-8 boundary integrity, digit alignment) that predict downstream language-modeling and task performance without full pretraining sweeps.
- **[Recirculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17981)** — Mozer et al. propose a training-free, inference-time recurrence mechanism letting a foundation model track a belief state as a dynamical system, cutting perplexity 23% and boosting GSM8K accuracy 21% with no added generation latency.
- **[Probing the Prefill: Detecting Code Vulnerabilities via Latent Activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16970)** — small probes on last-prefill-token activations from four LLMs show a coding model's own internal representation is informative about a code snippet's vulnerability status.
- **[Reflex-Guard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17556)** — a locally-run, embedding-based LLM prompt-safety guardrail hitting 95.9% recall at 37.6ms latency, roughly 7–20x faster than LLM-judge or cloud-API guardrails.
- **[Co-RL: Unsupervised Reasoning Emerges from Diverse Cohort in Multi-agent RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17253)** — decoupled, non-parameter-sharing models RL-optimized against rewards derived from each other's outputs, using cohort diversity to avoid the correlated-error collapse that plagues self-rewarding methods.
- **[Position: Fairness Failure in Generative Models is an Evaluation Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16974)** — argues generative-model fairness findings are rarely comparable across papers and proposes "Fairness Cards," a standardized reporting artifact for prompt families, counterfactual protocols, and metrics.
