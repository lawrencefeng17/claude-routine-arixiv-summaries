# arXiv cs.LG — Top Papers for June 26, 2026

Focused on: AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization, data attribution, subliminal learning.

## Top 10

### 1. Discovering Millions of Interpretable Features with Sparse Autoencoders
**[2606.26620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26620)** · He, Wang, Zhao, Ren, Li, Qiao, Wei, Qu

- Introduces **Qwen3-Instruct SAE**, a comprehensive suite of sparse autoencoders trained on the Qwen3 instruction-tuned model family (1.7B–8B parameters), covering residual streams and attention outputs.
- Demonstrates that selected SAE features can **causally steer** instruction-tuned Qwen3 models toward refusal behavior — a direct mechanistic handle for alignment interventions.
- Positions the release as a resource for studying feature-level mechanisms in deployed language models.

---

### 2. Localizing RL-Induced Tool Use to a Single Crosscoder Feature
**[2606.26474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26474)** · Shportko, Bhokare, Alzahrani, Cheng, Mercier, Hullman

- Shows that reinforcement learning reshapes LLM internals into a **compact, identifiable set of features** mediating tool-calling — discovered via Dedicated Feature Crosscoders on Qwen2.5-3B.
- Reconstructing along these RL-specific features improved tool correctness by ~31 pp and transferred tool-calling abilities to the base model by ~7 pp.
- Suggests RL-induced behavioral capabilities can be **localized and controlled without retraining** — with direct implications for understanding (and potentially auditing) agentic LLMs.

---

### 3. At the Edge of Understanding: Sparse Autoencoders Trace Transformer Limits
**[2606.26396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26396)** · Suresh, Stanley, Joseph, Scimeca, Bzdok

- Uses SAEs to track what happens inside LLMs when they encounter out-of-distribution inputs (typos, jailbreak attempts): OOD inputs activate an **increased number of spurious/fallacious concepts** in the residual stream.
- Proposes monitoring these internal activations as a mechanistically grounded OOD detector, expanding the OOD definition beyond input space to include **hidden computational state**.
- Connects interpretability tooling directly to safety-relevant deployment scenarios.

---

### 4. Beyond the Hard Budget: Sparsity Regularizers for More Interpretable Top-k Sparse Autoencoders
**[2606.27321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27321)** · Jacquier, Vakalopoulou, Hosseini

- Challenges the prevailing wisdom that Top-k SAEs should not use explicit sparsity regularization; introduces two complementary penalties (ℓ₁ on non-selected units; scale-invariant ℓ₁/ℓ₂ ratio) that **improve feature monosemanticity** without hurting reconstruction.
- The ℓ₁/ℓ₂ penalty additionally makes models more robust to varying inference-time choices of k and improves linear probe performance in low-budget regimes.
- Validated across multiple vision foundation models; offers practical improvements for mechanistic interpretability workflows.

---

### 5. Structure Before Collapse: Transient Semantic Geometry in Next-Token Prediction
**[2606.26749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26749)** · Zhao, Papadimitriou, Thrampoulidis

- Identifies a striking training dynamics phenomenon: **semantic geometry emerges early** — representations cluster by shared attributes — despite the one-hot label sparsity of next-token prediction.
- This structure is *transient*: a phase transition eventually drives representations toward the symmetric configuration predicted by Neural Collapse theory, erasing the semantic clustering.
- Uses Gram matrix analysis and synthetic controlled settings to characterize the dynamics; proposes modifications to theoretical models of Neural Collapse to accommodate this emergent then vanishing structure.

### 6. Radical AI Interpretability
**[2606.26523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26523)** · Herrmann, Levinstein

- Applies **philosophical radical interpretation** (Davidson) to mechanistic interpretability: asks how to reliably attribute beliefs, desires, and meanings to AI systems from computational facts alone.
- Central argument: beliefs, desires, and propositional content are interdependent and cannot be analyzed in isolation — a holistic constraint with direct implications for **detecting deception and misaligned goals**.
- Forthcoming as a Cambridge Element in the Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence; frames mech interp as providing the empirical substrate for answering foundational AI safety questions.

---

### 7. From Weights to Features: SAE-Guided Activation Regularization for LLM Continual Learning
**[2606.26629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26629)** · Ning, Xue, Lou, Guo

- Addresses catastrophic forgetting by regularizing in **activation space** (using pretrained SAE feature dictionaries) rather than in weight space — motivated by the observation that polysemanticity makes per-weight protection too coarse.
- Memory efficient: no previous-task data is required after mask construction; outperforms EWC and other weight-space methods on TRACE and MedCL benchmarks.
- Demonstrates that SAE's monosemantic decomposition is practically useful beyond analysis — it can guide training.

---

### 8. Reasoning Quality Emerges Early: Data Curation for Reasoning Models
**[2606.26797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26797)** · Jin, Yang, Ghaffari, Morato, Mirzasoleiman

- Shows that **problem difficulty can be detected from the loss on just the first 100 reasoning tokens**, making expensive strong-model filtering unnecessary for data curation.
- Examples with similar early-token loss patterns produce comparable gradient updates during fine-tuning — implying token-level loss is a proxy for gradient-level influence (a data attribution signal).
- Achieves up to 1.7% improvement over existing curation approaches while being 91% more token-efficient; validated on Qwen2.5-7B and Llama3.1-8B. (ICML 2026 poster.)

---

### 9. Dataset Usage Inference without Shadow Models or Held-out Data
**[2606.26257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26257)** · Łapacz, Pawlak, Dubiński, Boenisch, Dziedzic

- Tackles **data attribution at scale**: inferring what *fraction* of a dataset was used to train a model, without requiring expensive shadow models or confirmed non-member examples.
- Generates synthetic non-member samples and extracts multiple membership signals, framing estimation as a mixture proportion problem.
- Demonstrated on large image generative models; offers data owners a practical mechanism to audit whether and how much of their data contributed to a model.

---

### 10. When Are Likely Answers Right? Sequence Probability and Correctness in LLMs
**[2606.27359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27359)** · Zenn, Geiping

- Finds a **conditional/unconditional asymmetry**: sequence probability predicts correctness when *comparing different answers to the same question*, but increasing probability by changing hyperparameters or decoding methods does *not* reliably improve accuracy.
- Provides nuanced guidance for decoding strategies and self-improvement techniques: high probability is a relative signal within a question, not an absolute quality metric.
- Relevant to reward modeling and best-of-N sampling design where probability is often used as a proxy for quality.
