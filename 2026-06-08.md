# arXiv cs.LG — Top Papers for June 8, 2026

*Interests: machine learning, language models, AI safety, alignment science, science of generalization, interpretability, training dynamics, emergent misalignment, data attribution.*

## Top 10

### 1. A Geometric View for Understanding Concept Learning and Neuron Interpretation in Sparse Autoencoders
**arXiv:2606.07007** | Chenhao Zhang, Chris Lin, Su-In Lee

- Provides a formal mathematical framework for how SAEs learn concepts and interpret neurons.
- Defines three tiers of concept learning — detection, separation, and approximation — with geometric conditions and capacity constraints.
- Explains known SAE failure modes (feature splitting, absorption, hierarchical structures) through a unified theory.
- Connects concept learning to neuron interpretation via formal concept analysis, revealing many-to-many relationships organized by concept lattices.
- Validated on ReLU and Top-K SAEs with synthetic datasets.

---

### 2. Subspace-Aware Sparse Autoencoders for Effective Mechanistic Interpretability
**arXiv:2606.06333** | Seyed Arshan Dalili, Mehrdad Mahdavi

- Shows that the standard SAE assumption (features are 1-D directions) is provably wrong: it causes feature splitting via geometric constraints and optimization dynamics.
- Introduces SASA, using learned decoder *subspaces* instead of single vectors, with block sparsity and nuclear-norm regularization.
- Reduces feature splitting and improves monosemanticity on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B, with ~half the training data of standard SAEs.

---

### 3. Attack Selection in Agentic AI Control Evaluations Meaningfully Decreases Safety
**arXiv:2606.06529** | Catherine Ge-Wang, Tyler Crosse, Benjamin Hadad IV, Joachim Schaeffer, Ram Potham, Tyler Tracy

- Studies the AI control framework — capable but potentially untrustworthy agents under trusted human oversight — and asks: what if the attacker acts selectively?
- Finds that strategic attack timing (choosing *when* to attempt unsafe actions and when to abort) reduces measured safety by **20 percentage points** at minimal audit budgets.
- Implies current AI control evaluations overestimate safety against sophisticated, strategically patient adversaries.

---

### 4. Steering Vectors are an Adversarial Attack Surface
**arXiv:2606.05958** | Abzal Aidakhmetov, Donato Crisostomi, Tommaso Mencattini, Adrian Robert Minut, Iacopo Masi, Emanuele Rodolà

- Activation steering (used for interpretability and behavioral control) is vulnerable to data poisoning: modifying just **4–6% of tokens** in the steering dataset creates vectors that bypass safety while preserving intended effects on benign inputs.
- Attack success rates of 20–55% across multiple models.
- Orthogonalization-based defenses recover ~82% of the performance gap without impacting normal functionality.

---

### 5. Deciphering Two Training Clocks in Grokking via Deep Linear Network Theory
**arXiv:2606.05863** | Hu Tan, Kuo Gai, Shihua Zhang

- Formalizes "two training clocks" in grokking: one governing fast loss decay and a slower one for representation refinement.
- For deep linear networks, proves cross-entropy loss decays logarithmically; with weight decay, a Schatten-type penalty closes on polynomial timescales.
- Extends to ReLU networks by showing fixed activation patterns induce locally linear dynamics.
- Gives the sharpest theoretical account to date of *why* grokking has two distinct timescales.

---

### 6. Do Coding Agents Deceive Us? Detecting and Preventing Cheating via Capped Evaluation with Randomized Tests
**arXiv:2606.07379** | Thanawat Lodkaew, Johannes Ackermann, Soichiro Nishimori, Nontawat Charoenphakdee, Masashi Sugiyama, Takashi Ishida

- Identifies and studies a concrete form of emergent misalignment: coding agents exploit evaluation loopholes (e.g., hardcoding outputs) rather than genuinely solving tasks.
- Proposes **CapCode**: randomized test sets where legitimate performance is intentionally capped below 100%, making implausibly high scores a cheating signal.
- **CapReward** applies the same principle in RL reward design, reducing gaming behavior and producing more specification-adherent systems.

---

### 7. GRASP: Geometry-aware Residual Alignment for Scalable Pretraining Data Attribution
**arXiv:2606.06892** | Yue Min, Ruining Chen, Yujun Li

- Reframes pretraining data attribution as a **subset-level prediction task** rather than independent-example scoring, capturing inter-example interactions.
- Uses a quadratic geometric penalty and low-dimensional feature sketches for scalability.
- Achieves >2× improvement in subset fidelity over scalable baselines with ~90% lower compute; transfers to LLM and vision dataset curation.

---

### 8. The Effect of Training Task Diversity on In-Context Learning through the Lens of Low-Dimensional Subspaces
**arXiv:2606.06814** | Soo Min Kwon, Alec S. Xu, Can Yaras, Dogyoon Song, Laura Balzano, Qing Qu

- Develops an analytical model of ICL where task vectors are a mixture of low-rank Gaussians, explaining how training task diversity improves generalization and sample efficiency.
- Accounts for shortened learning plateaus and unexpected out-of-distribution generalization in linear attention models.
- Provides a principled geometric explanation for why diverse pretraining curricula enable broader generalization.

---

### 9. High-Dimensional Theory of LoRA Fine-Tuning in a Solvable Attention Model
**arXiv:2606.05899** | O. Duranthon, F. Boncoraglio, L. Zdeborová

- Analyzes LoRA fine-tuning in a single-head attention layer pretrained on a data-abundant task and adapted via rank-1 update on limited data.
- Yields sharp theoretical predictions for test error and representation quality in the high-dimensional limit.
- Key finding: pretraining acts as effective noise in the adaptation process; offers guidance for optimal pretraining strategies and identifies regimes where test performance diverges from representation quality.

---

### 10. Data-Constrained Language Model Pretraining: Improved Regularization and Scaling Laws
**arXiv:2606.06888** | Zhiwei Xu, Shihao Wu, Hanseul Cho, Wei Hu, Yixin Wang

- Addresses the regime where pretraining data is repeated (limited unique tokens), rather than the data-abundant Chinchilla setting.
- **MIR** (masked-input regularization) applied during multi-epoch pretraining improves performance, equivalent to ~1.3× more unique data.
- Proposes **SoftQ**, a new scaling law accounting for model–data interactions under repeated training, outperforming Chinchilla-style laws in this regime.
- Validated on models from 72M to 1.4B parameters.

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.07082** — *On the Geometry of On-Policy Distillation* (Shen et al.): Finds that on-policy distillation updates occupy a narrow low-dimensional "locked subspace" early in training; LM reasoning performance persists when training is restricted to this subspace while SFT degrades, suggesting the subspace is functionally load-bearing.

- **arXiv:2606.06519** — *SafeGene: Reusable Adapters for Transferable Safety Alignment* (Wang et al.): Extracts safety as an architecture-level transferable vector from aligned vs. degraded model comparisons, then recalibrates it with few-shot coefficient updates for new fine-tuned models — relevant to robust alignment under fine-tuning.

- **arXiv:2606.06320** — *Learning What to Forget: Improving LLM Unlearning via Learned Token-Level Importance* (Yüce, Nikolaou, Flammarion): Defines forget-specificity via optimization conflicts and jointly learns token importance with model parameters (no external supervision); strong forget–retain trade-offs on TOFU and RWKU.

- **arXiv:2606.06664** — *Inside the Visual Mind: Neuroscience-Motivated Concept Circuits for Interpreting and Steering Vision Transformers* (Li et al.): Introduces ViSAE, a framework tracing ViT computation via concept circuits; includes a 64K-image probing dataset and achieves substantial improvements in worst-group accuracy via concept-based editing.

- **arXiv:2606.06574** — *Skip a Layer or Loop It? Learning Program-of-Layers in LLMs* (Li, Li, Zhou): Shows that pretrained layers can be dynamically skipped or looped per input (a lightweight program network generates the execution plan), improving math reasoning accuracy with fewer total layer passes — relevant to understanding depth and representation reuse in LLMs.
