# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-28

Note: arXiv does not announce on weekends, and there was no Friday announcement after US Thanksgiving, so the cs.LG listing still shows **Thu, 26 Nov 2026** (143 new + 128 cross-lists). This is the third digest drawn from that listing. The picks below **leave out the 49 papers already in the 2026-11-26 and 2026-11-27 digests**, so the remaining pool is thinner. Summaries are based on the abstracts. Today's picks lean toward explaining *why* familiar techniques work or fail: guidance in diffusion models, state blow-up in linear attention, error build-up in low-bit optimizer states, and how much symmetry an architecture should assume.

## Top 10

### 1. [Guidance as Variance Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34801)
- At the scales used in practice, classifier-free-style guidance makes the model's prediction of its training target (the posterior mean) **worse**, even though samples get better.
- Posterior-mean predictions have less variance than the data, and guidance restores it. A closed-form **variance-matched guidance** scale, with no tuning, recovers the hand-picked scales of 11 widely used image, video, audio, motion and world models.
- Proposed mechanism: guidance rescales the *difference of two models' random errors* into a sample with the posterior's covariance. This correctly predicts that negative scales can work, and that self-guidance with a checkpoint needs a larger scale than guidance with an independent model.

### 2. [Stable Gated DeltaNet-2: Understanding and Mitigating Recurrent State Instability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34051)
- Traces GDN-2's long-context training failures to **non-normal erase operators**: reading and modification directions differ, so directional gains compound across tokens and the state grows exponentially even when nothing new is written.
- The fix uses symmetric erasure, which cannot expand the state, plus decay-regulated residual writes. It comes with a provable O(√T) bound on state growth and keeps chunkwise parallelism.
- At about 1.6B parameters: +6.8 points on 128K RULER (recurrent setting) and +0.62 average across nine downstream tasks (hybrid setting).

### 3. [The Symmetry Budget: How Much Group Structure Should a Network Architecture Assume?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33169)
- An exactly solvable model of weight tying: linear maps over ℤ_d, with architectures indexed by subgroups, ranging from a fully connected layer to a fully equivariant one.
- For generic symmetry defects, risk is affine in 1/k, so **intermediate tying is never optimal**. The choice is all-or-nothing, with a phase boundary at n* = σ²d(d−1)/ε². Structured (graded) defects bring back interior optima.
- Theory matches experiments on 108/108 phase-diagram cells, and a two-layer nonlinear network shows the same crossover. A clean piece of science on inductive bias and generalization.

### 4. [MonoQPM: Splitting Features into Concepts for Inherent Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33252)
- QPM models rule out superposition by design (each class uses a sparse binary set of features) but still show **polysemanticity**. This lets the authors study polysemanticity on its own.
- Proposes a *utility-based* measure of polysemanticity: how much interference-induced activation error goes down, read off as tighter conformal prediction sets.
- Splitting polysemantic features into monosemantic concepts gives 88% coverage on ImageNet with 66% of the original set size. Also releases CUBCars, a dataset with ground-truth shared concepts.

### 5. [BReD: Block Replay Dithering for Stable Low-Bit EMA Optimizer States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33951)
- Identifies a training-dynamics failure: because EMA optimizer states are re-quantized every step, **quantization error feeds back recursively**. Bias accumulates and variance is amplified, worst when β → 1.
- The fix is subtractive dithering with replayed seeds and block-shared dither values, so no extra random tensors need to be stored.
- 4-bit states match full precision (perplexity shift ≤ 0.5) for AdamW and Muon from 120M to 7B parameters. 3-bit states still converge stably.

### 6. [The Marauder's Map: Bézier Manifolds for Model Merging and Ensembling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33353)
- Goes beyond mode-connectivity paths and sheets. **Nimbus** learns a high-dimensional Bézier manifold connecting N independently initialized specialists trained on *different* tasks, and finds a region of multi-task solutions.
- Only interaction terms up to order 3 are needed, so the connecting manifold has surprisingly low complexity.
- Ensembles sampled from the manifold are flat in weight space and degrade gracefully under input and weight perturbations, with the biggest gains in NLL and calibration. Relevant to loss-landscape geometry and model merging.

### 7. [AliceAI-Foundation-80B-A3B-Base](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34529)
- An open (Apache 2.0) 80B-total / 3B-active MoE trained on 17.5T tokens. It uses Kimi Delta Attention mixed with full attention, Attention Residuals and **Muon**.
- Points relevant to training science: documents where **held-out loss fails as a proxy for downstream performance**, and adds an auxiliary loss on MoE outputs to keep training stable at the learning rate predicted by scaling laws.
- Agent trajectories mixed into pretraining improve agentic benchmarks after SFT. Reports 88.7% pass@8 on IMO-AnswerBench for a base model.

### 8. [Verifiable but Not Predictable: What Sentence Embeddings Know About Propositional Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33668)
- A careful probing study with a methodological focus. Once token count is controlled, every symbolic "idea density" measure reduces to a **length proxy**.
- A linear ridge probe on sentence embeddings is the only predictor with real signal (partial r ≈ 0.40–0.49), statistically matching inter-annotator agreement. Cosine geometry cannot verify whether a proposition belongs to a sentence, and an embedding is not a linear sum of its propositions' embeddings.
- Includes a retracted pilot study and a checklist for probing quantities confounded with length, which is useful for interpretability work in general.

### 9. [One Model, Many Modes: Evolving System Prompt Ensembles for Creative Generation (SEPO)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34760)
- Targets **mode collapse in preference-aligned LLMs** without training. It evolves a set of behaviorally distinct system prompts (greedy max-min selection under a quality constraint) and samples one prompt per response.
- Raises LLM-judged diversity from 1.92 to 3.43 and novelty from 1.00 to 3.34 (on a 1–5 scale), with quality falling from 3.72 to 3.30. Ranks first in 8 of 12 settings.
- Evidence that alignment-induced collapse can be partly undone through conditioning rather than weights.

### 10. [FlyingStart: Learned Cross-Token Warm Starts for Recurrent-Depth LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34492)
- In recurrent-depth models, a token's **fixed point can be predicted from where the previous token stopped**, after a learned linear change of coordinates. This is a structural fact about the latent dynamics.
- A closed-form warm start (costing under 5% of one loop) cuts decoding layers by 14–19% with accuracy unchanged, and stacks with self-speculative decoding.
- A contraction analysis predicts the loops saved on four models, including one prediction recorded before any evaluation.

## Honorable Mentions
- [Mitigating Overgeneralization in RND via Spectral Target Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33850): RND novelty is a cross-kernel residual variance shaped jointly by the target kernel and the predictor's NTK. Novelty collapse is "spectral under-excitation", and a Random Fourier Feature target fixes it.
- [Looped Models are Fast Multi-Token Predictors (Floodgates)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33896): shallow loops already predict several future tokens, giving up to 7.5× lossless decoding speedups. This depends on all-depth training.
- [BRIAR: Martingale Belief Smoothing on Reasoning Trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34746): treats prefix pass probability in RLVR as a bounded Doob martingale, whose credit variance vanishes once a trace is derailed or already settled. Uses this to steer tree rollouts and assign credit after the answer is determined.
- [LatentCP-DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34275): pessimistic DPO over conformal uncertainty sets on latent rewards, with matching upper and lower bounds. The lower bound shows a bias that persists as the optimization sample grows.
- [Dynamics of Collective Diversity in Human-AI Co-Creation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33192): a large crowdsourcing study. Artifact quality rises but collective diversity falls as more people adopt the same AI. Fitted scaling models describe the decline.
- [On the Learnability of Model Reprogramming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33889): splits the sample complexity of input/output wrappers around a frozen model into reachability and relabeling terms, with a matching lower bound.
- [Do We Still Need RL When We Can Read the World Model?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34656): coding agents that read environment source code write policies that match RL with far less experience.
- [Fine-Tuning Without Gradients? LLM-Written Closed-Form Classifiers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33972): LLM-designed closed-form heads on frozen RoBERTa beat full fine-tuning and LoRA in low-label regimes. Unusually transparent reporting, including 27 failed ideas.
- [The Missing Root: Geometric Imagination Without Images](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33841): continuous pose and shape tokens placed in the LM sequence form a persistent workspace that the model **causally relies on** for spatial answers.
- [Backpropagation as Dirac-Bracket Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34479): backprop errors are the Lagrange multipliers of layer constraints in a Hamiltonian treatment of dissipative training. A network is trained with no backward pass.
- [Routeability Before Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34825): across 66 LLMs and four benchmarks, most LLM-routing gains disappear against the single best model. Proposes audit diagnostics to report alongside routing results.
- [RefMix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33957): on-policy reward fine-tuning of flow models creates a feedback loop that collapses modes. Proposing training endpoints with help from a reference model brings back up to 44% more diversity.
- [Amortizing Exact Orthogonality (RTOFT)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33589): orthogonal fine-tuning that restores exact orthogonality only when drift crosses a tolerance. It bounds the orthogonality defect in every forward pass and runs at about LoRA speed.
