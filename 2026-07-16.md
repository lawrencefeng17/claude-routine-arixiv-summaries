## Top 10

### 1. Targeted Recovery of Weight-Space Mechanisms From Neural Networks
**[2607.13047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13047)** · Antoine Vigouroux, Lee Sharkey

- Extends parameter decomposition (which breaks a network into interpretable computational components) with "targeted parameter decomposition" (tPD): selectively recovers only the components processing a specific prompt or subtask, folding everything else into a single high-rank catch-all component to avoid decomposing the whole network.
- Validated on Pile-trained transformer LMs — recovers a mechanistically faithful CSS-only submodel from a 4-block transformer at substantially reduced compute, and surgically edits memorized sequences in a 12-block transformer with minimal collateral impact on unrelated inputs.
- A scaling-oriented contribution to mechanistic interpretability from Apollo Research's Lee Sharkey, directly relevant to making circuit-level decomposition tractable on larger models; accepted at the ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop.

### 2. The Entanglement Wall: Activation-Space Probes as Risk Detectors, Not Context Adjudicators
**[2607.13075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13075)** · Dominik Schwarz

- Tests whether residual-stream activation probes can distinguish genuinely harmful requests from benign ones with matching surface features (e.g. same topic, different intent), across three 7–8B model families.
- Finds a sharp generalization cliff: within-dataset AUROC reaches 0.996–0.999, but transfer to held-out test sets collapses to 0.590–0.819 — even though the same sensor blocks 95.5–97.7% of judge-classified compliant attacks on its training distribution.
- Concludes activation probes function as coarse risk detectors, not reliable adjudicators of context-dependent harm — a sobering result for anyone treating linear probes as a drop-in safety filter, and a clean empirical case study in probe generalization failure.

### 3. Algebraic Representability as the Limiting Regime of Grokking: An Exactly Solvable Model with Holomorphic Activations
**[2607.13749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13749)** · Chon-Fai Kam, Xavier Cadet, Miloud Bessafi, Frederic Cadet

- Studies two-layer networks with holomorphic monomial activations on modular arithmetic, deriving an exact representability criterion: a task is learnable iff its discrete Fourier support lies on the diagonal u+v ≡ k (mod p) — non-representable targets provably can't even fit the training set, regardless of width.
- Across 585 experimental runs, this criterion predicts outcomes with 99.8% accuracy, and reveals a binary success/failure pattern with no intermediate memorization-then-generalization phase.
- Frames this as the "limiting regime" of grokking — where representational capacity, not training time, determines whether learning happens at all — giving the grokking literature a rare exactly-solvable boundary case.

### 4. How the Hessian-Spectrum of Neural Networks Depends on Data
**[2607.13631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13631)** · Jasraj Singh, Enea Monzio Compagnoni, Antonio Orvieto

- Derives closed-form Hessian eigenvalues for linear networks of arbitrary width/depth under MSE loss, for datasets with arbitrary sample count, feature dimension, and label structure.
- Key result: solution sharpness is governed directly by the maximum class-proportion in the dataset — a simple, checkable data statistic predicts a core training-dynamics quantity.
- Progressively relaxes the linear-network assumption to test robustness to nonlinearities, finding the predictions hold up "considerably" — a useful theoretical anchor for sharpness-based generalization and optimization work (accepted at HiLD@ICML'26).

### 5. Transforming Rank: How Architecture Navigates the Spectral Pathologies of Depth
**[2607.14018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14018)** · Katie Everett

- Analyzes how Transformer blocks preserve (or destroy) matrix/gradient rank across depth at initialization, showing skip connections and normalization jointly trade off rank preservation against ensemble-like branch behavior depending on branch-to-skip scaling.
- Explains the Post-Norm vs. Pre-Norm split as different resolutions of this same rank/ensemble tradeoff, and shows the FFN's expand-then-contract structure prevents collapse via decorrelation and Marchenko–Pastur-governed width expansion.
- Demonstrates that initialization-time Jacobian rank predicts downstream trainability on CIFAR-10 — reframing architecture design as navigating competing pressures among rank collapse, ensembling, and parameter efficiency, a clean addition to the training-dynamics-of-depth literature.

### 6. When T2I Synthetic Data Backfires: Amplified Privacy Risks in Real-Synthetic Mix Training
**[2607.13541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13541)** · Na Li, Boyu Kuang, Hongsheng Hu, Liquan Chen, Hyoungshick Kim, Yansong Gao, Anmin Fu

- Shows that augmenting real training data with text-to-image synthetic data (Real-Synthetic Mix-Training, RSMT) — intended to help — can substantially increase membership-inference leakage of the real samples it was meant to supplement.
- Provides a theoretical account: synthetic data pushes real samples toward peripheral regions of feature space, causing the model to memorize them more intensely rather than generalize past them.
- Introduces RSMixLeak (a membership-inference audit tool) and a lightweight "leakage propensity indicator" computable from real data alone, flagging datasets unsuitable for RSMT before training — directly relevant to memorization/privacy risk assessment in synthetic-data pipelines.

### 7. Graded Entity-Familiarity Readouts in Language Models: Polish Adaptation, Cross-Language Robustness, and Refusal Steering
**[2607.13568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13568)** · Grzegorz Brzezinka

- Probes twelve instruction-tuned model families for a linear "familiarity" signal that distinguishes known entities from fabricated ones, using 1,440 Polish entities plus confabulated controls, and shows the probe correlates with real-world entity popularity (Spearman ρ 0.28–0.57).
- Finds the probe transfers near-losslessly across languages — retaining 96–101% of within-language AUROC when the question stem is swapped from Polish to English.
- Shows manipulating a single familiarity direction in one layer monotonically shifts refusal/abstention rates, evidencing a clean separation between a model's internal "do I know this" representation and the separate policy that converts it into a refusal — relevant to hallucination mitigation and refusal-steering interpretability.

### 8. Partially Correlated Verifier Cascades in LLM Harnesses: Concave Log-Odds, Polynomial Reliability, and Blind-Spot Ceilings
**[2607.13918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13918)** · Jiangang Han

- Models chained LLM verifiers (as used in agent harnesses/scalable-oversight pipelines) with a latent per-instance false-accept rate, moving beyond the standard independence assumption that predicts exponential failure decay from stacking checks.
- Derives that posterior log-odds are concave across cascading gates for any non-degenerate latent distribution, and that Beta-distributed latents give only polynomial failure decay (∝ k⁻ᵇ) rather than exponential — plus conditions under which certain gates hit a hard reliability ceiling or become actively counterproductive.
- On synthetic tests, naive independence-based extrapolation underestimates failure rate by 20× at only 5 cascaded gates — a cautionary, quantitative result for anyone stacking LLM-judge/verifier gates as a safety or reliability mechanism.

### 9. Consensus as Privileged Context for Label-Free Self-Distillation
**[2607.13643](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13643)** · John Gkountouras, Josip Jukić, Ivan Titov

- Introduces CANON: instead of using multi-sample agreement only as a filter or preference signal, a frozen model conditioned on the consensus-reaching solution supervises the trainee model with dense, token-level targets — turning "the answers multiple rollouts agree on" into a training signal without any gold labels.
- Reports up to 12-point pass@1 gains on reasoning benchmarks, exceeding label-free RL baselines at substantially lower compute, and — notably — the trained model solves problems it previously couldn't, rather than just sharpening probability on already-reachable answers.
- On unlabeled pooled data, generalizes to held-out benchmarks comparably to training with gold labels — a genuinely label-free self-improvement result relevant to the training-dynamics-of-self-distillation and self-improvement literatures.

### 10. Local Redundancy: An Information-Theoretic Measure of Plasticity from Synthetic Memorization
**[2607.13432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13432)** · Jiaxuan Cheng

- Proposes "local redundancy" — the worst-case redundancy of a local model family, grounded in compression theory — as a principled measure of a network's remaining plasticity (capacity to learn new tasks), addressing the intractability of exact computation with a lower bound derived from squared gradient norms on synthetic memorization tasks.
- Shows this metric outperforms existing plasticity proxies (effective rank, dead-neuron fraction) at predicting downstream-task performance, on both image classification and time-series learning.
- Proposes checkpoint selection as a practical application — a memorization-derived diagnostic tool linking the memorization and training-dynamics/plasticity literatures.
