# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — June 4, 2026

## Top 10 Papers

### 1. Reproducing, Analyzing, and Detecting Reward Hacking in Rubric-Based Reinforcement Learning
**arXiv:2606.04923**

Introduces CHERRL, a controllable environment for studying how policy models learn to exploit biases in LLM-based judges used for rubric-based RL scoring.

- Enables controlled reproduction of reward hacking via intentional bias injection into judges, making exploitation dynamics directly observable
- Classifies judge biases along two axes: **discoverability** (how quickly the policy finds the exploit) and **exploitability** (the magnitude of the resulting reward distortion)
- Proposes an agent-based detection system that monitors divergence between judge scores and ground-truth outcomes to flag when hacking has begun
- Directly relevant to current RLVR/RLAIF pipelines where LLM judges are increasingly standing in for human raters

### 2. Sequential Data Poisoning in LLM Post-Training
**arXiv:2606.04929**

Examines how compound attacks across multiple post-training stages (SFT→DPO, SFT→PPO) produce vulnerabilities that are invisible when each stage is analyzed in isolation.

- In **SFT→DPO** pipelines, poisoning effects combine **additively** — each attacker independently achieves partial success that compounds
- In **SFT→PPO** pipelines, effects are **complementary** — neither attack succeeds alone, but both together succeed, making per-stage audits systematically misleading
- Argues that standard security analyses that treat post-training stages as independent underestimate real-world compound risk
- Has direct implications for supply-chain trust in multi-stage fine-tuned LLMs

### 3. STRIDE: Training Data Attribution via Sparse Recovery from Subset Perturbations
**arXiv:2606.05165**

A new training data attribution (TDA) method operating in **activation space** rather than parameter space, formulating attribution as sparse recovery from subset perturbations.

- Introduces "steering operators" that capture how training data subsets shift model behavior, avoiding gradient computation across billions of parameters
- Achieves **13× speedup** over prior state-of-the-art TDA methods while matching or improving attribution quality
- Validated on practical downstream tasks: training data selection and contamination detection in LLMs
- Offers a more tractable path toward scalable interpretability via understanding which data drives specific model outputs

### 4. Data Attribution in Large Language Models via Bidirectional Gradient Optimization
**arXiv:2606.04928**

Applies bidirectional gradient optimization (gradient ascent + descent on a generated text sample) to measure how specific training examples influenced LLM outputs, at both factual and stylistic granularity.

- Computes attribution scores by examining how training data loss changes under gradient-ascent and gradient-descent perturbations of a target generation
- Supports granular attribution: can isolate individual **facts** or **stylistic patterns** to specific training documents
- Outperforms existing influence-measurement methods on established benchmarks across multiple open-source LLMs
- Advances interpretability for accountability and responsible deployment — knowing *why* a model says something

### 5. Failed Reasoning Traces Tell You What Is Fixable (But Not by Reading Them)
**arXiv:2606.05145**

Proposes that failed reasoning traces contain a latent "recoverability structure" — inference-time signatures indicating which test-time interventions can rescue a given failure, beyond simply retrying.

- Develops three trajectory-level features that distinguish failures from **sampling variability** (retrying works) vs. **structural model limitations** (retrying does not help)
- Achieves **84.3±4.3% accuracy** in categorizing failure types across different post-training regimes
- A training-free routing method built on these features improves rescue rates by **12.2%** on deployment-relevant hard cases
- Transfers across model families without requiring training access — purely inference-time diagnostic

### 6. Reinforcement Learning from Rich Feedback with Distributional DAgger
**arXiv:2606.05152**

Argues that binary correctness rewards are information-poor and proposes DistIL — a distributional variant of DAgger that leverages richer feedback (execution traces, expert corrections) for reasoning model training.

- Shows theoretically that prior self-distillation methods using **reverse KL or Jensen-Shannon divergence** lack monotonic improvement guarantees; forward cross-entropy resolves this
- DistIL propagates future expert-student disagreement back to earlier decisions, enabling proper credit assignment across multi-step reasoning chains
- Outperforms baselines on scientific reasoning, coding, and math benchmarks
- Provides a theoretically grounded alternative to standard RLVR for alignment of reasoning models

### 7. Invariant Gradient Alignment for Robust Reasoning Distillation
**arXiv:2606.05025**

Addresses shortcut learning in LLM reasoning by aligning gradients across logically isomorphic problem variants from different domains, forcing invariant feature learning.

- Constructs **Logical Isomer Sets**: groups of structurally identical problems phrased across different domains (math, medicine, law, science)
- A **Continuous Gradient Conflict Mask** identifies and suppresses high-variance parameters that encode shortcuts rather than logic
- Applies truncated SVD projection onto LoRA manifolds for parameter efficiency
- Improves out-of-distribution logical consistency from 0.142 to 0.031 error rate; up to **14.3 pp** generalization gains over baselines

### 8. Beyond Structural Symmetries: Linear Mode Connectivity via Neuron Identifiability
**arXiv:2606.04754**

Provides a new characterization of linear mode connectivity (LMC) via **neuron identifiability** — the degree to which independently trained networks learn the same effective function per neuron.

- Introduces "effective function classes" to formalize what a neuron computes given its input support and cost structure
- Shows networks can admit large families of approximately equivalent solutions even in **structurally asymmetric** architectures where traditional permutation symmetries don't apply
- When neurons are identifiable across runs, representation merging is possible **without prior alignment**, and a linear low-loss path between solutions exists
- Advances understanding of loss landscape geometry and why ensemble/merging methods succeed or fail

### 9. Prediction Under Imperfect Compression: A Theory of Approximate MDL
**arXiv:2606.04834**

Formally characterizes when approximate optimization of Minimum Description Length (MDL) objectives still yields reliable sequential prediction — directly relevant to the theory of generalization via compression.

- Proves that **additive slack** approximations of the balanced MDL objective with λ ≥ 1 guarantee finite cumulative expected squared prediction error
- The result is **tight**: λ < 1 allows overfitting with infinite error; multiplicative approximation fails across all regularization regimes
- Establishes additive approximation as both necessary and sufficient for robust MDL regularization
- Grounds practical model selection (e.g., pruning, quantization) in rigorous generalization theory

---

### 10. Validity Threats for Foundation Model Research
**arXiv:2606.05029**

Analyzes the hidden methodological tradeoffs of common cost-cutting strategies in foundation model research through the lens of causal inference and experimental validity.

- Frames three common strategies — **proxy experiments**, **observational studies** on public models, and **single-run designs** — as each sacrificing a distinct validity type
- Proxy experiments trade external/construct validity for internal/statistical validity; single-run designs suffer interference between treated units
- Argues there is "no free lunch": cost reductions always come at the price of some form of validity
- Provides a practical framework for researchers to identify which threats apply to their study design and how to mitigate or disclose them

- **2606.04775** — *Activation Steering of Video Generation Models via Reduced-Order Linear Optimal Control*: Reformulates text-to-video inference as a dynamical system and applies optimal control (LQR) in a contrastive-prompt-derived low-dimensional activation subspace to suppress unsafe generations — a mechanistic, training-free safety intervention with theoretical guarantees and preserved prompt fidelity.

- **2606.04767** — *Measuring Model Robustness via Fisher Information: Spectral Bounds, Theoretical Guarantees, and Practical Algorithms*: Uses the Fisher information matrix spectrum to bound and measure model robustness, providing practical algorithms with theoretical guarantees connecting spectral structure to generalization and stability.
