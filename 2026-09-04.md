## Top 10

### 1. [Legibility is Not Interpretability: Comparing Judged and Actual Importance in Chain-Of-Thought Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04194)

- Operationalizes the "true" importance of a chain-of-thought step as its advantage (the change in expected reward from including it, estimated via Monte Carlo rollouts), then asks whether LLM judges and step-level critics can recover this from the text alone.
- Judges beat a prevalence baseline but fall well short of a noise ceiling; a fine-tuned step-level critic improves a lot on incorrect responses but still can't reliably identify important steps in correct ones.
- A direct empirical warning against the common practice of using LLM judges on reasoning traces for process reward models and faithfulness evaluation — legible-looking text does not mean the model's actual computation is being read off correctly.

### 2. [You Can't Escape Your Own Activations: Evaluation Awareness and Multi-Agent Monitoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03035)

- Tests whether telling LLM agents that their internal activations are being monitored (with or without feedback on prior monitor scores) lets them evade activation-based collusion detectors in multi-agent games (blackjack and a prisoners'-dilemma variant).
- Activation probes stay accurate regardless of what agents are told, and the agents keep colluding even when explicitly informed a monitor is watching — output-level obfuscation doesn't translate into evading internal-state detection.
- Directly relevant to AI-safety monitoring infrastructure: a rare controlled test of whether "evaluation awareness" actually lets models game the safeguards built to catch them.

### 3. [ObserverBench: Testing Mechanistic Estimates for Intervention and Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03026)

- Argues that an interpretability "observer" (probe, SAE readout, monitor score) being accurate on average doesn't mean it's adequate for the intervention or control task it's meant to inform, and builds a benchmark that separates estimation accuracy from the loss caused by the action the estimate selects.
- On circuit-intervention tasks in GPT-2-small and Qwen2.5-7B, and safety-triage tasks across several models, observers trained for pure prediction accuracy often choose worse actions than observers trained on action loss directly, and AUROC can rank monitors in the opposite order from deployment loss.
- A methodologically sharp critique of how mechanistic interpretability results are usually validated — pushing toward evaluating interpretability tools by the decisions they enable, not just their estimation accuracy.

### 4. [Extracting Forgotten Prompts from Targeted Unlearned Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03662)

- Points out that prior unlearning-extraction attacks assume the adversary already knows which prompts were forgotten and only tries to recover the answers; this paper instead recovers the forgotten *prompts themselves* using only retained data and black-box access.
- The attack (Targeted Active Search) reconstructs up to 95% of forgotten prompts and identifies forgotten entities with 100% accuracy, using up to 99.7% fewer queries than naive probing, across three unlearning methods, three datasets, and three LLMs.
- A concrete new vulnerability for machine unlearning as a safety/privacy mechanism — shows current unlearning methods leave enough signal in the retained model to reconstruct what was supposedly deleted.

### 5. [The Geometry of Ignorance: LLMs Know When to Temper Bayesian Priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02959)

- Identifies a single "direction of ignorance" in the unembedding matrix — present across Llama, Qwen, Gemma, and Pythia from 0.4B to 405B parameters — that encodes the training corpus's unigram distribution, the fallback prior the model uses when context is uninformative.
- Projecting a model's final prediction state onto this direction yields a calibrated "prior loading factor" that declines as context becomes more informative, and decomposes the prediction exactly into a tempered-Bayesian-update form (prior^λ × likelihood).
- Shows the direction is causally active — steering along it shifts predictions toward or away from the unigram prior — giving a rare case of an interpretability finding with a clean probabilistic interpretation and direct causal verification.

### 6. [High-Dimensional Learning Dynamics of Attention-Indexed Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03858)

- Develops a high-dimensional theory of SGD training dynamics for attention mechanisms, showing that in a suitable limit the population loss landscape is governed by a finite set of trace order parameters even though the SGD trajectory itself involves an infinite hierarchy of matrix moments.
- Finds that attention parameterization is itself an implicit bias: tied attention ($S=WW^\top$) breaks symmetry automatically and reaches weak recovery in $\Theta(d^2\log d)$ samples, while untied attention ($S=UV^\top$) exhibits a fast-slow dynamic where a fast mean-direction phase must break symmetry before the slower overlap dynamics can recover signal.
- A rigorous theoretical addition to the training-dynamics literature specifically about attention (rather than generic MLPs), explaining why some attention parameterizations get stuck in uninformative optima and others don't.

### 7. [It's the Problem, Not the Path: Budget and Difficulty Confounds in LLM Reasoning Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03436)

- Re-examines two popular claims about LLM reasoning traces — that they contain identifiable "breakthrough" moments and that early tokens are outcome-predictive — by adding counterfactual controls (restart baselines, difficulty-matched probes) that prior work lacked.
- Finds essentially no prefix-limited "breakthrough" cells once restart dose-response is properly controlled, and shows a previously reported early-window outcome-prediction result (AUROC 0.873 pooled) collapses to chance (0.496) once evaluated within-problem rather than pooled across problems of different difficulty.
- A rigorous methodological corrective for the growing literature on interpreting reasoning trajectories — demonstrates how easily difficulty and compute-budget confounds masquerade as interpretability findings about reasoning dynamics.

### 8. [Inferred Generative-Process Diversity Predicts Correlated Failure Across Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03422)

- Argues that semantic similarity of outputs under-measures true diversity between language models, and proposes measuring diversity via Normalized Compression Distance between raw outputs (an Algorithmic-Information-Theory proxy for how different the underlying generative processes are).
- Across 38 language models and ten disjoint benchmark families, this "generative-process diversity" measure predicts cross-task correlated failure between model pairs (partial rank correlation −0.216) beyond what semantic similarity or capability differences explain.
- Directly relevant to safety-critical multi-model deployments (ensembles, juries, redundancy schemes): shows models can look behaviorally diverse yet still fail together, and offers a practical way to detect that risk in advance.

### 9. [Coupled Scaling: A Representational Accessibility Framework for Neural Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03533)

- Proposes that neural scaling exponents aren't determined by data geometry alone, but by the coupling between task structure and which representations an architecture-optimizer combination can actually reach at a given budget — formalized via a solvable mode-truncation model separating "target energy outside architectural support" from "unresolved supported tail."
- Derives a closed-form scaling exponent $\alpha = \rho(b-1)$ linking the acquisition rate (how fast the model covers high-value directions) and the intrinsic tail decay rate, and predicts that scaling-exponent orderings can even reverse across contrasting tasks depending on this coupling.
- Offers a testable theoretical framework for why identical data can produce different scaling behavior under different architectures/optimizers — a foundational contribution to the science of generalization and scaling laws.

### 10. [Sequential Beats Joint: On the Interplay between On-Policy Distillation and RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04108)

- Compares joint objectives that fuse on-policy distillation (OPD, dense token-level teacher supervision) with RL from verifiable rewards (RLVR, sparse outcome reward) against a simple two-stage "OPD-then-RL" schedule for post-training reasoning LLMs.
- The sequential scheme consistently beats pure OPD, pure RLVR, and all tested joint-objective baselines on logic and math benchmarks; pass@k and parameter-update analysis shows OPD expands the student's coverage of teacher-supported solutions while RL then sharpens within that support, whereas mixing the two signals in one step causes interference.
- A clean training-dynamics explanation for why signal *order* rather than signal *combination* matters in LLM post-training, plus a practical recipe (switch to RL once OPD validation score plateaus; OPD is a better RL cold-start than SFT).

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Correlated initialization of deep residual networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03589)** — proves that residual networks with layer-correlated weight initialization converge, in the infinite-depth limit, to a Young differential equation driven by a Hermite process (interpolating between the Brownian-motion limit of independent init and the ODE limit of perfectly correlated init), showing initialization correlation structure is a meaningful hyperparameter for deep-network training dynamics.
- **[Towards a Statistical Understanding of Mixture-of-Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03501)** — derives oracle risk bounds separating approximation, expert-learning, and router-estimation error for MoE architectures, giving a statistical account of why sparse top-K routing and shared experts (as in DeepSeekMoE) work, rather than relying on parametric-model theory as prior work has.
- **[Routing Is Not Enough: Diagnosing Intra-Adapter Subspace Contention in MoE+LoRA Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03150)** — shows that even when MoE routing cleanly separates domains (biomedical text vs. code vs. math), negative transfer still occurs because nearly-orthogonal domain gradients compete within the same low-rank LoRA adapter subspace, and fixes it by dynamically spawning gated sub-adapters when contention is detected.
- **[Modern Transformers Are Implicit Hybrids: From Functional Differentiation to Principled Hybrid Architecture Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02986)** — uses causal intervention metrics (not just behavioral probing) to show RoPE-based transformer heads split into retrieval heads and positional heads along a "Global Positional Band" tied to training-length scale, explaining zero-shot length-extrapolation failure and motivating a head-wise hybrid full/linear-attention architecture.
- **[The Head Complexity of Boolean Functions in Single-Layer Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04046)** — proves an unconditional hierarchy theorem (holding at unbounded embedding dimension and precision) that $k$ attention heads can compute $k$-bit parity but not $(k+1)$-bit parity, giving one of the few exact expressivity lower bounds for single-layer attention and covering the induction-head task as a special case.
- **[The Gradient Does Not See Rank: Rank-Indifference in Matrix-CODI on ProsQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03090)** — a careful negative result showing that matrix-valued latent chain-of-thought bottlenecks, despite motivating "rank should track parallel reasoning paths," produce accuracy curves completely flat across rank-truncation ablations regardless of readout architecture, suggesting the rank hypothesis for latent CoT superposition doesn't hold as expected.
- **[Gradients Know What Outcomes Don't: Unlocking Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning with Gradient-Aligned Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03342)** — derives a dense RL reward from cosine similarity between a rollout's own truncated-backprop gradient and an expert-anchor gradient, proving this decomposes into interpretable prediction-error and activation-pattern factors, and shows it improves over GRPO with under 9% overhead.
- **[Beyond Endpoint Scores: Time- and Capacity-Conditioned Evaluation of Continual Knowledge Updating](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03900)** — shows that continual-learning method rankings can completely reverse (an 11.6-point deficit becoming a 5-point advantage) depending on evaluation month and replay-adapter rank alone, arguing the field should report full trajectories and capacity sweeps rather than single-checkpoint comparisons.
