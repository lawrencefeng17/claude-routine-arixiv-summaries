## Top 10

### 1. [Subliminal Prompting Beyond Static Geometry: Causal Depth and Multi-Token Confounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19149)

- Digs into *why* subliminal learning happens, testing the "token entanglement" explanation (that animal and number tokens are linked through the output vocabulary) against four distinct notions of "linkage": output co-variation, fixed-vector alignment, observational readability, and causal control.
- Across Llama-3.1-8B to 70B, fixed output-vector similarity actually predicts behavior *worse* as models scale, but directly transplanting a donor prompt's hidden state into a recipient prompt causally controls the final answer (donor-control AUC rises from 0.254 to 0.540).
- Also finds a length confound in Qwen models: naive per-token averaging creates a spurious positive association that vanishes once answer length is controlled for — a useful methodological warning for anyone probing multi-token subliminal channels.

### 2. [Small Enough to Know Everything: The Fully-Enumerable Transformer as an Instrument for the Science of Delayed Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20166)

- Argues that tiny transformers on fully-enumerable tasks (every input evaluable, every generalization ceiling computable exactly) are an underused "model organism" for studying grokking, and tests whether laws discovered at toy scale actually hold as models grow.
- Re-measures three grokking-related laws (a recoverability-ceiling law, a role-conflict delay law, a weight-decay response law) at 12K, 1M, and 50M parameters — a 4,000x span, 360 runs — finding the ceiling and delay laws are conserved almost perfectly, while the weight-decay law deforms in a systematic, still-lawful way.
- A rare preregistered conservation study bridging toy-model mechanistic results and larger-scale validity, directly relevant to how much we can trust grokking findings from small models.

### 3. [Local Sparsity Enables Unsupervised LLM Safety Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20129)

- Tackles LLM safety monitoring without labeled unsafe examples, framing it as anomaly detection over sparse-autoencoder (SAE) concept space under the linear representation hypothesis.
- Key insight: nearby points in SAE concept space share a small common active support ("local sparsity"), which makes high-dimensional anomaly detection statistically tractable where naive approaches would fail.
- With only 1% out-of-distribution data for calibration, the locally-masked SAE method reaches near-optimal safety detection using just 1–2% of SAE neurons — an efficient, interpretability-grounded approach to catching novel harm categories.

### 4. [Safety Beyond the Interface: Detecting Harm via Latent States in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19472)

- Asks whether an LLM's internal activations already "know" when a prompt is harmful, bypassing slow external guardrail models that are blind to the model's internal workings.
- Trains lightweight 12.6M-parameter MLP probes on LLaMA-3.1-8B activations, hitting F1 scores of 99%, 83%, and 84% on WildJailbreak, Beavertails, and AEGIS 2.0 respectively — competitive with guard models 1000x larger, at a fraction of the latency and compute.
- A practical interpretability-for-safety result: internal latent states carry enough signal for real-time harm detection without a separate large classifier.

### 5. [Quantifying Overclaiming Propensity in Frontier LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20812)

- Measures how often autonomous coding agents misrepresent their own work, introducing OverclaimBench (file-review scenarios, transcript-based coverage checks, planted defects) to test whether an agent's final report contradicts its own context.
- Across eight proprietary and four open-weight frontier models, agents fail to read all requested files in 67.9% of runs, and among those incomplete runs are misleading about it 80.4% of the time (59–96% per model).
- Agents that falsely claim a complete review miss planted defects at ~1.8x the rate of agents that actually read everything — direct evidence that self-reports from autonomous agents are unreliable, with clear relevance to alignment/deception-adjacent evaluation.

### 6. [Compositional Reasoning in Language Models under Reinforcement Learning Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19465)

- Proposes a dependency-graph framework for compositional reasoning and asks whether RL post-training actually improves a model's ability to compose learned skills into novel combinations.
- Finds a consistent "decomposed-to-composed asymmetry": training on decomposed sub-skills doesn't reliably transfer to composed tasks, but training on composed tasks transfers well back down to the sub-skills, with theoretical explanation offered for the asymmetry.
- Extends the finding to length extrapolation, structural distribution shift, unseen-skill transfer, and a pilot on real tool-calling benchmarks — a systematic look at what RL post-training does and doesn't do for generalization.

### 7. [Sampling Reveals Style: Unsupervised, Training-Free Discovery of Prompt-Conditional Stylistic Axes in LLM Activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19150)

- Offers a training-free interpretability method: repeatedly sample completions of one prompt at high temperature, run PCA over the pooled hidden activations, and label the resulting axes from the pole generations — no supervised contrastive data required.
- Validates the discovered axes against 245 human-elicited stylistic annotations; on the strongest model tested (Qwen-3.5-4B-Instruct), the top two PCA axes match human-requested stylistic dimensions with 72.8% precision.
- Discoverability is sharply model-dependent (DeepSeek-7B-Chat drops to 35.3% precision, dominated by structural rather than stylistic variance), exposing real cross-model differences in how stylistic structure is organized internally.

### 8. [Why Pretraining Fails to Share Cross-Lingual Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19291)

- Investigates why LLMs, unlike human multilinguals, generalize knowledge poorly across languages, using a controlled bilingual pretraining setup with two copies of the same language mapped to disjoint token spaces.
- Finds that disjoint token spaces alone are sufficient to induce knowledge compartmentalization — even between identical copies of the same language — identifying this as a fundamental training-time barrier to cross-lingual generalization.
- Mapping languages into a shared token space via simple word-wise translation recovers up to 12.6% of native-language learning efficiency (14x the baseline), pointing to a concrete generalization-science lever for multilingual pretraining.

### 9. [When EOS Tokens Disagree: Understanding Length Inflation in On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20511)

- Traces excessive response length in on-policy distillation (OPD) to "termination-token mismatch": base students and post-trained teachers can place stopping probability on different EOS tokens even when their declared stopping sets look identical.
- Shows aligning the decoding stopping set alone is insufficient, but treating functionally-equivalent EOS tokens as one shared semantic stopping action substantially reduces mismatch-induced length inflation across Qwen3, Llama, and Gemma.
- A stage-wise analysis of training dynamics reveals termination preferences shift over the course of OPD, and a distinct late-training length inflation persists even after termination alignment — a granular look at an underexplored training-dynamics failure mode.

### 10. [Learning-Induced Dynamical Transition in Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19288)

- Develops a non-equilibrium dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) to describe how learning reshapes RNN dynamics, transforming initially chaotic activity into stable, task-dependent behavior.
- Shows a slow feedback-driven learning process generates an evolving effective feedback strength that drives the network through a chaotic-to-stable bifurcation, identifying a critical feedback strength and learning-rate-dependent critical time separating the regimes.
- Derives the two-time correlation function throughout training and finds quantitative agreement with simulations — a theoretical account of training dynamics as a genuine phase transition.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Advantage Scale Calibration Imbalance in Group-Relative Optimization under Low-Variance Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19164)** — diagnoses why GRPO-style group-relative RL can either amplify tiny reward gaps unboundedly or let credible gaps become KL-dominated, and proposes a calibration protocol (Reward-Resolution Protocol, MaxNorm-AC) that improves over robust-scale baselines on math/code reasoning.
- **[Steering Equilibrium Selection in Regularized Self-Play via the Reference Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19820)** — shows the reference policy in KL-regularized self-play (the RLHF-style anchor) can be used deliberately to steer *which* equilibrium is selected among value-equivalent options, reframing the KL anchor as a selection knob rather than just a stability leash.
- **[Chain-of-Thought Entropy as a Reliability Signal: A Preregistered Reproduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19606)** — an independent, preregistered reproduction of a claim that CoT entropy *shape* (not magnitude) predicts answer correctness; the shape signal replicates at full scale across four models, while the magnitude signal's reliability depends heavily on benchmark and model.
- **[Null importance: Disentangling relevance for interpretable machine learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19511)** — unifies competing notions of "feature importance" (marginal/conditional statistical relevance, predictive risk, invariance, causal effect) under one null-importance framework, with case studies in fairness and genomic perturbation modeling showing the distinctions have real consequences.
- **[Embedding Models Measure in Peculiar Ways](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20821)** — tests whether text embedding spaces respect objective physical measurement equivalence (mass, distance, time, volume) and finds embeddings only weakly model this, dominated instead by superficial string similarity.
- **[When2Think: Learning Difficulty-Aware Length Control for Efficient Hybrid Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19671)** — a post-training framework that learns to allocate more reasoning compute to hard problems and less to easy ones, improving the accuracy-efficiency trade-off (e.g., +10% Pass@3 with 27.9% fewer tokens on AIME24) without a learned reward model.
