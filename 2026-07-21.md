## Top 10

### 1. How Does Alignment Tuning Shape Representations of Sycophancy and Related Cue-Induced Biases in LLMs?
**[2607.18114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18114)** · Prakhar Gupta, Terry Jingchen Zhang, Florent Draye, Bernhard Schölkopf, Zhijing Jin

- LLMs flip correct answers under trivial prompt perturbations (a casual hint, a mislabeled few-shot example, a fabricated prior assistant turn). This paper traces the origin of that susceptibility across five model families using probing, transfer analysis, and causal intervention on hidden states.
- **Alignment tuning — not pretraining — installs these vulnerabilities**: base models are largely immune, while aligned models encode each bias as a single, decodable, steerable direction.
- Different biases stay representationally distinct, and steering against the extracted direction recovers a meaningful fraction of bias-induced errors while preserving correct answers — a debiasing-by-intervention result with direct relevance to emergent-misalignment-style side effects of alignment training.

### 2. Abliteration Is Not a Scalpel: Off-Target Effects of Refusal Removal on Decision Disposition Across Model Families
**[2607.17427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17427)** · Aleksander Fafuła

- Abliteration (deleting a model's refusal direction from its weights) is the standard recipe behind "uncensored" open-weight models, marketed as a clean, surgical edit. This paper tests that claim using 21,600 financial-forecasting decisions across base vs. abliterated variants of two model families.
- Consistent off-target effects appear in both families: **greater optimism, longer self-justifications, fewer uncertainty markers** — despite the edit nominally targeting only refusal behavior.
- Confidence shifts actually reverse direction between the two families from the identical operation, and the authors flag toolchain contamination in community reproductions — a concrete case study in how narrowly-targeted safety-relevant edits produce broad, unpredictable behavioral drift.

### 3. Persistent Sparse Autoencoders: Learning Feature Timescales in Language Models
**[2607.17117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17117)** · Haoyan Luo, Mateo Espinosa Zarlenga, Mateja Jamnik

- Standard SAEs encode each token independently, discarding information about what persists across a sequence. This work adds a per-feature **persistence coefficient**, letting the SAE learn how long each feature should endure.
- Recovers a spectrum of feature timescales without sacrificing reconstruction quality: fast features behave like local interpretable detectors, slow features carry topic-level semantics.
- In a prompt-injection monitoring application, the slow, persistent features **remain causally effective detection signals over long contexts** — a concrete interpretability tool for long-horizon monitoring.

### 4. Measuring Monosemanticity in Sparse Autoencoders via Latent Activation Coherence
**[2607.17770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17770)** · Katarzyna Filus, Sebastian Pokuciński

- Introduces the Tversky Monosemanticity Score (TMS), a **label-free** metric for SAE feature quality that operationalizes monosemanticity as activation-set coherence of binarized latents, avoiding reliance on external concept labels or embedding-model geometry.
- Validated across DINOv3, CLIP, and BLIP2 with varying SAE configurations and sparsity levels.
- TMS is markedly less sensitive to encoder anisotropy than embedding-based alternatives and better predicts probe-based concept-deletion effectiveness — a methodological fix for a known confound in SAE evaluation.

### 5. Decoder-Preserving Sparse Autoencoders: Which Readouts Survive Sparse Compression?
**[2607.17425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17425)** · Aniket Deshpande

- Points out that equal reconstruction error and sparsity can still preserve *different* linearly-decodable signals — reconstruction quality alone doesn't tell you which downstream readouts survive compression.
- Formalizes this with a matrix-valued distortion metric between optimal ridge-prediction operators, and trains "decoder-preserving" SAEs that combine this distortion term with the usual reconstruction loss.
- On GPT-2 small (block 8), achieves 10.6–11.4% reduction in held-out decoder distortion at matched reconstruction quality — evidence that "faithful reconstruction" and "readout-preserving" are genuinely distinct SAE design objectives, with implications for any downstream probing/steering built on top of SAE features.

### 6. The Anatomy of a Truth Direction: Knowledge-Dependent Dimensionality, a Relational Law, and a Convergent Category Geometry in Small Language Models
**[2607.16741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16741)** · Francesco Karim Vicidomini

- Studies how "truth" is represented internally, finding that its **dimensionality is knowledge-dependent**: a single compact axis for facts the model behaviorally knows well (held-out AUC 0.938), diffusing into higher dimensions as knowledge decreases.
- Attention propagates truth information forward while feed-forward blocks oppose it, and the SwiGLU value stream drives the eventual decay of the truth signal.
- Per-category truth axes converge into a consistent geometric pattern across model families (correlation strengthening from +0.42 to +0.74 with knowledge restriction) — the geometry tracks the knowledge domain, not the architecture, a nice piece of evidence for interpretability work on truthfulness probes.

### 7. Diagnosing Correctness Probes under Self-Judgement Confounding
**[2607.16799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16799)** · Yi-Long Lu

- Hidden-state probes can predict output correctness, but objective correctness (OC) and the model's own self-judgement (SJ) are usually correlated, leaving it ambiguous which signal a "correctness probe" actually decodes.
- Constructs cases where OC and SJ diverge and shows conventional probes track the model's **internal confidence, not ground-truth accuracy** — on these cases, objective-correctness signals perform worse than chance while self-judgement transfers reliably across math reasoning, MMLU, and TruthfulQA.
- A sharp methodological warning: cross-task transfer of a probe's predictions is not sufficient evidence that it measures actual correctness rather than self-assessed confidence — directly relevant to anyone building truthfulness/hallucination probes.

### 8. TRACE: Trajectory-Based Safety Patch Learning for LLM Post-Training Realignment
**[2607.16242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16242)** · Changyue Li, Jiaming He, Youliang Yuan, Jialin Wu, Boxi Yu, Zhicong Huang, Pinjia He

- Fine-Tuning-as-a-Service platforms let users customize LLMs, but customization can silently erode safety alignment; existing parameter-merging fixes suffer from "task-safety update entanglement" that makes merge strength hard to calibrate.
- TRACE instead **simulates harmful fine-tuning trajectories** to generate progressively corrupted model states, then optimizes a plug-in safety patch to recover alignment across that whole range of corrupted states — no online merge-strength tuning required.
- Reaches near-100% safety across six benchmarks and two LLMs while preserving task utility comparable to an undefended fine-tuned model — a practical defense against fine-tuning-induced misalignment.

### 9. Uncovering Latent Reasoning Strategies in Language Models
**[2607.17674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17674)** · Awni Altabaa, John Lafferty

- Models trained on reasoning tasks often blend multiple distinct solution strategies implicitly; this paper decomposes the response distribution into a router (input → distribution over latent strategies) plus a generator conditioned on the selected strategy.
- The main obstacle is **posterior collapse** — the generator, initialized from the base model, can already represent the full output distribution without ever using the latent variable.
- Fixes this with a variational objective that measures fractional information gain relative to the base model's loss, concentrating reconstruction pressure on the tokens the base model finds surprising; on multi-strategy algorithmic tasks, recovered latent codes align with the ground-truth reference strategies — a concrete method for un-tangling mixed reasoning strategies inside a single model.

### 10. A Method for Learning Value Systems in Generative AI
**[2607.16903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16903)** · Andrés Holgado-Sánchez, Holger Billhardt, Sascha Ossowski

- Proposes learning an explicit **value-system representation** — a weighted linear scalarization grounded in a multi-objective reward model — directly from pairwise preference data over prompt-response pairs, rather than collapsing preferences into a single opaque reward.
- Uses dynamic prioritization during training to keep the learned value system's grounding coherent as objectives compete.
- Matches baseline performance while improving explainability of *why* a given preference judgment was made — relevant to alignment approaches that want auditable, decomposed value trade-offs rather than a monolithic reward signal.
