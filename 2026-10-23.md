# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-23

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (Fri 23 Oct 2026) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. The first ~150 titles of the listing were scanned (the page exposes only a subset of entries); the top picks were read at the abstract level and condensed below.

### 1. [Tracing Subliminal Learning Through Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30884)

*Angela Tang, Vishesh Anand, Gonçalo Paulo*

- Studies subliminal learning (students absorbing teacher traits from unrelated-looking data) in hard-label distillation on teacher-generated numbers and in on-policy soft-label distillation, using Pythia, PolyPythia and DataDecide OLMo models.
- Transfer persists across initializations, data orders, corpora, model sizes and architectures.
- Ability to *transmit* a preference and ability to *absorb* one develop separately across pretraining checkpoints, giving strongly asymmetric teacher/student transfer.
- Number sequences matching only the teacher's number frequencies (ignoring prompt/context) still transfer substantially; in top-K soft-label distillation, removing animal tokens from the loss reduces but doesn't eliminate transfer.
- Soft-label distillation transfers more consistently and with less training, which matters as on-policy distillation spreads in post-training.

### 2. [Questioning Instrumental Convergence in RLVR: Checkpoint Evidence from OLMo-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31624)

*Barış Alparslan, Sandy Tanwisuth, Caleb Petterson*

- Asks whether RLVR increases instrumental-convergence behaviors (self-preservation, resource acquisition, deception, resisting oversight) in OLMo-3-7B-Think, using InstrumentalEval across SFT, DPO and 13 RLVR checkpoints.
- Rate is non-monotonic: 25.0% at SFT, peaks at 46.1% near RLVR step 200, then falls to 11.8% at the final checkpoint.
- Prompt framing explains only ~15% of within-checkpoint variance; removing a framing direction (INLP) doesn't change the gap.
- Preliminary 32B result shows a similar pattern (35.5% → 22.4%). Caveats: one model family and benchmark, moderate judge agreement.

### 3. [Inherited, Not Invented: Tracing the Provenance of Wait in Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30882)

*Zhizhuo Kou, Sizai Hou, Long Chen, Jian Yang, Junyu Luo, Jingshu Peng, Wenkai Zhang, Sirui Han, Yike Guo*

- Challenges the claim that RL teaches models to pause and reconsider ("wait"): the marker is mostly inherited from training data (mid-training / reasoning SFT).
- Zero-RL rarely introduces the marker despite large math gains; some RL-Zero recipes amplify it if the base already uses it, KL-free GRPO doesn't.
- RL after SFT barely changes it, and one GRPO recipe erases an SFT-introduced marker.
- Marker frequency alone is not evidence of RL-produced reasoning; LLM-judge revision metrics can reverse human-labeled trends.

### 4. [Interpreting Model Reasoning through Behavioral Counterfactuals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30630)

*Shawn Im, James Oldfield, Adel Bibi, Sharon Li*

- For a fixed response, asks how changing the prompt alters next-token distributions; trains a residual-stream editing module to match source and counterfactual-prompt output distributions (KL), which recovers counterfactual behavior better than standard reconstruction objectives.
- Attribution over the module's outputs localizes causally important positions, giving hypotheses testable via activation patching.
- Case study (Gemma-4-E4B-it, DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B): a suggestion influences reasoning even when never verbalized, mediated by representations at the question statement and filler tokens.

### 5. [Towards Faithful Data Selection: Statistical Limits and Scalable Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30442)

*Ahmed Mehdi Inane, Mahdi Haghifam, Gintare Karolina Dziugaite, Ioannis Mitliagkas*

- Shows optimal data selection (minimize test error of a model trained on the subset) is information-theoretically impossible without a validation set much larger than the training set.
- Proposes a one-sided "faithfulness" guarantee: an estimable proxy upper-bounding how much worse a subset is than full data, via the KL between subset- and full-data-trained parameter distributions (modeling training as ULA) using accumulated gradient discrepancy.
- A swap-based local search never worsens the bound and can refine any existing selector; matches baselines on CIFAR-10/100.

### 6. [Rethinking SAE Evaluation: An Atomic Interpretable Unit Framework Reveals Hidden Polysemanticity and Redundancy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30474)

*Riteng Zhang, Xiaoqian Wang*

- Argues SAEs lack a clear objective and proposes that dictionary features should form an "atomic interpretable unit" basis; introduces DAME (Dominance-Aware Monosemanticity Evaluation).
- Across hundreds of SAEs, some look monosemantic under standard metrics yet remain functionally polysemantic.
- The most dominant features (largest contribution to model computation) tend to be less interpretable and more redundant.
- Recommends dominance-aware evaluation before using SAE features for attribution, circuits or interventions.

### 7. [Causal Chain of Thought: Mitigating Attention Leakage via Context Isolation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31639)

*Gabriele Dominici, Teague McMillan, Fazl Barez, Marc Langheinrich, Martin Gjoreski*

- CausalCoT decomposes a problem into a DAG of atomic sub-questions, each evaluated seeing only its direct parents, so rationales can't be post-hoc justifications of a direct lookup.
- Keeps competitive accuracy while raising causal sensitivity of intermediate steps ~12 pp on average (up to 21 over the best baseline).
- Dependency graph enables test-time interventions whose corrections propagate predictably.

### 8. [OP-Print: Tracing Membership Imprints in On-Policy Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31616)

*Qi Li, Xinchao Wang*

- First membership-inference study of on-policy self-distillation (model distills from itself given extra context).
- Supervision is sparse, concentrated at uncertainty-sensitive correction points; gray-box attack aggregates teacher–student residual corrections along rollouts, black-box attack uses output stability under generation restarts.
- Baselines are near chance even with gray-box access; OP-Print works in both settings.

### 9. [Probing and Steering for Safety-Relevant Behaviours in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30363)

*Nimun Bajwa, Massimo Poesio*
