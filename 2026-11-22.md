# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-22

Note: arXiv does not announce on weekends, so the cs.LG listing still shows **Fri, 20 Nov 2026** (119 new + 94 cross-lists). This is the third digest drawn from that listing. The picks below **leave out the 42 papers already in the 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21 digests**, so the remaining pool is thinner. Summaries are based on the abstracts. Today's picks lean toward auditing and steering via sparse features, evaluation methodology (metrics that reward the wrong thing), and a major learning-theory result on sample compression.

## Top 10

### 1. [Game-Theoretic Data Allocation for Expert Specialization and Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25167)
- InflEns: a mixture of LoRA experts. The **same location game** in a fixed prompt-trait space decides both each expert's training-data weights and the inference-time routing, with no learned gate and no expert losses.
- This makes the division of labor **inspectable before training**. On a corpus built from seven safety benchmarks, the highest-share expert has the lowest held-out NLL on ~90% of prompts, versus ~15% with permuted allocations. It is roughly on par with learned gates.
- Safety payoff: because routing is fixed, harmful behavior can be **traced to a single expert** and fixed by retraining only that expert. HarmBench attack success drops from 0.51 to 0.02 while instruction following is preserved.

### 2. [Auditing Learned Dynamics in Weather Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25119)
- Trains SAEs on GraphCast and asks whether **causal relevance requires physical semantics**.
- Ablating a feature tied to GraphCast's *computational mesh*, which has no physical meaning, still raises global geopotential error. Interpretable association and causal utility come apart.
- Steering works: scaling up convection and low-level-vorticity features during the rollout cuts tropical-cyclone intensity error, with a **median 51% reduction over 7 storms**. Suppressing them weakens predicted deepening.
- A clean interpretability case study outside language models: sparse features serve as experimental coordinates for auditing and intervening.

### 3. [BuggyMLE: Scaling Machine Learning Engineering Difficulty with Bug Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25088)
- Relevant to automated AI R&D. Introduces the **Code Complexity Index**, ten structural repo/patch features that predict average solve rates across 8 coding benchmarks (R² = 0.951). Patch size and complexity are the strongest predictors.
- Agents inject controlled bugs into working ML repos, producing 6,600+ variants from 222 repos. The result is a difficulty-graded, repo-level MLE-Bench Lite: across 14 models, the **top solve rate falls from 95% to 9%** from the easiest to the hardest tier.
- A curriculum fine-tune of Qwen3.5-9B rivals frontier models on the benchmark and transfers outside ML (SWE-bench Lite 24% → 33%).

### 4. [The Price of an Explanation: What Demanding Verifiable Artifacts Costs, and What It Buys Back](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25896)
- On ARC-AGI-2, requiring the LLM to output an executable program rather than just an answer drops the score from 93 to 68 of 120 tasks.
- Key finding about generalization: on 27 of the 28 tasks that only the answer-only arm solved, some program **fit every demonstration yet failed the test**. The bottleneck is finding an explanation that *generalizes*, not finding one that fits.
- What verification buys back: a cheap evolutionary search over already-sampled programs adds about 9 tasks, and checkable programs allow **early stopping**, saving up to 49% of the budget. The method also produces interpretable quantum error-correction decoders.

### 5. [Near-Quadratic Sample Compression for All VC Classes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25011)
- **Proves the weaker form of the Floyd–Warmuth sample compression conjecture**: every class of VC dimension *d* has a compression scheme of size **O(d² log² d)**. The previous best bound depending only on *d* (Moran–Yehudayoff 2016) was exponential in *d*.
- This is a basic result linking simplicity and generalization, open since the 1980s and 90s. The strong O(d) conjecture remains open.

### 6. [What One Sample Cannot See: Why Fewer Denoising Steps Can Score Higher on Physics Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25632)
- Physics-IQ compares each generated video with a *single* real recording, so an exact decomposition shows it **rewards collapsed, low-variance predictions**. This is why blurrier two-step sampling can score higher.
- Proposes **Kernel Physics-IQ**, which removes this incentive in expectation using just two samples per condition. For CogVideoX, the official score rises by 0.055 with two-step sampling while the corrected score falls by 0.131.
- In systems with known outcome probabilities, the metric correctly prefers the true distribution. The lesson applies widely: evaluate world models by their distributions, not by matching one sample.

### 7. [NariQ: Sub-3-Bit Quantization for Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26157)
- Points out a hidden capability loss: existing ≤3-bit quantizations of Qwen3.8-27B match BF16 on AIME but solve **at most 72% as many DeepSWE tasks**. Perplexity and short-task evals miss this.
- Fix: trellis-coded post-training quantization (PTQ) **calibrated on tokens the BF16 model generated in agent sessions**. At 3.0 and 2.5 bits per weight, it stays level with BF16 on DeepSWE, Terminal Bench, AIME and RealWorldQA. The 2.0-bit checkpoint trails.
- Takeaway: calibration data should match the deployment distribution.

### 8. [Anchor and Check: Deterministic Cross-Prompt, Cross-Model Reconciliation for Auditable LLM Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25818)
- A simple contract for when an LLM pipeline may act unattended. Commit the greedy "anchor" answer, run 1–2 checks that reach the answer by a different route (another prompt or another model family), and act only on *Agree*.
- 48K model-item evaluations: **pooling samples from another model family** raises semantic-entropy AUROC by 1.7–4.0 points at every budget. A same-model temperature check is the weakest design.
- Under the strict gate it automates 70% of items and still acts on 16% of errors. Its main value is a committed answer and a decision that can be replayed from its record, which matters because hosted generation itself was not reproducible.

### 9. [When Is Program Structure Required? Certificate-Grounded Calibration of Program-Induction Difficulty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24975)
- Benchmarks can claim to test two-rule programs even when one rule explains all the examples. This paper builds matched tasks whose minimum solution length (1 vs. 2 rules) is **certified before inference**.
- For Qwen3-8B and Olmo-3-7B-Think, completion of the displayed task is 24–46 points lower on certified two-rule tasks. Yet success on an unshown witness pair is 8–13 points *higher*: the examples given determine which program the model induces.
- A single unchanged example can rule out a shortcut. This is a useful testbed for studying shortcut versus structured generalization.

### 10. [Measurable Does Not Imply Transportable: A Prospective Audit of Model Explanations in Multi-Year Field Trials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25692)
- Defines **prospective explanation transportability**: whether an explanation learned from past data holds up in a genuinely future context. Tested on multi-year maize trials with a prospectively locked test year.
- Coarse explanations transport well (median cosine 0.83). Fine-grained genotype × environment explanations reproduce *within* a context (0.77–0.95) but transport poorly across years (0.01–0.36).
- **Measurement reliability does not predict transport**: the most reliable year transported worst. Non-temporal validation overstates transport by +0.22. This warns against treating stable attributions as scientific claims.

## Honorable Mentions
- [Learn, Freeze, and Transfer an Activation Function Across Architectures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26201): a 5-parameter Fourier activation learned in one ViT run, frozen, and reused from scratch. It beats GELU and nine other activation functions in most of 16 architecture/dataset settings, a form of transfer that does not depend on weights.
- [HiSA: Sparsifying the Hidden Dimension of RoPE Attention in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26363): uses RoPE structure to compute only a partial, position-dependent q·k dot product. Trained from scratch, it *improves* over full attention, which says something about how RoPE frequency bands are used.
- [SKILL.PNG: Learning Agent Skills in Pixel Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26296): "skills" as images optimized by gradient through frozen VLM encoders. They transfer across an ensemble of models and beat LLM-optimized text skills in 9 of 16 settings. Also relevant as an adversarial-image steering channel.
- [Stability Bands for Feature Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25238): replaces single Shapley numbers with core/nucleolus ranges, so you can see whether a feature's attribution could be contested by a coalition of features.
- [Complete-SVD: A Functional Norm Approach to Low-Rank LLM Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26059): low-rank compression that minimizes the full-logit L2 change, via linearization plus Kronecker factors. The intractable factor is estimated without bias from random probes.
- [Towards Autoregressive Neural Network Parameter Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25538): tokenizes pretrained weights so that an autoregressive generator produces parameters for heterogeneous architectures from instructions.
- [Extending Rademacher Complexity-Based Generalization Theory to Domain Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25650): domain-generalization bounds that account for the geometry of the space of domain distributions.
- [A Control-Theoretic Approximation to Predictive Coding Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26319): shows that deep feedback control is a local approximation to predictive coding near fixed points, exact for shallow linear networks.
- [TwinLab-Bench: Evaluating AI Scientists in Agentic Twins of Real Labs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26248): 52 tasks in which agents design their own experiments and are scored against hidden real lab records.
