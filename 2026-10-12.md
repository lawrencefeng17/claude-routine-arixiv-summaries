# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-12

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (262 entries, Mon 12 Oct 2026, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. All 262 titles were scanned; the top 10 and honorable mentions were read at the abstract level.

### 1. [Defence against the dark arts of distillation: Unsupervised discovery of subliminal learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13536)

- Subliminal learning: a student distilled from a teacher with the same base model inherits the teacher's traits (including misalignment) from semantically unrelated data. Existing defences must know the trait in advance.
- Proves a trait can be recovered from parameter updates alone, without supervision. The trait is expressed consistently across diverse teacher-generated datasets, while task-specific capabilities vary.
- The estimator gives a weight-space direction that can be amplified to interpret the trait or projected out to suppress it.
- On the Cloud et al. (2026) benchmarks it recovers the transmitted animal-preference traits without trait labels and suppresses them with almost no task-performance loss.

### 2. [AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928)

- Capability evals assume hidden capabilities can be elicited. This paper shows the assumption fails under adversarial conditions.
- Backdoor construction embeds encrypted neural networks inside host model weights, using digital lockers. They are decrypted and run only on a secret trigger, and are provably hard to elicit or interpret under cryptographic assumptions even with full weight access.
- Proof of concept on a chemical reaction prediction task (a CBRN proxy). It resists fine-tuning, weight noising, gradient-based trigger search and latent adversarial training.
- Points to a fundamental limit of elicitation-based capability audits. Models released.

### 3. [Specialists Learn Faster: Knowledge Organization in Pre-training Shapes Knowledge Update Efficiency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13835)

- Compares pretraining on chronologically ordered knowledge ("specialists", tuned to recent facts) against shuffled order ("generalists").
- Specialists need far fewer steps to acquire new knowledge.
- Derives a first-principles law relating update cost to optimization dynamics, which closely predicts empirical results.
- Proposes a data-organization strategy that keeps stable historical knowledge while making updates cheap. The benefits extend to real pretrained models and other orderings.

### 4. [Simulating Training Spikes of Deep Neural Networks by Layer-wise Random Feature Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13524)

- Models LLM pretraining loss spikes with simple quadratic models on heavy-tailed, power-law inputs, in a per-layer training-dynamics simulator.
- In the Adam β₂≈1 setting known to cause spikes in Transformer LMs, the simulation predicts the bumps from a given checkpoint.
- Uses observed metrics to derive a β₂ schedule that empirically improves performance.

### 5. [What Should SFT Learn? Conditional Information Gain for Generalizable Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12995)

- Argues that reweighting SFT tokens by model compatibility is not the same as learning value. A weakly supported token may be exactly what the demonstration is meant to teach.
- CIG-SFT weights each token by conditional information gain: how much the task input raises the frozen reference model's likelihood of the demonstrated token.
- This yields a scalar-weighted SFT objective whose logit gradient matches a KL-regularized projected target. It needs no extra data or rollouts.
- Beats standard SFT and adaptive baselines on math and code, with the largest gains on harder out-of-distribution evaluation sets.

### 6. [A Privileged Teacher That Only Scores Cannot Search: Critique-Conditioned On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13744)

- Tests whether the dense signal in on-policy self-distillation (a teacher given the reference solution scoring the student's rollouts) actually carries corrective credit.
- Using gold error spans, neither the KL nor the token log-ratio localizes errors, pushes probability away from erroneous tokens, or separates good from bad rollouts.
- Argues this is structural: finding an error is itself a search problem, and a scoring teacher never searches. The same model localizes errors much better when it first writes a critique.
- Critique-conditioned self-distillation shifts probability toward backtracking tokens ("Wait", "But") and gives an advantage signal in cold-start GRPO where verifiable rewards give none.

### 7. [Studying the Effects of Task Diversity Using Small Mathematical Worlds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13750)

- Introduces IntWorld and PermWorld, procedurally generated families of distinct but related tasks, in the spirit of modular-addition studies but with many tasks.
- The number of tasks can be varied precisely, separating task variety from other training factors.
- Initial results suggest more task diversity gives richer internal representations.

### 8. [Why Heavy-Tailed Weight Spectra Predict Model Quality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13123)

- Gives a statistical justification for heavy-tailed weight-spectrum metrics (the data-free predictors of model quality).
- Derives a marginal likelihood from the final-layer weights alone. It predicts quality across large CNNs and LLMs.
- For High-Temperature Marchenko-Pastur spectra, the limiting log-marginal likelihood is convex in a heaviness parameter. The resulting optimal heaviness empirically predicts overfitting.

### 9. [The Limits of Query Monitoring against Distributed Model Extraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13083)

- Studies detecting distillation campaigns spread over thousands of accounts using only query content (1M real chat turns plus 17 held-out weeks).
- A campaign is visible only if it is more distinctive than the most distinctive group of benign users that week. Small differences buy detection time, not detectability.
- A calibrated monitor catches naive distributed extraction (0.86–1.00 of weeks). A topicality-discounted co-movement variant still catches campaigns padded with natural text.
- Campaigns that evaded every detector retained at most 42% of the capability of a detectable one. Limits: slow ramp-ups poison calibration, no deployable detector at ≤2.5% colluder share, and relayed user requests may be invisible.

### 10. [Space Over Statistic, Retraining Over Ranking: Auditing Training Labels Under Label-Only Backdoors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13051)

- Label-only backdoors (FLIP): flipping 3% of CIFAR-10 labels makes a ResNet-32 send 99.7% of triggered images to the attacker's class.
- Holding test, labels and metric fixed, the feature space decides detection. A nearest-neighbor check gets AUROC 0.546 on the poisoned model's features but 0.967 on frozen DINOv2 features.
- A prototype audit on frozen features removes less clean data than SPECTRE (6% vs 20%) with lower attack success, but leaves about a quarter of the attack on harder pairs.
- Better detection AUROC does not imply a safer model, so judge defenses by retraining. An adaptive attacker slips past the best static audit.

## Honorable mentions

- [On the estimation and validity of AI time horizons — a statistical look at the METR plot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12466): spline/IRT refit of METR time horizons. Human time maps to AI difficulty nonlinearly (flat from 2–30 min), so a 10× jump is not uniform in difficulty.
- [REDaCT: Collapse-Free Unlearning in Large Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12769): inference-time unlearning with a leak detector plus a learned corrective embedding, which avoids reasoning collapse.
- [A Critical Look at Power Sampling when Test Time Sharpening LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13653): power-distribution samplers don't converge. Their gains are mostly recreated by best-of-N, and fine-tuning to the power distribution gives noisy gains.
- [Kernelized Parameter Sensitivity Atlases for Amortized Conditional Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12737): NTK-derived amortized parameter-sensitivity and activation attribution, with much higher throughput and lower memory than backprop.
- [Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13015): f-divergence trust-region view unifying PPO, DAPO and others for LLM RL.
- [Static Gradient Attribution Underperforms a Density-Matched Random Mask on Loss-Based Forgetting Within LoRA B-Matrix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12744): a random mask beats gradient-attribution selection for forgetting in parameter-efficient continual learning on Qwen2.5-3B.
- [Prediction Is Not a Sufficient Memory State](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13881): theory of what a recurrent or attention memory must store beyond current predictions to keep learning.
- [A Distributional Framework for Behavioral Characterization and Risk Management of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12718): Monte Carlo response distributions with semantic interventions for black-box LLM risk monitoring.
