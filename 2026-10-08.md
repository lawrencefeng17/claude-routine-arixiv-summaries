# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-08

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (353 entries, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. All 353 titles were scanned; the top 10 and the first few honorable mentions were read at the abstract level.

### 1. [The Persona Hierarchy Model: Understanding Contextual Generalization in Fine-Tuning LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09384)

- Proposes that a shared default persona drives behavior across contexts. Fine-tuning that shifts this shared persona transfers broadly, while changes to local personas stay context-specific.
- Across 120 fine-tuned models (4 behaviors, 15 training contexts), narrowness of generalization correlates with the similarity between the training-context persona and the default persona (r = 0.72 on Qwen3-4B).
- Persona-preserving regularization (PPR) confines unwanted generalization. In RL it cuts reward hacking from 42–55% to ≤0.2% while keeping accuracy gains.

### 2. [Which Rollout Taught It That? BehaviorTrace and the Limits of Training-Data Attribution in Online RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10422)

- Open harness for testing data attribution in GRPO fine-tuning, using a planted behavior with a known cause.
- Much of the apparent attribution signal is confounded. Ranking steps by gradient norm alone reaches 4.2–4.5× chance and matches or beats the best targeted estimator on 2 of 3 seeds. Fluency predicts the behavior label about as well as any gradient method.
- Per-rollout results vary by seed and generation draw. Only the gradient of the trigger tokens reliably aligns with a target built where the behavior occurs. Gives a checklist for evaluating attribution in RL.

### 3. [How to train your model organism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10203)

- Argues that training model organisms only to install the target behavior is insufficient. Proposes also validating general-capability preservation and output naturalness (CoT, activations).
- In two public organism suites, chat quality and CoT naturalness degrade substantially, and these validation metrics predict how well interpretability methods recover the behavior.
- Introduces model-merging-based multi-objective training. On a new suite of clinical demographic-bias organisms, DPO stays closer to the base model than SFT.

### 4. [Removing Information Content Does Not Certify Tamper Resistance in Open-Weight Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09004)

- Shows that mutual information at release cannot universally certify slow recovery of removed harmful capability under fine-tuning attacks.
- Function-preserving reparameterizations leave information unchanged but change gradient-descent geometry. An explicit construction has zero weight–data and label–representation information yet recovers in one gradient step.
- Certification needs constraints on attack dynamics, not just information at release.

### 5. [Why Forget-Only Unlearning Needs Memorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10519)

- Studies unlearning given only the trained model and forget set, with no retain data.
- Different datasets can yield the same model but need very different post-deletion outputs, giving lower bounds on how well unlearning can match retraining.
- Successful forget-only unlearning requires the model to memorize extra information about the training data. For threshold learners this can be the whole dataset.

### 6. [Quadratic Weak-to-Strong Generalization in Random Feature Networks via Random Matrix Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09044)

- Analyzes weak-to-strong generalization in two-layer random feature networks, where width sets model strength.
- For ReLU and a pure spherical-harmonic target, the student's error scales as the square of the teacher's error, matching a known general lower bound.
- Characterizes regimes with quadratic, non-quadratic, or no improvement under general stopping times and multi-degree targets.

### 7. [How Do Agentic LLMs Decide to Call Tools? A Tool-Call Vector Shaped by Suppression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09624)

- Builds 500 minimal contrastive prompt pairs where one verb (write vs. discuss) flips the tool-call decision.
- Finds a vector μ_Δ that is causally necessary and sufficient, and generalizes to native multi-turn τ²-Bench trajectories.
- The scaffold sets a tool-call prior. Analysis verbs suppress it through "tool use unnecessary" features, while execution verbs leave it intact. The mechanism recurs across seven Qwen, Mistral and Granite models.

### 8. [When does a network's training history predict its future learning better than its current state?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09621)

- Pre-registered study of whether training path carries information about future learning beyond the current state. Uses a short-training probe (100 updates on a new task) whose validity was checked beforehand.
- In small MLPs, a history state of ≤4 dimensions did not beat a calibrated current-state model.
- In 1,560 synthetic runs, history forecasts final error better only early (12 epochs) and is indistinguishable by 48 epochs. History helps only while the current state is not yet informative.

### 9. [The Winner's Curse in LLM Self-Improvement Loops: Selection Noise, Lock-in, and Acceptance Rules](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09239)

- Treats "keep if better on a small eval set" as selection under noise. In loops where Qwen models rewrite their own instructions, most proposals after the first are harmful.
- Greedy loops overstate gains by 13–20 points with 16 selection items and by 1–5 points with 256. The tested acceptance rules did not beat greedy acceptance.
- Recommends reporting held-out gains with uncertainty. Scoring on 64 never-used items removes the average bias, but single estimates stay off by about 6 points.

### 10. [Are Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning Methods Really Different?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09122)

- Compares six PEFT methods on task performance, forgetting, and pretrained-weight geometry.
- LoRA-family methods already roughly preserve pretrained geometry, and restoring their drifted singular values largely keeps performance. Explicit spectral preservation may not be needed.
- LoRA limits forgetting most consistently, DoRA scores higher than LoRA in most comparisons, and PiSSA often costs more retention. Restoring dominant spectral components reduces drift the most.

## Honorable Mentions

- [Beyond the Sycophancy Score: How Task, Model, and Pressure Shape LLM Yielding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08840) — 104k graded replies show sycophancy is driven mainly by how costly the user's claim is to verify. Maximum reasoning removes concessions on deep puzzles.
- [How Fragile Is On-Device Language Model Safety?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09000) — Editing 0.19% of LLaMA-2-7B-Chat's weights (in `down_proj`) gives ~53% attack success with little capability loss.
- [Eigenvalues of the Hessian in Deep Learning: The Origin of Symmetry and Its Breaking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09919) — Explains Hessian bulk-and-outlier clusters as symmetry breaking from a nearby symmetric reference.
- [How Do Transformers Learn to Represent Symmetries?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10305) — Orders symmetry groups by learnability under finite augmentation and finds interpretable invariance mechanisms.
- [Pretraining Shapes Spectral Structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09709) — Predicts OOD robustness from pretrained weight spectra alone, conditional on architecture and pretraining strategy.
- [AI Safety Considerations for Agents With Limited Time to Act](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10285) — Proves that even a perfect agent cannot guarantee safety in some partially observed, time-limited environments.
- [RSIGym: A Flexible Environment for Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10310) — Environment for measuring how well agents improve data, training, and harness across five benchmarks.
- More: [On KL-Regularized Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08963), [Sparse Feature Policy Unlearning Mitigates State Hallucination in VLAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09496), [PatchBench: Collateral Damage in Activation Patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10276), [The Best Optimizer Depends on Batch Size](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08975).
