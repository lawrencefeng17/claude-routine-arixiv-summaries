# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-28

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (Wed 28 Oct 2026, 237 entries) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. Titles were scanned and top picks were read at the abstract level and condensed below.

### 1. [Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37481)

*Saket Reddy, Amro Abbas, Anshuman Suri, Ari Morcos, David Schwab, Pratyush Maini*

- Introduces SimpleLoRIF, a scalable gradient-embedding method that finds fine-tuning examples whose gradients align with unsafe behavior (backprop through only the last four blocks; scales to ~1M examples).
- On emergent misalignment, gradients link insecure-code training data to broad harmful behavior (AUC up to 0.865).
- Builds SafeTulu by dropping the 20% of Tulu 3 most aligned with unsafe responses; cuts GCG attack success by up to 22% on Qwen, Llama, and Olmo while keeping capability.
- Surfaces under-studied sources of safety degradation (e.g. tool-execution failures, narrative arithmetic).

### 2. [Base Models Can Detect Hidden Intentions that Prompted Classifiers Miss](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36202)

*Oz Amram*

- Builds on the Persona Selection Model (post-training selects a persona already learned in pretraining) and subliminal learning.
- Persona-header likelihood evaluation (PHLE) scores outputs under a pretrained base model with a persona header.
- Separates number sequences generated under "love owls" vs. "love eagles," and outputs of a model with a hidden desire to harm users (filtered for visible misalignment) from a neutral one, where prompted classifiers are near chance.
- Signal partly transfers across model families (Qwen base reads Olmo outputs and vice versa); suggests a tool for auditing hidden objectives.

### 3. [Exploring the Limits of the Delta Learning Hypothesis: Single-Pair Generator Biases in DPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37058)

*Giuseppe Curci, Stefano Simonazzi, Andrea Molinari, Andrea Zugarini*

- Tests whether one (strong, weak) model pair can supply preference data for DPO by always labelling the larger model's response preferred.
- Fixing a generator pair introduces systematic generator-specific biases that predict the label regardless of quality.
- DPO on single-pair data consistently underperforms multi-model preference data, with dynamics resembling reward hacking of those biases.
- Adding generators, even without an external judge, partly mitigates this; generator diversity matters.

### 4. [Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement: How Cross-Task Coupling Drives Policy Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37553)

*Peize Ding*

- Explains why label-free RLVR (reward = agreement with own majority vote) eventually collapses, sometimes to one identical answer for all problems, and only with many training problems.
- Solvable model: a shared perturbation is amplified through cross-problem parameter coupling, giving a critical problem count N_c ∝ 1/coupling² (up to logs).
- Initially confident models do not collapse; a protective coupling and marginal-entropy bonus can stabilize.
- Small-transformer experiments: removing the shared direction prevents collapse, restoring it causes collapse.

### 5. [Does LLM Pre-Training Typically Occur at the Edge of Stability?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36778)

*Yuhang Cai, Haodong Wen, Haofeng Huang, Deyi Liu, Yiyuan Ma, Kaifeng Lyu*

- Examines where quadratic approximations of training fail in LLM pretraining.
- Identifies an "Edge of Convexity": persistent negative curvature makes quadratic dynamics diverge regardless of learning rate while real training stays stable.
- Defines a generalized Edge of Stability for stochastic training with adaptive optimizers.
- Up to 787M params: EoC always appears; EoS is common but vanishes at small LR (after decay) or when batch size is below a threshold correlated with the critical batch size.

### 6. [On the Sparsity of DPO: Weight Disentanglement in the NTK Regime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37085)

*Kensuke Sasaki, Issei Sato*

- Explains the observed sparsity of DPO parameter updates via NTK linearization: the logistic loss gives an implicit max-margin bias.
- "Dual sparsity": updates are sparse combinations of sparse gradient features, from support vectors (data level) and gradient cancellation between chosen/rejected (parameter level).
- Bounds the number of support pairs with a margin-dependent spectral quantity; links this to weight disentanglement and model merging.
- Confirmed on GPT-2 and Llama-3-8B/Mistral-7B/Gemma-2-9B; DPO reduces cross-task interference up to 35× vs. SFT on GPT-2.

### 7. [Robust LLM Data Selection via Content-Invariant Gradient Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36755)

*Jia Gu, Liang Pang, Huawei Shen*

- Finds gradient-based data attribution has "surface-form bias": meaning-preserving rewording or formatting changes shift scores a lot.
- CIGA selects layers whose gradients are both influential and stable under such edits, then combines them with semantic representations.
- More stable rankings, better average downstream performance for instruction-tuning data selection, and lower attribution cost.

### 8. [LinMix: Data Mixing as Linear Domain Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37390)

*Hubert Baniecki, Przemyslaw Biecek, Martin Pawelczyk*

- Casts pretraining data-mixture regression as domain attribution.
- Samples mixtures at vertices of a capped simplex (hyperparameter-free, decorrelates domain effects) so linear coefficients are interpretable attributions; the optimal mixture keeps positive-attribution domains.
- Proves vertex sampling is optimal and Dirichlet sampling inflates attribution error.
- 1B OLMo2 and 4B Qwen3.5 pretraining: gains up to 46% on MBPP, 14% on GSM8K; +11% avg over best nonlinear baseline with 288 domains.

### 9. [Scaling and Renormalization in Neural Networks: An Effective RG Description of Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36113)

*Min Chen, Junqi Wang, Zeguan Wu, Wenhao Huang, Junyu Liu*

- Applies real-space renormalization-group theory to grokking in a two-layer net on modular addition.
- Uses squared weight norm as order parameter, with epoch and weight decay as control variables; builds a neuron coupling matrix whose Fiedler vector gives an effective geometry.
- Block-aggregating neurons yields scaling exponents; derived critical exponents match empirical estimates within 1–10%.

### 10. [Heads of Security: Learned Attention Gates Improve Prompt Injection Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37657)

*Igor Shilov, Xiaoxue Yang, Alexander Panfilov, Evgenii Kortukov, Matthieu Meeus, Peter Romov, Wojciech Samek, Jonas Geiping, Yves-Alexandre de Montjoye*

- Small set of learned per-head attention gates separates trusted instructions from untrusted data.
- Black-box RL-Hammer attack: 97% success on GPT-OSS-20B, 85% on SecAlign, only 10% on HoS; white-box attack needs ~3× more compute.
- Preserves base capability on benchmarks (where SecAlign/ASIDE degrade few-shot) and matches SecAlign on agentic tasks.
