## Top 10

### 1. Channel Location Constrains the Auditability of Subliminal Learning
**[2606.22019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22019)** · Tamas Madl

- Investigates when hidden traits transferred from teacher to student via distillation can be detected *before* transfer happens.
- Identifies three regimes determined by *channel location* — the carrier through which the trait reaches the student.
- In pretrained LMs, hidden information travels through convergent vocabulary geometry, making initialization-alignment audits ineffective.
- Removing named entities from training labels fails to prevent the student from learning them (held-out probability rises to 0.40 on average, ~2500×).
- Conditional behaviors like sycophancy transfer at ~63% of teacher effect while evading multiple audit types.
- Key takeaway: the soundness of an audit depends on matching it to the correct channel regime — applying audits outside their regime provides false safety assurance.

### 2. Quantifying the Agreement Between Data-Influence and Data-Similarity to Understand LLM Behavior
**[2606.23591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23591)** · Anders, Da Silva Gameiro, Daheim, Khan

- Compares two standard methods for tracing LLM outputs to training data: *data-similarity* and *data-influence*.
- Finds the two rankings agree significantly, but with a key asymmetry: data-similarity's top documents receive more consistent ranks from data-influence than vice versa.
- Pattern holds across OLMo2-1B, Qwen3-1.7B, LLaMA3.2-1B, Gemma3-1B, and GPT2.
- Leverages the asymmetry to improve cost-efficiency: use data-influence to refine data-similarity results.

### 3. The Geometry of Refusal: Linear Instability in Safety-Aligned LLMs
**[2606.22686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22686)** · Ratnakar, Vats

- Introduces *Contrastive Logit Steering* (CLS), which identifies refusal mechanisms by comparing safe vs. unrestricted prompt outputs.
- Safety implementation varies architecturally: Llama-3.1 decides late (95% attack success rate via steering), while Qwen-2.5 integrates safety earlier and more robustly.
- CLS outperforms activation-level steering methods.
- Inverting the safety steering vector strengthens models against jailbreaks without retraining — alignment creates a "safety axis" usable as both vulnerability and defensive tool.

### 4. Exposing the Illusion of Erasure in Knowledge Editing for LLMs
**[2606.23276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23276)** · Basani, Chhabra

- Knowledge editing (KE) methods fail to truly erase facts: edited knowledge resurfaces consistently across diverse model architectures.
- Mechanistic analysis shows low-rank updates redistribute knowledge in representation space rather than removing it — KE acts as suppression, not erasure.
- Edited knowledge occupies vulnerable regions of the loss landscape, making models highly susceptible to indirect prompting and adversarial attacks.
- Calls for reconsidering post-hoc KE in deployed LLM systems.

### 5. Do Sparse Autoencoders Learn Meaningful Concept Hierarchies?
**[2606.22994](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22994)** · Grandien, Steinmann, Friedrich, Kersting

- First rigorous evaluation framework for whether SAEs produce *hierarchically organized* concepts, drawing on semantic network and taxonomy literature.
- SAE feature spaces provide a foundation for reasonable hierarchies, but achieving strong hierarchical structure is difficult.
- Both hard and soft forms of *feature absorption* undermine hierarchy quality — a fundamental challenge for SAE-based interpretability.

### 6. Does RoPE Prevent or Degrade Retrieval Heads? A Mechanistic Analysis Across Model Families
**[2606.21249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21249)** · Bayram

- Tests across four 7–8B models whether rotary position embeddings (RoPE) impair retrieval heads (attention heads responsible for long-context recall).
- Masking detected retrieval heads in OLMo-2 collapses recall from 1.00 to 0.00; random head masking has no effect — causal essentiality confirmed.
- Higher RoPE theta does not reduce retrieval head count (contradicts the prevention hypothesis).
- Zeroing low-frequency RoPE dimensions of retrieval heads degrades recall dose-dependently across five models — RoPE frequency, not norm-utility, is the causal variable.

### 7. Interleaved Speech Language Models Latently Work In Text
**[2606.22473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22473)** · Sternberg, Maimon, Adi

- Logit lens analysis of interleaved speech-text LMs reveals an *implicit transcription phase* in intermediate layers: the text form of spoken words becomes decodable without explicit ASR training.
- This transcription appears among top candidate words for up to 77% of data, before the model predicts subsequent text and reverts to the speech domain.
- Provides a mechanistic account of how cross-modal knowledge transfer happens inside speech-text models.

### 8. Learning through Internalization
**[2606.20937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20937)** · Tsilivis, Joshi, Medvedev, Kempe, Srebro

- Studies how transformers *absorb* explicit computational procedures (provided as chain-of-thought tokens) into their weights as tokens are progressively removed.
- Internalization causes progressive degradation of out-of-distribution performance — the model trades generality for the internalized shortcut.
- Theory shows a one-layer transformer can first learn parity with explicit guidance, then internalize it; connects to the Positive Distribution Shift phenomenon.
- Implications for understanding what models actually learn vs. what they appear to learn via CoT.

### 9. On the Limits of Prompt-Conditioned Language Models as General-Purpose Learners
**[2606.23668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23668)** · Mguni, Ma, Wang

- Models user-LLM interaction as a game-theoretic scenario and proves two fundamental floors:
  - **Expressivity floor**: when task complexity exceeds language's channel capacity, distinct tasks become indistinguishable — an irreducible error that scaling cannot overcome.
  - **Objective-misalignment floor**: alignment constraints push ideal solutions outside the feasible output space, creating unavoidable distortion.
- Multimodal interfaces and external memory partially address these limits by expanding available task information.

---

### 10. Can Reasoning Models Detect Changes to their Chains of Thought?
**[2606.22085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22085)** · Napa, Singh, Xue, Wanner, Walden

- Tests whether reasoning models can identify when their chains of thought have been modified (during or after reasoning, or via prefilling).
- Models show only very modest detection accuracy, struggle to identify how modifications were made, and perform similarly whether detecting changes to their own vs. another model's reasoning.
- Practical implication: externally editing model reasoning (e.g., to insert stronger outputs or remove harmful steps) is largely undetected by the model itself.
