## Top 10

### 1. [Locating and Steering Refusal Beyond Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04721)

- Tests whether the single "refusal direction" found in transformer residual streams is architecture-specific by looking for it in state-space models (SSMs), which route information through recurrence rather than attention and share no token-mixing mechanism with transformers.
- Finds the representation survives: a rigid rotation aligns one model's refusal direction with another's across SSM, transformer, recurrent, and hybrid families, so a harm probe trained on a transformer flags an SSM's harmful inputs, and removing the aligned direction bypasses refusal on all four architecture families (holding even against an attacker tuned against the defense on the SSM).
- Pinpoints what *is* architecture-specific — not where the direction lives, but where it must be read (each layer's write site into the residual stream, before mixing) — giving safety tooling a principled way to port to new architectures rather than being rebuilt from scratch each time.

### 2. [Fractal basins trap latent reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04963)

- Investigates why reasoning models reason longer on harder problems by treating reasoning traces as trajectories of a dynamical system, finding that leading reasoning models exhibit transient chaos on tasks like Sudoku, maze solving, visual puzzles, and mathematical logic.
- Shows the basins of attraction in this dynamical system become measurably more fractal as task difficulty increases, and that reasoning gets trapped for extended periods near saddle points — which correspond to nearly-correct attempted solutions.
- Reframes reasoning slowdown as an inevitable consequence of problem hardness rather than an artifact of training or prompting, and proposes reasoning traces themselves as a new object of study for dynamical-systems analysis.

### 3. [How Does mHC Use Its Residual Streams? Selective Routing and Near-Identity Mixing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05309)

- Opens up manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC), which widen a transformer's single residual stream into four, to ask how a trained frontier model (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) actually uses that extra capacity — via effective stream counts, cross-stream residual weights, and inter-stream cosine similarity.
- Finds usage is concentrated (a typical block effectively uses ~2 of 4 streams) and residual mixing is largely confined to early layers; targeted interventions confirm this is functionally real — replacing late-layer mixers with identity barely hurts perplexity (+1.9%) while doing so early hurts a lot (+41%).
- A clean example of validating an interpretability claim about representation usage with causal ablations rather than just correlational statistics, on a real large-scale production-style model.

### 4. [Evidence Integration in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04290)

- Proposes a distributional theory of how LLMs update from external evidence (tool outputs, RAG, other agents, users) that already has an initial answer distribution forming, tested across 10 million trials, 12 LLMs from 4 families, and 8 domains including scientific-discovery tasks.
- Confirms three predictions — more-probable candidates persuade more, models integrate errors resembling their own more readily than foreign errors, and identical evidence helps weaker models while harming stronger ones — and shows models keep integrating candidate answers even after internally verifying them as invalid (up to 99.4% of the time).
- Causal interventions localize this to a late-network "structured sequence" that admits, promotes, and transports candidate answers into the model's final answer state, while verification signals are decodable but causally inert — a mechanistic account of sycophancy-adjacent failure modes relevant to agent and RAG safety.

### 5. [Conformity Breaks Conformal Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04445)

- Shows that a conformal prediction certificate calibrated on an LLM answering alone becomes invalid once the same LLM sees peer agents that unanimously assert a wrong answer — a "score-mechanism shift" where the model's confidence in the correct answer changes even though the underlying question hasn't.
- Across open-weight models and multiple-choice QA, coverage drops from a calibrated 90% to 74% under unanimous-wrong peers at the standard operating point, and an attacker targeting low-confidence items can nearly halve coverage on that subgroup (87% to 47%) while the reported average looks fine.
- Demonstrates the failure reaches decision-making itself — a system meant to escalate under uncertainty can instead become falsely confident enough to act on a manipulated wrong answer — and shows standard conformal fixes don't help because it's the model's scoring behavior, not the data distribution, that has shifted.

### 6. [Extremely Sparse Supervision Incentivizes Reasoning Ability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04565)

- Revisits the assumption that effective LLM post-training must be token-intensive by testing on-policy distillation (which naturally allows dense per-token teacher supervision) with training signal restricted to as few as one or two tokens per reasoning trajectory — about 0.05% of all generated tokens.
- Finds this extremely sparse supervision matches or surpasses full-token training on reasoning ability, consistently across nine teacher-student configurations, multiple model scales, math and coding tasks, and different model families (Qwen3, Llama) and training paradigms (distillation and RLVR).
- Suggests effective reasoning improvement resembles reflecting on a few critical steps rather than correcting every token, pointing toward a very different and more compute-efficient design space for post-training algorithms.

### 7. [SharedSAE: One Feature Dictionary Across Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04344)

- Tackles the scalability problem in mechanistic interpretability where sparse autoencoders (SAEs) must be separately trained and labeled for every model, by building a single shared feature dictionary with lightweight model-specific encoder-decoder pairs.
- Trained across four distinct 1B-scale model families, SharedSAE retains 96.6% of dedicated per-model SAEs' explained variance while its latent activations correlate 1.8x more strongly across models than post-hoc-aligned separate SAEs, and its latent feature descriptions transfer directly across models.
- A practical step toward cross-model interpretability infrastructure — new models can be adapted to the frozen shared dictionary cheaply while reusing existing feature descriptions, rather than re-deriving and re-labeling features from scratch each time.

### 8. [An Analysis of Self-supervised Pre-training with Dependent Samples](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05031)

- Addresses a gap between self-supervised learning theory and practice: theoretical analyses typically assume independent samples, but practitioners pool together multiple correlated augmentations of the same datapoint when estimating the invariant subspace used for downstream tasks.
- Proves that pooling dependent augmented samples, despite the obvious correlations between them, is never statistically worse than partitioning data into independent subsets — and for cases like masking or noise-injection augmentations over shallow networks, pooling achieves strictly faster estimation rates.
- Provides a theoretical account for a widespread empirical practice (using many correlated augmentations) that prior theory couldn't justify, contributing directly to the statistical science of generalization in representation learning.

### 9. [Client-Side Probing of Deleted Ridge Statistics in Federated Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04475)

- Targets federated unlearning systems that achieve exact deletion by storing compact additive summaries of training features and broadcasting an updated linear classifier after each accepted change, showing that these broadcasts leak the hidden summaries themselves.
- A malicious client submits known changes, observes the classifiers returned before and after an isolated deletion, and can exactly recover which sample, class, or client summary was deleted — achieving exact label recovery on MNIST and CIFAR-10 for every tested deletion under high-precision broadcasts.
- Complements the growing literature on unlearning vulnerabilities (in the same vein as recent forgotten-prompt-extraction attacks) by identifying a distinct algebraic mechanism specific to exact-unlearning federated systems, along with the precision/diversity conditions under which the attack fails.

### 10. [Interpretability for Turing Machines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04661)

- Extends "susceptibilities" — an interpretability technique originally developed to probe the local loss landscape of neural networks — to noisy Turing machines, asking whether the same tool can detect algorithmic structure outside the neural-network setting entirely.
- Proves that symmetries and path separation in the algorithm a Turing machine implements induce permutation symmetries and low-rank blocks in its susceptibility matrix, then empirically recovers algorithmic features of deterministic finite automata via PCA and clustering in susceptibility space.
- A methodologically interesting generalization test for interpretability tools: if a technique's success is really about detecting algorithmic structure rather than being tied to neural-network specifics, it should transfer to other computational substrates — this paper is direct evidence that it does.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Interface-Induced Trajectory Censoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03966)** — shows that a widely-used agent benchmark's measured tool-call rate can swing from 0.00 to 0.96 purely from serving-adapter mismatches (chat template × parser interaction) with the model, weights, and decoding held fixed, exposing evaluation-validity problems that reach into RL training loops themselves.
- **[Single-Query Black-Box Calibration Auditing via Logit Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05125)** — shows any LLM API exposing a `logit_bias` parameter can be manipulated to recover exact probability thresholds in one query per sample, enabling a provably consistent calibration-error estimator for commercial models that hide their output probabilities.
- **[Optimizer Memory Schedules for Outscaling the Overtraining Axis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04577)** — a systematic study of how AdamW, Muon, SOAP, and a momentum-scheduled method (ADANA) scale across 1x-256x overtraining factors, finding that optimal learning-rate schedules can reverse direction and weight decay should scale as sqrt(overtraining), establishing training horizon as an essential axis for optimizer comparisons.
- **[Amortizing Scaling Law Construction Costs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05016)** — formulates scaling-law data collection as Bayesian optimization over the compute-loss frontier, matching full dense-grid scaling law fits at 10-100x lower training cost.
- **[Same Request, Different Answer: Quantization Amplifies Cache-Induced Divergence in LLM Serving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04748)** — finds that prefix caching, treated as a transparent serving optimization, changes an agent's trajectory on 36% of episodes at 16-bit precision and 75% at 4-bit with everything else held fixed, tracing the nondeterminism to cache state being absent from the request and never reset by default.
- **[Privacy Failure in Split-LLM Training, The Returned Gradient Nullifies the Decoys](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04382)** — a systems-security case study showing that decoy rows mixed into split-LLM training activations are trivially unmasked because their gradients are exactly zero, letting an attacker identify real rows on every single frame across nine seeds even in configurations that pass standard privacy checks.
- **[Tracing Audio Grounding and Answer Selection in Audio LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04637)** — a mechanistic study of what changes inside an Audio LLM when training forces it to actually use audio rather than textual/linguistic priors, localizing the effect to specific early-to-middle layers shaping answer representations and middle-to-late layers determining final predictions.
- **[SiLR: Structure-Preserving Admission and Process Reward for LLM Tool Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04629)** — proves that scalar-score gates for admitting LLM tool-agent proposals are representationally insufficient (not just poorly tuned) for recovering from constraint violations, and that a product-order admission criterion recovers 21/21 episodes against 0/21 for terminal gates.
