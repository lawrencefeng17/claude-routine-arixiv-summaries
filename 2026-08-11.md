## Top 10

### 1. [Do All LLMs Know When They're Being Harmful? A Reproducibility Study of Latent-Space Safety Probes Across Model Families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08029)
*Alizishaan Khatri, Dun Li Chan*

- Reproduces and extends prior work showing lightweight MLP probes on the final-layer activations of a single 8B model (LLaMA-3.1-8B) detect harmful prompts at F1 competitive with guard models 1000x larger.
- Extends validation to Gemma-4-E4B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Qwen2-7B across three safety benchmarks (WildJailbreak, BeaverTails, AEGIS 2.0), reproducing the original result within 0.37 F1 points.
- The probe architecture generalizes across model families with comparable performance, and final-token latent vectors are stable across random seeds — evidence of a shared, robust underlying representation of "harmfulness."
- A concrete data point for latent-space safety probing as a cheap, generalizable substitute for large external guard models.

### 2. [The Knowing-Saying Gap: When Probes See Errors that Confidence Misses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07528)
*Jyotin Goel, Ipshita Bandyopadhyay, Justin Shenk*

- Linear probes detect corrupted context in multi-hop arithmetic reasoning chains with near-perfect accuracy, yet this doesn't translate into reliable prediction of whether the final answer is correct — a genuine dissociation between "knowing" and "saying."
- Forcing models into structured verbalized-confidence formats collapses outputs to two values with indistinguishable error rates; probe persistence across hops fails to separate correct from incorrect outcomes, refuting the authors' pre-registered "persistence beats peak" hypothesis.
- The knowing-but-not-saying pattern generalizes across model families, including reasoning models.
- Real-time monitoring interventions are sharply model- and error-type-dependent (branch-and-pick helps without breaking correct traces on Llama-3.1-8B; other interventions break roughly as many correct traces as they rescue) — no single fix dominates, pointing to model-aware, error-type-aware routing as the deployable answer.

### 3. [Safety Cost of Steering Vectors Is Separable and Reducible](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08383)
*Yuxiao Li, Gjergji Kasneci*

- Steering vectors — a lightweight tool for controlling LLM behavior — can unintentionally compromise safety mechanisms and increase compliance with harmful requests.
- Shows this safety cost arises from a separable component of the vector that contributes little to the actual steering objective.
- Isolates and removes this component via constrained (primal-dual) optimization, recovering a single direction whose ablation restores safety with minimal utility loss.
- Validated across models, steering behaviors, and unseen attack types — a general recipe for activation-level interventions without paying a "safety tax."

### 4. [Multimodal Model Diffing for Feature Discovery and Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09928)
*Hunar Batra, Lachin Naghashyar, Ashkan Khakzar, Philip Torr, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Constantin Venhoff, Ronald Clark*

- Introduces MMDiff, a sparse-autoencoder framework that identifies features altered by multimodal training, detects task-specific causal features, and enables feature-level control (removal or steering) in MLLMs.
- Removing identified features selectively degrades targeted behaviors (avg. 12% on spatial tasks, 17% on OCR) while preserving general VQA performance, demonstrating causal specificity.
- Feature steering conversely improves spatial (+3.6%) and OCR (+1.8%) accuracy.
- Evaluated across three model families (LLaVA-MORE, PaliGemma 2, InternVL3.5) — a mechanistic-interpretability toolkit for auditing and controlling multimodal behavior.

### 5. [A Mechanistic Diagnostic of Rank Collapse in Post-Norm Decoder Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09417)
*Xingjian Wang, Qingyu Han, Xiaodong Luo, Yin Zhang*

- Explains training instability in deep Post-Norm decoder transformers through a two-stage mechanistic account using token similarity as the diagnostic.
- At initialization, causal attention acts as an approximate prefix-averaging operator that increases token similarity with depth, only partially offset by SwiGLU.
- Once a high-similarity state is reached, residual-norm growth makes the backward pass inefficient — gradients to earlier layers decay geometrically.
- Validated on 48-layer models trained on C4: collapsed networks converge to a predictable, near-frequency-matching loss baseline, directly linking training dynamics to an interpretable failure mode.

### 6. [From Objectives to What Models Learn: A Landau Theory of Invariant Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09396)
*Pinli Wang, Yue He, Peng Cui*

- Applies a Landau-theory-style phase-transition analysis (borrowed from the statistical physics of magnetization) to invariant-learning objectives, deriving an effective free energy whose low-order coefficients predict regularization-path behavior.
- Quadratic terms set the phase boundary for mode elimination; quartic terms govern post-onset residual loading; higher-order terms explain non-monotone tails, instability, and collapse at high regularization strength.
- In a canonical bilinear model, yields closed-form phase boundaries and a "selective-retention window" distinguishing shortcut from stable feature modes.
- Predictions hold in 1–2 hidden-layer ReLU networks despite depth-dependent scale shifts — turns generalization/invariance objectives into a predictive theory of what representations models actually learn.

### 7. [Correlation flow governs learning at criticality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08350)
*Andrea Combette, Nelly Pustelnik, Antoine Venaille*

- Connects initialization-dependent signal/gradient propagation to learning dynamics via mean-field and random matrix theory, in the infinite-width, infinite-depth limit.
- Shows correlation propagates to infinite depth only at a single critical point in weight-bias variance space, where the end-to-end Jacobian vanishes algebraically and the Neural Tangent Kernel becomes exactly proportional to output correlation — a previously unidentified equivalence between information propagation and learning dynamics.
- Orthogonal initialization suppresses finite-size corrections present under Gaussian initialization.
- Validated on finite-width, finite-depth networks, clarifying why orthogonal initialization at criticality controls deep learning's asymptotic training dynamics.

### 8. [Can Webcam Gaze Constrain Mesa-Objectives in Driving Models? An Instrument Precision Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08947)
*Lennox Anderson, Ahmed Boutar, Jonah Mulcrone, Tal Erez*

- Tests whether cheap webcam eye-tracking data could serve as an auxiliary supervision signal to prevent driving-hazard-detection models from learning misaligned internal (mesa-)objectives.
- Collected 137,000+ frame-level gaze samples synchronized with hazard annotations across 388 dashcam videos, tested with two calibration protocols and two architectures (Random Forest, causal Transformer) with statistical testing.
- Found no statistically significant improvement from incorporating gaze data.
- Root cause: WebGazer's typical 130–257px error exceeds the 36px median hazard size, making object-level gaze attribution "physically impossible" at this instrument precision — a useful negative result for mesa-objective-constraining supervision schemes.

### 9. [Who Built This Model? Tracing LLM Lineage via Spectral Fingerprints in Weight Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07786)
*Yiwei Chen, Bingqi Shang, Sijia Liu*

- Asks whether open-weight LLMs carry intrinsic "fingerprints" in weight space revealing training lineage — a model-provenance question closely related to data/model attribution.
- Frames it as discriminating independently-trained vs. same-series vs. shared-base models via two geometric signals: spectral energy (singular-value distributions) for global magnitude, and subspace alignment for directional geometry.
- Spectral energy cleanly separates independently-trained models and different families; subspace alignment resolves fine-grained relationships (dataset size, post-training method) among closely related models.
- Tested on 110+ open-weight LLM pairs — a practical tool for auditing model provenance and IP/copying questions.

### 10. [Evaluator Ensembles Under Reward Hacking: Covariance Geometry and Finite-Search Guarantees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08002)
*Fariya Afrin, Ibne Farabi Shihab*

- Analyzes when LLM-judge/reward-model ensembles fail under reward hacking via the covariance geometry of evaluator disagreement.
- Proves that common-mode error (bias shared across judges) is *not identifiable* from internal judge scores alone — disagreement can be high despite robust aggregation, or low while systematic shared errors persist.
- Bounds best-of-K selection overstatement and target-quality regret under a joint sub-Gaussian error model, extending the guarantees to adaptive search; search-driven error scales as √(log K).
- Validated via 120 stress-test configurations and real-model audits — exposes fundamental limits of disagreement-based reward-hacking diagnostics as optimization pressure increases.
