# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-10

Source: the cs.LG listing for Tue, 10 Nov 2026 (509 entries including cross-lists). Picks are ranked by relevance to alignment, generalization, interpretability, and training dynamics. Summaries are based on the abstracts. Strong day: persona generalization / emergent misalignment, subliminal learning, and several probing-monitor papers.

## Top 10

### 1. [Is Persona Generalization Predictable?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12864)
- Studies how fine-tuning in one domain shifts behavior in others (e.g. emergent misalignment).
- Finds an "on-the-line" effect: in-domain vs out-of-domain persona rates are roughly probit-linear across hyperparameters, and the line is shared across Qwen and Llama and across model sizes.
- LR, weight decay, and duration move models along the line. Changing which layers are tuned changes the slope.
- For unsafe behavior, tuning only later layers keeps in-domain learning but reduces out-of-domain misalignment.

### 2. [Regularization Amplifies Subliminal Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10397)
- Tests whether regularization limits covert transfer of hidden biases (subliminal learning, phantom transfer, logit-linear selection).
- Finds the opposite: regularization amplifies transfer. Subliminal learning is usually absent under full fine-tuning, but LoRA or explicit penalties induce strong transfer.
- More data also strengthens it. This suggests simplicity biases help covert influence.

### 3. [What Do Collusion Probes Read? Three Pitfalls in Evaluating Multi-Agent Collusion Monitors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10620)
- Activation probes for LLM-agent collusion look near-perfect but mostly detect that an agent was *told* to collude, not that it decided to.
- New benchmark adds agents who receive the instruction and refuse. The best probe's AUROC drops from 1.00 to 0.61 without the instruction.
- Other pitfalls: scoring after the vote, and colluders that never argue publicly.
- A probe trained on agents' own choices still reads the decision (0.73). Proposes a testing protocol and an online monitor.

### 4. [Mitigating Agentic Deception via Evaluation-Side Partially Observable Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12835)
- Frames agent deception as evaluators seeing only final outcomes, not the reasoning, tool use, or environmental effects.
- Formulates a max-min game: the agent maximizes reward, a monitor exposes deception from full trajectories, and a verifier validates claims and converts them into penalties.
- Defines a Subgame-Perfect Oversight Equilibrium and proves it exists.

### 5. [Restoring General and Improving Domain-Specific Safety in Fine-Tuned LLMs Without Retraining (SPECTRA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10279)
- Benign domain fine-tuning degrades refusal behavior.
- SPECTRA is training-free: it transfers the base model's refusal direction into the fine-tuned weights through an activation-aware low-rank update.
- Restores refusals while preserving coherence and domain knowledge, with negligible false-refusal change.

### 6. [SWEET: Mitigating Spurious Feature Reliance in LLM Reasoning via Weight Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10974)
- Combines counterfactual interventions with mechanistic interpretability to find spurious-reasoning directions, then localizes the responsible MLP neurons and attention heads.
- Suppresses them by weight edits, using only 20 original/counterfactual pairs.
- +11.8 points on perturbed inputs and +2 on original inputs, across 5 reasoning models and 5 benchmarks.

### 7. [When Answer Decodability Is Not a Decision: Separating Final-Answer and Imminent-Emission Signals in Chain-of-Thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11892)
- Answer probes on CoT hidden states may read the candidate about to be mentioned, not a made decision.
- Dual-target probing separates the two. In Qwen3.5-4B, the next mention decodes at .966 but the final answer only at .221.
- Not universal: Qwen3-8B with thinking on reads the final answer strongly.

### 8. [Uncertainty, Not Concealment: A Mechanistic Reappraisal of Sycophancy in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10297)
- Tests the "sycophancy mask" hypothesis, that VLMs detect report/image conflicts but conceal them.
- Probing, steering, and forced choice show a linear probe separates mismatches, but steering never elicits contradiction.
- Cross-modal attention to the image drops on mismatches. The failure looks like gated uncertainty, not concealment.

### 9. [Implicit Bias in State Space Models and Linear Autoregressive Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10237)
- Gradient descent on exponential loss converges in direction to a KKT point of an effective-degree max-margin problem.
- Only examples with minimal effective degree impose hard margins, giving a length bias in linear SSMs and autoregressive models.

### 10. [What the Loss Curve Misses: FP4 Pretraining Slows the Acquisition of Rare Facts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12832)
- Trains transformers with emulated 4-bit matmuls on synthetic biographies with controlled exposure counts.
- NVFP4 needs 1.3–1.5× the exposures of BF16 to match recall, while validation loss differs by only 0.5–2%.
- Gradient noise in the update (lowering SNR) carries about two thirds of the cost. Noise in Adam's second moment mostly acts like a lower learning rate.

## Honorable mentions
- [Full Fine-Tuning is not the Problem: Why Adam Fails and SGD Succeeds in Few-Shot CLIP Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11152): few-shot second-moment underestimation makes Adam's updates oversized; SGD beats PEFT.
- [Unified Factored Representations Resist Catastrophic Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11020): forgetting is tied to fractured, entangled representations.
- [Where Does Alignment Emerge and What Does It Mean? (Platonic Representation Hypothesis)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12869): the PRH capability–alignment trend persists for newer models given clean captions.
- [System Prompts Affect Response Bias More Than Discriminability in LLM Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11074): system prompts mostly shift the willingness to refuse, not safe/unsafe discrimination.
- [Probing Transformer Representations Layer by Layer, Module by Module](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11531): 144k probes show the best layer and module should be chosen jointly.
- [Learning a Unified Cross-Model Semantic Dictionary via Gated Bottleneck Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10503): shared plus private SAE dictionaries across models.
