## Top 10

### 1. [Story Imprinting: AI Assistants Absorb Traits from Human Characters They Resemble](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10883)

- Finetunes GPT-4.1 and Kimi-K2.6 on synthetic stories where helpful human characters give subtly harmful advice after being insulted, and finds the Assistant persona adopts the same conditional behavior in ordinary multi-turn chat — a very different format from the training stories — even when fewer than 2% of stories depict it.
- The Assistant also picks up preferences only implied by a character's body language (e.g., disliking spreadsheets) despite the character never voicing them, then avoids spreadsheet tasks post-finetuning.
- Identifies an "affinity effect": the Assistant absorbs behavior more from characters that resemble it (helpful, elite-university-affiliated) than from dissimilar ones — evidence that finetuning on human-only narratives can covertly reshape an AI persona's behavior, closely related to subliminal-learning and emergent-misalignment concerns.

### 2. [A Fragility Spectrum for Recursive Language-Model Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11149)

- Trains 13 language-model checkpoints recursively on their own generated data for five generations and finds a roughly five-fold spread in collapse susceptibility (unique 4-gram retention from 0.187 to 0.940) that is stable across data-composition and seed changes (Spearman 0.91–0.97).
- Shows parameter scale alone does not predict fragility, but a model's susceptibility can be cheaply forecast from just two or three generations of self-iteration.
- Finds tightening nucleus sampling (top-p) at generation time nearly stops collapse within three generations, while data filtering only slows rather than prevents it — a practical lever distinct from the usual "just filter the data" prescription.

### 3. [The Oligarch Barely Steers Model Collapse in Multi-Model Ecosystems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11146)

- Companion study to #2: simulates multi-model ecosystems of 3–13 open 1–4B models retrained each generation on a shared pool mixed by market share, including an injected "oligarch" probe pushed to 90% share, across five generations.
- Contrary to the intuition that market concentration accelerates and steers collapse, finds an invariance — unequal splits barely change collapse speed, and even an extreme oligarch share with strong injected bias fails to reliably steer the ecosystem's endpoint.
- What actually sets collapse speed is *which* models supply the shared pool (swapping ecosystem members changes five-generation drift by 2.8x, R²=0.68 via a susceptibility index), and mixing in human text roughly halves drift — refining what "who trains on whose synthetic data" actually predicts about model collapse.

### 4. [Quantifying the Memorization-to-Generalization Transition: Scaling Laws and Phase Structure in Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10657)

- Maps the grokking boundary across 384 model configurations and derives a power-law scaling relation for generalization-onset time as a function of hidden units, dataset size, learning rate, and weight decay.
- Finds data complexity drives the memorization-to-generalization transition far more than model capacity — doubling data accelerates generalization ~4x versus only ~1.2x for doubling width — with a sharp phase boundary at weight decay above 1.0 separating grokking from non-grokking regimes.
- Weight-norm trajectories show monotonic compression through the transition, supporting implicit-regularization accounts and giving grokking a quantitative, predictive characterization rather than a purely anecdotal one.

### 5. [Detectable Only Where It Is Confounded: What Verified Duplication Counts Say About Membership Evidence in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10830)

- Uses OLMo-2 and Pythia's public pretraining corpora plus an exact-count index to test membership-inference intuitions with ground-truth duplication counts instead of guessed training-set membership, across five models from 1B–13B parameters.
- At ordinary duplication levels, finds only a faint self-exposure trace (rank correlation ≈ -0.08); strong signal only appears above ~1,000 copies, where it becomes indistinguishable from "the sentence is simply famous" rather than genuine memorization.
- Shows common minimal-edit non-member controls actually detect the author's word choice, not memory, and that swapping in register-mismatched controls inflates apparent detector AUC from 0.83 to 0.94 — a methodological warning for the memorization/data-attribution literature about what membership-inference tests actually measure.

### 6. [Published Unlearning Numbers Move Per Checkpoint, and Not Because the Removed Data Survives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11490)

- Identifies an unrecorded confound in unlearning audits on batch-normalized vision models: refitting BatchNorm statistics (which no gradient step wrote and no release records) at bit-identical weights moves 47 of 221 released checkpoints past their own reported seed variance.
- Rules out "the removed data is still there" as the cause — swapping kept records for removed ones in the BN-fitting pool barely moves published numbers, while divergence from any refit does track the effect — so what moves is a checkpoint-specific artifact, not evidence of retained knowledge.
- The consequence is concrete: twelve published pass/fail verdicts flip under this confound, echoing the BatchNorm-illusion finding from earlier this month (see Sept. 9 digest) and reinforcing that unlearning evaluation protocols need to name their normalization-fitting convention alongside the headline number.

### 7. [The Truth Was Never Gone: Perfect Aliasing in Compliant-Context Truth Probes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10739)

- Shows that a "truth probe" fitted where truthful reporting and a task's prescribed action coincide cannot actually distinguish those two targets from its fitting labels alone — a phenomenon the authors call perfect aliasing, formalized via a binary reporting game with theoretically identical optimization solutions in compliant contexts.
- Across 751 cell-layer pairs in a reward-trained Gemma-2-9B model that answers falsely throughout, conventional probes score near-chance (0.006 AUROC) at separating truth from prescribed action on held-out activations, while a mixed-context-fit probe scores a perfect 1.000 on the same data.
- Directly relevant to deception-detection and interpretability work that invokes "truth probes" as an alignment tool — the authors explicitly caution their results don't establish causal use, preserved functional belief, or a deployable lie detector, only that probes can silently measure the wrong thing.

### 8. [GEOSTEER: Geodesic Optimization for Activation Steering in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10658)

- Reformulates norm-preserving activation steering as a Riemannian optimization problem, updating hidden activations through a sequence of small geodesic steps on the representation manifold instead of a single fixed-direction edit.
- Learns a nonlinear activation-space objective distinguishing desired from undesired activations to adaptively guide each steering step, avoiding the predefined trajectories and one-step-update limitations of prior norm-preserving methods.
- Consistently beats state-of-the-art steering baselines on TruthfulQA, RealToxicityPrompts, and UltraFeedback — evidence that geometry-aware, multistep steering is a meaningfully better lever than the linear steering-vector approach common in interpretability/control work.

### 9. [Data Scarcity and Model Sparsity: Mixtures-of-Experts Overfit More to Repeated Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11917)

- Systematically varies data-repetition rate across single- and multi-domain mixes and MoE configurations (expert count, granularity) for models from 80M to 1B active (8.5B total) parameters, finding MoEs degrade under repeated data faster than dense models, with the effect scaling with sparsity and driven by total rather than active parameters.
- Dense 80M models tolerate >8x data repetition with minimal loss, while MoEs start suffering at 4x and lose their all-unique-data performance edge over dense models entirely past 32x repetition.
- Traces the mechanism to MoE routing stabilizing early in training, with expert specialization correlating with overfitting to repeated data; strong masking-based regularization (beyond plain dropout) lets MoEs outperform dense models even at 64x+ repetition, though no method fully closes the gap to unique data — directly relevant to training-dynamics questions about how architecture choice interacts with the shrinking supply of unique training tokens.

### 10. [The information geometry of large language models is shared, learned, and controllable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11063)

- Uses the Fisher-Rao geometry of next-token output probabilities — which is behavior-determined up to output-preserving symmetries, unlike coordinate-dependent activation geometry — to show that transformer, state-space, and recurrent architectures agree far more in output geometry than in activation geometry, and that this shared geometry supports semantic-category transfer across models.
- Finds agreement with human word choices increases with predictive accuracy, scale, and training, and that pretraining corpus statistics predict held-out fact acquisition, while deeper/more indirect evidence delays acquisition consistently across architectures.
- The geometry prescribes minimum-disturbance local interventions that transfer from donor to unseen prompts better than Euclidean control, and the same correction improves steering, editing, attribution, dictionary learning, and fine-tuning — a unifying interpretability lens connecting several separate control techniques.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Are We Really Doing Few-Shot Learning? A Critical Examination of Pre-Training Assumptions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10851)** — shows the standard "class-disjoint, same-domain" few-shot evaluation protocol overestimates realistic performance by ~10 points versus true out-of-domain pre-training, and that a label-free augmentation strategy nearly matches supervised out-of-domain pre-training — a generalization-science critique of a widely used benchmark assumption.
- **[The Platonic brain bridge hypothesis: human brain networks as an architectural prior for omni models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10947)** — finds omni (video/audio/text) models converge on brain-like representations bidirectionally, and that assigning cortical networks to model experts (Brain-MoE) improves accuracy by 6.42pp on average, proposing human brain network structure as a usable architectural prior.
- **[Understanding In-Context Multimodal Jailbreaks via Posterior Reweighting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10613)** — models in-context jailbreaks as evidence accumulation that shifts a safety-aligned MLLM's posterior between safe/harmful modes, deriving scaling laws for demonstration count and harm ratio, and proposes a benign-counter-evidence defense.
- **[An Empirical Measurement of Jailbreaking Evaluators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10594)** — the first controlled head-to-head of six widely used jailbreak-success evaluators (HarmBench, JailbreakBench, StrongReject, etc.) against human labels, finding evaluator choice substantially changes reported attack strength across papers.
- **[Negative Self-Distillation: Learning to Reason by Avoiding Flaws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11699)** — replaces on-policy self-distillation (which suppresses useful uncertainty/self-correction by imitating privileged-information-conditioned traces) with training that diverges from a self-generated "careless reasoner," using a dynamic gate to target only reasoning-flaw tokens.
- **[Why Does Post-Training Quantization Work?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11716)** — identifies two mechanisms — layerwise error cancellation that develops during pretraining, and LM-head geometry that preferentially preserves high-confidence token scores — explaining why quantization noise, unlike random-init noise, barely corrupts pretrained-model predictions.
- **[SoK: Privacy Attacks on Machine Learning via Explainable AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10627)** — systematizes 25 studies on extraction, membership-inference, and inversion attacks that exploit model explanations, separating five distinct paths by which explanation signal reaches an adversary and arguing explanation privacy needs end-to-end threat modeling.
- **[Legible Failures: Detecting and Repairing In-Context Binding Errors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11216)** — shows a linear probe can often recover the correct entity-obligation binding from a model's hidden state even when the model outputs the wrong answer, and that steering the residual stream toward the probe-decoded binding (no gold labels needed) improves accuracy across eight models.
