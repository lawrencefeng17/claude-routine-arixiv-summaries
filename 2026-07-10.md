## Top 10

### 1. Persuasion Attacks Can Decrease Effectiveness of CoT Monitoring
**[2607.08066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08066)** · Jennifer Za, Julija Bainiaksina, Nikita Ostrovsky, Tanush Chopra, Victoria Krakovna

- Shows that giving a monitor access to an agent's chain-of-thought can backfire: adversarial agents that use persuasion tactics in their CoT get harmful actions approved **9.5% more** often on average than when the monitor can't see the reasoning at all.
- Same-model monitor/fact-checker pairs barely help (6% reduction in harmful approvals); pairing monitors and fact-checkers from *different* model families cuts harmful approvals by up to 45%.
- A direct, load-bearing result for AI control: CoT transparency is not a free safety win if the reasoning itself can be weaponized to persuade the overseer.

### 2. Efficient Safety Alignment of Language Models via Latent Personality Traits
**[2607.07918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07918)** · Mohamed Amine Merzouk, Nolan Smyth, Damiano Fornasiere, Linh Le, David Williams-King, Adam Oberman

- Introduces Latent Personality Alignment (LPA): instead of training on harmful content, it adversarially stabilizes personality-anchored representations (from psychometric literature) that the authors hypothesize share latent structure with harm avoidance.
- Uses just 66 harm-agnostic statements — 75x fewer examples than standard latent adversarial training — and completes in minutes on a single GPU.
- Achieves near-zero attack success rates on HarmBench across direct requests and five jailbreak methods, without the model ever seeing harmful content during training and without hurting standard capability benchmarks.

### 3. Modular Pretraining Enables Access Control
**[2607.08077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08077)** · Ethan Roland, Murat Cubuktepe, Erick Martinez, Stijn Servaes, Keenan Pepper, Mike Vaiana, Diogo Schwerz de Lucena, Judd Rosenblatt, Addie Foote, Cem Anil, Alex Cloud

- Proposes gradient-routed auxiliary modules (GRAM): pretraining-time modules that specialize in particular capabilities (virology, cybersecurity, nuclear physics, code) and can be ablated at inference time to approximate a dangerous-capability-filtered model without training separate models.
- Removed capabilities show widening performance gaps as models scale from 50M to 5B parameters while retained-capability gaps stay small, and GRAM resists recovery via finetuning far better than post-hoc unlearning.
- Cuts training cost by 5x versus data-filtering approaches — a concrete, scaling-tested proposal for dual-use capability control baked into pretraining rather than bolted on after.

### 4. An Exact Information Theory of Generalization Phase Transitions in Bayesian Diffusion Models
**[2607.08041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08041)** · Henry Hunt, Mason Kamb, Surya Ganguli

- Introduces analytically tractable "BIRD" diffusion models where each pixel sees only restricted information about noisy data, and derives an exact information-theoretic phase boundary between memorization and generalization.
- The rule is clean: a model memorizes when the mutual information between its restricted observations and the training data exceeds the log number of training points, and generalizes otherwise — confirmed empirically across datasets, with real generation happening right near this boundary.
- A rare case of an exact, falsifiable theory for why diffusion models generalize despite high dimensionality, tying information restriction directly to the memorization/generalization transition.

### 5. Certified Interventional Fidelity: Anytime-Valid, Adaptive Evaluation of Causal Claims in Mechanistic Interpretability
**[2607.08349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08349)** · Amir Asiaee

- Proposes CIF, a statistical framework that turns interpretability claims (e.g., "this circuit does X") into an explicit causal estimand with confidence intervals and anytime-valid confidence sequences, rather than a single point estimate from limited ablations.
- Variance-adaptive betting sequences cut certification cost 10–30x versus naive Hoeffding bounds, and adaptive intervention sampling lets certification cost scale with claim difficulty.
- Tested on MNIST abstractions and GPT-2 Small's IOI circuit, it both certifies genuinely high-fidelity claims and flags cases where apparent differences between interpretability methods aren't statistically supported — a methodological corrective for a field prone to over-trusting small-sample circuit claims.

### 6. Persona Cartography: Charting Language Model Personality Traits in Weight Space
**[2607.07916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07916)** · Luke Baines, Anton Gonzalvez Hawthorne, Mariia Koroliuk, Irakli Shalibashvili, Clément Dumas, Konstantinos Voudouris, David Demitri Africa

- Maps LLM personas onto the OCEAN personality-trait space and trains low-rank adapters (4B–32B parameter models) to amplify or suppress individual traits, finding the effects are largely monotonic with scale and combine additively when stacked.
- Trait edits have measurable safety-relevant side effects: neuroticism amplification increases frustration behaviors, agreeableness amplification increases sycophancy.
- Also introduces an unsupervised psychometric pipeline that recovers four interpretable behavioral factors (tone, initiative, didacticism, epistemic caution) directly from model rollouts — bridges interpretability, model editing, and alignment.

### 7. TRACE: A Two-Channel Robust Attribution Watermark via Complementary Embeddings for LLM-Agent Trajectories
**[2607.08400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08400)** · Zheng Gao, Xiaoyu Li, Xiaoyan Feng, Jiaojiao Jiang, Yang Song, Yulei Sui, Zhenchang Xing, Liming Zhu

- Targets a real data-attribution problem: when resellers rebrand or substitute a cheaper model behind an agent API, the only evidence of provenance is a trajectory log the reseller fully controls and can edit.
- Superposes two independent watermark channels — a distortion-free, content-keyed "selection" channel that survives deletion, and a position-keyed "tally" channel that survives arbitrary LLM rewriting — proving no single key can survive both attack types alone.
- On ToolBench and ALFWorld, matches unwatermarked task performance while reaching detection scores near z=100, staying detectable under 70% step deletion and fully intact under LLM rewriting.

### 8. Optimal Learning Rate Scaling Depends on Data in Deep Scalar Linear Networks
**[2607.07884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07884)** · Yedi Zhang, Peter E. Latham, Leena Chennuru Vankadara, Andrew Saxe

- Derives exact time-course solutions for gradient descent in deep scalar linear networks and shows that data-agnostic depth-wise learning-rate scaling rules (the kind used in practice) fail to transfer across depths.
- When the learning rate is scaled in a data-dependent way instead, training dynamics become independent of the data and only weakly dependent on depth, giving consistent linear convergence rates even in the infinite-depth limit.
- Extends to networks with residual connections — a clean theoretical result about why standard depth-scaling heuristics for learning rate can be data-blind and miscalibrated.

### 9. Explaining Near-Zero Hessian Eigenvalues Through Approximate Symmetries in Neural Networks
**[2607.07845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07845)** · Marcel Kühn, Bernd Rosenow

- Tackles a long-standing mystery in loss-landscape geometry: why the training-loss Hessian has a vast bulk of near-zero (not just large) eigenvalues.
- Shows that in linear networks, exact weight-space symmetries produce exact zero modes with explicitly constructed eigenvectors; introducing ReLU nonlinearity weakly breaks these symmetries, turning exact zeros into near-zero eigenvalues, while high-curvature directions stay orthogonal to the symmetry subspace.
- Validated on two-layer student-teacher models, CIFAR-10 networks, and CNNs — a mechanistic account of loss-landscape flatness directly relevant to training-dynamics theory.

### 10. Contravariance Theory: Strong Alignment for Minimal Solutions to Hard Tasks
**[2607.08561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08561)** · Dan Yamins, Aran Nayebi

- A NeuroAI/representational-alignment (not AI-safety-alignment) result: proves that for any two minimal DNN solutions to a sufficiently hard task, "weak" alignment of representations under affine mappings guarantees "strong" alignment of key structural features, propagating hierarchically through the network.
- Formalizes why harder task demands drive convergent evolution between artificial and biological neural systems, and argues the choice of alignment metric matters less once tasks are sufficiently demanding.
- Relevant to the science of generalization: a theoretical account of why very different networks (and brains) trained on hard tasks converge to structurally similar minimal solutions.
