Focused on: AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization, data attribution, emergent misalignment, subliminal learning.

## Top 10

### 1. Emergent Misalignment Recruits a Pre-existing Persona Subspace
**[2607.21356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21356)** · Mohammed Suhail B Nadaf

- Studies why fine-tuning Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct on a narrow stream of bad advice makes it broadly misaligned on unrelated questions ("emergent misalignment"), and finds the mechanism: fine-tuning activates a pre-existing, shared low-rank persona subspace already present in the base model.
- Projecting that subspace out of the residual stream throughout fine-tuning prevents broad misalignment almost entirely (27.7% → 0.0% of judged generations), while injecting the same structure into an untouched model induces misalignment proportional to dose.
- The same intervention that blocks misalignment also erases the narrowly trained behavior — suggesting the "bad advice" skill and the broad misalignment are two faces of the same underlying persona direction, not separable effects.

### 2. Preference Tuning as Spectral Update Reorganization
**[2607.20438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20438)** · Peiyan Zhang, Haibo Jin, Liying Kang, Haohan Wang

- Decomposes the parameter updates induced by RLHF/preference optimization into spectral components and finds a consistent "head-tail" structure across model families and algorithms: a compact head that emerges early and drives the visible behavior change, plus a heterogeneous residual tail.
- Plug-in intervention shows the head alone accounts for endpoint behavior but is not sufficient for full generalization, especially out-of-distribution — the tail is needed even though it looks inert in isolation.
- Reframes alignment training as structured update reorganization rather than a monolithic correction, tying alignment gains and coverage gaps to how the learned update is internally organized.

### 3. Weight-norm Criticality: A Mechanism for Loss Spikes Induced by the Normalization and Weight Decay
**[2607.21005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21005)** · Xiaolong Li, Zhangchen Zhou, Zhi-Qin John Xu

- Identifies a training-instability mechanism distinct from the well-known Edge-of-Stability: the interaction between normalization layers (scale-invariant weights) and weight decay, which relentlessly shrinks those weights' norms.
- As weight decay grows, scale-invariant weight norms are driven toward a critical boundary; crossing it sharply increases loss-landscape curvature and triggers abrupt loss spikes.
- Explains empirically why weight decay helps generalization but can't be cranked up indefinitely — a concrete, testable mechanistic account of a common training pathology.

### 4. The Active Ingredient in Muon's Grokking
**[2607.20512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20512)** · Yufeng Wang

- Isolates why the Muon optimizer reaches the grokking threshold faster than AdamW on modular arithmetic, via multi-seed/multi-LR ablations: the speedup comes specifically from orthogonalization (Newton-Schulz iteration), not the spectral-norm constraint.
- Orthogonalizing optimizers converge to lower-norm solutions at ~3x lower spectral norm; cutting Newton-Schulz iterations from five to one speeds up grokking but makes the solution fragile and prone to transient collapse, especially at higher learning rates.
- Methodological point that generalizes beyond Muon: "faster grokking" claims can invert depending on whether you measure first-crossing or sustained-grok time — a caution for the optimizer/grokking literature.

### 5. Single-Token SAE Features: Causal Necessity and Layer-Depth Effects
**[2607.20596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20596)** · Seonglae Cho, Zekun Wu, Kleyton Da Costa, Rishi Kalra, Ilham Wicaksono, Adriano Koshiyama

- Tests whether a sparse-autoencoder feature's causal role is stable across SAE training methods, using single-token features (activating on one vocabulary item) as a clean ground-truth case, across 3.9M features, six models, and three SAE families.
- Zero-ablating these features produces significant logit changes in the large majority of full-layer conditions, but causal behavior diverges sharply across SAE families on the same base model — GemmaScope/BatchTopK features stay causally anchored while LlamaScope features are often locally redundant.
- Bottom line for interpretability practice: cross-paper claims built on different SAEs may not be comparable, since causal robustness tracks training recipe more than architecture or scale.

### 6. Training Large Language Models for Self-Explanation Faithfulness
**[2607.21090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21090)** · Yeoktatt Cheah, María Pérez-Ortiz, Noah Y. Siegel, Oana-Maria Camburu

- Directly optimizes LLMs to produce faithful self-explanations — reasoning that actually reflects the factors driving the model's answer — by turning an existing faithfulness metric (Phi-CCT) into an RL reward, rather than just evaluating or prompting for faithfulness after the fact.
- RL fine-tuned Llama3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B show large faithfulness gains in-distribution (near-zero → 0.664) and generalize to held-out tasks like StrategyQA (up to 0.691), with some inconsistent cross-intervention transfer between random-word and user-bias perturbation types.
- The authors specifically checked for reward gaming and found the improvement isn't just an artifact — a rare example of directly training for a core alignment-relevant property rather than merely measuring it.

### 7. The Dark Room in the Reward Channel: Dense Prediction Rewards Collapse GRPO-Trained LLM Agents
**[2607.21273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21273)** · Yu Wang

- Shows that adding dense, well-intentioned per-step reward (predict-your-next-observation) to sparse-reward LLM agents under GRPO doesn't just fail to help — it collapses the policy into a degenerate "dark room" state (perfect prediction accuracy, zero task success) across Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B on ALFWorld.
- Localizes the cause to GRPO's std (z-score) normalization: in all-fail groups the normalized advantage becomes invariant to the reward-shaping coefficient, so bounded auxiliary rewards turn into unbounded optimization pressure that annealing can't fix.
- Proposes a general "variance-profile" criterion predicting which dense signals are safe to add to GRPO training — directly relevant to reward hacking / reward-channel design in RL post-training.

### 8. How Many Bits Can an Adapter Write? Measuring the Capacity and Memorization of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
**[2607.21351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21351)** · Kaizhen Tan, Heqing Du, Yang Feng

- Extends compression-based memorization analysis to LoRA adapters, directly measuring in bits how much training data a frozen-base adapter actually encodes, rather than assuming parameter count is the right proxy.
- Finds capacity depends heavily on *where* parameters sit (MLP holds ~2x more than attention for the same budget) and collapses if the frozen base's structure is stripped away; applied to Qwen2.5 fine-tunes, measured bits-written predicts privacy leakage better than nominal parameter count.
- Cleanly separates supervised fine-tuning (which verbatim-copies secrets into the adapter) from RL-on-verifiable-rewards (which doesn't record them) — a concrete, measurable framework for reasoning about what fine-tuning "writes," relevant to data attribution and memorization research.

### 9. Gradient Concentration, Not Weight Saliency, Explains Representation-Level Class Unlearning
**[2607.21353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21353)** · Billel Habbati, Alessio Merlo, Luca Verderame, Meriem Guerar

- Runs a controlled ablation of the saliency-based weight-masking mechanism (SalUn) widely used in machine unlearning, comparing it against random masks of equal sparsity under matched compute on CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-18.
- Finds saliency masks, random masks, and unconstrained updates are statistically equivalent at representation-level forgetting — because forget gradients are already concentrated in the last layers (~92% of squared gradient energy) before any mask is applied, so all strategies operate in the same subspace regardless of which weights they nominally target.
- Challenges a common unlearning-method design assumption: representation-level forgetting is governed by gradient geometry, not by cleverly selecting "important" weights — suggests the field should target latent representations directly instead.

### 10. Token Budget Saturation and Mechanistic Early Detection of Reasoning Non-Convergence in Chain-of-Thought Models
**[2607.21433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21433)** · Renuka Oladri, Niveda Jawahar, Abdirisak Mohamed

- Characterizes a bimodal convergence pattern in DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B: chains either terminate within budget and are highly accurate (90.3% on AIME) or exhaust their token budget without concluding and are near-random (6.6%), with 62% converging overall.
- Trains linear probes on hidden-state activations at early token positions (50–300) and finds layer-20 activations at token 150 predict eventual convergence above chance (AUC 0.608), beating behavioral baselines like entropy/repetition statistics — though the effect is modest and not fully significant at their sample size.
- A concrete mechanistic-interpretability angle on CoT training dynamics, pointing toward early-exit inference and adaptive compute allocation informed by internal representations rather than surface text.
