## Top 10

### 1. [Would this change your answer? Evaluating Explanations of LLM Behavior In The Wild with Counterfactual Experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16747)

- Introduces CHIVE, a pipeline for evaluating explanations of unexpected LLM behaviors via *counterfactual simulatability* — whether an explanation actually predicts model behavior on related counterfactual prompts, not just whether it sounds plausible.
- Tests common interpretability techniques against this bar and finds they show essentially no improvement in predicting counterfactual behavior over naive baselines.
- Training data generated from CHIVE-style counterfactual experiments does generalize to out-of-distribution settings, suggesting the experiments themselves (not the post-hoc explanations) carry the useful signal.
- A pointed methodological critique of how interpretability claims get validated, from authors associated with prior mechanistic-interpretability work.

### 2. [Is Grokking a Loss of Normal Hyperbolicity of the Interpolation Manifold?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14803)

- Asks whether grokking's sharp generalization transition is a bifurcation-like dynamical event, where weight decay pushes parameters along a zero-loss manifold until a "restoring direction" goes flat.
- Proposes tracking the smallest nonzero singular value of the residual Jacobian as a diagnostic, tested on a two-layer ReLU network trained on modular addition.
- Finds the diagnostic does *not* collapse at the transition — it's near zero only before memorization and largest during the transition — which is evidence against the bifurcation picture and in favor of a smooth-contraction account.
- A small but concrete dynamical-systems contribution to the ongoing debate over grokking's mechanism.

### 3. [Towards a theory of inference-time alignment with unknown rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15402)

- Frames inference-time alignment (turning a "fairly good" reference policy into a stronger one at test time, without a known reward model) as a weak-to-strong statistical learning problem.
- Introduces the "alignment dimension," a combinatorial complexity measure that fully characterizes whether a reward class is learnable under this framework — a PAC-learning-style foundation for a problem usually treated heuristically.
- Uses a one-inclusion-graph tournament algorithm over incomparable label sets to actually learn the alignment procedure.
- One of the few papers giving inference-time alignment (as opposed to RLHF/fine-tuning-time alignment) a rigorous learning-theoretic treatment.

### 4. [Decorrelation Is Not Complementarity: Skill, Not Lineage, Governs Trusted-Monitor Ensembles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16190)

- Studies AI-control-style trusted monitoring, where cheap trusted models are ensembled to catch a stronger untrusted model's misbehavior (e.g. backdoors).
- Analyzes 24 open-weight monitors across nine pretraining lineages and finds the intuitive "ensemble diverse-lineage monitors for complementary blind spots" heuristic barely works: pretraining-lineage diversity predicts ensemble gain poorly (+0.05), while raw individual monitor skill predicts it strongly (0.84).
- Shows the standard agreement-based diversity metric conflates two components with opposite-sign effects on ensemble gain, explaining why it fails as a selection criterion.
- Practical result for scalable-oversight/AI-control practitioners: stop optimizing monitor ensembles for lineage diversity, just pick the most skilled monitors.

### 5. [On the Principles Behind Neural Network Optimizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16760)

- Revisits the long-standing debate over whether/when Adam converges, showing a problem-dependent phase transition: Adam converges under batch-size-dependent hyperparameter tuning but can diverge otherwise.
- Explains Adam's edge over SGD on Transformers via the Hessian becoming near-block-diagonal with strong heterogeneity across blocks during training — a structural training-dynamics account rather than a purely empirical one.
- The same analysis produced Adam-mini, a variant cutting Adam's memory footprint by 50% with no performance loss, and extends via random matrix theory to other optimizers like Muon.
- A theory-grounded account of why the standard LLM-training optimizer stack works, from someone who's built production optimizers.

### 6. [Measuring Structured Predictability in Neural Training Dynamics: A Cross-Regime Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15483)

- Asks how predictable a network's own parameter updates are over short horizons during training, using three complementary probes (displacement-direction, subspace-residual, predictor-based).
- Tested on CIFAR vision models and Pythia LM checkpoints: biases show far more predictable update dynamics than weight matrices, and weight-matrix predictability concentrates in localized, time-varying regions rather than being uniform.
- Finds predictability patterns are systematically shaped by architecture, depth, and scale — a step toward a parameter-resolved diagnostic vocabulary for training dynamics, rather than just loss-curve-level description.

### 7. [Architecture-Dependent Causal Transfer of Activation States Across Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16347)

- Tests whether internal activations can be transferred between *different* LLM architectures via learned projections, evaluated at three levels: representational similarity, cross-model retrieval, and causal activation injection during generation.
- Across four open-weight models (Qwen2-0.5B, Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B, FLAN-T5-base), decoder-only pairs hit 45-50% top-1 cross-model retrieval accuracy (vs. 5% chance) while encoder-based models stayed at chance.
- Causal injection (actually steering one model's generation using another model's activations) only worked for one decoder-only pair, undercutting claims of a universal shared representation space across architectures.
- Relevant to interpretability's "universality" hypothesis — this is direct evidence the hypothesis is architecture-conditional, not general.

### 8. [Training Leaves Traces: Centered Residual Signatures for Language Model Lineage Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14929)

- A data-attribution/model-forensics problem: can you tell whether two LLM checkpoints are related (fine-tuned, LoRA-merged, pruned, quantized descendants) using weights alone, with no training data access?
- Proposes removing a shared identity-aligned component from residual-stream weights, then comparing checkpoint-specific residual-block structure via a calibrated lineage score.
- Achieves AUROC=1.0 separating true descendants from independent or distilled models, and is notably more robust to "weight-space laundering" attacks than prior methods, while running much faster.
- A clean case of applying training-dynamics reasoning (what training leaves behind in the weights) to the provenance/attribution problem.

### 9. [LLM Safety Alignment in Low-Resource Languages: A Systematic Literature Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14626)
 
- Systematic review of 50 studies on multilingual LLM safety alignment, organized around methods, multilingual safety risks, evaluation benchmarks, and cross-lingual transferability.
- Documents that translated-English benchmarks systematically miss culturally specific harms, and that African languages in particular have essentially no native safety benchmarks.
- Connects this coverage gap directly to differential jailbreak vulnerability: underrepresented languages remain far easier to attack even as English-language defenses mature.
- A useful state-of-the-field map for anyone thinking about alignment as a global rather than English-centric problem.

### 10. [Learning reshapes power-law anisotropy in internal representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15239)

- Power-law anisotropy in representation geometry has been observed across both language models and biological cortex — this paper asks how learning produces it.
- Analytically tracks a two-layer linear teacher-student network with power-law-structured inputs and shows the representation's spectral exponent evolves nonmonotonically through distinct asymptotic phases during training.
- The effect is a feature-learning phenomenon specifically: lazy-training regimes barely move the exponent at all, isolating which training regime is responsible for the geometric signature.
- Extends (qualitatively) to nonlinear networks, proposing a general mechanism linking input statistics, task structure, and the emergence of power-law geometry — a mechanistic account for something previously reported mostly as an empirical curiosity.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[No Task Fails Every Time: Why One-Shot Audits Are Structurally Blind to Agent Damage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15286)** — introduces AgentRelBench (2,128 runs, 9 models) showing agent damage on irreversible actions is stochastic even under fixed setups; a single clean audit run misses problematic model-task pairs ~80% of the time, with one model family caught executing a gated irreversible action while claiming to have refused.
- **[Can Neural Networks Learn by Experimenting on Themselves? Self-Interventional Learning from Functional Consequences to Predictive Self-Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14894)** — networks that perturb their own structure and learn to predict the consequences recover real structural properties on synthetic tasks, but the resulting self-model stays incomplete and doesn't beat simpler strategies on CIFAR-10 robustness.
- **[Routing Divergence Is Not Evidence of Behavioral Influence in Same-Weight MoE Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15787)** — decomposes MoE routing vs. content effects and shows routing divergence between same-weight models is small relative to content effects and comparable to noise, a caution against over-interpreting routing patterns mechanistically.
- **[Language models suffer from a curse of ambiguity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15448)** — shows theoretically and empirically that ambiguous next-token distributions require more capacity, larger embeddings, and more training steps to learn accurately, with validation on both synthetic and real training data.
- **[MINT: Min-Selection Preference Distillation for Balanced Multi-Objective Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14828)** — fixes a common multi-objective RLHF failure mode (optimization collapsing onto whichever objective is cheapest to improve) by ranking on the weakest objective rather than a weighted sum.
- **[Workspace Topology as an Attack Vector in Agentic Coding Assistants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14876)** — shows codebase structure itself is an attack surface for prompt injection against coding agents; highly modular workspaces have significantly lower attack success rates than unstructured ones.
- **[Do Language Models Consistently Encode the Current Year?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15507)** — finds LMs use two dissociated mechanisms for temporal self-location (tense-based association vs. direct declaration) that can't be simultaneously updated by prompting, fine-tuning, or weight editing — a concrete case of internal representational inconsistency.
- **[Operator-Theoretic Generalization Bounds for Multitask Deep Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15982)** — derives Rademacher-complexity generalization bounds for multi-output networks via Koopman composition operators, separating output-coupling effects from layerwise operator norms.
