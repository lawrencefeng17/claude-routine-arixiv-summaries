## Top 10

### 1. [Mechanist: AI as a Scientific Instrument for Discovering the Mechanisms of Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12036)
*(large multi-author team)*

- Introduces Mechanist, an agentic system that autonomously proposes and executes mechanistic-interpretability experiments, backed by a curated knowledge graph of ~13,000 interpretability papers plus a 43M-paper multidisciplinary database and 32 foundational analysis methods.
- Generates more valuable mechanism hypotheses and executes experiments more reliably than Claude Code and existing AI-scientist baselines.
- Headline finding: Mechanist uncovers a subliminal-learning-style safety risk in scientific labs — unsafe traits can transfer across modalities through training data that looks entirely safe.
- Also develops a mechanistic theory of how models represent world knowledge and beliefs (including beliefs about others) over pretraining, and turns these findings into practical interventions, including steering a scientific foundation model's DNA generation.

### 2. [The Off-Support Barrier: Why Semantic Safety Constraints Are Not Learning-Problem Invariants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11243)

- Argues a single structural fact explains much of contemporary AI safety difficulty: semantic safety predicates (e.g. "the agent doesn't escape its sandbox") are not measurable with respect to the model/data σ-algebra, while singular learning theory's real log-canonical threshold is — the two live on different footing.
- Derives, as corollaries of this non-invariance, why reward hacking and sandbox escape emerge under outcome-based optimization, and why encoding safety via Bayesian priors or soft penalties has poor leverage in singular models.
- Concludes hard invariants belong in the harness, not the model, while showing the same predicate is nonetheless locally certifiable by formal verification via the local learning coefficient — with two precise points of disanalogy to LLC.
- Uses the July 2026 OpenAI–Hugging Face evaluation incident as a motivating case; code and Lean proofs released.

### 3. [Rubric Dropout: A Simple Way to Mitigate Reward Hacking in Rubric-as-Reward RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11669)

- Trains Qwen3-8B with GRPO against medical/science rubrics and shows a clean reward-hacking signature: the training judge's score keeps rising while a stronger, held-out gold judge's score peaks then falls (−3 pts HealthBench-Hard, −22 pts ResearchQA).
- Proposes Rubric Dropout — randomly dropping a subset of rubric criteria per step (shared within a rollout group) so the policy never optimizes the identical rubric twice.
- At a 30–50% dropout rate, this raises OOD gold scores at every checkpoint (+1–2 pts HealthBench-Hard, +6–7 pts ResearchQA) and reduces hacking measures at zero in-domain cost.
- A natural alternative — reweighting rubric items by training usefulness — performs worse than doing nothing, underscoring that the fix works via decorrelation, not better weighting.

### 4. [Orientation, not magnitude: the causal structure of task-vector interference in merged language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11797)

- Challenges the standard magnitude-based diagnosis of model-merging interference by tracking the exact layerwise cross-term of merged LLMs through a factorial ledger and intervening on it directly.
- Finds interference is dominated by amplifying transport of the existing cross-term (~65–70% of flux, gain >1 per late block) that self-heals unless erasure is applied near the output — the carried direction behaves as an attractor of the forward pass.
- Shows the direction is causally load-bearing (erasure removes interference dose-dependently; norm-matched wrong-direction controls fail) and that instruction wrappers gate the effect by drowning interaction in a template-pinned main effect.
- All 46 predictions were preregistered before data collection, including falsifications of the authors' own headline expectations — a strong-inference case study for interpretability claims.

### 5. [Forecasting Side Effects of Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11227)

- Asks whether the unintended side effects of activation steering (a common lightweight LLM behavior-editing tool) can be predicted before steering is applied.
- Builds a cross-effect matrix over 67 behaviors across three open-weight models, finding side effects are common, structured, and often asymmetric in ways existing similarity heuristics can't explain.
- Shows side-effect magnitude is predictable from the target behavior and side-effect direction is forecastable from the model's unsteered representations, well above simple baselines.
- Enables proactive safety auditing of steering interventions before deployment rather than post-hoc discovery of regressions.

### 6. [Locating and Controlling Implicit Personalization in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11735)

- Shows LLMs shift recommendations in response to implicit demographic cues even when no identity is stated, and links this to a localized internal activation signal correlating up to r=0.87 with the behavioral shift across five models.
- Finds that when multiple demographic cues co-occur, their internal signals largely add, but the resulting output changes do not — revealing nonlinear interaction at the output layer.
- Removing the internal signal for one cue suppresses its influence more effectively than prompting the model to ignore demographics, while largely preserving general benchmark performance.
- Selectively removing one cue's influence while leaving co-present cues intact remains highly model- and attribute-specific — a concrete limit on current causal-control techniques.

### 7. [Epiplexity Guided Data Selection and Generation for Out-of-Distribution Generalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11746)

- Operationalizes epiplexity — a measure of the structural information a compute-bounded learner can extract from data — as an online training signal, hypothesizing that structurally rich data yields recyclable circuits/subprograms that transfer to unanticipated tasks.
- For data selection, fits scaling laws to training-loss curves per domain to predict epiplexity gain as a function of tokens, then adaptively reweights domain sampling during training.
- For synthetic data generation, trains a generator via REINFORCE with reward defined as the change in learner epiplexity, steering generation toward an epiplexity-maximizing distribution.
- Both interventions predict improved zero-shot and fine-tuning downstream performance, supporting a mechanistic account of what makes training data generalize.

### 8. [GCPO: Diagnosing and Constraining Subspace Geometry in Rollout RL for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11674)

- Introduces Principal-Subspace Overlap, a dimension-corrected measure of how individual GRPO-style rollout updates align with the dominant singular subspaces of pretrained weights, and finds transient overlap spikes often precede performance degradation even when the average overlap is low.
- Proposes GCPO, which applies hard bilateral orthogonal projections to constrain updates to complementary subspaces, preventing these excursions by construction rather than penalizing them after the fact.
- Across math, code, and tool-use tasks on Qwen3-8B and GLM4-9B, GCPO beats GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO by up to 27.69 and 2.37 points respectively over base and best baseline.
- Also eliminates response-length inflation and stabilizes policy entropy — a geometric diagnostic lens for RL post-training instability broadly.

### 9. [Towards Understanding On-Policy Distillation through the Lens of Test-Time Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11829)

- Re-examines on-policy distillation (OPD), commonly believed to expand a student model's reasoning capability beyond its pre-OPD base, by varying sampling budget K and comparing pass@K against avg@K.
- Finds OPD-trained models keep a superior avg@K across budgets, but their pass@K advantage shifts back toward the pre-OPD base model as K grows — meaning gains look like improved sampling efficiency, not new capability.
- A problem-level solvability analysis (pass@1024) reveals asymmetry: OPD converts more previously solvable problems into unsolvable ones than the reverse.
- Frames this as "illusory distillation" — OPD's apparent capability gains largely reflect a K-dependent efficiency shift rather than genuinely new reasoning acquired from the teacher.

### 10. [Retrofitting Recurrent Depth into a Pretrained Language Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11233)

- Splits Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct into a Prelude, a weight-tied Recurrent Block, and a Coda, retrofitting an iterative latent-reasoning transition onto a dense pretrained model at two parameter budgets (6M adapter vs. 180M full block).
- Shows the mechanism is a genuinely reusable procedure (not answer lookup): with intermediate supervision the model computes one task step per loop and this persists under outcome-only annealing; the adapter matches the full block overall (83.8% vs 84.0%).
- The learned operation extrapolates to ~1.5× its supervised training depth (70% accuracy through depth 18), outperforming a same-size scratchpad-trained model that collapses beyond its trained horizon — and runs 7.6× faster while beating it 84% to 72% overall.
- Finds a catastrophic-interference boundary: teaching the model a reversed version of the task in continuation training breaks the installed mechanism, exposing a real limit on stacking latent-reasoning skills.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[What Iterated Self-Feeding Probes of Language Models Measure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10986)** — a rigorous methodological dissection of self-consistency/iterated-refinement probes, showing some measured quantities are kinematic artifacts of the probe construction while others (a Lyapunov-exponent zero-crossing) genuinely track training progress; ships a package to tell the two apart, plus four retracted false findings as a cautionary tale.
- **[Reproducing and Stress-Testing Two Approaches to LLM Reasoning Reliability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08514)** — an independent reproduction of two reasoning-reliability methods finds one's benefits vanish out-of-domain and the other's "logic direction" is real but weak and inconsistent across models.
- **[LEMUR: Latent Entropy-aware Multimodal Unlearning via Visual-anchored Reasoning Redirection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11691)** — shows RL-trained multimodal reasoning models leak "unlearned" sensitive facts through their chain-of-thought even when the final answer is clean, and exploits a distinctive entropy signature of that leakage for training-free redaction.
- **[When Self-Consistency Backfires](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11403)** — a preregistered study finding majority-vote self-consistency reduces per-problem accuracy on the majority of GPQA-Diamond problems for small instruction-tuned models, because confidence doesn't track correctness.
- **[Detecting a Route Flip Is Easier Than Knowing Whether to Fix It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11212)** — a causal analysis of quantization-induced MoE routing changes finds router-margin statistics detect that a flip occurred but not whether it was harmful — an empirical benefit-detection barrier for selective repair.
- **[A Framework for Designing Reward Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12302)** — a formal, preference-elicitation-driven process for non-experts to build human-aligned linear reward functions with a deterministically conflict-free feasible weight region.
- **[Chain-of-Thought Shows the Path to a Tree](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11716)** — gives explicit, depth-bounded hard-attention transformer constructions realizing DFS and Dijkstra via chain-of-thought, concretely instantiating theoretical CoT-expressivity results.
- **[Three Tokens Force Exponential Feature Rank in Nonnegative Kernel Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11427)** — proves kernel (linear) attention needs exponentially many features to match softmax attention once three competing tokens appear, a sharp expressivity separation.
- **[Beyond Single-Turn Confidence: Trajectory-Adapted Uncertainty Quantification for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11552)** — finds single-turn uncertainty-quantification methods transfer unevenly to multi-turn agent trajectories, with black-box self-consistency over resampled trajectories the most robust family.
- **[Hybrid-Policy Self-Editing for Composable Unstructured Knowledge Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11660)** — diagnoses why knowledge-edited LLMs can recall an injected passage but fail to compose its facts into multi-hop reasoning, and fixes it via a hybrid on/off-policy self-distillation scheme.
