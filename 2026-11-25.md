# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-25

Listing: **Wed, 25 Nov 2026** (144 new submissions + 89 cross-lists; replacements not considered). Summaries are based on the abstracts. Highlights: subliminal learning shown to cross from text into image generation (with a "weight lens" that reads the hidden trait off the weights), a network-neuroscience signature of sandbagging that also helps elicit hidden capability, and a striking result that RL post-training can be reproduced by plain maximum likelihood on its own archived rollouts. Two papers point the same way: most adaptation amounts to an input-independent constant shift of the residual stream.

## Top 10

### 1. [Subliminal Learning Crosses Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31870)
- A preference (e.g., a favorite animal) distilled subliminally through number sequences into a VLM student **carries over to images**: a diffusion model that uses the student as its text encoder generates the trait animal more often.
- Only some concepts transfer. The animal (which depends on the whole prompt) shifts, but color or style (encoded in a single token) does not.
- Mechanism: a **constant component** of the student's weight change explains much of the representation shift. Adding it uniformly is enough to change the generated animal.
- Introduces **"weight lens"**, which identifies the subliminal trait from a single trained model's weights for nearly every student, including traits that never show up in images.

### 2. [Eliciting Sandbagged LLM Capability via Structural Interventions on Attention-Head Functional Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31615)
- Builds network-neuroscience-style functional graphs from attention-head activations and compares honest and sandbagging runs with the weights held fixed.
- A consistent signature across architectures and community-detection methods: sandbagging **reduces within-community functional connectivity**.
- Ablating the heads whose connectivity changes most **recovers suppressed performance** while largely preserving honest-mode accuracy. These signals also improve existing inference-level and weight-level elicitation methods.

### 3. [Reinforcement Learning Can Be Recovered From Its Own Rollouts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33102)
- Replaying the RL algorithm on archived rollouts collapses catastrophically. Plain **maximum likelihood on the archived rollouts, in generation order, with no correct/incorrect distinction** recovers online-RL performance.
- One MLE step per rollout batch tracks the whole RL learning curve. MLE on just the last few steps' rollouts often recovers the final model.
- Takeaway: much of RL's compute goes into *discovering* a better sampling distribution, not learning from it. This matters for training dynamics, distillation, and data attribution of RL gains.

### 4. [On Potentiality and Actuality of Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32991)
- Splits the updates from fine-tuning, LoRA, etc. into an input-independent shared component and an input-dependent residual.
- The shared part accounts for most of the gain. A **"shift adapter" with one constant vector per layer** reproduces LoRA and full fine-tuning.
- They frame this as a "potentiality principle": the base model already contains the capability, and adaptation just actualizes it. It has implications for steering and cross-model convergence, and explains "neural thickets."
- Fits with the constant-component findings in #1 and in the "Bias in Disguise" paper below.

### 5. [Steerable by Design: Conditional Pretraining for Robust Control of Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32199)
- Separates the control and data channels **from pretraining onward**. An encoder over semantic features of the upcoming text feeds the LM via cross-attention, and the token stream can't write back into the control channel.
- **Mancelle-3B** steers more strongly than prompt- or activation-steering baselines while staying fluent, and it **resists adversarial black-box and soft-prompt counter-steering** that defeats those baselines. Reproduced at 400M against a tighter control.
- A pretraining-level alternative to alignment applied in the last 1% of training, targeting jailbreaks and in-context drift.

### 6. [Understanding the Importance of Massive Activations through Adversarial Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32585)
- Through the spline lens, massive activations **push representations away from nonlinearity boundaries** (the "region migration" behind delayed robustness), which simplifies the local input-output map.
- The phenomenon isn't transformer-specific. It shows up in residual MLPs, ResNet18 and ViTs on simple supervised tasks.
- In LLM pretraining, the rise of massive activations **tracks adversarial accuracy** under embedding perturbations. This is a training-dynamics account of a well-known interpretability oddity.

### 7. [Reward Basis Decomposition for Interpreting Preference Heterogeneity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31640)
- Splits per-user reward weights into a **shared population direction plus per-user directions**, then interprets each with concept alignment and SAEs.
- It recovers planted per-user directions in a synthetic control. On PRISM and Community Alignment, however, per-user terms add **no reliable predictive benefit**: a single reward direction explains most preferences.
- This is a useful negative result for pluralistic alignment, and an interpretability tool for reward models.

### 8. [Scaling Laws of Ensemble Uncertainty in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32801)
- Bayesian deep-learning methods confined to one model family miss **capacity error**, which follows the usual loss-vs-size power law.
- A second power law describes the shrinking disagreement within ensembles. It decays more slowly, so ensembles capture a growing share of epistemic uncertainty at scale.
- A third power law describes the loss of diversity as members share more training before branching. This limits LoRA ensembles and Laplace-style methods.

### 9. [Rethinking Reasoning Effort Training: On-Policy Distillation or RL?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32131)
- Length-penalized RL compresses a single policy well (−70% thinking on AIME25 with no accuracy loss) but **collapses effort tiers** when they're trained jointly. Multi-teacher OPD keeps the tiers separate.
- Traced to the signal: a sequence-level reward can't distinguish tier-specific from shared behavior, while OPD gives dense per-state targets, including when to stop. One token-level KL anchor on the high tier fixes RL.
- The generalization caveat is notable: dense-target tier separation **doesn't transfer to unseen agentic tasks**, while compression from sequence-level reward does.

### 10. [Chain-of-Thought Is Not Explainability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31271) *(position paper)*
- Synthesizes the evidence that CoTs are frequently unfaithful to the computation that actually drives predictions.
- An audit of 1,000 recent CoT papers finds **~21.7% treat CoT as load-bearing interpretability**, including in high-stakes domains.
- Proposals: don't treat CoT as sufficient, verify faithfulness before downstream use, and report those checks, while still using CoT for monitoring and communication.

## Honorable Mentions

**Safety, oversight & interpretability**
- [Calibrated Reading of Latent Reasoning and Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32143): A compact reader for latent-reasoning (CODI) states and KV messages, with conformal guarantees. It matches a 1.7B LatentQA verbalizer at ~500× fewer FLOPs and catches 93% of message substitutions at 5% FPR.
- [A Bias in Disguise: Tiny Gradient Edits Stop a Small LM's Thinking Loops](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33133): Fine-tuning only the 140 to 140k highest-gradient weights fixes Qwen3.5-0.8B's non-terminating reasoning. The edit is effectively **three constant residual-stream vectors**, and it complements a frontier model's gradient-free weight edit.
- [ReQOR: Repairing Quantization-Conditioned Backdoors in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32755): A training-free defense that finds the dominant directions of module-local high-precision vs. quantized discrepancies and repairs them.
- [Auditing the Judge (HUB-J)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33065): Discovers, quantifies and mitigates LLM-judge biases using human–LLM disagreement cases instead of predefined bias lists.
- [Can a Frontier LLM Repair a Quantized Network by Tool-Free Weight-Space Programming?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33031): Claude models get a CNN's weights as text and are asked to improve on round-to-nearest 4-bit quantization. Part of the recent thread on models reading and editing weights.
- [Measuring Layer-wise Intrinsic Dimensionality of FFNs via PCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32782): Across 58 open LLMs (70M–72B), FFN intrinsic dimensionality follows a recurring middle-heavy profile.

**Training dynamics & post-training**
- [Fix How It Acts, Not How It Thinks: Surgical Fine-Tuning of Frontier LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32064): Small LoRA updates mostly vanish when merged into BF16 or FP8/NVFP4 releases (up to 93% lost). Merge-aware fine-tuning trains directly through the rounding.
- [Learning What vs. Learning How: Task vs. Interface Competence in Agentic Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31427): Separates knowing how to solve the task from knowing how to operate the harness, and shows that harness-conditioned training outcomes depend on how the two interact.
- [Evolve to Learn: Gradient-Aligned Experience Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32390): Experiences that help at test time can be harmful to internalize via self-distillation. Gradient alignment predicts which ones are worth training on (data-attribution flavor).
- [DAiSy: Online Data Selection by Adaptive Utility Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32294): A linear model on frozen embeddings predicts gradient-based data-utility scores, so pretraining data selection becomes cheap.
- [Branch and Consolidate: Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32515): A recipe for merging specialists that have drifted far from their shared checkpoint.
- [Beyond the Last Loop (ELGO)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31939) and [The Teacher Is Already Inside (ELPSD)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32452): Looped LMs contain a stronger "extrapolated-loop" teacher that can be used for RL or self-distillation.
- [How to Build and Train Your Recurrent-Depth Model: A Fixed-Point Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31513): Input injection is exactly Halpern iteration, which comes with convergence guarantees for truncated backprop. The analysis yields the Sparrow optimizer, a drop-in replacement for Muon.
- [SGD with Large Learning Rates: General Analysis & Improved Rates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32762): Proves that large-stepsize acceleration (O(T⁻²)) survives stochastic noise for linear models and two-layer networks under cross-entropy.
