# arXiv cs.LG — Top Papers for July 13, 2026

Focused on: AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization, data attribution, emergent misalignment, subliminal learning.

## Top 10

### 1. Statistically Undetectable Backdoors in Deep Neural Networks
**[2607.09532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09532)** · Andrej Bogdanov, Alon Rosen, Neekon Vafa

- Shows a malicious model trainer can plant backdoors that are statistically undetectable even in the white-box setting — the backdoored and honestly-trained models are close in total variation distance given the full weights.
- The backdoor grants invariance-based adversarial examples for every input (mapping distant inputs to unusually close outputs), yet without it, generating such examples is provably impossible in polynomial time under standard cryptographic assumptions.
- A clean theoretical demonstration of a fundamental power asymmetry between model trainers and model users — directly relevant to supply-chain trust and detection-based AI safety arguments.

### 2. How are linear representations learned? Exact solutions to the dynamics of abstraction
**[2607.08843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08843)** · William W. Yang, Andrew M. Saxe, Peter E. Latham

- Derives exact training-trajectory solutions for how linear concept directions ("abstraction") emerge in minimal linear networks, showing data/target geometry sets the end-of-training alignment, abstraction improves with depth, and initialization scale caps how much abstraction is reached mid-training.
- Extends to nonlinear networks: erf networks track the linear theory, while ReLU abstraction depends more on input geometry than target geometry; proves a general attenuation law where nonlinearities weaken abstraction in activations relative to preactivations.
- Validates the attenuation law in DINOv3 and Gemma 4, then uses the theory to improve linear-probe generalization in LLMs — a rare exact dynamical account directly underpinning linear-representation-hypothesis-based interpretability and steering methods.

### 3. Optimizing Against Safety Representations: Activation-Guided Adversarial Suffixes and the Geometry of Refusal
**[2607.08883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08883)** · Ege Çakar, Hannah Guan, Kayden Kehe

- Introduces Activation-Guided GCG, which optimizes adversarial suffixes directly against a model's internal refusal direction rather than output logits, finding that suppressing refusal across all layers/positions beats targeting a single layer — evidence that safety representations are distributed, not causally localized to one site.
- Proposes Soft-GCG, a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of discrete suffix search that is 33x faster than standard GCG while improving attack success.
- Finds smaller models stay vulnerable while larger, better-safety-trained models resist both activation- and suffix-based attacks under matched compute — concrete evidence for how alignment training reshapes (and sometimes fails to reshape) internal safety geometry.

### 4. Present but Rescaled: Chat-to-Agent Transfer of Additive Activation Steering
**[2607.09156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09156)** · Lucas Pinto

- First systematic study of whether activation-steering vectors calibrated on single-turn chat transfer to tool-using ReAct agents; finds the injected direction reaches late layers at near-full strength everywhere, but the *behavioral* effect is rescaled unpredictably per model/context.
- A refusal-bypass vector amplifies in agentic mode on some models (up to 2x) and attenuates on others, with the rescaling localized to the ReAct scaffold itself rather than to tool-observation dilution, and directional ablation (vs. additive injection) shows the effect is mechanism-specific.
- Direct safety implication: a deployment cannot assume a steering-based jailbreak defense (or attack) calibrated in chat mode generalizes to the agentic setting the model actually ships in.

### 5. Training, Reading, and Editing Legible Transformers
**[2607.08946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08946)** · Mark Oskin

- Builds transformers from operators that are legible by construction (bounded, named, fuzzy-set-like units instead of dense activations), diagnoses why a naive crispness penalty collapses units into dead constants (it's a variance-minimizer blind to detector vs. constant), and fixes it with a per-channel variance floor.
- Given the choice, the model routes 87% of load-bearing computation through crisp operators rather than reserved GELU units, reaching 78% legible feed-forward operands and 50% legible attention value channels — with per-head legibility rising from 18% (shallow) to 78% (deep layers) — at parity with a conventional baseline.
- Legible units support far more localized edits (50–184x more local in deep layers) and expose a decorrelation "legibility dial" trading circuit reuse for independence — a concrete mechanistic-interpretability-by-construction result rather than post-hoc probing.

### 6. Forget Narrowly, Retain Broadly: Unlearning as an Asymmetric Generalization Problem
**[2607.09236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09236)** · Amit Peleg, Naman Deep Singh, Naama Pearl, Bibhabasu Mohapatra, Matthias Hein

- Reframes LLM unlearning evaluation as an asymmetric generalization problem: forgetting must generalize across paraphrases (else "under-forgetting" lets knowledge resurface), while retention must hold over an enormous, implicitly-defined complement set (else "over-forgetting" silently destroys unrelated capabilities).
- Introduces SUITE, an evaluation protocol and training corpus with fine-grained forget/retain boundary annotations, showing training-data quality matters as much as algorithm choice.
- Proposes JensUn++, which achieves the best forget-retain trade-off across three LLMs in both sequential and joint unlearning — a methodological corrective for a benchmark space prone to overstating unlearning success.

### 7. Neural Collapse Is Forbidden: Information Floors in Language Models
**[2607.09487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09487)** · Bruno Abrahao

- Argues within-class representational variance in LLMs isn't incomplete neural collapse but *allocated information storage* obeying a law: across 14 models, macro-category structure carries only 4–12% of representational variance while within-token context carries 79–91%, stable across a 100x parameter range.
- Derives that token-level weight decay penalizes categories by type count (not occurrence mass), and proves a converse information floor forcing within-category dispersion to track conditional mutual information I(token; context | category) — validated model-free and even cross-model (one model's information predicts another's dispersion).
- Over pretraining, category variance share overshoots, decays, and partially recovers — a mechanistic account of why LLM representations resist full neural collapse, relevant to any interpretability method built on the collapse assumption.

### 8. A Unified Approach to Interpreting Knowledge Distillation for Large Language Models via Interactions
**[2607.08776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08776)** · Qingzhuo Wang, Ruiyang Qin, Zhenxin Qin, Wen Shen, Zhihua Wei

- Decomposes LLM output scores into a sum of nonlinear input-variable interactions and finds a common mechanism across diverse knowledge-distillation methods: student models get better by sparsifying which interactions they retain, suppressing the rest to near-zero.
- Shows performance variance across KD methods tracks their ability to handle *complex* interactions specifically — better methods induce higher sparsity among complex interactions, not just interactions in general.
- Proposes a plug-and-play Complex Interaction Penalty loss that consistently improves diverse KD methods in- and out-of-distribution — a mechanistic, falsifiable account of why distillation works rather than a purely empirical recipe.

### 9. Influence Diagnostics in High-dimensional M-estimation: Precise Asymptotics
**[2607.09250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09250)** · Hugo Cui

- Extends classical leave-one-out influence theory (well understood in the low-dimensional n→∞, d=O(1) limit) to the high-dimensional regime n≍d for convex M-estimation under Gaussian design, where a sample's influence develops nontrivial dependence on every other training sample.
- Derives a sharp characterization of the limiting distribution of influences across the training set — an exact theoretical handle on data attribution in the regime most relevant to modern overparameterized models.
- Connects the theory to a standard active-learning heuristic by showing influential samples concentrate near the decision boundary, tying data-attribution theory directly to data-selection practice.

### 10. The Count Is There, but Misaligned: Understanding and Correcting Counting Failures in VLMs
**[2607.09544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09544)** · Ahmed Oumar El-Shangiti, Abzal Nurgazy, Hilal AlQuabeh, Nikolai Rozanov, Kentaro Inui

- Uses nonlinear activation probes across four VLMs and five counting datasets to show models often encode the correct object count internally even when they verbalize the wrong answer — a gap between latent knowledge and output, not missing knowledge.
- SVCCA analysis shows probes trained on ground truth vs. on model outputs share a partial activation subspace but read out along misaligned directions; a causal steering intervention confirms strengthening the count-identified direction improves counting accuracy.
- Builds a detector-guided self-correction method (re-prompt only when an internal error probe fires) that improves counting accuracy by up to 15.6 points with zero parameter updates — a practical case study of using interpretability to fix a knowledge/output misalignment.
