# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-14

Note: arXiv does not announce on weekends, so the "recent" cs.LG listing still showed Fri, 13 Nov 2026 (340 entries) as its latest day. The picks below are drawn from that list and **exclude the 25 papers already featured in the 2026-11-13 digest**. Summaries are based on the abstracts. Picks lean toward training dynamics, post-training data, and attacks that hide inside fine-tuning or retrieval.

## Top 10

### 1. [On the Difficulty of Sequence-Level Supervised Learning for Autoregressive Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16736)
- Asks whether you can replace teacher forcing with sequence-level losses that "see" inference-time decoding (greedy or sampling), to fix exposure bias.
- Two natural constructions both yield losses that are **unbounded below**. Via autoregressive Gumbel tricks, the sampling loss is exactly the expected greedy loss under Gumbel noise.
- Stopping gradients through the sampler gives a moment-matching direction: not the gradient of any loss, but provably a descent direction for NLL in function space. Its variance is too high to be practical.
- Conclusion: teacher forcing may be all you need for the supervised phase.

### 2. [The Illusion of Diversity: Aligning LLM Exploration via Effective Entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15493)
- Argues that token- or sequence-level entropy bonuses in RLVR mostly reward chaotic, wrong trajectories instead of real exploration.
- Defines **effective entropy**: diversity measured only within the subspace of valid (correct) trajectories. VEPO adds it to the RLVR objective, which pulls up the probability of under-explored correct solutions.
- On R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B across six math benchmarks: gains over GRPO, and more distinct strategies on MATH500 problems at matched correct counts.

### 3. [Human or Synthetic? What a Fixed Budget Buys for Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16895)
- Compares human and synthetic preference data for SFT+DPO at a **dollar-matched** budget, not a sample-matched one.
- Synthetic data wins decisively at low-to-moderate budgets: high win rates for $200–$400.
- On helpfulness, human data catches up only at about 100× the budget. On summarization it never catches up within the tested range.
- Human preference datasets vary a lot in reliability: one widely used source underperforms another, even after domain restriction and deduplication.

### 4. [Poison-then-Hide: Finetuning-Activated Backdoor Attack on Pretrained Vision Encoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14970)
- Plants a **dormant** backdoor in a pretrained encoder. It looks clean at release and becomes active only after a victim fully fine-tunes on *clean* downstream data.
- Recipe: trigger optimization, then encoder poisoning (learned jointly with a benign target task, which turns out to be critical), then targeted unlearning to hide the backdoor.
- Up to 97% attack success across 6 datasets and 3 architectures. Standard defenses don't remove it, and it can come back after benign fine-tuning.
- Relevant as a concrete example of latent behavior that fine-tuning re-elicits.

### 5. [One Fabrication Is Enough: A Fake News Article Changes the Forecasts of LLM Forecasting Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15102)
- The weakest attacker publishes one fabricated article and knows only the question. They have no access to the model, the retriever, or its queries.
- Once the article is retrieved, it reverses 74–82% of the opposing forecasts on every frontier model tested. Agentic forecasters that search aggressively retrieve it about 90% of the time.
- Average accuracy barely changes, so accuracy metrics can't detect the attack. When given two contradictory articles, models stay confident instead of hedging.
- The best retrieval-time defenses are bypassed by small attack changes and discard 39% of genuine news.

### 6. [Where Is Uncertainty Observed? A Mechanistic Understanding of Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15735)
- Survey and framework that organizes LLM uncertainty estimators by *where in the computation* they look. Four lenses: structural (component updates), representational (hidden states), distributional (token probabilities), semantic (distributions over meanings).
- Links the lenses through the residual stream and orders them by how much information they carry. This gives a principle for choosing a UQ method for a given target.
- Also separates evaluation targets, criteria, and metrics, and lists open problems linking UQ to model internals.

### 7. [Benchmarking AI risk behaviors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16318)
- Toolkit for building **risk-propensity profiles** of LLMs: how risk-averse or risk-seeking they are, and how consistent that is across contexts and task demands.
- Across generations, 2026 frontier models are more stable, more consistent, and more risk-neutral.
- The authors attribute this to newer models more often using expected-value maximization as an explicit decision procedure. That is relevant to how models' decision-making dispositions are drifting.

### 8. [Cleaning an Agent's History Hurts When the World Still Holds the Error](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14654)
- Freezes agents mid-task, edits either the history or the world state, and lets a receiver model continue.
- Adding the correct value as a truthful note next to the preserved error raises recovery (51% → 66%). Adding it as a "completed action" lowers recovery below the unedited history (50% → 44%).
- On AppWorld, a success receipt when the effect is *absent* drops task success from 45% to 28%.
- Takeaway for context compaction and handoff: a history that looks clean while the world is still broken is worse than no edit at all.

### 9. [Learning Rate Transfer and Feature Learning Across Depth for Constrained Spectral Optimizers: Complete Scion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15291)
- Extends CompleteP-style depth-wise hyperparameter transfer to LMO-based optimizers (Frank–Wolfe and projected non-Euclidean steepest descent, e.g. Scion).
- Derives that 1/L residual scaling still applies, by a different argument than for Adam. This gives depth- and width-wise LR transfer while keeping feature learning (avoiding the kernel regime).
- Checked on nanoGPT/FineWeb, DeiT/ImageNet, and MLP-Mixer/CIFAR-10.

### 10. [Can a search hold its own bar? In-loop deflation and its exploits in autonomous strategy discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14539)
- A small Goodhart case study: when LLM-driven search uses an in-loop overfitting correction (a deflated-Sharpe bar), **the bar becomes something the search optimizes against**.
- Variance-dilution exploit: near-duplicate mediocre candidates lower the bar. The paper proposes two defenses: a pre-registered variance floor and a monotone high-water mark.
- Across 53 audited searches with frontier models: gated searches report "no certifiable discovery" in 36 of 37 runs, ungated searches always "discover" something, and every out-of-sample CI includes zero.

## Honorable Mentions
- [Maximizing Capacity Utilization of LoRA Adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17191): LoRA updates have effective rank far below their configured rank. An SVD-free Rényi-2 effective-rank regularizer improves the learning–forgetting frontier.
- [Characterizing the Edge of Stability in Variational Training Without Priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15990): training on the expected loss alone runs at a modified edge of stability set by gradient SNR and the number of MC samples. Larger LR and fewer samples give flatter minima.
- [Sharp Covering-Number Bounds for ReLU Networks via Parameter Symmetries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15300): proves scaling and permutation are the only function-preserving symmetries, which gives sharp capacity bounds and explains flat directions.
- [Effective Teacher Support for Reasoning Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14850): distilling from only the teacher's top 0.01% of the vocabulary costs no MMLU-Pro accuracy (Qwen3), with large compute savings. The safe cutoff depends on the model family.
- [Reasoning Supervision and Capability Retention in LoRA Domain Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17072): label-only LoRA beats training on teacher reasoning traces in-domain, and general capabilities are retained in both.
- [Compiling Known Rules into Compact Neural Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16981): LLMs write network weights directly from game rules, producing sparse, program-like nets. Standard PPO fine-tuning erases most of the written behavior.
- [Classical Energies Learn to Reason 1,000x Faster than Looped Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17309): a learned Hopfield–Tank energy solves Sudoku-Extreme and mazes in milliseconds of training, a caution about trivially solvable reasoning benchmarks.
- [Rectified Looped Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16265): interpolating feedback between loops keeps hidden-state norms bounded and improves perplexity and robustness.
- [An Approach to Hinton's Mother-Child Proposal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15523): learned "care" constraints with certified preservation under further learning, in a finite world.
- [Scaling public input on AI policy with pluralistic agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16028): a preregistered study (N=746) of AI agents that deliberate on people's behalf. An agent's influence depends on how it is prompted.
- [Experience-Handoff RL for Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16409): RL that also trains the reflection and playbook-writing roles an agent hands to future rollouts.
- [Constructing and Validating Memorisation Audits for EHR Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15578): weak cohort-level memorization but no reliable patient-level disclosure, with positive controls.
