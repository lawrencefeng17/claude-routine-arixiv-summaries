## Top 10

### 1. The Kinetics of Training: A Driven-Nucleation Rate Law for Emergence, Plasticity Loss, and Circuit Control in Language Models
**[2607.27281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27281)** · Lei Dong

- Proposes that capability emergence during training is rate-limited by the "joint alignment" of separate circuit components coming together, analogous to nucleation kinetics in physical phase transitions.
- Analysis of Pythia checkpoints shows partial-credit / smooth-improvement accounts don't explain the observed abruptness of capability formation.
- Introduces a rate-equation model for when capabilities emerge and a mechanism for preventing plasticity loss — directly bearing on training-dynamics and emergent-capability questions.

### 2. Fairness Pruning: Locating Demographic Bias in GLU-MLP Layers via Differential Activations
**[2607.28319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28319)** · Pere Martra, Eugenio Martínez Cámara, Alfonso Ureña López

- Identifies specific neurons in GLU-MLP layers that are differentially activated by demographic attributes, using differential activation analysis on Llama-3.2-1B.
- Zeroing at most 40 neurons materially changes the model's demographic-bias behavior while retaining 99.49% of general task capability.
- A clean mechanistic-interpretability result: demographic bias is confirmed as a dissociable circuit that can be causally located and surgically intervened on.

### 3. Position, Not Provenance: Separating Reasoning Mediation from Sycophancy in Medical Vision-Language Models
**[2607.27304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27304)** · Supratik Bhowal, Subhrajyoti Basu, Aritra Gir Mahanta, Anik Pal Chowdhury

- Tests whether chain-of-thought reasoning in medical VLMs (LLaVA-Med, MedGemma) is causally used in predictions, or just post-hoc rationalization — a core CoT-faithfulness question.
- Finds that prompt *position* (prefix-forced continuation vs. re-prompting), not the stated source of the reasoning, determines how much the model actually relies on it.
- Removing genuine visual evidence increases reliance on injected reasoning text — a concrete faithfulness/sycophancy failure mode relevant to interpretability of reasoning models generally.

### 4. Harnessing the Potential of Optimizing Data Mixtures via Bayesian Domain Reweighting
**[2607.27928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27928)** · Xiang Yuan, Kaiqing Lei, Zhenyu Jin, Jun Shu, Deyu Meng, Zongben Xu

- Proposes a Bayesian method that infers pretraining domain-mixture weights from a Dirichlet distribution, rather than fitting a scaling-law curve per domain.
- Achieves stable, efficient domain-weight learning at substantially lower data/compute cost than prior function-fitting approaches.
- Relevant to data attribution and training dynamics: which data a model is trained on, and how much, is treated as a first-class optimization target.

### 5. Generalization Bounds on Optimal Control for Transformer Training and Wasserstein Distributional Robustness
**[2607.27975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27975)** · Kağan Akman, Naci Saldi, Serdar Yüksel

- Derives finite-sample generalization bounds for Transformers by casting training as an optimal-control / dynamic-programming problem over a measure-valued state.
- Connects the resulting bounds to Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization, giving a principled link between training dynamics and worst-case generalization.
- A rare theory-grounded contribution to the science of generalization specifically for Transformer architectures (rather than generic deep nets).

---

### 6. Subtract or Replay? Exact Deletion from Language-Model Memory
**[2607.27539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27539)** · Vishwajith Ramesh

- Compares two mechanisms for exactly deleting a memorized item from a language model: algebraic subtraction of its parameter contribution vs. replay-based unlearning.
- For support-vector-style memories, the two approaches agree almost perfectly (median KL ≈ 5.4×10⁻¹⁵), establishing exact deletion as a property of how memory is represented rather than of the deletion algorithm.
- Directly relevant to data attribution and machine unlearning: it pins down *when* a memorized fact can be provably and exactly removed.

### 7. Beyond Binary Rewards: A Comparative Study of Reward Design for Reinforcement Unlearning
**[2607.27968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27968)** · Efstratios Zaradoukas, Davide Gabrielli, Bardh Prenkaj, Gjergji Kasneci

- Studies how reward shaping affects RL-based machine unlearning, moving beyond simple binary "forgotten / not forgotten" rewards.
- Proposes exponential and PageRank-inspired reward schemes that converge roughly 3x faster than binary rewards while forgetting just as thoroughly.
- Useful practical input for alignment/unlearning pipelines that use RL as the forgetting mechanism.

### 8. Compliance2LoRA: On-Demand Safety Alignment on Arbitrary Policy Subsets via Hypernetwork-Generated LoRA Adapters
**[2607.27594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27594)** · Pankayaraj Pathmanathan, Furong Huang

- A hypernetwork generates policy-compliant LoRA adapter weights on the fly, letting a single base model be safety-aligned to an arbitrary *subset* of a policy without retraining a separate model per policy configuration.
- Enables on-demand adjustment of which safety rules are enforced, without sacrificing task performance, across multiple model sizes.
- Relevant to scalable alignment: decoupling "which safety behaviors are active" from "which model weights are deployed."

---

### 9. STEREODISCO: Discovering Stereotypicality in LLMs
**[2607.27824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27824)** · Farane Jalali Farahani, Corina Dima, Mojtaba Nayyeri, Raphael H. Heiberger, Steffen Staab

- Adapts the semantic-differential method from psychology to probe how LLMs internally represent social-group stereotypes, recovering ~2,000 semantic axes derived from WordNet.
- Finds that LLMs agree with each other on social-group stereotype judgments substantially more than they agree with humans.
- An interpretability-flavored bias audit: it locates stereotype representations as a shared, model-agnostic internal structure rather than idiosyncratic noise.

---

### 10. TriShield: Zero-Utility-Loss Defense Against Privacy Backdoors in Federated Language Model Fine-Tuning via Orthogonal Gradient Projection and Optimizer State Entanglement
**[2607.27940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27940)** · Cheng Wei

- Defends federated LLM fine-tuning against "NeuroImprint" privacy-backdoor attacks, where a malicious server plants parameters that later let it reconstruct client data.
- Combines parameter-artifact detection, stateful virtual-iteration entanglement, and orthogonal gradient projection to reduce attack reconstruction success to 0% across all tested variants.
- Achieves this with under 5% GPU overhead and no measurable utility loss — a concrete data-security result adjacent to the broader training-dynamics/data-attribution theme (what can be recovered about training data from model weights).
