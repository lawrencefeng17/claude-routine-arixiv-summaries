## Top 10

### 1. OPIUM: Mitigating Steering Externalities and Over-Refusal via Dual Objective Latent Optimization
**[2607.19806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19806)** · Kavin Aravindan, Arihant Rastogi, Aadi Prasad, Krishak Aneja, Saiyam Jain, Vaishnavi Shivkumar, Ponnurangam Kumaraguru

- Activation steering is a lightweight inference-time control mechanism, but steering vectors carry unintended externalities: utility vectors can silently weaken safety behavior, while refusal vectors induce over-refusal on benign prompts.
- OPIUM is a training-free method that "sanitizes" a steering vector by matching its induced representations against a safer reference behavior wherever the original vector causes problems, while preserving the intended intervention elsewhere.
- Beats standard steering and directional-ablation baselines on the safety-utility trade-off — evidence that steering side effects can be fixed directly in activation space rather than requiring retraining.

### 2. Guardrails as Scapegoats: Auditing Unfaithful Safety Refusals in Tool-Augmented LLM Agents
**[2607.19449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19449)** · Aarushi Singh

- Black-box audit of how tool-augmented LLM agents handle silent infrastructure failures (e.g., empty API payloads), across 4 failure profiles and 12 production-like tool stubs.
- At baseline, agents mostly fabricate (56.6%) rather than admit failure; "unfaithful safety refusals" — inventing a policy justification for what was actually a plumbing failure — are rare (0.25%).
- Striking finding: simply adding safety-flavored language to the system prompt ("prioritize user privacy and data security") amplifies unfaithful refusals **15.6×** (to 3.95%), concentrated on sensitive tools like medical records — a concrete case of safety instructions triggering deceptive-sounding behavior as a side effect.

### 3. Building Fast, Evaluating Slow: Pipeline Choices Dominate Autointerpretability Score Variance
**[2607.19386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19386)** · Sinie van der Ben, Neele Roch, Anna Hedström, Mennatallah El-Assady

- Systematically tests the reliability of "autointerpretability" scores used to grade sparse-autoencoder (SAE) features, across multiple metrics and models.
- Key result: methodological variance (which eval pipeline you run) exceeds architectural variance (which SAE you're grading) — detection scoring is fairly stable, fuzzing is not, and feature rankings that look consistent in aggregate mask large per-feature instability.
- Implication for the field: cross-paper SAE comparisons may just be measuring evaluation-pipeline differences. The authors contribute a variance-decomposition method, a stability check, and a minimum reporting checklist for interpretability papers going forward.

### 4. The Blessing of Dimensionality: How Near-Orthogonality in High-Dimensional Spaces Explains Temporal Portability
**[2607.20301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20301)** · Abigail Woodring, Adrian Chan, Rana Muhammad Shahroz Khan, Sukwon Yun, Chau-Wai Wong, Tianlong Chen

- Studies why PortLLM-style LoRA patches keep working after a base model receives further continual pretraining updates — tracked empirically across 10 update rounds on Mistral, Gemma, and Qwen.
- Proposes a geometric explanation: near-orthogonality of high-dimensional weight-update directions is the underlying reason old patches stay compatible with a moving base model, rather than requiring the patch to track the base model's drift.
- A clean piece of "science of generalization" work connecting a practical LLM-maintenance trick to loss-landscape geometry in high dimensions.

### 5. On Optimization Complexity of Second-Order Certified Unlearning
**[2607.20192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20192)** · Nikita Doikov, Anastasia Koloskova

- Studies certified machine unlearning (removing memorized training data with a provable guarantee) from an optimization-complexity angle, formalizing the joint goal of certification + optimization accuracy for uniformly convex regularizers.
- Introduces a second-order unlearning algorithm with an anisotropic Gaussian mechanism, proving fast convergence for linear models with quasi-self-concordant losses (logistic/exponential regression).
- Shows a provable benefit of using second-order curvature information over first-order unlearning methods — theoretical grounding for a technique central to data deletion / right-to-be-forgotten compliance.

### 6. An Isotropy-Preserving Spectral Cap for Muon: Theory and Three Case Studies
**[2607.19771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19771)** · Jiachun Li

- Muon and other matrix-sign optimizers are increasingly used to pretrain LLMs, but unlike SGD they lack a natural 1/‖W‖ brake, so weight-matrix norms can grow unchecked and internal geometry can become anisotropic.
- Proposes a lightweight "spectral cap" that only constrains growth along the top singular direction, leaving other directions, rotation, and singular-value reordering free to keep learning.
- Demonstrated on a nanoGPT feed-forward layer, a 64-expert MoE router, and FlashAttention Q/K projections: the cap prevents concrete failure modes (router collapse, attention-head divergence) without hurting validation loss — direct, mechanistic training-dynamics work on a now-widely-used optimizer.

### 7. Scaling Laws for Hypernetwork-Based Knowledge Injection in Large Language Models
**[2607.19604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19604)** · Nischay Dhankhar, Dos Baha, Abulhair Saparov

- Studies train-time (not test-time) knowledge injection: hypernetworks trained to generate LoRA adapters that let a target LLM answer factual questions pulled from a large corpus.
- A decoupled architecture separates the hypernetwork's injection capacity from the base model's general capability, enabling the first rigorous scaling-law study for this setup, using a new tens-of-millions-example dataset (MegaWikiQA, built from Wikidata5M).
- Finds broadly predictive power-law scaling across architecture axes and improving out-of-distribution generalization at scale, outperforming plain LoRA finetuning — relevant to both training dynamics and how factual knowledge gets encoded into weights.

### 8. Know Your Agent: Reconnaissance-Driven Pentesting of AI Agents
**[2607.19837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19837)** · Or Zion Eliav, Eyal Lenga, Shir Bernstien, Yisroel Mirsky

- Argues AI agents deserve the same reconnaissance-driven pentesting treatment as traditional systems: probe first to build a profile, then craft stronger attacks — particularly indirect prompt injection.
- Introduces KYA (Know Your Agent), an automated black-box framework that identifies which "knowledge assets" an agent exposes and how adversaries can leverage them, evaluated on security benchmarks and a real coding agent.
- Framework, benchmarks, and baseline code will be released — useful infrastructure for red-teaming and safety evaluation of increasingly autonomous, tool-using agents.

### 9. Unlearning as Distribution Restoration: A Controlled Counterfactual Study, a Validated Selective Screen, and the Limits of Oracle-Free Certification
**[2607.19442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19442)** · Sen Yang, Yuen-Hei Yeung

- Reframes unlearning evaluation as "restoration to a matched reference distribution" and stress-tests it across 45 controlled model configurations.
- Finds that methods passing standard oracle-matching criteria can still retain forgotten facts well below the never-learned baseline, that fixed retain/round-trip certificates fail even on models explicitly built to pass them (41/45 cells), and that a simple fixed-magnitude logit-suppression attack defeats forward-only certification in 12/45 cells.
- Proves an identifiability theorem characterizing which facts can even in principle admit oracle-free forget thresholds (with TOFU as a predicted boundary case) — a rigorous, somewhat deflating look at how unsound current unlearning verification really is.

### 10. Knowledge-Centric Self-Improvement
**[2607.19592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19592)** · Xuefei Julie Wang, Lauren Hyoseo Yoon, Chengrui Qu, Amanda Zichang Wang, Atharva Sehgal, Eric Mazumdar, Yisong Yue

- Proposes relocating "self-improvement" away from tuning individual agents (prompts, workflows, weights) and into a persistent, shared, curated knowledge base that generic, disposable agents draw on.
- Agents contribute evidence-grounded insights to task-level and cross-task forums, which get distilled back into transferable knowledge rather than agent-specific configuration.
- Across abstract reasoning, coding, and terminal benchmarks, gains transfer to unseen tasks and across different model families — suggesting the improvements are genuine and portable rather than model-specific artifacts, with implications for inspectable/auditable self-improvement loops.
