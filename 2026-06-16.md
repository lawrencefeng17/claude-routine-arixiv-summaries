### 1. Scalable Circuit Learning for Interpreting Large Language Models
**arXiv:2606.16939** | Naiyu Yin, Dennis Wei, Tian Gao, Amit Dhurandhar, Karthikeyan Natesan Ramamurthy, Yue Yu
*Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop @ ICML 2026*

- Proposes **CircuitLasso**, which uses sparse linear regression to discover circuits over SAE features, recovering interpretable semantic pathways at far lower computational cost than intervention-based methods.
- Achieves structural accuracy matching state-of-the-art approaches while showing how "human-interpretable semantic features propagate through the model and influence its predictions."
- Validates circuit insights on a domain-generalization task, demonstrating that circuit-level understanding can translate to practical performance gains.

### 2. Demystifying Variance in Circuit Discovery of LLMs
**arXiv:2606.16920** | Frank Zhengqing Wu, Francesco Tonin, Volkan Cevher

- Identifies **three sources of instability** in circuit discovery: resampling variance (across data batches), rephrasing variance (across prompt phrasings), and sample-wise variance (in faithfulness scores).
- Introduces **CEAP**, a theoretically-grounded method that reduces resampling variance; finds that different prompt templates activate distinct circuits — fundamentally complicating the notion of a canonical circuit for a given behavior.
- Argues that some poor faithfulness scores reflect "selective contribution scaling" (a neural mechanism affecting measurement magnitude) rather than actual circuit defects.

### 3. Constitutional Value Potentials: Reading and Steering Internal Priority Margins in Language Models
**arXiv:2606.15420** | Tong Che, Rui Wu

- Introduces **CVP**, which extracts "internal pressure" signals from neural activations to detect which value a model prioritizes when facing value conflicts — before any response is generated.
- Achieves **AUROC up to 0.95** for predicting conflict violations, outperforming hidden-state probes, and generalizes to held-out synthetic conflicts across model scales.
- Demonstrates that constitutional priorities are accessible as stable activation-space patterns, enabling both monitoring and real-time steering toward intended value orderings.

### 4. Understanding Diversity Collapse in RLVR via the Lens of Overtraining
**arXiv:2606.15455**

- Explains why RL with verifiable rewards causes **Pass@1 to improve while high-k Pass@k degrades**: most gradient updates concentrate probability mass on already-solved problems instead of expanding reasoning coverage.
- Shows that restricting updates to problems with zero observed success lifts Pass@256 above the base model on difficult benchmarks, confirming overtraining as the root cause.
- Introduces **Bayesian Boundary Gating**, which redirects optimization toward genuinely novel problems and improves average Pass@k across multiple reasoning benchmarks.

### 5. Adaptive Inference and Function Vectors in Deep Transformers
**arXiv:2606.16694** | Ravin Raj, Gautam Reddy

- Proposes a **mean-field theoretical framework** treating deep transformers as distributed inference systems subject to communication, locality, and depth constraints.
- Shows how transformers use **function vectors** — internal representations of latent context — to progressively refine task inference across layers, connecting this to mechanistic interpretability and formal in-context learning theory.
- Validated with constrained linear attention; finds feedforward blocks + depth together support a richer class of in-context learning algorithms than shallower architectures can express.

### 6. AI Engram: In Search of Memory Traces in Artificial Intelligence
**arXiv:2606.14997** | Kwon et al.
*ICML 2026 Oral*

- Formalizes neuroscientific memory trace properties (specificity, reactivation, sufficiency, necessity) as a constrained mathematical problem and derives a **closed-form solution** that extracts individual memory traces from distributed network parameters.
- The solution corresponds to **natural gradient updates on the parameter manifold** (Fisher information geometry), grounding engram extraction in principled optimization theory.
- Enables direct manipulation of learned knowledge through linear operations without retraining; scales from MLPs to LLMs — a foundational tool for data attribution, knowledge editing, and unlearning.

### 7. Emergent Retokenization Symmetry in Large Language Models: Phenomenology and Applications
**arXiv:2606.15521** | Kanishk Jain, Matthew Day, Tankut Can

- Discovers that LLMs develop partial **segmentation symmetry** during training — treating alternative valid tokenizations of the same byte sequence as equivalent — despite being trained only on canonical tokenizations.
- Uses **retokenization** (swapping tokenization while preserving bytes) as a clean interpretability probe that isolates segmentation effects without altering syntax or semantics.
- Retokenization serves as a novel inference-time sampling strategy complementary to temperature-based sampling, sometimes recovering correct solutions that standard decoding misses.

### 8. Rational Sparse Autoencoder
**arXiv:2606.14990** | Naiyu Yin, Yue Yu

- Replaces fixed SAE activations (ReLU/TopK) with **trainable rational functions** that can uniformly approximate any existing SAE activation family on compact domains while adapting to the actual pre-activation geometry of the model.
- Two-stage pipeline: initialize from baseline SAE weights with mathematically optimized rational coefficients, then fine-tune on reconstruction objectives — fast and parameter-efficient.
- Improves both reconstruction metrics and downstream behavior across three language models, suggesting flexible activation primitives are an underexplored axis of SAE design.

### 9. Size Doesn't Matter: Cosine-Scored Sparse Autoencoders
**arXiv:2606.15054** | Silen Naihin, Lev Stambler
*Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop @ ICML 2026*

- Identifies that standard inner-product scoring in SAEs under BatchTopK allows **high-norm tokens to claim dictionary slots** based on magnitude rather than content alignment — a mismatch with what sublayer normalization actually preserves.
- Replaces inner-product scoring with a **learned blend of cosine similarity and input magnitude**; models consistently converge to cosine-dominated weighting during training, without manual specification.
- Cosine-scored SAEs detect human-recognizable concepts far more often than standard SAEs, using dictionary slots more efficiently across multiple tasks and network depths.

### 10. Cascaded Sparse Autoencoders Learn Multi-Level Visual Concepts in Multimodal LLMs
**arXiv:2606.16193** | Yusong Zhao, Hengyi Wang, Tanuja Ganu, Akshay Nambi, Hao Wang

- Introduces **Cascaded SAEs (CSAEs)**: a second-level autoencoder trained on the decoder weights of the first, learning "concepts of concepts" and organizing visual features hierarchically rather than as a flat dictionary.
- Tested on Qwen3-VL, Gemma-3, and LLaVA; demonstrates improved interpretability and successful concept-level interventions in multimodal outputs.
- Addresses a core limitation of flat SAEs for vision-language models: visual semantics exhibit natural hierarchical structure (e.g., "fur" → "animal" → "pet") that single-level dictionaries cannot capture.

## Honorable Mentions

- **arXiv:2606.16407** — *A Mechanistic Understanding of Pronoun Fidelity in LLMs*: Identifies three causal factors (group entity binding, recency bias, stereotypical bias) accounting for 91–99.5% of pronoun handling behavior; discovers two distinct copying pathways with different mechanistic underpinnings. Strong circuit-level interpretability.

- **arXiv:2606.15821** — *The Truth Stays in the Family: Enhancing Contextual Grounding via Inherited Truthful Heads in Model Lineages*: Context-truthfulness patterns in attention heads are preserved within model families despite instruction tuning or multimodal adaptation; TruthProbe soft-gating on these heads reduces hallucinations on HaluEval, POPE, and CHAIR.

- **arXiv:2606.15980** — *Do Safety Monitors Stay Reliable After an Update? Benchmarking and Predicting Activation-Monitor Staleness*: Fine-tuning (especially QLoRA) significantly degrades activation-based safety monitors while quantization alone does not; privacy/PII monitors are most vulnerable; degradation is predictable from pre-deployment features.

- **arXiv:2606.16768** — *Taming Curvature: Architecture Warm-Up for Stable Transformer Training*: Fast online estimator for the largest (preconditioned) Hessian eigenvalue; progressive depth warm-up reduces training instabilities in billion-parameter transformers with minimal convergence overhead. Relevant to training dynamics.

- **arXiv:2606.17024** — *ExpRL: Exploratory RL for LLM Mid-Training*: Uses human-written QA pairs as "reward scaffolds" for an LLM judge that assigns dense partial-credit rewards during mid-training; outperforms SFT and sparse-reward RL on math reasoning tasks. Relevant to RLHF/alignment training dynamics.
