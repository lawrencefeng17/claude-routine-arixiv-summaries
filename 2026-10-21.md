# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-21

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (249 entries, Wed 21 Oct 2026, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. All 249 titles were scanned; the top picks were read at the abstract level and condensed below.

### 1. [Influence Is the Target-Response Jacobian Times Residuals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27689)

*Paul Geertsema, Simon Geertsema*

- Introduces the target-response Jacobian J = ∂ŷᵢ/∂yⱼ: how predictions move when individual training *targets* change (vs. TDA's sensitivity to sample *weights*). Computable matrix-free at the same cost as influence.
- Shows influence = J · diag(residuals) exactly at stationary points (canonical-link losses; multiclass version for softmax CE).
- Predicts three failure modes of influence: interpolation collapse (small residuals), direction washout (mixed-sign residuals), true-class collapse (softmax).
- Versus refitting: J reaches R² 0.99–1.00 on tanh nets (up to N=10⁶), while influence's R² is negative on every dataset; 0.92 vs negative on MNIST.

### 2. [Transformers Learn Two Competing Sorting Algorithms, and Only One Length-Generalizes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26974)

*Gatmiry, Henry, Su, Abbe, Bengio, Weber, Kelner, Bartlett*

- Two-layer transformers trained to ~zero loss on sorting land in one of two mechanistically distinct solution classes, depending on initialization.
- One class ignores the 2nd attention layer and fails to length-generalize; the other uses both layers and generalizes perfectly despite fixed-length training.
- Initialization can steer which solution is learned; the gap traces to input types rare at train length but common at longer lengths.
- On "find the minimum" the pattern reverses: the two-layer solution generalizes *worse*.

### 3. [Can Native Safety Travel? Reusing Safety Pretraining Across Models via On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27555)

*Shang, Zhang, Chen, Wang, Chang, Shen, Liu*

- Poses "native safety reuse": transfer robustness from a small safety-pretrained teacher to standard-pretrained students.
- On-policy distillation (student generates, teacher supervises token-level; cross-tokenizer alignment for different families).
- After subsequent fine-tuning, a 1.7B student's attack success rises only 5.0% vs 11.6% for safety post-training, while utility (48.2%) far exceeds the teacher's (27.4%).

### 4. [Poisoning-Based Backdoors Are Not Cryptographic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28045)

*Masahiro Kaneko*

- Argues no attacker/defender asymmetry can arise from poisoning-based backdoor installation: a procedure that installs a trigger verifier from poisoned data is itself a learner for that verifier.
- So any verifier poisoning can install is reconstructible by a white-box defender with comparably many labeled trigger observations; a secret inference-time key is likewise observable.
- Experiments on instruction-tuned LLMs: no configuration gives a backdoor that is both effective and unrecoverable.

### 5. [Relative Representations Enable Portable White-Box Monitoring Across Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28366)

*Tim Bakker, Christos Louizos*

- Expresses each example via angular distances to shared anchors (global, all-layer construction) so a probe trained on one model can be reused on another with no labeled target data.
- Transferred probes keep ≥85% of same-model AUROC-over-chance on trivia, safety-monitoring, and math-reasoning tasks; robust to scale/prompt changes and better after model updates.
- Caveat: transfer is task/target-dependent and can fail even when the property is decodable in the target.

### 6. [Orthogonal Boundary Unlearning for Dual-Use Concepts in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27490)

*Niccolò Ronchetti*

- Targets "concept bleeding" when unlearning hazardous dual-use capabilities (cyber, bio) that share representations with benign science.
- Extracts a benign capability subspace via SVD of activation covariances and restricts updates to its orthogonal null space (Projected LoRA, or closed-form weight surgery).
- On four open models: strong dual-use separation, mitigates in-context priming attacks, resists adversarial relearning without hurting benign competence.

### 7. [Present but Hard to Read: Probing Latent Reasoning with Minimal Pairs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28498)

*Aur Shalev Merin*

- Shows single-prompt probes badly underestimate what's encoded: a copied digit is readable at ~0.9 via minimal-pair contrast, but only 0.36 (Ouro-1.4B) / 0.19 (Qwen2.5-1.5B) with standard probing.
- Also cautions that successful probes are ambiguous (any varied token leaves a trace); gives a protocol with a synthetic null.
- Patching shows an intermediate value (a+b) can be readable but *not used* by the model.

### 8. [Claims of Emergence in AI Should Be Grounded in Information Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28479)

*Christoph Riedl, Fernando E. Rosas*

- Position paper: emergence/synergy claims are usually informal; proposes partial information decomposition (unique, redundant, synergistic) as a falsifiable criterion.
- A behavior counts as emergent only if it carries synergistic information about task-relevant outcomes.
- Applied to attention heads, agents, and human-AI teams.

### 9. [Do Neural Models Prefer Their Submodels?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28439)

*Ribot, Alexandr, Seigal*

- Geometric framework asking whether local minimizers lie in submodels (e.g., inactive neurons).
- Shallow MLPs never prefer such subnetworks; deep MLPs under conditions; lightning self-attention rarely prefers low-rank, with boundary exceptions.
- Conclusion: implicit preference for simpler submodels must come from training dynamics, not model geometry.

### 10. [On the Fragility of Tasks under Composition: Implicit Budgeting in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27190)

*Li, Brockmoeller, Jegelka, Gupta, Karalias*

- Models err far more when tasks are composed in one prompt, even if each is solved reliably alone; not explained by independent error accumulation.
- Evidence of an implicit thinking budget: fewer tokens per task under composition, so tasks needing long traces are most fragile.
- Grouping by fragility cuts token usage 43% while preserving accuracy.
