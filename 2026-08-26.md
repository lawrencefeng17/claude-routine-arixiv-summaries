## Top 10

### 1. [NeuronGuard: Robust LLM Safety Alignment via Ablation-Aware Safety Signal Redistribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23959)

- Notes that jailbreak prompts and post-deployment neuron-pruning attacks share a common weakness they exploit: safety information concentrates in a sparse neuron subset.
- Fine-tuning-stage defense that dynamically identifies safety-critical neurons via periodically refreshed per-layer classifiers, forces refusal behavior under deliberate ablation of those neurons, and redistributes safety signal across a broader neuron set via KL regularization.
- Provides a formal upper-bound guarantee on attack success rate and reports near-zero ASR across 3 LLMs, 6 attack strategies, and multimodal settings, including against white-box adaptive adversaries, while preserving task accuracy.

### 2. [Beyond Static Interpretability: Anticipating Post-SFT Mechanisms from Pre-SFT Parameters for Better Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24482)

- Points out a blind spot in "locate-then-tune" interpretability-guided fine-tuning: interpreting a pre-SFT model is retrospective, so the neurons it identifies as important can differ drastically from the ones that actually end up governing the post-SFT model, biasing the tuning.
- Proposes a forward-looking localization framework that uses Taylor expansion to model SFT as continuous parameter evolution, predicting the *post-SFT* mechanistic state from pre-SFT parameters and the target dataset alone.
- Dual-granularity (neuron- and component-level) pipeline improves SFT guidance and scales robustly across model sizes — an attempt to make mechanistic interpretability predictive rather than purely descriptive.

### 3. [Mechanistic Circuit Identification for Controllable Data Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24065)

- Bridges training-dynamics-based data valuation with mechanistic interpretability: instead of heuristic prompt-based control of synthetic data generation, it identifies model-internal circuits that causally govern three data-utility axes (learnability, challenge, alignment).
- Uses these circuits as a controllable interface to steer generation toward utility-targeted data, then schedules circuit-steered data generation to match the model's evolving optimization needs (SAMS: Stage-Aware Mechanistic Scheduling).
- On multiple-choice QA, yields more diverse, precisely controlled data than prompt-based baselines and consistently improves downstream performance and calibration — mechanistic interpretability used as a practical control lever, not just an analysis tool.

### 4. [Revenge of Monosemanticity: Specialized Neurons Improve Data Efficiency in MLPs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24007)

- Challenges the standard feature-learning picture (a single global low-dimensional predictive representation) by showing that MLPs trained on clustered regression data spontaneously develop monosemantic neurons, each aligned with a specific predictive feature local to a region of input space.
- The result is a collection of local low-dimensional representations that jointly span a high-dimensional space, rather than one global low-dimensional embedding.
- Proves this specialization gives MLPs a provable data-efficiency advantage over methods relying on a single global low-dimensional representation — theoretical support for monosemanticity as a functional, not just interpretability-convenient, property (Belkin lab).

### 5. [Discovering Cross-Language Reasoning Invariance in LLMs with Geometry-Invariant Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23809)

- Asks whether multilingual LLMs solving the same math problem in different languages share underlying features or just produce similar outputs via language-specific computation, using MGSM reasoning traces in 6 languages across 5 models.
- Introduces Geometry-Invariant SAEs (GI-SAE), trained with an added contrastive loss so encoder activations match across languages for the same problem, then tests functional interchangeability by swapping feature values between languages and measuring output KL divergence.
- Key finding: higher geometric similarity between languages' representations does *not* consistently imply functional interchangeability — cross-language feature sharing is highly model/architecture-dependent (helps in Qwen, no benefit in Gemma, mixed in Llama/Phi), a useful caution against equating SAE-feature alignment with shared computation.

### 6. [Generalization, memorization, and overfitting for diffusion models trained in the lazy high-dimensional regime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23938)

- Extends the theory of benign overfitting and algorithmic regularization (developed for overparameterized supervised learning) to score-based generative models, studying denoising score matching in a vector-valued RKHS in the proportional high-dimensional regime (n ≍ d).
- Derives exact risk trajectories under gradient flow and identifies three distinct phases: a generalizing spectral estimator, a pure-noise interpolating estimator, and a memorizing empirical-Bayes estimator.
- Characterizes how these phases combine along the reverse-time sampling SDE, revealing both familiar supervised-learning mechanisms (kernel linearization, self-induced regularization) and phenomena specific to generative models — a rigorous theoretical account of when/why diffusion models generalize versus memorize.

### 7. [Effective Learning Rate Governs Loss Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24814)

- Reports "ELR collapse": loss trajectories in LM pretraining collapse onto each other when runs are matched on the *ratio* of learning rate to parameter norm (the effective learning rate), even with substantially different raw LRs and norms — collapse errors are smaller than seed-to-seed noise.
- Holds across optimizers, architectures, datasets, and scales; identifies normalization design and the timescale of LR-norm variation as the key determinants of how precisely collapse holds.
- Shows weight decay and other norm-control mechanisms act on loss dynamics primarily *through* the ELR schedules they induce, and that swapping LR for ELR lets a single fitted scaling law transfer across different norm-control methods — a unifying coordinate for training-dynamics/scaling-law work.

### 8. [Steering Recurrent Reasoners at Inference Time with Readout Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24136)

- Existing inference-time scaling for recurrent reasoning models (more steps, more sampled trajectories) ignores information already revealed within a single trajectory's intermediate predictions.
- Introduces Readout Feedback (RoFB): converts a recurrent model's own intermediate readout probabilities into token-wise pairwise coupling forces fed back into the latent dynamics, without retraining.
- Across three recurrent architectures (AKOrN, ItrSA++, TRM) on Sudoku/Maze, gets gains unattainable by simply running more steps or trajectories, at comparable or lower cost — a lightweight, training-free steering mechanism for latent reasoning dynamics.

### 9. [Parameter-Level Attribution of Symmetry in Trained Networks Through Parameter-Wise Functional Sensitivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24700)

- Formalizes when a learned function's symmetry (e.g., rotational invariance) can be "lifted" into an actual motion through parameter space, via a condition on whether the symmetry orbit's tangent space lies in the image of the network's parameter-to-function differential.
- Derives, per parameter, two interpretable local directions from least-squares relaxation of this condition: one following the symmetry orbit, one descending toward the equivariant subspace — giving a parameter-level attribution of "how much" each weight contributes to (versus deviates from) a symmetry.
- Validated on a rotationally-invariant classifier and Hamiltonian neural networks (including one where equivariance isn't architecturally enforced) — a fine-grained tool connecting parameter attribution to functional/geometric structure in trained networks.

### 10. [Semantic Overlays: Mitigating Prompt Injection with Annotations Beyond Tokens and Steering Vectors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23873)

- Diagnoses prompt injection as a consequence of LLMs having no channel to track span identity (user input vs. tool output vs. instructions) beyond the text itself, which attackers can scramble.
- Introduces Semantic Overlays: small learned adapters applied at chosen prefill positions on a frozen model's residual stream, creating an out-of-band annotation channel that can't be replicated by tokens — trained, composable, and selectively applied, unlike static steering vectors.
- An overlay marking a span "non-executable" pushes SEP separation from 24.3%→96.5%, TensorTrust ASR from 34.8%→6.6%, and all four PIArena attack families to 0% compliance, with marked content staying 92.5% exactly readable — a steering-vector-adjacent defense that generalizes to complex asserted semantics (e.g., "this span is written in a different language than it appears").
