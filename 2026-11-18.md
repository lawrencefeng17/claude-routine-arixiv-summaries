# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-18

Source: [cs.LG new listings for Wed, 18 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/list/cs.LG/new) (107 new submissions + 104 cross-lists; replacements excluded). Summaries are based on the abstracts.

## Top 10

### 1. [Chain-of-Thought Controllability Can Reduce Chain-of-Thought Monitorability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21799)
- Stress-tests CoT monitoring via **persona-control training**: SFT on math traces rewritten in diverse personas' voices — no monitor-evasion examples at all.
- This generalizes into a broad ability to shape CoT: on covert side tasks, gpt-oss-20b monitorability (g-mean²) drops 0.94 → 0.68 with no hiding instruction, → 0.22 when told to hide; extends to a Kimi-K2.6 organism in agentic settings.
- Longer/multi-step hidden objectives stay more monitorable; targeted **transparency prompts can recover** monitorability, sometimes beyond baseline. Model organisms released.

### 2. [Large-scale Neuron Community Discovery Reveals Neuron-Sparse Behaviors in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23112)
- Unsupervised method: gradient attribution → co-attribution graph → community detection, run on Llama-3.1-8B over **10M prompts**, yielding thousands of candidate neuron communities.
- Communities pass held-out causal tests (necessity, within-layer sufficiency, layer necessity) and implement agreement, calibration, fixed phrases, in-context copying, addition, multiplication.
- With ≤128 neurons, ~10% of held-out prompts have at least one layer explained neuron-sparsely (3.3% for ≥12 layers) — reusable components exist but coverage is limited.

### 3. [Latent Fact Composition under Next-Token Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21921)
- Studies latent two-hop reasoning where the bridge entity never appears in two-hop training examples, under **full next-token loss** (not answer-only) on a synthetic corpus where every loss term is exactly computable.
- Standard/looped transformers fail; new **Baton** looped transformers (pass bridge entity as a token embedding) succeed under answer-only training; three design conditions explain success/failure.
- Under every-token training, accuracy **rises then declines**; gradient decomposition shows the decline isn't from dataset-specific terms and arises in the second hop while both facts are still recalled.

### 4. [Margin Dynamics for Large Language Model Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21766)
- Pairwise preference methods → vector fields on the simplex; projected to a closed-form 1-D **margin ODE**.
- Predictions: DPO margins grow unboundedly (O(log t)), IPO converges to 1/(2τ); label noise causes a **phase transition** in DPO (unbounded → bounded) but only quantitative change in IPO; gradient concentration governs trainability. Confirmed on 5 model families.
- Shows label-smoothed SimPO has a finite margin attractor; the theory-derived config gets top IFEval on three 8–9B models.

### 5. [How Many Training Labels Are Wrong? Auditing a Fitted Model through Gradient Allocation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22449)
- Data-attribution-flavored auditing: normalize per-example gradient magnitudes into an **allocation** and flag examples that fit their nearest other class better. Threshold-free; no clean data or retraining.
- Even after the model fits 99–100% of wrong labels, AUC 0.94–0.96 on CIFAR-10N vs 0.73–0.76 for loss/grad-norm/self-influence; on briefly trained models the noise-rate estimate is within 1.7pp of truth.

### 6. [Disambiguating Moral Circuits and Moral Features in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21705)
- 227 validated minimal pairs across moral vs. other norms (prudence, etiquette, aesthetics, evidence) vs. descriptive, on 14 open models (1B–14B).
- The heads that flip moral answers form a **general normative-evaluation circuit**, not a moral-specific one (effect ratio ≈1.00); ablating them removes 37–39% of ETHICS separation vs 1–3% for random heads.
- Located **SAE features mostly track the cue word "morally"**, not content — a caution for feature-level claims.

### 7. [What Does a Bias Head Measure? Non-Identifiability in Head-Level Bias Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23218)
- Audits head-localization-and-ablation debiasing on Qwen-2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B. Head maps correlate strongly with response length, but interventions show **prompt identity explains 64%** of variance, length only 1%.
- Within a prompt, fairness label, length, and protected-token presence are collinear (φ=0.95), so the statistic is **non-identifiable** observationally. No head set reduced unfairness when ablated; the complement reliably destroys refusals.

### 8. [What MoE Routing Controls: Causal Interventions on Expert Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22120)
- Steers Qwen3-30B-A3B by biasing router logits toward expert paths associated with a concept or format.
- **Format paths change generated text** (and self-reinforce once started); **concept paths shift next-token probabilities** (even cross-lingually) but barely change text. Effects concentrate in middle layers; religion and money steer most.

### 9. [Ambient LLMs: Data-Efficient Supervised Fine-tuning via Temperature Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23229)
- Fights SFT overfitting/forgetting of distribution tails: fine-tuned model's **high-temperature** conditionals must match the frozen pretrained model's high-temperature conditionals.
- Temperature interpolates between SFT (T→∞) and distillation (T=1); up to 21% average relative gain over SFT across domain adaptation, instruction tuning, and reasoning.

### 10. [Conserved Verdicts, Drifting Commitments: Identifying Harmful Overthinking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21946)
- Distinguishes **commitment** (P(correct if stopped now)) from **verdict** (P(final answer correct)); proves the verdict, which final-correctness probes estimate, is a martingale and can't decide when to stop. The gap equals the value of stopping.
- On MATH-500, when R1-Distill-1.5B holds a correct answer, continuing loses 3.6 pts on average. Across 6 models, declining accuracy-vs-budget curves are mostly a **selection effect** (which chains are still running), not chains degrading; oracle stopping gains ≤2.5 pts.

## Honorable Mentions

- [Get Hint-Augmented GRPO Done Right Through Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23124) — learns a steering vector capturing the hint-induced behavior shift so hints aren't needed at inference; gains on AMC/AIME/GPQA.
- [Large Language Models as Nonlinear Dynamical Systems: The Three Clocks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21983) — dictionary mapping dynamics concepts to depth/training/generation; repetition-loop escape follows an Arrhenius law.
- [SOTA or Luck? The Winner's Curse in LLM Leaderboards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22227) — m-out-of-N bootstrap corrects rank-induced bias; 13 of 173 SWE-bench Verified models tie for the top tier.
- [Higher-Scoring Coding Models Did Not Get More Work Done](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23216) — natural experiment (export-control suspension): no significant PR merge-rate gain from higher-benchmark models.
- [Do LLM Agents Still Need to Be Told How to Self-Improve?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23219) — capable frozen agents invent their own self-learning loops in games; artifacts transfer.
- [RSI-Prime: Training Models to Train Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138) — 350 training-experiment trajectories with decision-level loss masking; +32% relative on PostTrainBench.
- [What RL on Expert-Built Tasks Teaches Nemotron 3.5 Super](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22161) — per-domain LoRA RL adapters with qualitative analysis of what behaviors each learns.
- [Weight-Space Programming Doesn't Need Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22055) — LLMs writing weights by hand choose interpretable sparse lookup tables over transformers.
- [What's in a Tree? Hierarchical n-gram models as controlled approximators of language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23093) — tree-grams as tunable synthetic language for μP transfer and fine-tuning studies.
- [Do Diffusion Models Learn to Generalize Basic Visual Skills?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22427) — controlled testbed: weak extrapolation; combination diversity beats dataset size for compositional generalization.
- [Lost in a Few Heads: Diagnosing and Repairing Block Selection in Sparse Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21917) — a few "blind heads" cause most sparse-attention retrieval loss; full attention there recovers it.
