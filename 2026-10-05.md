# arXiv cs.LG Digest — 2026-10-05

Top 10 papers from today's cs.LG listing (297 entries, including cross-lists) most related to ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, generalization, interpretability, data attribution, and training dynamics. All 297 titles were scanned; the top 10 and the first few honorable mentions were read at the abstract level.

### 1. [From Behavior to Provenance: Attributing Tabular Foundation Models to Synthetic Pretraining Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02347)

- Argues controlled synthetic pretraining makes training-data attribution experimentally testable: every pretraining task carries explicit lineage (mechanisms, confounding, shortcuts, shift).
- Combines behavior-conditioned attribution with counterfactual retraining. Removing the top-attributed 5% of tasks drops ROC-AUC by 0.013 vs 0.002 for random removal; removing bottom-attributed tasks helps slightly.
- Provenance discrimination is only modest (AUROC 0.55–0.62), so provenance association and interventional faithfulness need not coincide.

### 2. [When Does Synthetic Relational Data Teach Models to Use Relations?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03057)

- Frames "which property of synthetic pretraining data induces relational computation?" as a data-attribution problem, using four Relational Transformers trained identically on different generators.
- Hypothesis: relational mechanisms emerge when cross-table information is predictively necessary for the masked-cell objective.
- The model trained on the generator with the largest foreign-key predictive gain is uniquely sensitive to FK interventions, and this localizes to a serial cross-table pathway. Disrupting that mechanism removes its advantage.

### 3. [What Is Lost in Post-Training? Default Collapse and the Loss of In-Context Steerability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02614)

- Fine-tuning toward one side of cultural-value disagreements makes that side dominant by default, while the ability to recognize and enact the opposing view declines.
- Post-training thus degrades in-context steerability, not just default behavior.
- Proposes reward maximization subject to a prescribed distribution over expressed perspectives (stance-distribution matching).

### 4. [All Work And No Play Makes Jack a Dull Boy: Catastrophic Strategy Collapse in RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03835)

- Late-stage GRPO-style collapse is a harmful contraction of effective strategy capacity, not benign pruning.
- Proves RLVR objectives concentrate probability mass on a single strategy, while sustaining accuracy needs a minimum strategy capacity. This conflict explains the collapse.
- Offers the Mirrored Entanglement Index as an online warning signal and "Mesh Learning" to keep multiple strategies alive (up to +13.4 pp on reasoning benchmarks).

### 5. [Learning Style, Forgetting Semantics: A Case Study of SFT and RFT on Classification Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02437)

- Asks why SFT forgets more than RFT even when demonstrations are semantically correct.
- In a linear-softmax policy, semantic updates are parallel but style dynamics differ: SFT with a nonuniform teacher drifts off-axis in style, RFT preserves symmetry.
- Proves a strictly positive lower bound on SFT forgetting while RFT retains zero semantic error under stated conditions.

### 6. [Drive vs. Decay: On the Training Dynamics of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02344)

- Early-training stability theory for JEPA collapse: linearizing around the trivial fixed point gives a driving force vs a decay effect, with a per-mode stability ratio.
- Predicts a phase boundary (confirmed across 800+ configurations) and unifies predictor scaling, masking ratio, and EMA as ways to shift the ratio.
- Introduces ResidualPred (identity-biased attention predictor), which improves effective rank and accuracy.

### 7. [Muon Learns Facts Better: Understanding the Role of Spectral Orthogonalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02798)

- Tractable factual-recall model comparing gradient flow, spectral GF (Muon-like), and sign GF (Adam-like).
- Plain GF learns relation info before subject info (learning-time ratio ~√(S/R)). Spectral GF removes the separation (ratio ~1) and gives much faster error decay.
- GF and spectral GF are equivariant to orthogonal embedding transforms, while sign GF is not, so Adam-like dynamics can depend on the embedding basis.

### 8. [Certified Mechanistic Edits: Behavioral Guarantees for Skill Removal and Preservation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03502)

- Certifies the behavioral effect of an edit (ablation, weight edit, steering): one skill is removed and another provably preserved for every input in a region.
- Demonstrated from toy ReLU nets up to a softmax + LayerNorm transformer using sound bound propagation.
- Proves no finite deterministic black-box test can certify removal, via an edit that passes exhaustive testing but fails on an arbitrarily small "survivor pocket."

### 9. [LESSER: Post-Training Data Selection with Output-Layer Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03702)

- Output-layer gradients suffice for gradient-alignment data selection and need only a forward pass.
- Cuts feature-extraction cost 9.7× for SFT and 3.0× for RL while tracking full-gradient performance.
- Even when per-sample rankings differ, the selected batches have aligned gradients.

### 10. [Divergence Controls Entropy in Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03529)

- Proves forward KL (and so cross-entropy) inflates student entropy above the teacher's. Reverse KL deflates it until the gap grows too large.
- The lower entropy of on-policy distillation comes from token-level reverse KL, not on-policy sampling.
- The divergence acts as an implicit entropy regularizer, which matters for self-distillation with privileged information.

## Honorable mentions

- [Frequency Is Not Sensitivity: Identifying Safety-Sensitive Experts in Sparse MoE LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02910) — router-gradient sensitivity finds experts whose suppression cuts refusals more than activation frequency does (OLMoE: 34→9 of 100).
- [The Fragility of Trigger-Tag Mechanisms for Misuse Detection in Open-Weight LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03124) — attacks render token- and weight-level trigger-tags ineffective.
- [Predicting and Repairing Merge Collapse in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03199) — task-vector variance predicts merge collapse. Sign-conflict statistics are anti-predictive.
- [Does Physics Live in the Activations? Localizing Physical Quantities in Video Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03154) — linear probes decode physics early in denoising, and the probe directions can steer outputs.
- [Prompted to Discriminate: Generalizing Malicious-Input Probes in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02413) — appending a classification suffix improves out-of-distribution activation-probe detection.
- [Do ResNets Route? Sparse Interaction Experts in Residual Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02907) — Möbius decomposition shows dense ResNets do implicit soft routing.
- [OptiSelect: How does the Optimizer Shape Data Curriculum?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03432) — optimizer-aware online data selection. Lion/Muon preconditioners cap the achievable gains.
- [Fisher-Guided Submodular Data Selection for Continual Pre-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02593) — Fisher-aware selection mitigates forgetting with 10× token efficiency over replay.
