# arXiv cs.LG — Top Picks for 2026-11-01

Note: arXiv does not announce on weekends, so the "recent" cs.LG listing still showed Fri, 30 Oct 2026 (372 entries) as its latest day. The picks below are drawn from that list and **exclude papers already featured in the 2026-10-30 digest**. Slim day for core alignment topics; picks lean toward safety evaluation, interpretability, and training dynamics.

## Top 10

### 1. [Surprise Alone Learns Lies: Evidence-Gated Continual Learning for LLMs from Streaming Claims](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39840)
- Surprise-based data selection for continual learning is unsafe when the stream contains falsehoods: a planted lie about known facts is highly surprising by construction, so the learner absorbs it at about the rate of random selection.
- Tested with LoRA students (Qwen2.5, Llama-3.2) on streams of true/false claims (invented people, CounterFact).
- A verifier gate removes lies only as well as the verifier is accurate, and real LLM judges are overly cautious, rejecting unsourced truths and source-backed updates.
- Relevant to data poisoning and what models learn from untrusted data.

### 2. [Rethinking LLM Jailbreak Evaluation: Continuous Risk Landscapes Beyond Binary Outcomes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38595)
- Argues attack success rate (binary) hides the distribution over generations a jailbreak prompt induces.
- Proposes a continuous, context-conditioned risk landscape over first-token branches, estimated by fixing openings and sampling continuations.
- Yields two measures: current decoder exposure and safe rerouting capacity, which binary metrics obscure.
- A more faithful way to measure safety robustness.

### 3. [Closed Gates Leak: Decomposing Adjacent-Layer Cancellation in Gated Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39068)
- Revisits why adjacent transformer layers write against each other in the residual stream.
- Earlier view: the next layer selectively rejects features the previous layer proposed.
- Finding: much of the "damping" comes from neurons whose gates are *closed*. SiLU/GELU are slightly negative below zero, so a closed gate still passes a small negative multiple of its input.
- Cautions against over-reading cancellation as deliberate feature rejection in mechanistic analyses.

### 4. [Spokes, not a Ring: Revisiting the Weekday Manifold in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37796)
- Tests the assumption that multi-dimensional concept manifolds are continuous.
- Weekday representations behave like seven discrete states arranged in a circle: sensitivity has seven minima, and on-manifold steering gives a step-like change in behavior.
- No other concepts (time of day, uncertainty, etc.) were found along the manifold between the discrete points.
- Implications for manifold-based interpretation and steering.

### 5. [Factorized Representations of Concurrent Temporal Variables in Pretrained Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40328)
- Probes how LMs track several independently evolving context variables (language-switch and topic-change timers).
- Both are linearly decodable from the same hidden states across ten checkpoints. Ablating one timer's centroid plane hurts that variable but barely the other, though the planes are non-orthogonal.
- The structure persists across 3 to 51 timer values and across Qwen3 sizes.
- Evidence for factorized, partly shared internal state.

### 6. [One Vector or Many? Clustered Optimal-Transport Steering under Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40055)
- Compares difference-of-means (DiM) activation steering with cluster-wise optimal-transport steering (CHaRS) for toxicity mitigation in Gemma-2-2B and Granite-3.1-2B.
- Proves the cluster-size-weighted average of the cluster displacements equals DiM, so the two differ only for tokens whose routing weights depart from cluster sizes.
- Also checks whether the benefit survives INT8/NF4 quantization.
- Clarifies what the more elaborate steering adds over a single vector.

### 7. [Jailbreak Connectivity: Towards Diverse, Transferable, and Universal Multimodal LLM Jailbreak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38091)
- Observes that effective adversarial images are connected through low-loss regions, and optimizes continuous adversarial paths rather than isolated examples.
- Path sampling gives diverse jailbreaks, surrogate classifiers aid transfer, and a distribution-level objective gives universal attacks.
- Reports ASR of 84%/86%/98% on Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B and Gemma-3-12B, beating baselines by up to 68 points.
- Red-teaming evidence that MLLM safeguards are brittle.

### 8. [Representation Complexity Along Learning Trajectories: Trading Width for Precision and Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39027)
- Asks why overparameterization helps during learning, given networks traverse transient representations more complex than the final predictor needs.
- Uses an oracle model and Kolmogorov n-widths to show the complexity needed to make a target statistically visible through gradient feedback can exceed that needed to predict it.
- Combined with statistical-query lower bounds, gives a task-agnostic lower bound on the initial effective width.
- Theory of training dynamics.

### 9. [Rethinking GFlowNet Training: Negative Reinforce Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38994)
- The full gradient of GFlowNet balance losses includes an anti-exploratory mechanism that standard semi-gradient training ignores.
- Shows it corresponds to min-min dynamics between the GFlowNet and a decoupled sampler, which reduces observed errors by avoiding high-error regions.
- Motivates a bridge between two-player games and single-policy training.
- Relevant to exploration and RL-style training dynamics.

### 10. [TrussTutor-Bench: Benchmarking LLMs on Solution Accuracy, Sycophancy, and Error Detection in Statics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38917)
- Benchmark of 200 verified truss problems paired with automatically generated student solutions.
- Evaluates 13 LLMs on solving, confirming correct work when a student doubts it (sycophancy), and finding errors in flawed work.
- Shows final-answer accuracy alone does not capture the capabilities tutoring needs.
- A concrete sycophancy measurement in a domain with ground truth.

## Honorable mentions
- [Formalizing the Intrinsic Dimension of Objective Landscapes by a Generalized Notion of Co-dimension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39674) — formalizes the Li et al. intrinsic dimension of loss landscapes as a generalized co-dimension, with bounds for student-teacher ReLU networks.
- [Bouba Beats Kiki, and Other Regularities in the Subjective Preferences of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37692) — studies how LLMs choose among equally valid options (numbers, baby names, cities) and identifies 29 aspects driving their preferences.
- [Weaken to Strengthen: Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38681) — uses a quantized or pruned copy of the model as a weaker contrast for self-distillation, with no privileged information.
- [Learning to Reason from Reference Samples Alone: Reinforced Adversarial Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38851) — a co-trained LLM judge turns reference answers into the sole reward for on-policy reasoning post-training; matches RLVR on verifiable tasks.
- [Prompt-agnostic Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37683) — finds paraphrase gradients conflict in sign for 57% of parameters, and applies TIES/DARE-style merging to prompt gradients for robust instruction tuning.
