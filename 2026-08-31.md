## Top 10

### 1. [Memorization Is Not Extraction: Tight Differential-Privacy Bounds and Audit Blind Spots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27782)

- Pins down exact differential-privacy constants for two distinct, practically-important notions — counterfactual memorization and adaptive extraction — and proves neither controls the other, contrary to how DP is usually treated as a single proxy for "memorization risk."
- Derives tight bounds (e.g. the extraction-advantage cap $\tanh(\varepsilon/2)$ under pure DP, with a closed-form staircase correction for duplicated data) and shows the two measures provably diverge inside the local-score class used in real audits.
- Demonstrates the resulting blind spot on billion-parameter models: a reserved secret is recovered verbatim from a single prompt while every loss-based memorization audit in current use certifies the model clean — a sharp warning for anyone relying on standard unlearning/memorization verification.

### 2. [Quantization-Triggered Backdoors in Language Models: Cross-Quantizer Transferability and the Validation–Deployment Gap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27512)

- Formalizes "Quantization Behavioral Equivalence Classes" and proves that passing full-precision safety checks does not imply behavioral equivalence after INT8/4-bit compression, giving a theoretical basis for backdoors that lie dormant until quantized.
- Embeds latent payloads via a three-stage adversarial fine-tuning process that satisfies source-precision certification yet activates targeted misbehavior post-quantization — e.g. translation models flip from zero measured corruption at FP16 to 85% inversion after quantization, and a stance classifier shifts ideologically by Δ=0.33.
- Shows attack persistence depends on the specific quantization scheme and architecture, not nominal bit-width — a concrete, testable case of behavior that is invisible under standard validation and only emerges under a specific downstream transformation, structurally similar to sleeper-agent-style threats.

### 3. [How Do Linear Probes Emerge? A Circuit-Tracing Framework with Concept-Targeted Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27510)

- Introduces Concept-Targeted Attribution (CTA): rather than tracing attribution graphs to next-token logits, it trains them against a linear probe direction, yielding circuits that explain *why* an internal concept representation arises, independent of whether it's ever expressed in output.
- Graph-level features from these probe-targeted circuits predict probe accuracy across four concept categories (ρ=0.91), and causal ablations show probe-targeted and logit-targeted circuits are functionally distinct — removing probe-relevant features kills the internal concept score while leaving generated tokens intact, and vice versa.
- Moves interpretability from "does this probe work" to "which internal computations make it work," directly useful for auditing safety-relevant internal representations that a model may hold without ever emitting them.

### 4. [Towards a mathematical theory of superposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27540)

- Builds a rigorous theory of neural superposition using frame theory and compressed sensing: sparse features are encoded via an overcomplete dictionary and recovered via ReLU(WᵀWx+b), directly formalizing the toy-model-of-superposition picture from mechanistic interpretability.
- Proves high-probability support-recovery theorems for random-support sparsity (up to order d/log n) and derives a sharp, computable criterion for worst-case support recovery, applied concretely to Gaussian random matrices and equiangular tight frames.
- Determines the exact recovery threshold for real equiangular tight frames in terms of coherence — a genuinely new mathematical result (via a novel Gram-matrix sign-distribution characterization) that puts hard limits on how much superposition a network can pack into given dimensionality before features become unrecoverable.

### 5. [Landau theory of quenched criticality in linear in-context learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28059)

- Reformulates the well-known double-descent singularity in linear in-context learning as a critical phenomenon of a quenched disordered system, using statistical-physics machinery (cavity method, Landau potential) rather than treating it as a numerical curiosity.
- Identifies sample-to-sample fluctuations of learned parameters as the microscopic origin of the singular error, with an order parameter (renormalized ridge parameter ξ) whose critical exponents (β,δ,γ)=(1,2,1) are derived and independently confirmed by numerics.
- Predicts a pseudogap-like regime in the large-context limit where the order parameter is suppressed — a rare instance of a fully worked statistical-physics theory of a generalization phenomenon (interpolation criticality) that's normally only observed empirically.

### 6. [A Deeper Analysis of Block-Sparse Featurizers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27515)

- Follows up on the recently-introduced block-sparse featurizer (BSF), an SAE variant whose atomic unit is a small subspace rather than a single direction, designed for features on low-dimensional manifolds (common in vision).
- Finds BSFs still suffer classic SAE failure modes — feature splitting and composition — despite the block structure, and proposes a Tournament Top-K selection rule that substantially reduces feature splitting.
- Extends the block paradigm to crosscoders — incremental but concrete progress on making sparse dictionary-learning interpretability tools more reliable.

### 7. [More Data Cannot Break a Symmetry: Identifiability by Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27651)

- Shows that unsupervised representational alignment (recovering stimulus-by-stimulus correspondence from geometry alone) is fundamentally bounded by the automorphism group of the stimulus geometry — a limit that exists before any data is collected, and that no amount of additional data can fix.
- Demonstrates the failure is structural, not statistical: with a symmetric design, 64× the restart budget leaves the degenerate solution unmoved, while an asymmetric design at equal N recovers reliably every time; discriminating representational models and recovering correspondence turn out to be essentially uncorrelated objectives (r=-0.02).
- Turns this into an actionable pre-registration diagnostic — checking a design's symmetry group before data collection cuts catastrophic alignment failures from 75% to 2% across 93 model representations, directly relevant to identifiability arguments used across representational/model comparison research.

### 8. [GRACE: Gradient-guided Coreset Selection for LLM Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28361)

- Tackles a realistic unlearning setting where only a handful of examples of undesired behavior are given, and the actual forget/retain sets must be inferred from a heterogeneous corpus rather than assumed pre-specified.
- Computes a "forget direction" from seed examples via non-negative orthogonal matching pursuit on gradients, then selects a compact forget coreset aligned with that direction, and a retain coreset chosen after projecting the forget direction out of gradient space.
- A concrete data-attribution-flavored approach to unlearning: gradients are used to trace which training examples causally drive an undesired behavior, improving utility preservation over prior gradient-based selection methods across two domains, two model families, and four unlearning algorithms.

### 9. [CURA: Certified Runtime Alarms for Computer-Use Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27808)

- Documents a stark oversight failure in capable computer-use agents: on 361 OSWorld tasks, 90% of the agent's own failures ended with a self-reported success claim, and an explicit "failure" affordance was used essentially never (0 times in ~9,100 calls) — self-report is cheap but fails exactly where oversight matters most.
- Introduces an external monitor reading only harness-visible telemetry (no model internals, no extra LLM calls) that frames the running trajectory as a sequential statistical test with certified false-alarm control, catching 42% of failures a median of 31 steps before termination.
- A rare empirically-grounded study of AI oversight infrastructure with formal guarantees (bounded false-alarm rate) rather than heuristic monitoring — directly relevant to scalable oversight and agent safety research.

### 10. [Not to Break, but to Attest: Adversarial Probes for Privacy-Preserving LLM Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27954)

- Addresses AI governance under proprietary weights: how can a third party verify that a deployed model hasn't silently changed since approval, without the deployer revealing the weights?
- Proposes zk-SNARK-verified adversarial probes (in the spirit of adversarial examples) that amplify logit drift between an approved and modified model, with token-based (black-box), embedding-based (gray-box), and stress (extended-interface) probe families trading off sensitivity against access requirements.
- Shows the black-box token-based probes are consistently most sensitive despite requiring the least access, and that the ZK proving pipeline stays practical (≈1–2s proving, ~0.84s verification) as the probe set scales to 50 — a concrete building block for auditable AI deployment commitments.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[Exact Risk Ratios for Weighted Data Selection in Linear Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28007)** — resolves several cases of a COLT 2025 open problem on the worst-case loss ratio achievable by selecting and weighting a subset of training data for least-squares ERM, with matching upper/lower bounds and polynomial-time selection algorithms — core data-attribution/data-selection theory.
- **[Biologically Inspired Mechanisms for Facilitating Grokking in Multilayer Perceptrons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28184)** — systematically ablates biologically-inspired regulatory mechanisms (homeostasis, structural plasticity, lateral inhibition, etc.) in MLPs, finding homeostasis and structural sparsification most reliably accelerate the memorization-to-generalization transition on grokking benchmarks.
- **[Curvature-Conditioned Multiscale Momentum with Sphere Constraints for LLM Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28442)** — targets the slow progress along flat loss-landscape directions that dominates late-stage pretraining loss reduction, accelerating Muon across dense/MoE architectures from 0.12B–2.3B parameters via a curvature-aware multiscale momentum with a sphere constraint to prevent parameter inflation.
- **[When Muon Meets Task Interference: A Spectral Perspective on Continual Learning and Model Merging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27518)** — unifies catastrophic forgetting (continual learning) and weight-disentanglement error (model merging) as the same "task interference" phenomenon, bounded by the optimizer-controllable spectral norm of parameter updates, and shows Muon's spectral-norm control improves both settings.
- **[Deriving Scaling Laws for OpenEuroLLM Models: Learning Rate, Batch Size and Loss](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28308)** — a large open-sourced empirical study of jointly-optimal and marginal learning-rate/batch-size scaling under Warmup-Stable-Decay schedules, characterizing both undertraining and overtraining regimes.
- **[OpenStamp: A Watermark for Open-Source Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27899)** — encodes watermarking logic directly into model weights (only the final unembedding layer) so it survives white-box access, showing stronger robustness to paraphrasing and fine-tuning scrubbing than prior open-source watermarks.
- **[The Calls are Coming from Inside the Model: Investigating Probe-based Detection of Tool-Calling Errors in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27750)** — trains linear probes on hidden states across 18 tool-calling LLMs to catch incorrect tool calls (including wrong-value-right-type argument errors invisible to standard logging), finding probes generalize to novel error types.
- **[How Proper Scoring Rules Shape LLM Forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28482)** — trains LLM forecasters under five different proper scoring rules and finds that, despite sharing the same truthful-reporting incentive in theory, each rule produces a different profile of calibration, bias, information, and noise — reward choice shapes *how* a model is wrong, not just how often.
