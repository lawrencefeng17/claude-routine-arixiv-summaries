# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — June 3, 2026

*Topics: machine learning, language models, AI safety & alignment, interpretability, training dynamics, generalization*

## Top 10 Papers

### 1. Neuron Populations Exhibit Divergent Selectivity with Scale
**arXiv:2606.03990**

Studies "Rosetta Neurons" — neurons with consistent activation patterns across independently trained models — in LMs up to 30B and vision models up to 5B parameters.

- Rosetta Neurons grow in absolute count but shrink as a fraction of total neurons with scale
- **Neuron Polarization Effect**: Rosetta Neurons become increasingly monosemantic (single-concept) at larger scale, while the broader neuron population becomes *less* selective
- Sublinear power-law scaling explained analytically by the tension between feature utility and neuron capacity
- Domain specialization of these neurons also increases with scale; demonstrated practical use for targeted data filtering in continued pretraining

### 2. How Quantization Changes Interpretable Features: A Sparse Autoencoder Analysis of Language Models
**arXiv:2606.03002**

Uses sparse autoencoders (SAEs) as measurement tools to track which interpretable features survive post-quantization in Pythia-70M and Gemma-2-2B across INT4–INT8.

- Feature degradation is **graded**, not binary: 62.4% of features survive at INT6 on Pythia-70M, 51.3% on Gemma-2-2B
- Feature survival is highly predictable from full-precision statistics alone (cross-validated AUC 0.92–0.97); peak activation is the strongest predictor
- Critical finding: **perplexity improvement ≠ feature preservation** — INT7 improved perplexity on Gemma-2-2B while damaging 18.7% of features
- Quantization and magnitude pruning damage strongly overlapping feature sets, suggesting shared vulnerabilities

### 3. The Shape of Addition: Geometric Structures of Arithmetic in Large Language Models
**arXiv:2606.03645**

Mechanistic analysis of the residual stream geometry during multi-operand addition, uncovering a novel representational structure underlying LLM arithmetic.

- Identifies the **Iso-Raw-Sum Trajectory (IRST)**: representations anchored by semantic digits and modulated by continuous "carry fibers"
- Proposes a **Noisy Quantization Model**: arithmetic errors are geometric slippages caused by internal neural noise pushing latent carry potentials across quantization thresholds
- Lightweight probes can disentangle coexisting signals (e.g., truth vs. hallucination) from a single activation vector
- Introduces a geometric consistency check for detecting and correcting arithmetic failures at inference time

### 4. When RLHF Fails: A Mechanistic Taxonomy of Reward Hacking, Collapse, and Evaluator Gaming
**arXiv:2606.03238**

Analyzes 1,920 checkpoint transitions across PPO, DPO, and uncertainty-penalized PPO to classify the distinct failure modes of RLHF post-training.

- Builds a **mechanistic taxonomy** of failure modes: proxy over-optimization, proxy-judge misalignment, reward collapse, and evaluator-specific gaming
- Aggressive PPO exhibited 14.45% reward-hacking rate; uncertainty-penalized PPO substantially lower
- Row-level (localized) analysis reveals failures invisible to checkpoint-averaged metrics in ~25% of tested settings
- Predictive model achieves ROC-AUC 0.821 in anticipating reward-hacking events from pre-transition features

### 5. Multi-component Causal Tracing in Large Language Models
**arXiv:2606.03085**

Extends causal tracing — which typically examines one component at a time — to efficiently identify joint subsets of attention heads and MLP neurons most causally responsible for a target behavior.

- Frames multi-component tracing as a continuous optimization problem via soft interventions and metric transformation, bypassing exponential combinatorial search
- Flexible metric support: accuracy, fairness, or any differentiable target
- Outperforms single-component and random-subset baselines on identifying high-impact component sets
- Code released publicly

### 6. Denoise First, Orthogonalize Later: Understanding Momentum in Muon via Spectral Filtering
**arXiv:2606.03899**

Provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why momentum benefits the Muon optimizer, which has recently shown strong empirical performance in LLM pretraining.

- Proves momentum acts as a **spectral filter**: suppresses gradient noise while preserving dominant signal components, widening the spectral gap
- Wider spectral gap stabilizes singular subspaces, making the orthogonalization step more reliable
- Proves that "denoise first, orthogonalize later" ordering strictly dominates the reverse or momentum-free variants
- Empirically validated across diverse tasks including LLM pretraining

### 7. Expert-Aware Causal Tracing of Factual Recall in Sparse MoE Language Models
**arXiv:2606.03780**

Extends causal tracing to sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) models with expert-level granularity, asking which specific experts mediate factual recall.

- Corrupts subject token embeddings, then patches individual experts or expert coalitions to identify which restore correct predictions
- On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, layer 44 and expert L44E069 are consistently critical for factual recall
- On Mixtral-8x7B, signal requires multi-expert updates — localization is **model- and protocol-dependent**, not universal
- Provides a framework for architectural comparison of knowledge storage in MoE vs. dense transformers

### 8. An Asymptotic Theory of Chain-of-Thought in In-Context Learning
**arXiv:2606.03217**

Develops an exact theoretical account of how CoT reasoning depth affects generalization error in-context, using linear regression + random matrix theory.

- Derives **exact formulas** for generalization error as a function of reasoning depth, pretraining data volume, and context length
- Discovers a **sharp phase transition** separating exponential improvement, polynomial improvement, saturation, and overthinking as depth increases
- Deeper reasoning is most beneficial with rich pretraining and context; limited data causes error amplification
- Validated on both learned linear attention and softmax attention models

### 9. Representational Capacity: Geometric Limits on Feature Representation in Transformer Language Models
**arXiv:2606.02765**

Formalizes how model dimension (d_model) limits the number of near-orthogonal feature directions a transformer can represent, grounding the Superposition Hypothesis in a measurable capacity bound.

- Uses embedding matrix orthogonality as an empirical proxy, classifying dozens of open-source LMs into two structural classes
- The Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma substantially *underestimates* actual trained capacity; derives an improved formula based on vector-to-dimension ratios
- Representational capacity is **exponentially sensitive** to orthogonality deviation
- Larger models prioritize tighter orthogonality constraints over raw capacity — revealing a stability-capacity trade-off

### 10. Learning to Solve, Forgetting to Retain: Correct-Set Turnover in RLVR
**arXiv:2606.03087**

Documents a systematic hidden cost in RLVR training: models regress on previously mastered problems while acquiring new ones ("correct-set turnover").

- Establishes the **repair-window principle**: the cost of restoring a regressed problem grows sharply with review delay, creating a narrow low-cost window that standard RLVR ignores
- Proposes a retention-aware review mechanism that reintroduces mastered prompts via pre-rollout batch replacement — no additional rollout overhead
- Consistent improvements over GRPO, DAPO, and replay baselines across 20 benchmarks with Qwen3-VL and Qwen2.5-Math
- Reveals that headline accuracy gains in RLVR can mask substantial capability regression on earlier problems

## Honorable Mentions

- **2606.02628** — *Hallucination Is Linearly Decodable from Mid-Layer Hidden States in Quantized LLMs*: A linear probe on a single mid-network layer achieves 0.904–1.000 AUROC at detecting hallucination across three 7B–8B quantized models; MLP probes rarely beat it, suggesting minimal nonlinear structure in the truthfulness signal.

- **2606.02860** — *Forgetting is Not Erasure: Recovering Latent Knowledge via Transport Keys*: Reframes catastrophic forgetting as interface drift rather than representational loss; compact "transport key" operators aligned via model stitching recover most original-task performance after sequential training.

- **2606.03234** — *Right Makes Might: Aligning Verified Hidden States Empowers RL Reasoning*: Finds that correct reasoning paths naturally cluster in representation space at the anchor token (~0.84 cosine similarity); proposes Hidden-Align, an auxiliary loss encouraging this alignment, yielding 3.8–6.2 pp gains on Qwen models with no overhead.

- **2606.03089** — *Constitutional On-Policy Safe Distillation*: Formalizes why constitutional conditioning collapses teacher distribution in safety-aligned OPSD (geometric leakage under safety boundaries); proposed COPSD achieves stronger safety–helpfulness tradeoff across 12 benchmarks.

- **2606.03979** — *Language Models Need Sleep: Learning to Self-Modify and Consolidate Memories*: Proposes a "Sleep" paradigm for continual LLM learning — on-policy distillation transfers short-term memories to a larger model, followed by RL-driven self-improvement via synthetic data generation.

- **2606.02993** — *Neural Networks Provably Learn Spectral Representations for Group Composition*: Proves that two-layer networks trained on group composition tasks converge toward individual irreducible representations with exponential convergence rates, providing a rigorous theory of feature learning grounded in representation theory.

- **2606.02841** — *Learning Coherent Representations: A Topological Approach to Interpretability*: Introduces "coherence" — a geometric constraint requiring features to activate on contiguous state-space regions — proves coherent matrices preserve topological compatibility between sample and feature spaces, and provides a differentiable training objective enforcing it.
