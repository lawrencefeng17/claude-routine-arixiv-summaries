# arXiv cs.LG Highlights — May 23, 2026

*263 papers submitted. Top 10 picks across ML, LLMs, AI safety, alignment, interpretability, generalization, and training dynamics.*

## Top 10

### 1. Represented Is Not Computed: A Causal Test of Candidate Algorithmic Intermediates in a Transformer
**[2605.22488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22488)** — Ishita Darade, Sushrut Thorat

A sharp mechanistic interpretability result: a transformer trained on base-digit extraction encodes intermediate values detectable by linear probes, but causal interventions show those intermediates **do not flow through the model's actual computation**. The model uses separate pathways that merge only late in processing. Highlights the gap between "what's represented" and "what's computed" — a critical methodological warning for probe-based interpretability.

---

### 2. Post-Training is About States, Not Tokens: A State Distribution View of SFT, RL, and On-Policy Distillation
**[2605.22731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22731)** — Dong Nie

Argues that post-training methods (SFT, RL, distillation) should be understood through **state distributions** (prompt + generated prefix), not just loss functions. Key empirical findings on Qwen3-0.6B-Base / GSM8K:
- Mild SFT improves with minimal forgetting; intensive SFT causes major retention loss
- On-policy distillation from a *degraded* teacher still outperforms that teacher
- Lightweight on-policy RL improves performance while preserving retention

*Where training states originate matters as much as the supervision signal.*

---

### 3. On-Policy Consistency Training Improves LLM Safety with Minimal Capability Degradation
**[2605.21834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21834)** — Andy Han et al.

Proposes **On-Policy Consistency Training (OPCT)**: instead of offline-generated safety data, training objectives are computed from the model's own live responses via contrastive prompts. Results across three safety axes:
- Sycophancy: 15.4% → 8.1%
- Jailbreak resistance: ~99% (vs. 87% for SFT)
- No 28-point math-reasoning regression caused by standard SFT

Relevant to alignment: on-policy generation is a better inductive bias than offline data for safety generalization.

### 4. Reading Task Failure Off the Activations: A Sparse-Feature Audit of GPT-2 Small on Indirect Object Identification
**[2605.22719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22719)** — Mahdi Nasermoghadasi

Uses sparse autoencoder (SAE) features at layer 8 of GPT-2 Small to identify correlates of failure on the IOI task (79.7% accuracy). Found 146 significant features; feature 17,491 (strongest, d=+2.93) fired on "the keys" as transferred object, where the model fails 93.3% of the time. Includes causal ablation controls and releases full code/data. Presents a **lightweight, reproducible interpretability audit pipeline** for failure mode analysis.

### 5. Same Architecture, Different Capacity: Optimizer-Induced Spectral Scaling Laws
**[2605.21803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21803)** — Nandan Kumar Jha, Brandon Reagen

Reveals that **scaling laws depend on the optimizer**, not just architecture. Using spectral-rank metrics on FFN representations:
- AdamW: weak scaling on rare-token representations (β=0.44)
- Muon: near-linear scaling (β=1.02) — a **2.3× improvement**
- Matching validation loss does **not** imply matching representation structure

Positions optimizer choice as a first-class axis of representation scaling — architecture and optimizer should be co-designed.

### 6. Why SGD is not Brownian Motion: A New Perspective on Stochastic Dynamics
**[2605.22644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22644)** — Igor Ignashin et al.

Challenges the standard Langevin/SDE model of SGD. Instead, treats minibatches as inducing a **fluctuating loss landscape** rather than additive noise. Derives a discrete Fokker-Planck equation showing SGD dynamics deviate from standard Langevin at O(η²). Key finding: flat directions along the Hessian's eigenbasis **have no stationary distribution** — variance grows indefinitely, creating effective diffusion along loss valleys. Validated on CV and NLP tasks.

### 7. Value-Gradient Hypothesis of RL for LLMs
**[2605.21654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21654)** — Arip Asadulaev et al.

Provides theoretical grounding for why critic-free RL (PPO, GRPO) works for language models. Under differentiable-rollout + additive-noise conditions, actor updates are shown to function as **value-gradient operations in expectation**, with autodifferentiation through attention producing empirical cost states bounded by sampling gap and policy entropy. Proposes decomposing RL impact into (1) value-gradient signal and (2) reachable reward headroom — useful criteria for when RL adds most during pretraining.

### 8. Relational Linear Properties in Language Models: An Empirical Investigation
**[2605.22532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22532)** — Giovanni Valer, Luigi Gresele, Marco Bronzini, Emanuele Marconato

Investigates **relational linearity** in LMs: can the unembedding of an object (e.g., "trumpet") be predicted from the embedding of its subject (e.g., "Miles Davis") by a fixed linear map for a given relation? Develops a KL-divergence-based probing method more efficient than prior approaches. Findings: relational linearity varies across models, shows consistent layer-wise patterns, and is sensitive to how relations are phrased.

---

### 9. Representation Gap: Explaining the Unreasonable Effectiveness of Neural Networks from a Geometric Perspective
**[2605.21692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21692)** — David Perera et al.

Introduces the **Representation Gap** metric — correlated with generalization error, with more stable asymptotics than prior measures. Using equivariant diffusion models + optimal quantization, they derive an asymptotic formula governed by **intrinsic dimension** (connected to architectural equivariances). Provides a geometric theory of why networks generalize, validated on synthetic and realistic datasets.

---

### 10. Winner-Take-All Bottlenecks Enforce Disentangled Symbolic Representations in Multi-Task Learning
**[2605.22472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22472)** — Julian Gutheil, Simon Hitzginger, Robert Legenstein

Proves that WTA bottlenecks in deep networks extract **categorical latent factors** under multi-task learning conditions, producing representations where individual neurons encode single abstract features (objects, colors, positions). Empirically validated beyond the theoretical assumptions. WTA mechanisms enhance generalization and offer a path toward bridging symbolic and subsymbolic AI.

## Honorable Mentions

- **[2605.22817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22817) Vector Policy Optimization: Training for Diversity Improves Test-Time Search** — RL training method using vector rewards to produce diverse solutions; improves inference-time search, especially as compute budgets grow.

- **[2605.22481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22481) When Stronger Triggers Backfire: A High-Dimensional Theory of Backdoor Attacks** — High-dim analysis showing stronger backdoor triggers *paradoxically* reduce attack success and increase clean accuracy, with the most harmful direction aligning with the minimum eigenvector of data covariance.

- **[2605.21801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21801) Why Semantic Entropy Fails: Geometry-Aware and Calibrated Uncertainty for Policy Optimization (GCPO)** — Identifies anisotropic and calibration gaps in entropy-based uncertainty estimators for LLM RL; proposes geometry-aware calibrated policy optimization.

- **[2605.22703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22703) Clipping Bottleneck: Stabilizing RLVR via Stochastic Recovery of Near-Boundary Signals** — Addresses hard-clipping artifacts in GRPO-style training; stochastic retention of near-boundary tokens recovers lost gradient signals and outperforms DAPO/GSPO on 7B–30B models.
